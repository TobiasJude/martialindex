Frame and Circle

Frame and circle means posting frames on the head, hips, or shoulders, then circling your feet toward center so a Fence Clinch cannot keep you flat on the cage.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Frames first, then feet.
  • Circle toward open space, not into their underhook.
  • Add a whizzer against deep underhooks.
  • Goal is angle, not a blind shove.
  • Escalate to full cage escape when needed.

How Frame and Circle Works

Insert forearm/hand frames → hip in or sit weight briefly → circle the lead foot toward open mat → re-pummel or Clinch Break.

Add a Whizzer when they have a deep underhook; frames alone may not stop a body lock.

Mechanically, successful frame and circle work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

Steering drills (whizzer + head frame) teach the same idea: move their head offline, then walk your hips off the wall.

Common Mistakes

  • Framing with elbows flared into their hooks.
  • Circling into the underhook side blindly.
  • Both hands framing with no underhook threat.
  • Staying flat-footed on the fence.
  • Treating frame and circle as a highlight instead of a position-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down or hips light.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe frame and circle as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor fence awareness, or grips that collapse when strikes enter the round.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is frame and circle?

Posting frames then circling off the fence so clinch pressure cannot keep you flat on the cage.

Vs cage escape?

Frame-and-circle is the first-line peel; Cage Escape covers fuller reverses and stand-ups.

Strikes while framing?

Short framing strikes exist, but priority is structure and angle.

Beginner cue?

One solid forearm frame, step the lead foot off the wall, hands high.
