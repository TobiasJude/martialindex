Arm Triangle (Kata Gatame)

The arm triangle (kata gatame / head-and-arm choke) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu blood choke that traps the opponent’s arm against their own neck while your head, shoulder, and clasped hands supply the pressure—most often finished from mount or side control in gi and no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Arm triangle uses their arm + your shoulder/arms to choke—not a leg figure-four.
  • Mount and side control are the high-percentage homes.
  • Gi and no-gi both; collar can help isolation in gi but is optional.
  • Different tool from the triangle choke (legs).
  • Legal adult IBJJF; tap to blood chokes.

What Is the Arm Triangle?

Also called kata gatame in judo terms, the arm triangle traps one of the opponent’s arms across their neck while you clasp your hands and drive shoulder pressure to compress the carotids. It is a top-game staple and appears in MMA as well as sport BJJ. It is closely tied to mount control.

How Arm Triangle Works

Isolate the head and near arm, get your shoulder under their chin (not on the jaw alone), clasp (gable/S-grip), then dismount to an angle—often forehead toward the mat—while driving with the legs. Depth of the arm wrap before you dismount decides many finishes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar grips can help you force the arm across; the finish still does not require fabric.

No-gi. Same shoulder-and-angle story; expect greasier escapes, so depth before dismount matters more.

Mount culture. Both rule sets finish heavily from mount; side-control entries are common when they turn into you.

Types / Variations

From mount

Classic high-percentage path.

From side control

When they turn in, the head-and-arm appears.

Shoulder and angle details

Chin placement, palm orientation, and angled dismount separate taps from stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter on flared elbows from mount/side. Escape by getting the trapped arm out early, framing, and bridging—tap when the choke is locked.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal adult IBJJF/ADCC.

Safety

Tap to chokes. Do not drive irresponsibly into the cervical spine—shoulder under chin, not neck crank.

Common mistakes

  • Dismounting before the arm is deep.
  • Shoulder on the jaw only.
  • Squeezing arms with no leg drive or angle.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Kata gatame means what?

Judo name often used for this head-and-arm hold/choke family.

Why don’t people tap to mine?

Usually shallow wrap or bad shoulder placement—fix depth and angle before squeezing harder.

Same as darce?

Related head-and-arm family, different body positions. See darce.

Works without a gi?

Yes—very common in no-gi and MMA.

From closed guard?

Possible but far less common than mount/side; guard usually prefers leg triangles.

Safe?

With early taps and clean mechanics, yes.

Vs triangle choke naming?

“Triangle” alone usually means the leg triangle; say arm triangle / kata gatame for this one.

The Bottom Line

The arm triangle (kata gatame) chokes by trapping their arm against their neck under your shoulder pressure—especially from mount and side control. Keep it distinct from the leg triangle choke, and finish with depth plus angle.
