Outside Heel Hook

The outside heel hook is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu rotational leg lock that attacks from outside ashi-style control—trapping the heel and turning through the knee—central to no-gi/heel hook systems, with critical IBJJF vs ADCC legality checks.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Outside ashi geometry + heel seat + knee-line control.
  • Pain can lag ligament damage—tap early culture.
  • Usually illegal in IBJJF gi; confirm no-gi year-by-year.
  • Link to SLX, 50/50, and inside heel hook.
  • Train only under qualified coaches.

What Is Outside Heel Hook?

Outside heel hooks typically appear when you control the opponent’s leg from an outside ashi / SLX-oriented entanglement, isolate the heel (often in the elbow crease), and rotate the foot to torque the knee. Martial Index keeps a live hub at heel hook; this page zooms the outside variant.

Partner pages: inside heel hook, kneebar, single-leg X, 50/50.

How Outside Heel Hook Works

Win outside entanglement, secure knee line so they cannot hide the heel, seat the heel on bone, pinch the foot, then rotate through the hips/shoulders as a unit—not a frantic forearm twist alone.

If the heel slips, re-win control before cranking.

Never “test” flexibility with explosive snaps.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi / IBJJF. Heel hooks are generally illegal in standard IBJJF gi—verify current book.

No-Gi / ADCC-style. Primary habitat for outside heel systems.

Gym policy may allow drilling knowledge even when contest-illegal—follow your coach.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From SLX, Alex enters outside ashi, seats Jordan’s heel, controls the knee line, and applies controlled rotation until Jordan taps immediately.

Under IBJJF gi rules the same position might threaten ankle lock/toe hold instead.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: SLX, outside ashi, K-guard, some 50/50 transitions.

Counters: hide heel, clear knee line, stand with inside position carefully, hand-fight the grip.

Escapes: relieve rotation without inventing worse knee angles—tap early.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Heel hooks and related knee-line controls differ by organization. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Reaping definitions also vary—do not invent legality from gym lore.

Safety

Ligament injuries can precede loud pain. Slow pressure; instant release; coach oversight required.

Common mistakes

  • Cranking without knee-line control.
  • Training explosive snaps.
  • Assuming IBJJF legality.
  • Ignoring ankle-lock backups when rules ban hooks.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an outside heel hook?

A rotational knee attack from outside ashi-style control that traps the heel.

Legal in IBJJF gi?

Generally no—verify current rulebook.

Vs inside?

Different entanglement geometry; both torque the knee via the heel.

Where to learn?

Only with qualified coaches and safety culture.

Related positions?

SLX, outside ashi, K-guard.

Defense first principle?

Protect heel and knee line early.

Backup when illegal?

Straight ankle lock / toe hold per rules.

The Bottom Line

Outside heel hook is SLX-side rotation: win the entanglement, seat the heel, control the knee line, rotate as a unit. Read heel hook, compare inside, and verify rules.
