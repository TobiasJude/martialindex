Guard Passing

Guard passing is the Brazilian jiu-jitsu skill of clearing the bottom player's legs and stabilizing a pin—side control, mount, or north-south—using pressure, speed, or connection-based strategies.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Pass = clear the legs + stabilize (not just step over once).
  • Major styles: pressure, speed, and connection/body-lock families.
  • Gi grips change timing; no-gi favors wrestling connection and athleticism.
  • Passing without a pin plan gets re-guarded—connect to the hips.
  • Opposing skill: guard retention.

What Is Guard Passing?

Guard passing is the top player's project against any bottom guard: remove the legs as obstacles and establish a stable dominant position. A flashy step-over that gets re-guarded is not a completed pass in sport scoring or in good training habits.

How Guard Passing Works

You control distance, clear frames, choose a path (around, under, or through the legs), and connect to the torso/hips so they cannot recover hooks. Styles differ in tempo and tools, but the end state is the same: past the legs with control.

Bottom player's job. Retain and recover using frames, angles, and layers—see guard retention.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve/collar/pants grips enable toreando, knee cut, and stack systems with slower, stickier control.

No-gi. Body locks, underhooks, and athletic speed passes dominate; fewer cloth anchors.

MMA context. Passing under strikes favors tight elbows and posture that avoids upkicks.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex addresses Jordan's open guard, chooses a knee-cut path, clears the frames, and stabilizes side control for three seconds—pass complete.

Gi. Pants and sleeve grips steer Jordan's legs.

No-gi. Alex connects a body lock and walks the hips clear.

Types / Variations

Pressure passing family

Smash, over-under, stack—see pressure passing.

Speed passing family

Toreando, leg drag, long step—see speed passing.

Connection / body-lock family

Body lock and chest-to-chest walking passes—see connection passing.

Stabilize and score

Hold side control/mount with hips free of their legs until criteria are met.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries to passing. From standing after a pull; from combat base inside open guard; from failed submission attempts on top.

When passes fail. Reset distance rather than forcing a dead path into their best retention.

Bottom counters. Retention systems, sweeps, and back takes from near-passes (e.g., quarter guard).

Passing vs Retention

Factor Passing Retention
Initiative Top clears legs Bottom keeps / recovers legs
Tools Pressure, speed, locks Frames, hips, layers
Score Pass points when stabilized Sweep points / denied pass

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF awards pass points for clearing the legs and stabilizing. ADCC emphasizes control and submissions—passes still matter tactically.

Safety

Do not dump partners on their necks during stacks. Control knee-line pressure in entanglements.

Common mistakes

  • Stepping past without connecting to the hips.
  • Forcing one pass against a specialist's best retention.
  • Posture broken over active guards.
  • Ignoring style matching (pressure vs speed).
  • Celebrating early before stabilization.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What counts as a completed guard pass?

Clearing the legs and stabilizing a dominant top position per your rule set—usually side control or mount.

What are the main passing styles?

Pressure, speed, and connection/body-lock families—most athletes mix them.

Should beginners learn pressure or speed passing first?

Many coaches start with posture, combat base, and a simple knee cut or toreando—then add pressure systems.

Gi vs no-gi passing?

Gi uses more grips and slower control; no-gi favors body locks, underhooks, and athleticism.

Why do my passes always get re-guarded?

You cleared legs without hip connection. Pin the hips/torso before you celebrate.

How does guard retention stop my passes?

Frames, angles, and layered connections break your preferred path—study retention to understand counters.

Is the knee cut a pressure or speed pass?

It can be either: smashed knee cuts feel like pressure; agile knee cuts blend with speed systems.

The Bottom Line

Guard passing clears the legs and stabilizes a pin. Build a mix of pressure, speed, and connection passing—and study retention to understand the other side.
