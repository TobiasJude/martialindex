North-South

North-south is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu pin where you chest the opponent with heads in opposite directions—used to reset frames after side control and to finish the north-south choke with shoulder pressure in both gi and no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Opposite headings, chest-to-chest: the rotate-past-the-head pin.
  • Home of the north-south choke—finish with shoulder weight, not arm curl.
  • Works in gi and no-gi; fabric mostly helps control, not the choke itself.
  • Common path: side control to north-south when near frames return.
  • If they turn to knees, be ready to take the back.

What Is North-South?

North-south is a top pin where your chest faces the opponent's chest but your head points toward their legs (or past their head the opposite way), so your bodies form a straight line in opposite directions. In Brazilian jiu-jitsu it is both a control position after side control and the home of the famous north-south choke—Marcelo Garcia's trademark finish.

You arrive here by walking past the head from side control, by spinning from knee on belly, or by scrambling when they try to underhook and sit up.

How North-South Works

By rotating past the head, you remove their ability to frame your hips with the near knee the same way they do in side control. Your chest and shoulders pin their upper body; your hips stay heavy and low. From here you can hold, take the back if they turn, attack kimuras on either arm, or drop a shoulder into the throat for the north-south choke.

Choke mechanics. The choking arm wraps the neck; the finish is mostly shoulder and body weight walking toward their far shoulder—not an arm curl. Danaher-style instructionals stress locking the hands and driving the shoulder down the centerline; Chewjitsu and Marcelo emphasize chin control and clearing their defensive arm.

Gi vs No-Gi

Choke. The north-south choke works in both, but gi athletes sometimes mix in collar assists; pure no-gi relies on the arm wrap and shoulder drop. Marcelo Garcia's high-level finishes are famously no-gi friendly.

Control grips. Gi: belt and pants help keep hips from escaping while you spin. No-gi: underhooks, wrist control, and chest angle replace fabric.

Other attacks. Kimuras, armbars, and back takes appear in both. Paper-cutter and baseball-bat variants need lapels.

What Does North-South Look Like in Practice?

From side control, Sam clears Jordan's near frame, walks toward the head, and settles chest-to-chest in north-south. Jordan reaches to push the hips; Sam wraps the neck, locks hands, and walks the shoulder toward the far side until Jordan taps to the north-south choke.

Gi. Same spin, with a belt grip stabilizing hips before the choke arm sinks.

No-gi. Sam uses a tight near-side elbow and chest slide—no belt—then finishes with two-hand locking and shoulder weight exactly as modern instructionals describe.

Types / Variations

Control north-south

Heavy chest, hips low, arms controlling theirs—stall and transition platform.

North-south choke

Arm wrap + shoulder drive; the signature submission of the position.

Kimura from north-south

Isolate either arm when they post to escape the choke.

Back take from north-south

When they turn to their knees to escape, seatbelt and hooks appear.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Walk from side control; spin from KOB; follow a failed underhook sit-up; scramble after a kimura attempt.

Bottom escapes. Frame the hips, turn to your knees carefully, connect to turtle or guard recovery without giving the back; peel the choking arm before bridging blindly.

Top answers. If they turn, take the back; if they frame hard, return to side control; if they push the head, sink the choke deeper.

Common path Arrives from side control Arrives from the pass

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

North-south is a legal pin and a legal choking platform in IBJJF and ADCC. It does not have its own point value beyond whatever pass or prior position scored. Stalling without progress still risks referee attention.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. The north-south choke compresses the neck—tap early and apply with shoulder pressure, not neck cranks. Drill hand placement carefully with partners.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to curl the choke with biceps instead of walking the shoulder.
  • Floating the hips so they shrimp out during the spin.
  • Leaving their arms free to frame your neck.
  • In the gi: forgetting belt control that keeps them from turning out.
  • Spinning to north-south before clearing the near frame.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why can't I finish the north-south choke?

Most people curl with the arm. Lock your hands, control the chin, clear their defensive arm, and walk your shoulder toward their far side with body weight.

Is north-south only for the choke?

No. It is also a control and transition pin—kimuras, armbars, and back takes all live here when they react to the choke threat.

Gi or no-gi—does it matter?

The choke is healthy in both. Gi belt and pants grips make it easier to keep them from turning out while you set the wrap.

How do I get to north-south without losing the pass?

Clear the near frame in side control first, keep chest weight, then walk. Spinning early is how they reinsert the knee.

What if they turn to turtle?

That can be a gift—take the seatbelt and hooks. If you are not ready, stay heavy on the shoulder so the turn is slow. See turtle.

Does north-south score points?

Not by itself in IBJJF. It is a pin you use after a scoring pass or as a submission seat.

The Bottom Line

North-south flips your heading past the opponent's head to kill side-control frames and hunt the north-south choke with shoulder pressure. See side control for the usual entry and back control when they turn to their knees.
