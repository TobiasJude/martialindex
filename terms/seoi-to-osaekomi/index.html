Seoi to Osaekomi

Seoi to osaekomi is the throw-to-pin concept: after Seoi Nage (Judo) family attacks, keep chest contact and secure Osaekomi—often Kesa Gatame or shiho pins.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Concept under Seoi Nage (Judo).
  • Pin literacy: Osaekomi + live pins.
  • Yuko/waza-ari/ippon pin clocks are live in 2026–28.
  • Compare armlock path Ippon Seoi to Juji Gatame.
  • Chest contact after landing is the skill.
  • Do not pose after a partial throw.

What is Seoi to Osaekomi?

Seoi to osaekomi catalogues the habit that wins matches when the throw is not ippon: stay heavy, rotate into a recognised hold-down, and let the clock work. Parent hub: Seoi Nage (Judo). Pin system: Osaekomi. Common finishes: Kesa Gatame, Yoko Shiho Gatame, Kami Shiho Gatame.

Sibling concept Ippon Seoi to Juji Gatame hunts the armbar instead. Both should be trained so athletes can choose. Filed under judo transition literacy on the judo hub, not as a fake throw name.

How it works

As uke lands, keep chest-to-chest or chest-to-side contact. Choose the pin that matches the fall angle: kesa when you are beside the head/armpit line; yoko-shiho when you are more lateral; kami when you can take the north-south/upper four-quarters story. Clamp, control far arm or head as your coach teaches, and listen for osaekomi calls in contest simulation.

Under IJF 2026–28, hold-downs score yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19, and ippon at 20. Yuko is live. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Knowing the clock changes whether you chase a risky armbar or ride a stable pin.

If uke turns belly-down, do not invent a new pin—transition toward back control or restart attacking; osaekomi requires the correct conditions. See ne-waza pillar Ne-Waza.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Judogi friction helps pins stick. No-gi pin transitions from seoi-like throws still use chest weight and hip position—less sleeve control.

What it looks like in practice

Drill: seoi entry → soft landing → three pin choices called by a partner. In Randori, count how often you lose contact in the first second after impact—that number is your real curriculum.

World Tour film: watch incomplete seoi that still win on osaekomi clocks. Mute the highlight throw bias.

Types / variations

Seoi to kesa-gatame

Common when rotation leaves you scarf-hold adjacent.

Seoi to yoko-shiho

Lateral chest control after side-falling uke.

Seoi to kami-shiho

When you can take upper four-quarters quickly.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter from any live seoi family attack—morote, ippon, drop, otoshi. Counters for uke: shrimp, frame, turn in, extract near elbow. Combinations: threaten juji to open the pin; threaten pin to open juji.

IJF notes & safety

Pin scoring under IJF 2026–28 uses yuko (5–9s), waza-ari (10–19s), and ippon (20s). Yuko does not stack into waza-ari. See Judo Rules and Osaekomi.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Posing after a partial throw.
  • Kneeling beside uke without chest weight.
  • Chasing juji every time a pin was free.
  • Not knowing the yuko clock.
  • Ignoring escape frames.

Training notes

Weekly seoi landings into each live pin. Quiz athletes on 5/10/20 second thresholds. Pair with ukemi so partners accept volume.

Study notes

Clock drills: coach calls “yuko!” at five seconds, “waza-ari!” at ten, “ippon!” at twenty while athletes hold a pin after a soft seoi landing. The nervous system learns thresholds faster than lectures.

Escape literacy belongs in the same week: uke practices frames and turns so tori’s pins are tested, not handed over. Pins that only work on frozen partners fail on the World Tour.

Drop-seoi athletes especially need this concept—knee-drop landings often leave messy angles where kesa appears first. See Drop Seoi Nage.

Scoring literacy—Ippon, Waza-Ari, and live yuko on osaekomi clocks—belongs in the same conversation as technique names.

Deep notes

Angle library: after a right seoi, uke often lands with their right arm nearest you—kesa on that side is frequently the shortest path. After a failed rotation, you may be more lateral—yoko-shiho becomes natural. Kami appears when you can circle to the head before uke re-frames. Drill each landing intentionally rather than hoping improvisation appears under fatigue.

Scoreboard literacy changes behaviour: a stable yuko may be worth more than a failed juji that loses position entirely. Teach the math in the same voice you teach the grips.

Partner veto: pins after hard throws require breathing room and clear taps for discomfort (ribs, neck). Osaekomi training is still partner training.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is seoi-to-osaekomi a throw name?

No—concept under Seoi Nage (Judo).

Which pin is best?

Match angle—see live pin pages.

What about yuko?

Yuko live at 5–9s—see Osaekomi.

Juji instead?

See Ippon Seoi to Juji Gatame.

Works after drop seoi?

Yes—including Drop Seoi Nage landings.

Main mistake?

Losing chest contact in the first second.

Parent?

Seoi Nage (Judo).

The Bottom Line

Make seoi-to-osaekomi automatic: land, stay heavy, choose a live pin, and know the yuko/waza-ari/ippon clocks cold.
