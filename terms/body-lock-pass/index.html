Body Lock Pass

The body lock pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu low pass in which the top player locks both arms around the bottom player’s waist—pinning the hips—then methodically clears the knee line to pass butterfly, half, and open guards, with slightly different grip fights in gi versus no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Body lock = arms around the waist to cage the hips, then clear knees and finish the pass.
  • A modern no-gi staple popularized in systems like Lachlan Giles’s staged body-lock passing; also works in gi.
  • Head position and “skydiving” hips help deny guillotines and elevator sweeps while you lock up.
  • Compare with over-under: waist cage vs under-the-legs sandwich.
  • Failure modes: neck exposed while locking, or stopping before the knee line is cleared.

What Is the Body Lock Pass?

The body lock pass (sometimes called a double-over pass) clamps your arms around the bottom player’s waist so their hips cannot freely elevate or shrimp. From that cage you walk, sprawl, and pummel until their knees no longer block your path to side control or mount. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

It is especially prominent in contemporary no-gi, where fabric grips are absent and hip control must come from the lock itself. Gi athletes use it too—often with pants assists—but the concept is waist-first, not lapel-first.

How the Body Lock Works

Enter the lock. From seated or butterfly guard, clear dangerous feet, dive low, and connect hands behind the waist with elbows tight.

Pin and posture. Head position (usually toward the passing side) and hip sprawl deny guillotines and hook sweeps.

Clear the knee line. Walk up the body until knees are no longer between you and the torso, then turn and settle the pin.

Teaching systems often break this into numbered stages (entry → pin → knee line → half → finish) so each failure point has an answer.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. Primary home. Without pants, the body lock replaces many standing grip fights. Expect more guillotine awareness and faster hip fights.

Gi. Still excellent; pants grips can help you enter or finish. Lapels are optional extras, not requirements.

Leg entanglement rules. In no-gi events that allow heel hooks, be ready for the bottom player to enter SLX/K-guard if your lock is loose. In IBJJF gi, those threats change with belt/age rules—but the pass itself stays legal.

Vs pressure under legs. When under-the-legs is available, some gi passers prefer over-under; when the waist is available, body lock shines.

What Does the Body Lock Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits butterfly. Jordan grip-fights, dives low, locks hands behind Alex’s waist, and sprawls the far hip. Alex tries to elevate; Jordan’s head and chest kill the hook. Jordan walks the free knee past Alex’s knee line, forces half guard briefly, pummels free, and settles side control.

Types / Variations

Staged body-lock system

Entry, hip pin, knee-line clear, half-guard problem-solving, finish—each coached as its own round.

Vs butterfly and seated guard

The textbook entry: deny feet on hips, lock waist, remove hooks.

Half-guard finishes

Most body locks visit half guard. Have smash, knee-cut, or back-step answers ready.

Entries, Counters, and Retention

Entries. Vs seated/butterfly; after a toreando stalls near the hips; as a reset from headquarters.

Counters. Bottom: guillotine while they dive, elevators before the lock closes, omoplata on an overcommitted arm, or hip escape to recompose. Top: head position first, then lock; never give the neck for free.

See also over-under pass.

Hands and lock styles. Gable grips, S-grips, and palm-to-palm locks all appear. What matters is elbow tightness and waist height—locking too high on the ribs lets hips escape; locking too low on the hips without chest pressure invites elevation.

After the pass. Side control is the default finish, but many body-lock passers take the back when the bottom player turns away from pressure. Have a plan for the rear naked choke once the pass becomes a back take.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in both. Extremely common in ADCC-style no-gi. In IBJJF, still fully usable in gi and no-gi divisions.

Safety

Protect your partner’s ribs and neck while locking. Do not crank the spine to “force” the cage. Tap early if something feels wrong.

Common mistakes

  • Locking up with the head up—gift wrapping a guillotine.
  • Stopping at half guard without a finish plan.
  • Loose elbows that let them shrimp free.
  • Diving onto feet still planted on your hips.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is body lock the same as double under?

No. Double under goes under the legs. Body lock goes around the waist.

Why do I always get guillotined when I shoot the lock?

Head position and posture are late. Get the head to the passing side and elbows tight as you connect—or fake entries until the neck is safe.

Does body lock work in the gi?

Yes. Pants can help you enter and finish, but the waist cage is the core.

What are the “stages” people mention?

Teaching frameworks (popularized in modern instructionals) that split entry, pin, knee-line, half-guard, and finish so each problem has drills.

Body lock or knee cut—which first?

Different tools. Body lock shines vs seated/butterfly; knee cut shines from headquarters. Most passers want both.

Can they still play legs from under a body lock?

If your lock is loose, yes—especially in heel-hook-legal no-gi. Keep hips pinned and hands connected.

How do I finish when stuck in half guard?

Treat it as half-guard passing: free the knee, smash, or back-step. The body lock only got you there.

The Bottom Line

The body lock pass cages the waist, pins the hips, and clears knee lines to finish past butterfly and half guard. It is a modern no-gi cornerstone that still translates to gi. Pair it with over-under and knee cut so every distance has an answer.
