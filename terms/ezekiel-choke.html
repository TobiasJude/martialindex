Ezekiel Choke

The Ezekiel choke (sode guruma jime) threads a sleeve (gi) or biceps grip (no-gi) across the neck from pins like mount or side control—a sneaky collar-family cousin that can survive without gripping their lapel.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Hide the grips; elbows together finish more than raw squeeze.
  • Gi uses your own sleeve; no-gi uses biceps/wrist structures (Craig Jones-style details).
  • Works when cross-collar hand fighting is busy—different grip path.
  • Common from mount, side control, and even inside closed guard in some systems.
  • Still a neck attack—tap early in drilling.

What Is Ezekiel Choke?

The Ezekiel is a sliding sleeve choke: one arm behind/around the neck, the other hand inside your own sleeve (gi), then elbows drive together. No-gi versions replace the sleeve with a grip on your own biceps or wrist structures.

It clusters with pin submissions on mount and side control and sits beside collar chokes even though it may not grip their collar.

How Ezekiel Choke Works

From mount/side, slide the choking arm into place (often while they are busy framing), feed the free hand into your sleeve or biceps grip, hide fingers from peels, and compress elbows while dropping weight.

Stealth. Telegraphing lets them clear hands. Keep grips under armpits/hidden lines.

Bottom. Peel hands early, chin tuck, and escape mount/side before the sleeve sets.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Classic sleeve feed—sode guruma jime. Works even when their collars are defended.

No-Gi. Viable with biceps/wrist grips; mechanics change but the elbow-together finish remains. Not a lapel choke.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Mount: crossface arm becomes the neck wrap, free hand into sleeve, elbows pinch as hips settle. Side control: similar feed when double underhooks or crossface lets the arm slide.

Types / Variations

Gi sleeve Ezekiel

Hand inside own sleeve across the neck.

No-gi biceps Ezekiel

Grip own biceps/wrist instead of sleeve.

Guard Ezekiel

Less common; some closed-guard sleeve feeds exist.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: mount while they fight collars, side control during mount attempts, failed cross collars that leave sleeves free.

Counters: hand fight early, keep chin down, shrimp mount, frame side control.

Chain with americana, arm-triangle, and cross collar when they over-defend the Ezekiel path.

Ezekiel vs Cross Collar

Factor Ezekiel Choke Cross Collar Choke
Uses their collar? Often no Yes
Uses your sleeve? Gi yes No
No-gi viable? Yes (adapted) No
Stealth level High when hidden More telegraphed grips

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in gi. No-gi legal when applied without illegal neck cranks—standard blood-choke mechanics. Follow event rules on neck attacks.

Safety

Do not combine with harsh neck cranks. Apply compression gradually in training.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving fingers peelable in front of the neck.
  • Squeezing without elbow connection.
  • Abandoning mount base for the choke.
  • Forcing no-gi grips that become cranks.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an Ezekiel choke?

A strangle using your sleeve (gi) or biceps grip (no-gi) across the neck, finished by connecting elbows.

Is Ezekiel gi-only?

The classic sleeve version needs a gi; no-gi adaptations exist.

Best positions?

Mount and side control are most common.

How is it different from collar chokes?

It often uses your sleeve rather than their lapels—useful when collars are defended.

Why do people call it sneaky?

Grips can hide while they focus on collar defense or frames.

Defense?

Peel hands early, tuck chin, escape the pin.

Related pin chokes?

Paper cutter, baseball, cross collar, north-south choke.

The Bottom Line

Ezekiel is the hidden-sleeve (or biceps) strangle from pins: feed, hide, elbows together. Keep it as a backup when collar fights stall—and learn the no-gi adaptation separately.
