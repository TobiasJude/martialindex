Low Single

A low single attacks the ankle-height single—penetrate low, secure the foot or shin, and finish with corner drive.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Low single targets ankle height.
  • Corner pressure finishes the attack.
  • Higher sprawl risk if you stall.
  • Chains with ankle picks and high singles.
  • Extract when the shot is dead.

What Is Low Single?

The low single is a Single Leg (Wrestling) variant that captures near the ankle rather than high on the thigh.

It is associated with elite freestyle pressure—John Smith’s low single being a canonical model—but it also scores in folkstyle.

Compared with High Single, you are lower, more committed, and often more exposed to crossfaces if you stall.

In Wrestling, low singles punish opponents who sit heavy on their heels or reach high.

Martial Index files Low Single under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Low Single behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Low Single Works

Force a lead-foot plant, Level Change deeply, and Penetration Step to the ankle with head position coached for your finish.

Secure the ankle/shin, keep driving the corner so they cannot hop freely, and dump or elevate as available.

Chain to Ankle Pick (Wrestling) timing or come up to a higher single if they hop into you.

If stuffed, extract and recover—do not become a turtle under a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Mechanically, successful low single work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → Level Change → entry or control → finish or recovery.

If stance collapses or the Penetration Step stalls short, even textbook grips fail—too high and you meet a Sprawl (Wrestling); too loose and you invite a Whizzer or scramble.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: shots need sprawl literacy; ties need hand-fighting; pins need Near Fall awareness in folkstyle.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, low single is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how low single is valued once the wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-attack cousins of low single either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle par terre.

Watch potentially dangerous head positions in low scrambles.

Complete control criteria before stopping wrestle.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Freestyle: low single to lace or exposure chain.

Folkstyle: low single to mat return and ride.

Match picture: low single appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply low single after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Shooting low without a plant.
  • Head buried, getting crossed.
  • No corner—just holding the ankle.
  • Staying down after the shot dies.
  • Treating low single as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent sprawls or bridges.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle low single inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so low single connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether low single appears with a real Level Change and clean hip position.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, front headlocks, and pinning combinations.

Shadow low singles with cone corners.

Partner hop drills: keep the ankle through three hops.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe low single as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or a Level Change that never gets under the opponent’s hips.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of low single stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Low single vs ankle pick?

Low single is a shot to the leg; ankle pick often uses upper-body load then a hand pick. They overlap in scrambles.

Harder than high single?

Different risk: lower commitment, more sprawl danger if mistimed.

Best for freestyle?

Excellent freestyle tool; still valid in folkstyle with control finishes.

Defense?

Sprawl, crossface, and force them to their knees without a corner.

Do I need elite athleticism?

Timing and corner beat raw athleticism for beginners.

Parent page?

The Bottom Line

Low singles reward deep level changes and relentless corners—own the ankle only if you also own the angle.
