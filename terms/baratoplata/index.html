Baratoplata

The baratoplata (Barratoplata) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu shoulder lock associated with Rafael “Barata” Freitas—often entered when a triangle is defended—using leg and hand geometry related to omoplata and kimura pressure.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Common off triangle defense when the arm hides.
  • Shoulder attack—not a choke.
  • Works gi and no-gi with grip adjustments.
  • Chain with omoplata, kimura, armbar.
  • Control the wrist/elbow line before cranking.

What Is Baratoplata?

Baratoplata isolates an arm against your body/legs and finishes toward the shoulder, frequently when the opponent posts or hides an arm to survive a triangle. It occupies the same “upper-body lock from guard” neighborhood as omoplata, with a distinct grip/leg configuration named for Barata Freitas.

Links: omoplata, kimura, triangle, closed guard.

How Baratoplata Works

From a failed/defended triangle, clear space, figure the attacking arm, use your legs to pin the shoulder line, control the wrist, and finish by extending/rotating into the shoulder like a hybrid omoplata-kimura.

Details vary by school—learn one clean academy model rather than mash random Instagram steps.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control helps freeze the wrist.

No-Gi. Wrist control and head position replace cloth.

Legal as a shoulder lock in typical adult rules—confirm juveniles/beginners if relevant.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex throws a triangle; Jordan tucks the arm and posts. Alex transitions to baratoplata, pins the shoulder with the leg structure, and finishes the shoulder lock.

Types / Variations

Triangle-defense branch

Most cited entry.

Closed-guard direct

Some systems enter without a full triangle.

Mount / side adaptations

Less common but taught in some Freitas curricula.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: triangle defenses, high-guard arm isolations, some mount transitions.

Counters: free the elbow early; do not freeze the wrist for them; turn into the pressure.

Escapes: straighten and extract the arm; tap if shoulder loads.

Baratoplata vs Omoplata

Factor Baratoplata Omoplata
Family Shoulder lock Shoulder lock
Famous entry Triangle defense Guard swing to shoulder
Naming Barata Freitas Classic BJJ lexicon
Chain mates Kimura / armbar Sweeps / triangle

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Shoulder locks of this type are generally legal for adults in IBJJF-style rules with standard joint-lock conventions—confirm age/belt charts. Not a spinal twist.

Safety

Shoulder injuries. Apply gradually; many partners have prior AC joint issues.

Common mistakes

  • Cranking without pinning the shoulder.
  • Losing the wrist.
  • Forcing from a loose triangle.
  • Ignoring omoplata when it is cleaner.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is baratoplata?

A shoulder lock often entered from triangle defense, named via Rafael Freitas (“Barata”).

Same as omoplata?

Same family, different geometry/entry fame.

Gi or no-gi?

Both.

Legal?

Typically as a shoulder lock for adults—verify charts.

Main entry?

Defended triangle.

Defense?

Free elbow/wrist early; turn in.

Chain?

Omoplata, kimura, armbar.

The Bottom Line

Baratoplata is the triangle-defense shoulder lock: pin the shoulder line, own the wrist, finish smoothly. Keep omoplata and kimura one transition away.
