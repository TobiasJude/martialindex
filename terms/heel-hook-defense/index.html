Heel Hook Defense

Heel hook defense is ligament-first survival against heel hooks from inside ashi, saddle, and 50/50. Hide the heel, keep a safer knee line, hand-fight, and tap early. Verify IBJJF / ADCC.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Parent: heel hook. Habit one: hide the heel (shorten the leg).
  • Positions: saddle, inside ashi, 50/50 heel hook.
  • Do not roll the wrong way into more torque.
  • Rules literacy: reaping, IBJJF, ADCC.
  • Tap to knee pain immediately—ACL/MCL injuries are career-scale.

What Is Heel Hook Defense?

Defensive system versus inside and outside heel hooks. Up: ashigarami. Across: ankle-lock defense, toe-hold defense.

How It Works

As soon as ashi appears, curl the heel toward the hip and hide it. Keep the knee in a safer line relative to hip/ankle. Hand-fight their grip. In saddle, get the knee inside a safer line before attempting a heel slip. In 50/50, shorten, cross feet, and crowd rather than spinning into the finish.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Often fewer heel-hook exposures in restricted divisions—still train defense for no-gi and open rules.

No-gi. Daily habit. Drill slowly with clear intensity agreements.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Saddle locks → hide heel → knee inside line → toes point and slip only when safe → extract or reverse.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Hide-the-heel prevention.

Competition variation

Saddle heel-slip after knee-line fix.

Related option

50/50 hand-fight and cross-feet defense.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by saddle entries and ashi entries. Offense answers: better secondary-leg control and grip connection.

How It Compares

Vs ankle-lock defense: rotation vs extension. Vs toe-hold defense: heel/knee vs forefoot/ankle.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Cross-read reaping and heel hook.

Safety

Leg attacks load ligaments fast. Tap early, drill slowly, and never crank through pain. Partners should release immediately on tap or freeze.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling away and exposing more heel.
  • Slipping while the knee is outside a dangerous line.
  • Ego sparring heel hooks at full speed.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is heel hook defense?

Habits that prevent heel exposure and escapes that relieve knee torque.

First habit?

Hide the heel by shortening the leg toward the hip.

Saddle slip safe?

Only after the knee is in a safer line—otherwise you can injure yourself.

50/50 tip?

Cross the feet, hand-fight, and avoid turning into the finish.

IBJJF relevance?

Even if heel hooks are restricted in your division, defense literacy matters for open rules and no-gi.

Related defenses?

Ankle-lock defense, toe-hold defense.

Tap cue?

Any knee ligament pain—immediate.

The Bottom Line

Heel hook defense is knee insurance—hide the heel, fix the line, and tap early inside ashi battles.
