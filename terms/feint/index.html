Feint

A feint sells a punch or step you don’t fully throw—so their flinch, shell, or foot move opens the real attack.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Feint = sell, read, punish.
  • Attach a real follow-up.
  • Less is more.

What Is Feint?

Concept linking Jab, Combination, and Counterpunch. Works at every Boxing Range.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Feint behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Feint Works

Flash the shoulder or start a jab without full commitment, read their reaction (parry, pull, step), then throw the real shot to the opened lane—often a Cross, Lead Hook, or entry step.

Mechanically, successful feint work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, feint lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill feint daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Jab feint → overhand. Foot feint forward → Pull Counter if they lunge. Level feint to body → head hook.

Fight picture: feint appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply feint after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Jab / shoulder feint

Most common—partial lead hand.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Foot feint

Step threat without full entry.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Level feint

Eyes/shoulders sell body then go head (or reverse).

Common Mistakes

  • Feinting without a planned second action.
  • Over-feinting so nothing is believed.
  • Dropping hands while faking.
  • Treating feint as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox feint inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so feint connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether feint appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe feint as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put feint behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep feint scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on feint, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for feint under your next card (glove size, round length, amateur vs pro judging) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Do feints score?

Not by themselves—they create the conditions to score.

How many feints per combo?

Often one clean feint beats three noisy ones.

Can beginners feint?

Yes—simple jab feints on the bag and mitts.

The Bottom Line

Make them move first—then throw the punch they just confessed how to stop.
