High-Elbow Guillotine

The high-elbow guillotine is the arm-out guillotine finishing school: you create space between your chest and their shoulder, keep the hands high, and shelf the choking elbow toward the ceiling / over the trap so the choke bites the neck instead of cranking it. Closely related to the marcelotine.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Three battles: space (shoulder vs chest), hand height, and head position.
  • Usually arm-out—compare arm-in when the shot gives you their arm.
  • Marcelo’s details live on the marcelotine page; this page is the broader school.
  • Front headlock and guard sit-ups are the two main platforms.
  • Do not let the hands drift toward the hips as you arch.

What Is High-Elbow Guillotine?

A high-elbow guillotine is less a brand-new choke than a finish standard for the arm-out guillotine. The elbow of the choking arm rides high—up the neck and onto or over the trapezius—so the forearm and wrist can compress the carotids instead of folding the cervical spine.

Danaher-style teaching frames it as winning space: if their shoulder collapses into your chest, the elbow cannot rise. If your hands drift low, you start arching and the choke becomes a crank. If your head stays on the wrong side, the elbow never shelves.

Martial Index keeps this page for “high elbow guillotine” searches and the marcelotine for Marcelo Garcia’s specific grip-and-crunch details. Read both; they overlap on purpose.

How High-Elbow Guillotine Works

From front headlock or a guard sit-up, get the arm out when you can. Chin-strap or ten-finger grips can start the jaw off the chest so the choking hand can slide deep.

Connect the support hand and lift the choking elbow. Your head shifts across their centerline; your shoulder of the choking side stays on their neck to hold the pocket of space.

Finish by crunching and bringing the elbow higher, not by throwing your hips to the ceiling. A hook on their back or a closed guard stops the roll-out.

If you cannot win the space battle, switch to arm-in, darce, or a snap-down rather than ripping a low-elbow crank.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar ties and sleeve frames help create the space pocket; the finish is still a naked choke.

No-gi. The modern default. Pummeling and chin-straps replace cloth.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex wins front-headlock space, shelves the elbow on the trap, hooks a leg, and taps without arching.

Jordan’s hands slide to the hips, the choke becomes a crank, and they reset to a chin strap instead of forcing it.

Types / Variations

Front-headlock high elbow

Snap-down or sprawl into the space battle, then shelf the elbow.

Guard high elbow

Sit-up from closed or butterfly; close the legs and crunch.

Ten-finger / chin-strap entry

Palms crossed under the jaw to start the slide into a high elbow (Tonon-style details).

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: stuffed shots, snap-downs, and guard sit-ups when the arm is out. Counters: collapse the space (shoulder into their chest), hide the chin, circle. Escapes: extract the head toward the free side before the elbow shelves.

High-Elbow Guillotine vs Marcelotine

Factor High-elbow (school) Marcelotine (Marcelo details)
Scope Broad arm-out finish standard Named Garcia grip/crunch details
Grip talk Many (chin strap, gable, ten-finger) Fist / ball-and-socket, wrist bone under chin
Body Space + high hands + head shift Knees to chest, elbow on trap, rarely fall straight back

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult guillotines are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

High elbow is the safer training habit because it aims at a choke. If you feel only neck bend, stop and reset structure.

Common mistakes

  • Hands drifting to the hips while you arch.
  • Letting their shoulder collapse your chest pocket.
  • Head on the wrong side so the elbow cannot rise.
  • Confusing a low-elbow crank with a finished high elbow.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is high elbow the same as the marcelotine?

Same family. Marcelotine is Marcelo’s named detail set; high elbow is the broader school.

Do I need long arms?

You need space and head position more than limb length.

Arm in or out?

High elbow is typically arm-out. Use arm-in when that is what you catch.

Standing finish?

Possible, but sitting to guard often raises percentage.

Gi useful?

Collars help the snap; the finish is still naked.

Why do I only get cranks?

Hands are low or the shoulder pocket collapsed. Reset space before squeezing.

Next if they hide the neck?

Darce, anaconda, or keep front-headlock control.

The Bottom Line

High-elbow guillotine is a space-and-structure finish: keep the pocket, keep the hands high, shelf the elbow. Cross-read the marcelotine and the parent guillotine.
