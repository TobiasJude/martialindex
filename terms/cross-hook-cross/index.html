What Is Cross-Hook-Cross?

This string appears when you are already past the jab or countering with a rear hand first (Counterpunch entries).

Related to the back half of 1-2-3-2 Combo (the 2-3-2 portion) and to pad calls that skip the jab.

Parent: Combination. Compare with Cross and One-Two-Three.

Martial Index files Cross-Hook-Cross under Boxing so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Cross-Hook-Cross behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Cross-Hook-Cross Works

Fire a committed but recoverable Cross. Turn into the Lead Hook. Reload the rear hand for the finishing cross.

Keep the Power Line honest on both crosses—no leaning past the feet.

Use when their lead hand is busy and the center line opens twice.

Mechanically, successful work with Cross-Hook-Cross is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, cross-hook-cross lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill cross-hook-cross daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a Slip outside the jab: cross-hook-cross.

Tony-style teaching often stacks hook-two finishes after straights—see four-minute beginner progressions that end on 2.

Fight picture: cross-hook-cross appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply cross-hook-cross after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Counter first cross

First cross is the Counterpunch; hook and second cross collect.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Light hook, hard last cross

Middle hook as Setup Punch; last cross as Finishing Punch.

Body hook in the middle

Common Mistakes

  • Three arm punches with frozen hips.
  • Overreaching the first cross.
  • No guard on the hook.
  • Treating cross-hook-cross as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox cross-hook-cross inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so cross-hook-cross connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether cross-hook-cross appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe cross-hook-cross as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put cross-hook-cross behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep cross-hook-cross scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on cross-hook-cross, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for cross-hook-cross under your next card (glove size, round length, amateur vs pro judging) so training intensity matches the bout.
