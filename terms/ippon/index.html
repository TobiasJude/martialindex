Ippon

Ippon is judo’s full point—the score that ends a contest immediately via a decisive throw, a 20-second pin, a submission, or two combined waza-ari.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Ippon ends the contest.
  • Throw, 20s pin, or tap.
  • Two Waza-Ari = ippon.
  • Yuko never becomes ippon by itself.
  • Bridging to save the back still costs you.
  • See Judo Rules for the full ladder.

What is ippon?

Ippon (一本) is judo’s full point—the score that ends the contest immediately.

You can earn it by a qualifying throw, a 20-second Osaekomi, or a submission to shime/kansetsu. Two Waza-Ari also combine into ippon (waza-ari-awasete-ippon).

It sits at the top of the Judo Rules hierarchy above waza-ari and the reinstated yuko.

How it works

Throw ippon: largely on the back, with control, force, and speed—four criteria working together.

Pin ippon: valid Osaekomi held 20 seconds after the call.

Submission ippon: tap, verbal surrender, or referee stop for a legal choke/armlock.

Bridging to avoid a back landing is still treated as ippon—do not risk your neck for aesthetics.

Judogi vs no-gi

Scoring definitions are rule-set ideas, not fabric ideas. Gi competition is where most athletes feel classic throw ippon; no-gi rule sets may score takedowns differently (BJJ/MMA).

In practice

A clean Uchi Mata (Judo) that plants uke flat with control draws ippon and a bow. A side-heavy landing may be only Waza-Ari or yuko—know the difference.

Routes to ippon

Throw ippon; pin ippon (20s); submission ippon; awasete from two Waza-Ari.

Training implications

Drill throws to the back with control, not just ‘any takedown.’

From pins, learn to ride to 20 without losing chest pressure.

Compare with waza-ari

Waza-Ari is the near-ippon score. One does not end the match; two do. Yuko never upgrades into waza-ari.

IJF notes & safety

Under IJF 2026–28 rules, osaekomi scores yuko at 5–9s, waza-ari at 10–19s, and ippon at 20s. Yuko is live again and does not add up to waza-ari; two waza-ari make ippon.

Golden score: first technical score (including yuko) can win overtime when regulation is level on higher scores.

Safety: never bridge on your head.

Common mistakes

  • Celebrating a side landing as ‘ippon’ in training culture.
  • Abandoning a 15-second pin.
  • Cranking after the tap.

Ippon culture shapes training. Dojos that only reward trips to the knee teach athletes to expect scores the IJF will not give. Demand back exposure and control in nage-komi even when partners are cooperative.

Pin ippon is a stamina and angle problem as much as a strength problem. Chest pressure, hip blocking, and hand fighting the escape matter more than lying beside someone for twenty lazy seconds.

Submission ippon requires honesty: if the choke is on, tap; if the arm is extended, tap. Referees may stop late; elbows do not always forgive late taps.

Scoreboard literacy: athletes sometimes quit attacking after a waza-ari when an ippon finish is available. Others dive for a miracle ippon and gift the counter. Decision-making belongs in randori themes, not only in lecture.

See also waza-ari for the near-miss layer and osaekomi for the clock that produces pin ippon.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Does ippon always end it?

Yes.

Pin time?

20 seconds.

Two yukos?

Do not make ippon or waza-ari.

Golden score ippon?

Any technical score can win GS; ippon still ends immediately.

Submission?

Legal shime/kansetsu → ippon.

Almost on the back?

May be Waza-Ari or yuko.

Awasete?

Two waza-ari combine.

Train for ippon?

Back landings + pin rides + honest taps.

The Bottom Line

Chase clear Ippon, understand Waza-Ari, and read Judo Rules so yuko doesn’t surprise you.
