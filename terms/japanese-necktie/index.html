Japanese Necktie

The Japanese necktie is a front-headlock strangle that weaves a leg and clamps the neck—usable in gi and no-gi, often from pins, dogfight, or after stuffed passes—cousin to Peruvian necktie and the darce family.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Control the head/arm, weave the near leg, clamp and extend to finish.
  • Works without lapels—unlike most Batch 5 collar chokes.
  • Common from knee-on-belly, quarter guard, and front headlock scrambles.
  • Contrast with rear naked choke (back) and loop/clock (gi turtle/guard).
  • Learn grip preference (gable vs seatbelt-style) from your gym’s necktie system.

What Is Japanese Necktie?

The Japanese necktie is a front-headlock submission: wrap the head and near arm, insert/weave a leg across, and finish by squeezing and extending so the neck compresses. 10th Planet and no-gi scenes popularized variants (Richie Martinez et al.), while gi athletes hit related entries from knee-on-belly and pins.

Unlike collar chokes, it does not need a lapel—making it a bridge between Batch 5 gi stranglers and no-gi front-headlock systems.

How Japanese Necktie Works

Win a front headlock or head-and-arm control, step the near leg over/around into the necktie weave, connect hands (gable or preferred grip), and finish by driving the shoulder/hips while the leg blocks escape.

Head position. Keep their posture broken; if they posture up, the necktie dies or becomes a scramble.

Bottom. Tuck chin, peel the headlock, and come up on a single or reverse before the leg weaves.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Fully usable; some entries add collar grips for control, but the finish is not a lapel choke.

No-Gi. Native environment—pairs with darce, anaconda, and Peruvian necktie chains.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From knee-on-belly or quarter guard: force the head down, lock the front headlock, weave the leg, finish the necktie. From dogfight/front headlock after a stuffed pass: same clamp with a tighter scramble.

Types / Variations

Knee-on-belly / pin necktie

Pin entry into front headlock weave.

Dogfight / scramble necktie

From front headlock battles after passes or turtling errors.

System necktie (10th Planet etc.)

Chained with darce and Peruvian necktie options.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: knee-on-belly, quarter guard, stuffed double-legs, front headlock after turtle mistakes, side control when they turn in wrong.

Counters: peel headlock, posture, come up on a leg, roll carefully, or fight the weaving leg before it locks.

If necktie fails, transition to darce, arm-triangle, or back take depending on their turn.

Japanese Necktie vs Rear Naked Choke

Factor Japanese Necktie Rear Naked Choke
Orientation Front headlock / facing Behind the back
Leg use Weave essential Hooks / body triangle
Gi needed? No No
Family Front headlock / neckties Back control chokes

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Generally legal as a choke in IBJJF gi and no-gi; avoid crank-only neck attacks. Confirm youth/teen restrictions in your federation.

Safety

Neckties can crank if misapplied—pursue carotid pressure, not pure twist. Tap early; drill with controlled weaves.

Common mistakes

  • Weaving the leg before head control is secure.
  • Finishing as a pure neck crank.
  • Letting them posture out mid-weave.
  • Ignoring darce/anaconda when the necktie angle dies.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the Japanese necktie?

A front-headlock choke that weaves a leg across and clamps the neck—usable in gi and no-gi.

Is it gi-only?

No. Unlike most Batch 5 collar chokes, it works without lapels.

Japanese vs Peruvian necktie?

Related front-headlock family; Peruvian often rolls differently—learn both as a system.

How does it relate to darce?

Same front-headlock neighborhood; many systems chain necktie and darce when angles change.

Best entries?

Knee-on-belly, quarter guard, and stuffed shots into front headlock.

Defense?

Peel the headlock and posture or stand before the leg weave locks.

Why include it with collar chokes?

Batch 5 groups neckties with gi neck attacks as strangle literacy—this one is the no-gi-friendly bridge.

The Bottom Line

Japanese necktie is front-headlock strangling with a leg weave: control the head, weave, clamp. It is Batch 5’s reminder that not every neck finish needs a collar—chain it with darce and keep RNC for the back.
