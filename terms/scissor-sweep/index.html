Scissor Sweep

The scissor sweep is a foundational Brazilian jiu-jitsu sweep from closed guard that “scissors” one shin across the opponent’s midsection while the other leg chops their base, pulling them over into mount.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Break posture, win a sleeve/collar or underhook, scissor, and come up to mount.
  • Pillar: closed guard sweeps.
  • Cluster: pendulum, flower, hip bump, elevator.
  • Gi grips glue the pull; no-gi needs tighter underhooks and timing.
  • Failed scissor → triangle/armbar or sibling sweeps—do not stall flat.

What Is Scissor Sweep?

The scissor sweep is often the first closed-guard reverse white belts learn: one leg blocks across the torso (or hip line), the other chops the base knee/ankle, and upper-body grips pull them over your shin into mount.

Pillar home is closed guard. Cluster siblings in Batch 4 include pendulum, flower, hip bump, and elevator—same bottom game, different off-balances.

How Scissor Sweep Works

Open closed guard with control, place the near shin across their body, chop the far base with the other leg, and pull with sleeve/collar (gi) or underhook/head control (no-gi) while bridging the hips.

Top player’s job. Keep posture, base wide, free the chopped leg, and posture out of the shin trap—or stand into open-guard passing (toreando / knee cut).

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Cross-collar and sleeve grips make the pull reliable. Pants grips can help control the chopped leg.

No-gi. Underhook and head control replace collar/sleeve; timing must be sharper because grips slip.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From closed guard, break posture, place the shin across, scissor the base, and finish in mount—then hunt armbars or take the back if they turn.

If they post hard on the chopped side, switch to pendulum or flower using the same grip fights.

Types / Variations

Classic scissor to mount

Shin across + chop base + pull to mount.

Scissor into back take

When they turn away from the chop, climb the back instead of mount.

No-gi underhook scissor

Underhook + head control replace collar/sleeve.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: broken posture in closed guard, failed hip bump that returns to closed, collar-sleeve frames. Counters: strong posture, knee slide, standing pass. Chain to pendulum/flower/hip-bump when the scissor is read.

Scissor Sweep vs Pendulum Sweep

Factor Scissor Pendulum
Off-balance Chop base + shin across Pendulum swing under/around base
Pillar Closed guard Closed guard
Common finish Mount Mount / side / attack
Cluster mates Flower, hip bump, elevator Flower, scissor, armbar

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal at all belt levels in IBJJF gi and no-gi. Core sport fundamental.

Safety

Do not crank their knee sideways while chopping. Control your own neck when they stack in response.

Common mistakes

  • Scissoring without breaking posture.
  • Flat hips—no pull.
  • Ignoring sibling sweeps when they post.
  • Ending the drill without coming up to mount.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the scissor sweep?

A BJJ sweep that reverses top/bottom using the scissor sweep mechanics described above.

Gi or no-gi?

Both, with different grips. Gi uses sleeves/pants/collar; no-gi uses underhooks, head control, and hooks.

Where does it sit in the catalog?

Under closed guard; across to pendulum, flower, hip bump.

Good for white belts?

Often yes when drilled slowly with a clear hip path—confirm with your coach for your body type.

What if it fails?

Chain to a sibling sweep or attack (armbar/triangle/kimura) instead of forcing one path.

Scissor vs flower?

Scissor chops with a shin-across structure; flower often uses a pendulum-like sleeve/pant pull from closed.

Can I scissor from open guard?

Variants exist, but the classic is closed-guard. Open-guard cousins include tripod and hook sweeps.

The Bottom Line

Scissor sweep is closed-guard literacy: break posture, scissor, mount. Keep it linked to the closed-guard pillar and Batch 4 siblings pendulum, flower, and hip bump.
