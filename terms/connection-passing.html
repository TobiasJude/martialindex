Connection Passing

Connection passing is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu guard-passing style that prioritizes a locked upper-body connection—especially body locks—then walks or rides the hips past the legs without releasing that connection.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Lock the torso first; legs second.
  • Body-lock pass is the flagship—see also chest-to-chest rides.
  • Huge in modern no-gi; gi versions exist with belt/underhook hybrids.
  • Losing the lock mid-pass invites re-guards and guillotines—keep it glued.
  • Related: body lock pass, pressure passing.

What Is Connection Passing?

Connection passing is the guard-passing approach that secures a dominant upper-body lock—most famously the body lock—then clears the legs while never giving up that torso connection. It sits between pure smash pressure and upright speed circling. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

The live sibling technique page is body lock pass; this entry explains the broader concept.

How Connection Passing Works

Win underhooks or lock hands around the torso, flatten or control their posture, then walk your hips past their legs or fold the legs while the lock stays. Because you remain connected, many retention frames never get space to reset.

Bottom player's job. Prevent the lock, frame inside, threaten guillotines/front headlocks if they dive, and shrimp before the walk completes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Belt grips and underhook/lapel hybrids can mimic body-lock connection; pure no-gi body locks are less common but possible.

No-gi. Home of modern body-lock passing at the highest levels.

MMA context. Connection limits striking from bottom and advances position safely when done with head position awareness.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex locks a body lock around Jordan's torso from half/open, walks the hips free of Jordan's legs, and stabilizes side control without releasing the lock until the pin is safe.

Gi. Alex may use a belt grip plus underhook as the connection.

No-gi. Alex locks hands behind the back and rides the pass.

Types / Variations

Body-lock connection

Hands locked around the torso—flagship connection pass.

Chest-to-chest rides

Heavy chest connection without a full body lock, walking past frames.

Connection to mount / side

Finish paths that keep the lock into side control or mount.

Mixing with pressure and speed

Use connection when speed fails; switch to smash if the lock cannot form.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From half guard top; from standing body locks; after stuffing butterfly underhooks.

Counters. Block underhooks, frame inside the lock attempt, guillotine threats, and force space with knees.

Failure mode. Soft locks that open mid-walk.

Connection vs Pressure Passing

Factor Connection Pressure
Primary tool Torso lock / underhooks Shin smash / stacks
Feel Ride while glued Fold and grind
No-gi bias Very high High
Signature pass Body lock pass Smash / over-under

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Dominant at ADCC-style no-gi. Stabilize for IBJJF pass points.

Safety

Watch partners' ribs and breathing under tight body locks in training. Avoid dumping onto the neck.

Common mistakes

  • Locking too high (around the neck) and getting guillotined.
  • Releasing the lock before hips are clear.
  • No head position—getting rolled.
  • Forcing body locks against strong frames instead of clearing frames first.
  • In the gi: ignoring belt/underhook hybrids that achieve the same connection.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is connection passing?

A passing style that locks the torso (often with a body lock) and clears the legs without releasing that connection.

Is connection passing the same as the body lock pass?

Body lock pass is the flagship technique; connection passing is the broader concept including related chest-to-chest rides.

Why is connection passing big in no-gi?

Without sleeve grips for toreando, locking the torso becomes one of the highest-percentage ways to pass.

How do you stop connection passing?

Deny underhooks, frame inside, threaten the neck if they dive, and create knee space before the walk.

Does it work in the gi?

Yes via belt grips and underhook hybrids, though pure body locks are less common than in no-gi.

Connection vs pressure passing?

Connection emphasizes the torso lock and ride; pressure emphasizes smash/fold mechanics—they overlap heavily.

What if I get guillotined while body locking?

Your lock is too high or your head is on the wrong side—relock lower around the torso with safer head position.

The Bottom Line

Connection passing locks the torso and walks past the legs—body lock passing is the flagship. See body lock pass and guard passing.
