Outside Trip (Clinch)

The outside trip (clinch) is a close-range takedown: from over-under or collar-and-underhook ties, you hook the opponent's outside leg and drive so they fall over the blocked base.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Win an underhook or strong collar tie before fishing for the leg.
  • Step outside, hook the far leg, drive the torso.
  • Especially strong near the fence.
  • Chain to inside trip if they hop.
  • Protect the head in dirty-boxing range.

How Outside Trip (Clinch) Works

Establish over-under or collar + underhook → create angle → step outside → hook/reap the far leg → drive torso the opposite way.

On the fence the wall magnifies the trip because they cannot step the free leg back—see Fence Takedown trip finishes.

Mechanically, successful outside trip (clinch) work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

If the hook misses, keep the underhook and switch to Inside Trip (Clinch) or Knee-Block Takedown.

Common Mistakes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an outside trip in the MMA clinch?

Hooking or reaping the outside leg while clinch control drives them over that blocked base.

Outside vs inside trip?

Outside attacks the far leg; inside reaps the near/inside leg after stepping between.

Work without the fence?

Yes—the fence just makes escapes harder.

Beginner drill?

Cooperative over-under entries, then add resistance and a fence.

The Bottom Line

Outside trips win when the underhook is real and the far leg is truly blocked—then drive; if they hop, chain inside.
