Double Kick

Double kick is a two-beat turning phrase. Compare with Narae Chagi (named double roundhouse) and Fast Kick (Bal Bal) so you know whether you mean air-switch, re-chamber, or rapid singles.

What Is Double Kick?

Double kick in sport Taekwondo usually means two Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick)-family kicks in one phrase—rear-then-lead, lead-then-lead, or a hop between.

Narae Chagi is the Korean classroom name many schools use for a double roundhouse. This page keeps the English sport label athletes shout in class.

Compare with Fast Kick (Bal Bal): bal-bal is often a rapid same-leg or stutter-leg flurry; double kick emphasizes the two-count structure.

Under Electronic Scoring (PSS), two valid Palding (Trunk Scoring) or mixed body/head impacts can stack if each kick meets force and surface rules.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Double Kick behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Double Kick Works

Chamber the first kick, score or threaten, keep the knee alive, and fire the second before the opponent resets distance.

Do not fully drop the Chamber (Bal Deung) to the floor unless the variation is a planted one-two. The hang time is the identity.

Hips stay quieter than a single power Rear-Leg Kicking shot so the second kick can still turn over.

Recover to Taekwondo Stance. Two kicks that end in a spin-out gift a Counter Kick.

Mechanically, successful double kick work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, double kick is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill double kick daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Two kicks can each score trunk or head values; there is no automatic “combo bonus” beyond what World Taekwondo Rules currently award for technique type.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Kyorugi: lead body, then same-phrase head—classic double that changes the scoreboard if PSS agrees.

Pads: coach calls “body-body” or “body-head”; you keep the knee up between beats.

Fight picture: double kick appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply double kick after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Body-body double

Two Body Kick (Momtong Chagi) impacts. High volume under Palding (Trunk Scoring).

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Body-head double

First kick to trunk, second to Head Kick (Olgul Chagi).

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Air / jump double

Both kicks before a landing—closer to demo Narae Chagi.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Dropping the chamber so the second kick is just another single.
  • Turning the hip so far on kick one that kick two has no rotation left.
  • Reaching with a straight leg on both beats.
  • Ignoring which kick actually beeped.
  • Calling every flurry a double without a two-count plan.
  • Treating double kick as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick double kick inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so double kick connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether double kick appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe double kick as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put double kick behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep double kick scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on double kick, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for double kick under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Double vs narae?

Narae Chagi is a common Korean name for double roundhouse. Double kick is the English sport label for the two-beat family.

Double vs fast kick?

Fast Kick (Bal Bal) is rapid re-chamber volume; double kick is specifically the two-count phrase.

Do both kicks score?

Each impact is judged separately under Electronic Scoring (PSS).

Lead-lead or rear-lead?

Both exist. Rear-then-lead is a common classroom double.

Scissor?

Scissor Kick switches height or legs more like a scissor; related, not identical.

The Bottom Line

Train double kick as two honest impacts—not as noise that hopes Electronic Scoring (PSS) is generous.
