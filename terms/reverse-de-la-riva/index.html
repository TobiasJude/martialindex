Reverse De La Riva

Reverse de la Riva (RDLR) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard hook where your outside foot hooks inside their lead thigh/knee—opposite the classic outside de la Riva—and feeds berimbolos, sweeps, and back takes.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Inside hook on their lead leg = reverse DLR (not the classic outside hook).
  • Signature modern paths: berimbolo, kiss of the dragon, light sweeps.
  • Gi sleeve/collar grips amplify control; no-gi uses wrist/ankle control.
  • Often paired with collar-sleeve and seated open guard.
  • Parent family: open guard / DLR.

What Is Reverse De La Riva?

Reverse de la Riva is an open-guard configuration where you hook their lead leg from the inside with your outside foot—creating a mirror of classic de la Riva, which hooks from the outside. Combined with sleeve, collar, or ankle control, RDLR became a cornerstone of modern gi open guard (berimbolo era) and remains relevant in no-gi with adjusted grips.

How Reverse De La Riva Works

The inside hook off-balances their lead base and creates inversion space. From there you can berimbolo to the back, sweep them forward/sideways, or elevate into other open-guard hooks. Upper-body grips (especially collar-sleeve) time the spin. Without a live hook and a grip, RDLR collapses into a free pass.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar and sleeve make RDLR sticky and berimbolo-friendly—Espen Mathiesen and many competition systems built careers here.

No-gi. Use ankle sleeves, wrist control, and faster transitions into K-guard, SLX, or seated retention rather than long collar-fed berimbolos.

What Does Reverse De La Riva Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits in open guard, hooks inside Jordan's lead thigh with the outside foot, grips Jordan's near sleeve and collar, and initiates a berimbolo that finishes on Jordan's back.

Gi. Classic collar-sleeve RDLR berimbolo.

No-gi. Alex controls the ankle/wrist and may enter K-guard if Jordan circles off the hook.

Types / Variations

Basic RDLR hook

Inside hook + upper-body grip—retention and sweep platform.

Berimbolo / back-take path

Invert around the hooked leg to expose the back—modern gi signature.

Sweeps and stand-ups

Forward trips, light side sweeps, and wrestling-ups when they overcommit.

Passing reverse DLR

Clear the inside hook, pin the hips, toreando or long-step; do not feed the berimbolo spin.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From seated guard when they step a lead leg; from classic DLR by switching hook sides; from collar-sleeve pulls.

Bottom threats. Berimbolo, sweeps, back takes, transitions to worm/lapel or K-guard.

Top counters. Kill the hook early, keep posture, circle away from the spin, pass with toreando or body lock.

Reverse DLR vs Classic DLR

Factor Reverse DLR Classic DLR
Hook side Inside of lead leg Outside of lead leg
Famous path Berimbolo / kiss of the dragon Classic DLR sweeps / back takes
Gi dominance Very high in modern sport High foundational
No-gi Viable with grip changes Viable with grip changes

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Berimbolos that expose the back set up rear mount scoring and chokes. Watch reaping rules if RDLR turns into inverted leg entanglement.

Safety

Drill berimbolos with controlled pace—neck and knees are exposed in bad spins. Tap early to heel hooks if the game goes inverted in no-gi.

Common mistakes

  • Hook without grips—empty RDLR.
  • Forcing berimbolo when a simple sweep is free.
  • In no-gi: hunting collar habits that do not exist.
  • Inverting into a reaping foul under IBJJF.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How is reverse DLR different from regular DLR?

Hook side: reverse hooks inside the lead leg; classic DLR hooks outside.

Do I need berimbolo to use RDLR?

No. Sweeps and retention work without the spin—but berimbolo is the famous finishing path in gi.

Does reverse DLR work in no-gi?

Yes with ankle/wrist control and faster transitions into K-guard or SLX.

Best grips for RDLR?

In gi: collar and near sleeve (collar-sleeve). In no-gi: wrist and ankle or pants-equivalent controls.

How do I pass reverse DLR?

Clear the inside hook, deny the spin, keep hips heavy, toreando or long-step past the legs.

RDLR vs K-guard?

RDLR is an open-guard hook system; K-guard is a shoulder-roll / leg-entanglement entry platform often reached from similar seated positions.

The Bottom Line

Reverse de la Riva hooks inside the lead leg for berimbolos, sweeps, and modern open-guard offense. Gi loves collar-sleeve; no-gi adapts grips and exits to K-guard. See de la Riva and open guard.
