Hip Heist

A hip heist is an explosive hip switch—often from a sit-out or bottom turtle/guard—to create space, reverse, or stand. It cousins bridge and shrimp, and appears in escapes like trap and roll setups and wrestling stand-ups.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Heist = hip switch + elevation, not a random flop.
  • Wrestling import; huge in BJJ bottom game.
  • Pairs with frames and hand control.
  • Used to reverse, stand, or enter guards.
  • Train slow before live speed.

What Is Hip Heist?

Borrowed from wrestling, the hip heist rotates and lifts the hips to off-balance a top player or clear weight.

In BJJ it shows up from bottom side, turtle, and some guard recoveries—not only folkstyle ride escapes.

Compare bridge (more vertical) and shrimp (more lateral escape).

How Hip Heist Works

Post hands, load hips, and snap them through toward the top player’s weak angle while peeling grips.

Combine with a sit-out or turn-in to face them; establish posture and base on the exit.

From mount threats, a heist can set up the bridge-and-roll family.

Don’t heist into their underhook without a hand-fight plan.

In BJJ classes the heist often appears as a chain: peel the far wrist, sit through, switch the hips, and either face them for a scramble or stand into a collar-tie / body-lock fight. Treat it as a movement vocabulary word you can drop into escapes and sweeps alike.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Extra grips make peeling harder—fight sleeves first.

No-gi. Closer to pure wrestling heists; sweat helps and hurts.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits out, hip heists to clear a ride, and stands to a collar tie.

Jordan uses a heist from bottom half to reverse into top position.

Types / Variations

Sit-out hip heist

Classic wrestling escape path.

Guard / half heist

Create angle to sweep or recover.

Stand-up heist

Clear to the feet with posture.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Used when bottom and stuck under weight. Counters from top: chop the far arm, sprawl, ride the hips. “Escapes”: chain heist into shrimp if the first attempt fails.

Hip Heist vs Bridge

Factor Hip heist Bridge
Primary motion Rotational hip switch Vertical hip drive
Common home Sit-out / turtle / half Mount escape / upa
Feel Turn the corner Launch over
Related shrimp trap-and-roll

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Movement itself is unrestricted; resulting positions score normally.

Safety

Warm up hips and necks—explosive heists on cold mats cause strains.

Common mistakes

  • Heisting with no hand control.
  • Belly-flopping instead of switching hips.
  • Stopping on the knees without posture.
  • Telegraphing every attempt.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a hip heist?

An explosive hip switch used to create space, reverse, or stand from bottom.

Wrestling or BJJ?

Both—wrestling origin, BJJ adoption.

Different from bridge?

Heist rotates/turns; bridge drives more vertically.

When to use?

Bottom turtle, ride escapes, some guard recoveries.

Gi complication?

Grip fighting first.

Common error?

No hand control.

Related escapes?

Trap-and-roll and knee-elbow chains.

The Bottom Line

The hip heist is a turn-the-corner hip switch—pair it with hand fights, then stand or reverse with posture.
