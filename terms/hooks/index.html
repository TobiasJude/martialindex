Hooks (Back Control)

Hooks in back control are your feet seated inside the opponent’s thighs (or hip line), preventing them from turning out—pairing with seatbelt control so you can climb the rear naked choke or transition to a body triangle.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Hooks control hips; seatbelt controls the upper body—need both ideas.
  • Insertion order matters: often far hook first in many curricula, then near.
  • Butterfly / short hooks vs deep hooks change mobility and escape windows.
  • Clearing one hook is the classic escape start—defend the second intentionally.
  • Hooks score relevance depends on the current rulebook—verify before competing.

What Is Hooks (Back Control)?

In BJJ, hooks usually means the rear hooks of back control: dorsiflexed feet inside the thighs that pin the hips to your pelvis. Without them (or a body triangle substitute), the opponent can hip-escape, turn in, and reclaim guard.

Hooks are also a scoring/positional concept in many sport frameworks—control with both hooks in is the textbook back mount picture. Martial Index covers the mechanical meaning first, then reminds athletes to check federation definitions.

How Hooks (Back Control) Works

From a seatbelt ride on turtle or after a back take, shoehorn one foot in, flatten your shin along their thigh, then insert the second. Keep heels engaged—floppy feet peel easily.

Use hooks to walk your hips up toward the shoulders for choking angles, or to lift and tilt them into arm exposures for armbar from back.

If a hook is cleared, fight to reinsert before they turn belly-down fully; sometimes switch to body triangle rather than chasing a lost foot.

Hooks are a conversation with their hips: every time they clear one foot, your next action—reinsert, body triangle, or climb—should already be chosen.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips can help retain hooks; be aware of illegal gripping nuances by rule set.

No-gi. Pure shin angle and heel pressure—expect more dynamic hook fighting.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Lee inserts the far hook on a turtle, drags them flat, seats the second hook, and begins RNC hand fighting.

Sam loses the near hook, immediately switches to a body triangle instead of surrendering the back entirely.

Types / Variations

Deep double hooks

Classic hip pins—maximum control, less free movement.

Butterfly / short hooks

More dynamic tilting; easier to lose if lazy.

Single-hook rides

Transitional—must upgrade quickly or risk the turn-in.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: back takes from turtle, failed passes where they turn away, berimbolo-style finishes. Counters: peel heels, clear one hook, elbow-escape the hips. Escapes: remove near hook first in many systems, turn into the seatbelt gap, reclaim half guard.

Hooks vs Body Triangle

Factor Hooks Body triangle
Hip control Feet inside thighs Figure-four torso lock
Mobility Higher Lower
Escape start Clear one hook Wedge/peel figure-four
Best next step RNC climb or upgrade to triangle Ride out hand fights

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Whether “both hooks in” defines scored back control depends on the current federation text. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Do not kick heels into the groin as a control cheat. Keep hooks technical; tap if abdominal pressure becomes dangerous.

Common mistakes

  • Pointing toes / floppy feet that peel instantly.
  • Inserting hooks without a seatbelt.
  • Chasing a lost hook while they complete the turn-in.
  • Crossing feet in front of the body (losing back structure).

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Which hook first?

Curricula differ; many teach far hook first on turtle—learn your coach’s order and why.

Are hooks required for points?

Often central to back-control scoring definitions—verify the current rulebook.

Hooks vs body triangle?

Hooks = mobility; triangle = immobilization. Both belong in a complete back game.

How do I keep hooks?

Heels active, knees pinching, seatbelt alive.

What if I only get one hook?

Threaten the second immediately or switch to body triangle / technical mount paths.

Gi pants gripping?

Rule-dependent—verify legality.

Beginner priority?

Seatbelt + two hooks before fancy arm traps.

The Bottom Line

Hooks are hip control from the back: active heels, paired with a live seatbelt. Upgrade to a body triangle when you need to freeze the torso for hand fighting.
