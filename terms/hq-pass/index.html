HQ Pass

The HQ pass is passing from headquarters—the split-squat / knee-pin station between open and half—via float-to-mount, knee-cut, and pressure branches—clustered with knee-cut, open hub, and half hub.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • HQ is a platform, not one finish.
  • Float to mount when their knee is soft.
  • Knee-cut when the knee floats offline.
  • Bridge open-guard speed and half-guard pressure.
  • Gi and no-gi staple.

What Is HQ Pass?

HQ pass names finishes launched from headquarters (HQ): you pin their near thigh with your shin/knee while standing or kneeling in a split stance. Across: knee-cut, float, force half, company.

How It Works

Enter HQ by controlling pants/ankles and pinning the near knee, keep heavy hips, then float/pummel to mount, knee-cut to side, or smash into half and continue. Hands often post near shoulders for float entries.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips make the knee pin sticky.

No-gi. Ankle rides and body connection; same platform logic.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Open guard → you step to HQ → float pummel to mount, or cut the knee when they frame.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Float / handstand-style clear to mount from HQ.

Competition variation

Knee-cut from HQ to modified side.

Related option

Force half and continue with half-hub tools.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From toreando pressure, DLR clears, seated open. Counters. RDLR hooks, butterfly elevators, wrestle-ups—see RDLR sweeps.

How It Compares

Vs raw knee-cut: HQ is the station that often precedes it. Vs company pass: company emphasizes continuous torso connection; HQ emphasizes the knee-pin platform.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal sport passing station.

Safety

Float entries need controlled timing—protect their knees/neck.

Common mistakes

  • Soft knee pin (they rehook).
  • Knee-cutting without underhook/crossface plan.
  • Standing still in HQ with no branch.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an HQ pass?

Any finish from the headquarters knee-pin platform—float to mount, knee-cut, or force half.

Is HQ only no-gi?

No—gi and no-gi both use it.

How do you enter HQ?

From open-guard pressure: pin the near knee while controlling legs.

What next?

Mount, side, or half-hub passes.

Common mistake?

Camping in HQ without a branch.

Beginner-friendly?

Yes as a concept; float timing is intermediate.

Martial Index home?

Under headquarters next to knee-cut and open/half hubs.

The Bottom Line

HQ pass is platform thinking—enter HQ, then float, cut, or force half on purpose.
