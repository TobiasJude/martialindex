Gi (Kimono)

The gi (kimono) is Brazilian jiu-jitsu’s traditional uniform—jacket, pants, and rank belt—whose fabric creates grips, friction, and entire guard systems that do not exist in no-gi.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Gi = jacket + pants + belt; fabric is both handle and constraint.
  • Collar, sleeve, and pant grips slow the game and enable lapel attacks.
  • Competition gis must meet the event’s uniform measurements—verify live rules.
  • Pair with a rashguard underneath for hygiene when your gym allows.
  • Cross-train no-gi so you are not fabric-dependent.

What Is Gi (Kimono)?

Borrowed from judo’s judogi and adapted for BJJ, the kimono turns cloth into an extension of wrestling clinches. You can control sleeves to stop punches in self-defense mythos, and in sport you can build spider, lasso, and collar-sleeve open guards.

Friction from cotton changes escapes: arms do not slip free as easily as in no-gi, so technical grip breaks and posture matter more. See grip fighting if live on your site, and posture battles in closed guard.

Equipment literacy includes fit, weave, shrinkage, and belt tying—plus washing culture so mats stay healthy.

How Gi (Kimono) Works

Learn legal/useful grips: collar depth, sleeve cuff vs mid-sleeve, pant grips near the knee—then learn to strip them.

Build at least one gi-only attack (cross-collar family) and one grip-independent attack so you transfer to no-gi.

For competition, check jacket length, sleeve length, and patches against the current rulebook for your federation.

Layer a fitted rashguard under the jacket when permitted for skin protection.

Gi vs No-Gi

This page is the gi. Training in the kimono is the default traditional path.

Vs no-gi: expect slower pace, more grip fights, lapel chokes, and pants-based control.

Many athletes own multiple gis for hygiene rotation.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex’s spider guard vanishes on no-gi night—sleeve grips were the whole game—so they add butterfly hooks.

Jordan buys an oversized fashion gi and fails IBJJF measurement—uniform rules are real.

Sam uses pant grips to finish a knee-cut pass that stalls in sweaty no-gi.

Types / Variations

Single weave / lighter

Cooler for hot rooms; less armor.

Pearl / higher weave

Common modern competition feel—durability varies by brand.

Competition cut

Trim fit aimed at passing uniform checks—verify current standards.

Training beater

Daily workhorse you wash constantly.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries to gi mastery: grip drills, collar posture breaks, and lapel guard introductions.

Counters: grip breaks, sleeve control prevention, and standing posture.

Escapes from grip dependence: weekly no-gi rounds.

Gi vs No-Gi

Factor Gi No-gi
Handles Collar/sleeve/pants Wrists, head, body locks
Pace Often slower Often faster / scramble-heavy
Signature tools Lapel chokes, spider/lasso Front headlocks, many leg games
Attire link Kimono + belt Rashguard + shorts/spats

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF and other federations publish gi measurement and patch rules. ADCC flagship is no-gi. Always verify the event you entered—see IBJJF Rules and ADCC.

Safety

Trim nails; wash the gi; do not train in a soaked moldy kimono. Tap regardless of expensive patches.

Common mistakes

  • Never washing the gi.
  • Only learning lapel tricks.
  • Buying based on Instagram, failing weigh-in measurements.
  • Skipping belt hygiene and mat burns by refusing rashguards when allowed.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a BJJ gi?

A kimono uniform: jacket, pants, and belt used for fabric-grip grappling.

Gi or kimono?

Same idea; “gi” is common English usage in BJJ.

Why train gi?

Grip skill, friction control, and traditional sport pathways.

Can I wear a rashguard under it?

Usually yes for training; competition underlayers follow the event book.

How many gis do I need?

At least two if you train often—so one can dry.

Does belt color go with the gi?

Yes—the belt displays your rank.

Gi vs no-gi for beginners?

Either works; many schools start gi for slower feedback loops.

The Bottom Line

The gi is portable handles. Learn to use them—and to survive when they disappear in no-gi.
