Darce from Side Control

The darce from side control is the classic darce (D’Arce / no-gi brabo): from side control they reach an underhook, you thread your arm through that armpit and behind their neck, lock a figure-four, then finish with depth and body pressure. It is the positional sibling of the anaconda from front headlock.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • The trigger is their underhook or wrestle-up from bottom side—not a random neck hunt.
  • Depth before squeeze: the hand must clear the neck line before you lock.
  • Parent: darce. Thread cousin: anaconda (opposite insert).
  • Finish by walking back, sitting to a hip, or dropping chest weight—not a biceps curl.
  • Gi brabo variants exist; the no-gi darce is the search default.

What Is Darce from Side Control?

From side control, a darce is the punish for the most common escape hand: the underhook. As they swim for your hip, you beat the armpit, thread deep past the neck, and lock a head-and-arm strangle that is mechanically an arm-triangle with your own arm as the cutter.

It also appears when they wrestle up to their knees or turtle from side. The geometry is always armpit-to-neck. If you cannot get that line, you do not have a darce—you have a headlock.

Martial Index isolates the side-control path because that is the phrase people search, then links the parent darce and the anaconda from front headlock for the rest of the front-headlock family.

How Darce from Side Control Works

When the underhook appears, drop your near shoulder, slide the threading arm through the armpit palm-down, and keep traveling until the hand shows past the far side of their neck. Gable or figure-four (bicep catch) to connect.

Do not squeeze yet. Walk your knees toward their hips or sit to the opposite hip so their trapped shoulder packs into their neck. Chest pressure closes the leftover space.

If the thread is shallow, use it as control: dump them back to the mat, spin behind, or switch to a front-headlock guillotine. A shallow squeeze just burns your arms.

Gi players can add a brabo-style collar assist; the no-gi finish still needs the same depth.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Brabo / collar-assisted versions raise tap rate; you can also pin the underhooking sleeve before you thread.

No-gi. Pure darce. Expect wrestle-ups—thread as they rise, then dump them back to their side.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex feels the bottom underhook in side control, threads deep on the rise, sits to a hip, and finishes without a scramble.

Jordan’s first thread is shallow; they dump the wrestle-up, reset side control, and thread again with depth.

Types / Variations

Bottom-underhook darce

The classroom trigger from side control.

Wrestle-up / turtle darce

They stand their hips up; you catch armpit-to-neck on the rise.

Front-headlock darce

Same thread after a snap-down—see parent darce and anaconda pages.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: underhook from bottom side, wrestle-up, or they give the armpit while escaping north-south. Counters: hide the underhook, keep the elbow tight, extract the head. Escapes: straighten the trapped arm and circle before depth; after the lock, tap to the choke.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult darces are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

A shallow darce can crank the neck. Prioritize depth and tap early. Do not rip gator rolls on unprepared partners.

Common mistakes

  • Squeezing before the hand clears the neck.
  • Threading so wide they simply extract the arm.
  • Ignoring the wrestle-up and letting them stand out.
  • Confusing a front headlock hug with a locked darce.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Darce or D’Arce?

Same choke. Spelling varies; Martial Index uses darce as the slug.

Why do I never finish?

Usually depth. Get the hand past the neck before you lock and walk your body.

Gi brabo vs darce?

Related head-and-arm idea; brabo often uses a collar. This page is the no-gi thread from side.

What if they wrestle up?

That is still a darce window—thread on the rise, then dump them to their side.

Anaconda instead?

If you are in front headlock and the neck-side insert is easier, yes. See anaconda from front headlock.

Beginner friendly?

The trigger is beginner; the finish is intermediate. Drill depth first.

Legal?

Standard adult choke—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

Darce from side control is the underhook punish: thread armpit-to-neck, get depth, then move your body. Pair it with the parent darce and the anaconda from front headlock.
