Over-Under vs Butterfly

Over-under vs butterfly is the matchup between the over-under pass and butterfly guard. Under guard passing, it pairs with pressure passing and compares to the body-lock system.

Key Takeaways

  • Kill hooks before you tripod.
  • Over + under on the legs pins hips.
  • Butterfly wants elevation—deny lift.
  • Compare body lock when hooks are sticky.

What Is Over-Under vs Butterfly?

Butterfly’s hooks elevate and sweep; over-under answers by trapping one leg under and one over while driving chest pressure.

This is a matchup page—see over-under-pass for the base mechanic.

Also bridge to smash and body-lock when the over-under stalls.

How Over-Under vs Butterfly Works

Close the distance, insert the underhook behind one thigh and the overhook/shoulder on the other.

Tripod on toes, stretch the underhooked leg, and walk to the underhook side while keeping hips heavy.

If they pummel a clean underhook on your torso, switch to body lock or re-angle.

Finish by clearing the near hook and landing in side control with crossface.

Think of butterfly as an elevator: if hooks stay alive, you float. Over-under’s job is to pin that elevator before you walk. When the underhooked leg stretches and the overhooked shoulder stays heavy, their hips stop translating into sweeps—and that is when the pass actually begins.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips reinforce the underhooked leg.

No-gi. Shoulder pressure and head placement replace fabric.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex stuffs both hooks, locks over-under, tripoding, and passes to the underhook side.

Jordan meets strong elevation and switches to a body lock instead of forcing the smash.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from standing open guard and after toreando fails. Counters: elevating sweeps, arm drags. Escapes: shrimp and rebuild hooks before the tripod settles.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Standard pass rules—clear legs and stabilize per current PDF.

Safety

Don’t drive shoulders into necks while stacking butterfly players who invert.

Common mistakes

  • Tripoding before hooks die.
  • Walking to the wrong side.
  • Ignoring guillotine when they sit up.
  • No abandon to body lock.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why over-under against butterfly?

It pins the hooks and denies the elevation butterfly wants.

Key detail?

Kill hooks, then tripod—order matters.

Gi grips?

Pants on the underhooked leg help stretch.

When to body lock instead?

When they regenerate hooks or win a torso underhook.

Common sweep against me?

Elevation into a butterfly sweep when your head floats.

Related pass?

Stack pass when they pull you forward.

Pillar?

Guard passing.

The Bottom Line

Over-under vs butterfly is a hook-killing smash—pin the legs, deny lift, or abandon into the body-lock system.
