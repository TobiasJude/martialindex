Slash (Passing)

The slash pass is an outside guard passing idea—cutting an angle past the legs with speed and posture rather than settling into a smash. It sits near speed passing, toreando / bullfighter, and long-step ideas. Pair it with frames awareness and guard retention counters.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Slash = outside angle cut, not a single trademarked move.
  • Lives under the guard-passing pillar next to speed / toreando families.
  • Wins by posture, grips, and lateral commitment—not chest pressure alone.
  • Chains into knee-cut or body-lock when the bottom player sits up.
  • Train against active retention, not cooperative feet.

What Is Slash (Passing)?

In gym slang, slash describes driving past the guard on an outside path—hips turning, lead leg clearing, hands pinning or redirecting legs—rather than camping in half guard.

It overlaps toreando and some long-step / X-pass family mechanics; Martial Index keeps a dedicated page because searchers look for the name as a system label.

Contrast with pressure passing and body lock: slash prioritizes mobility and timing.

How Slash (Passing) Works

Establish posture and a safe grip map (pants, ankles, or no-gi wrist/shin control). Force the bottom player’s feet off your hips, then commit laterally so their recompose can’t catch both legs.

Keep your head and shoulders stacked—broken posture turns a slash into a sweep magnet. Use base steps, not crossover trips.

If they sit up into butterfly or half, switch to knee cut or body-lock system rather than forcing the outside path.

Finish by clearing the near hip and settling into side control or north-south—don’t celebrate mid-pass.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant and sleeve grips make slash entries sticky; watch lapel traps that punish overcommitment.

No-gi. Underhooks and shin rides replace fabric—speed and chest orientation matter more.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex pins both shins, slashes past the near leg as the bottom player reaches for De La Riva, and lands in side control.

Jordan fakes a toreando, draws a hip escape, then slashes the opposite side into a knee-cut headquarters.

Types / Variations

Grip-led slash

Pants or ankle control redirecting legs like a fast toreando.

Posture-led slash

Hands light, hips heavy forward—more wrestling pass than grip pass.

Slash into knee-cut / body lock

When the outside path stalls, fold into knee cut or body lock.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from standing open guard, failed stacks, and after opening closed guard. Counters: long hooks, collar drags, K-guard. Escapes mid-pass: shrimp and invert early.

Slash / Outside Speed vs Pressure Pass

Factor Slash / outside speed Pressure / smash
Primary tool Angle + timing Chest weight + pins
Risk Sweeps if posture breaks Stalling if frames win
Common siblings Toreando, long step Over-under, body lock
Pillar home Guard passing Guard passing

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Standard positional scoring applies when the pass clears the legs and stabilizes—verify current IBJJF / event definitions for pass criteria. No special slash foul.

Safety

Outside passes can dump weight onto knees and necks in scramble—control your landing and train tap-friendly intensity.

Common mistakes

  • Crossing feet and getting swept.
  • Diving without posture.
  • Ignoring the second leg after clearing the first.
  • Refusing to chain into knee-cut when slash fails.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the slash pass in BJJ?

An outside, angle-cutting guard pass concept emphasizing speed and posture over smash pressure.

Is slash the same as toreando?

Related but not identical—toreando is the classic bullfighter family; slash is gym language for a cutting outside system.

When should I slash vs pressure pass?

Slash when legs are extended and mobile; pressure when you already have chest connection.

Does it work no-gi?

Yes—replace pant grips with shin/wrist control and keep posture.

What do I do if they sit up?

Chain to knee-cut or body-lock rather than forcing the outside path.

Common counter?

Collar drag, long hook, or inversion into the passer’s committed angle.

Where does it live in Martial Index?

Under the guard-passing pillar next to speed passing and toreando.

The Bottom Line

Slash passing is an outside angle under guard passing—commit with posture, clear both legs, and chain when the path closes.
