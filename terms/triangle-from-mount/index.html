Triangle from Mount

The triangle from mount is a triangle choke setup path: you already own mount, isolate one arm, thread a leg over the shoulder/neck line, and lock a figure-four—often falling to the side once the bite is deep. Searchers also use mounted triangle for the locked-on-top finish; the pages overlap on purpose.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • This page is the path from mount, not a second parent triangle: start on top, isolate, thread, lock.
  • You can finish while staying high or concede mount and drop to a classic front triangle—both count.
  • Sibling finish: the named mounted triangle (locked control that stays on top).
  • Chains with armbar from mount when they pull the arm free.
  • Gi sleeve pins help isolation; no-gi uses wrist rides and head-and-arm frames.

What Is Triangle from Mount?

From mount, a triangle is available whenever one of their arms is trapped high and the far shoulder can be excluded from the lock. The triangle-from-mount is the how you get there story: pin a wrist, lift a shoulder, walk a knee behind the head or through the armpit, then throw the far leg across the neck.

Unlike the triangle from guard, you begin already on top. That changes risk: a sloppy thread can dump you into their closed guard, but a deep lock lets you fall to the side on purpose once the figure-four is safer than staying mounted.

Martial Index splits this path from the mounted triangle so “how do I triangle from mount?” and “what is a mounted triangle?” can both rank without cloning copy. Cross-read both; the mechanics share a family.

How Triangle from Mount Works

Stabilize low mount first. If they frame on your hips or neck, capture a wrist and pin it to the mat or walk it toward their ear so one elbow cannot return to the ribs.

Create a split-squat or high-knee behind their head: the near knee plugs the space they want for an underhook, and the far leg becomes the strangling thigh. Grab your own ankle if needed so the foot cannot stall on the biceps.

Lock the figure-four (shin behind the opposite knee), pinch the knees, and decide: stay mounted and squeeze, or roll toward the trapped-arm side into a classic front triangle with a perpendicular angle.

If they shrimp hard, convert to an armbar from mount or settle into S-mount rather than forcing a shallow bite.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar pins freeze the isolated arm; you can also use their own lapel to keep the far shoulder out of the lock while you thread.

No-gi. Wrist control and forehead pressure replace cloth. Expect more scramble athleticism—keep chest weight until the ankle is through.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex pins a wrist in mount, walks a knee behind the ear, grabs the ankle, and locks a triangle without ever giving up top until the figure-four is already tight.

Jordan threads early, feels them explode, and falls to the trapped-arm side—same choke geometry as a guard triangle, just entered from mount.

Types / Variations

High-mount / split-squat thread

Knee behind the head, far foot pumped over the neck—Craig Jones–style entries that try to stay safe on top until the lock is real.

Fall-to-side front triangle

Once the figure-four bites, roll off mount into a perpendicular front triangle and finish like the guard variant.

Armbar branch

If they extract the arm, the same isolation often hands you S-mount or a straight armbar instead of the choke.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered from low or high mount when one elbow floats. Also appears after a failed americana from mount if they extend to defend.

Counters: keep both elbows glued to the ribs, bridge and shrimp before the ankle clears, or pummel the trapped arm back inside. Escapes after the lock: posture, stack, or pull the head out—tap early if the angle is already perpendicular.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Standard adult triangles are generally legal sport chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Triangles are blood chokes. Partners should tap to pressure, not wait to go out. Do not crank the neck sideways while adjusting the angle.

Common mistakes

  • Threading the leg before the arm is isolated—inviting the underhook.
  • Falling off mount with a loose figure-four and landing in their closed guard.
  • Squearing square instead of cutting toward the trapped-arm side.
  • Treating this page as a duplicate of mounted triangle instead of a setup-path companion.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is triangle from mount the same as a mounted triangle?

Often the same family and overlapping search intent. This page is the setup path from mount; the mounted-triangle page is the locked-on-top named finish. Read both.

Do I have to stay in mount to finish?

No. Many high-percentage finishes fall to the side once the figure-four is deep.

What if they pull the arm out?

Switch to an armbar or S-mount. The isolation work is not wasted.

Gi or no-gi first?

Learn the isolation and thread no-gi, then add sleeve pins in the gi.

Does this work for short legs?

Yes if you exclude the far shoulder and cut angle—same lesson as the parent triangle.

How does this relate to closed guard triangles?

Same figure-four geometry; different start position and risk (dumping off mount vs stacking from guard).

Legal in IBJJF?

Adult triangle chokes are standard legal submissions—verify your current division book.

The Bottom Line

Triangle from mount is a path: isolate in mount, thread, lock, then stay high or fall aside. Cross-read the mounted triangle for the named on-top finish and the parent triangle choke for sankaku theory.
