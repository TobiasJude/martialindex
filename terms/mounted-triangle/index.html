Mounted Triangle

The mounted triangle is the named triangle you can finish while remaining on top in mount: the figure-four is already locked around neck and one arm, and you do not have to concede bottom to finish. Searchers also type triangle from mount for the setup path—read both.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • This page is the locked position / named finish; triangle-from-mount is the how you got here path.
  • You can stay mounted and squeeze, or later fall to a front triangle on purpose.
  • Same sankaku rules: exclude the far shoulder, pinch, cut a small angle even from top.
  • Chains with armbar from mount if they extract the arm.
  • Heavy cross-links on purpose—the SERP overlap is real.

What Is Mounted Triangle?

“Mounted triangle” in gyms and search bars usually means the triangle is already on and you are still the top player. That is a different question than “how do I start a triangle when I have mount?”—which is why Martial Index also has triangle from mount.

The value of staying mounted is positional: if the choke fails you still have mount points and the next attack. The value of falling aside is finishing percentage: a perpendicular front triangle often bites harder. Good players can do both from the same lock.

Do not treat these two slugs as duplicates. This page emphasizes the locked-on-top state, S-mount-adjacent control, and finishing without immediately giving up top. The path page emphasizes isolation and the thread.

How Mounted Triangle Works

Once the figure-four is on from mount, keep your base. A posted hand and a heavy hip prevent the bridge that dumps a greedy finisher into closed guard.

Pinch the knees, lift their head, and slightly walk toward the trapped-arm side even while mounted—one knee may stay down, one may float. Pull the trapped arm across.

If they are about to explode you off a shallow lock, either tighten on top or fall to the safe side into a standard front triangle. A shallow lock plus a fall is how you lose mount and the choke.

If they pull the arm free, you are often in a beautiful S-mount / armbar spot. Take it.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control on the trapped arm lets you stay mounted longer while you finish.

No-gi. Wrist rides and head control. Expect more explosive bridges—decide early: stay or fall.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex locks a mounted triangle, posts a hand, and finishes without ever sitting to guard.

Jordan feels a huge bridge, falls to the trapped-arm side, and finishes the same figure-four as a front triangle.

Types / Variations

Stay-mounted finish

The named picture: figure-four on, still top, squeeze.

Mounted lock to front triangle

You already have the bite; you fall on purpose.

S-mount adjacent triangle

High mount / S-mount control that is one step from both triangle and armbar.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

You arrive here via the triangle-from-mount path, a failed mount armbar, or a scramble that leaves you on top with a leg already over the shoulder. Counters: extract the arm, bridge before the lock, hide the far shoulder. Escapes after the lock: stack from the bottom if they fell, or explode the shallow top lock before it tightens.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult triangle chokes are standard legal submissions. Mount points (if scored) depend on control—verify the current book. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Do not crank the neck to stay mounted. If you fall, protect their head. Tap to chokes.

Common mistakes

  • Writing off the mounted triangle as “just triangle from mount” without reading the path page.
  • Squeezing a shallow lock and getting bridged to closed guard.
  • Never pulling the trapped arm across.
  • Forgetting the armbar when they extract the arm.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is this the same article as triangle from mount?

No. Same family, different job. This is the locked-on-top name; that is the setup path. Cross-read them.

Should I stay mounted or fall?

Stay if the lock is deep and your base is good. Fall if falling makes the choke real and the lock is already safe.

Points if I fall?

Depends on the rule set and whether you keep control. Verify the current book.

Short legs?

Yes if the far shoulder is out—same as any triangle.

Gi help?

Sleeves help you stay mounted while you finish.

Next sibling?

Triangle from mount, then armbar from mount.

Legal?

Standard adult triangle—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

Mounted triangle is the locked-on-top name: finish while you still have mount, or fall only after the figure-four is real. For the setup story, use triangle from mount.
