Reverse Seoi Nage

Reverse seoi nage is a modern opposite-side seoi entry—often linked to Olympic champion Choi Min-ho—distinct from classical Ippon Seoi Nage. IJF legality has changed: banned then returned for seniors/juniors; cadets still see shido.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Modern competition entry under Seoi Nage (Judo).
  • Compare classical Ippon Seoi Nage.
  • Seniors/juniors: allowed again in LA 2028-cycle rules; cadets: shido / no score.
  • Safety: neck/shoulder control and ukemi matter.
  • Not a classical Kodokan gokyo title.
  • Follow to Ne-Waza carefully after twisted landings.

What is Reverse Seoi Nage?

Reverse seoi nage (also called reverse seoi) is a modern competition shoulder throw in which tori loads uke in a non-classical direction—typically turning so the throw runs opposite the familiar ippon-seoi path. It is not a traditional Kodokan gokyo catalogue name; it is a sporting innovation that spread worldwide after elite success.

JudoInside and contemporary reporting credit South Korea’s Choi Min-ho as the originator visible at world level—seen as early as the 2003 Osaka World Championships and scored at the 2004 Athens Olympics after an earlier non-score. The technique became trendy globally, then faced IJF restriction on safety grounds around late 2021, then returned for senior and junior events in the rule updates debuting at Paris Grand Slam 2025 for the LA 2028 cycle. Cadet events still penalise reverse seoi with shido and no score.

Martial Index files reverse seoi under Seoi Nage (Judo) so it sits beside Ippon Seoi Nage, Drop Seoi Nage, and Korean Seoi without pretending it is classical kata material. BJJ cousin seoi-nage stays BJJ-labelled.

How it works

Classical ippon-seoi loads along a familiar turn; reverse seoi twists the loading so uke is thrown across a less expected line—often with lapel/sleeve involvement that feels “backwards” relative to textbook seoi. Forward or rotational Kuzushi still matters; reverse is not a magic spin from flat postures.

Because the fall line can be unusual for uke, control and clear direction are the safety core. IJF materials have criticised seoi variations that twist without control, deny ukemi, or dump toward the neck. Coach reverse only when partners can fall safely and tori can finish with a known direction.

Standing or dropping finishes both appear in film. Do not confuse reverse seoi with merely dropping classical seoi—see Drop Seoi Nage and Seoi Otoshi for those. If the attack fails, avoid wrenching the neck; disengage or transition to ordinary ne-waza.

Scores, when legal for the age band, still use Ippon / Waza-Ari criteria. Cadet reverse seoi currently draws shido without score under the LA 2028-cycle explanations.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Gi grips enable many reverse entries. In the Judogi, unusual lapel twists are common in the pattern—train legal gripping and positive attacking purpose under Judo Rules.

No-gi reverse shoulder turns exist in other grappling, but IJF age-band rules are a judo competition story. Transfers: unexpected loading angles. Does not transfer: assuming senior legality equals cadet legality.

What it looks like in practice

After the ban years, reverse seoi became rarer on the World Tour even when re-allowed—athletes and cadets who grew up under restriction simply practiced it less (as contemporary observers noted). Expect uneven distribution: some clubs never stopped drilling it; others must rebuild from zero.

In Randori, introduce reverse only with informed partners and mat space. Twisted landings complicate pin angles—still hunt Osaekomi when chest contact remains, via ideas in Seoi to Osaekomi.

Film study: watch whether tori keeps a clear throw direction or merely spins into chaos. Chaos is not high-level judo.

Types / variations

Standing reverse seoi

Upright opposite-side load finishing on the feet—demand clear ukemi lanes.

Dropping reverse seoi

Knees down with reverse loading—compounded risk; coach carefully.

Age-band restricted practice

Cadets: treat as illegal scoring tool under current shido rule; drill only if your national body and coach explicitly structure educational, safe practice outside contest habits.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter from grips that already create rotational break. Counters include posture changes, peeling twisted lapels, and circling out of the reverse turn. Combinations should connect to ordinary seoi and foot sweeps so reverse is not a single-trick identity.

Moving Uchi-Komi at reduced intensity comes before nage-komi. Partners must know the fall line before speed increases.

Compare

Versus Ippon Seoi Nage: classical one-arm path versus opposite-side modern path. Versus Korean Seoi / Drop Seoi Nage: deep drop aesthetics versus reverse directionality (related Korean sporting history, different mechanic). Versus Morote Seoi Nage: two-hand classical versus reverse competition innovation.

IJF notes & safety

Under IJF rules for the LA 2028 cycle (debuted internationally at Paris Grand Slam 2025), reverse seoi-nage is allowed at senior and junior events. At cadet events it is penalised with shido and receives no score. Always verify your current national adaptations. Safety first: uncontrolled twisting dumps toward the neck are outside Martial Index coaching recommendations regardless of score potential. See Judo Rules.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Teaching reverse before classical seoi literacy.
  • Ignoring cadet vs senior legality.
  • Spinning without a clear fall direction.
  • Neck wrenching on failed entries.
  • Assuming the 2022-era ban is still universal for seniors.
  • Assuming re-legalisation means risk-free.

Training notes

Rebuild reverse only under coaches who watched the rule changes. Pair classical Ippon Seoi Nage weeks with limited reverse introductions for seniors/juniors who need it tactically.

Ukemi drills for unusual fall lines before any competitive speed. Tap training intensity the moment landings look scary.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Who popularised reverse seoi?

Widely credited to Choi Min-ho at world/Olympic level in the 2000s.

Is reverse seoi legal now?

Seniors/juniors allowed; cadets shido—verify Judo Rules / national body.

Is it a Kodokan gokyo throw?

No—modern competition innovation under Seoi Nage (Judo).

Reverse vs Korean seoi?

Different ideas—see Korean Seoi.

Main safety issue?

Uncontrolled twists that deny Ukemi or load the neck.

Compare to ippon-seoi?

See Ippon Seoi Nage.

Parent?

Seoi Nage (Judo).

The Bottom Line

Treat reverse seoi as a modern, rule-sensitive weapon: learn classical Ippon Seoi Nage first, verify age-band legality, and never trade neck safety for a highlight reel.
