Anaconda from Front Headlock

The anaconda from front headlock is the anaconda started from a front headlock: your choking arm enters on the neck side, travels under the far armpit, you connect a grip, then often gator-roll to tighten the head-and-arm strangle. It is the sprawl-side sibling of the darce from side control.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Insert is opposite the darce: neck first, then far armpit.
  • The roll is a tightening tool, not a circus flip—keep the grip through it.
  • Parent: anaconda. Control layer: front headlock.
  • Sprawl → front headlock → anaconda is a complete standing-to-finish chain.
  • If the thread will not go, stay in front headlock and hunt a guillotine or darce instead.

What Is Anaconda from Front Headlock?

After a sprawl or snap-down you often sit in front headlock: chest on their shoulders, hands controlling the neck. The anaconda converts that control into a choke by wrapping the neck and far arm into one loop, then rolling so their own shoulder packs the carotid.

People confuse it with the darce because both are front-headlock head-and-arm chokes. Memory hook: darce goes through the armpit toward the neck; anaconda goes around the neck toward the armpit.

Martial Index isolates the front-headlock path because that is how competitors actually catch it—rarely as a random side-control hunt.

How Anaconda from Front Headlock Works

From a stable front headlock, release just enough to thread the choking arm deep across the neck and under the far armpit. Connect gable or figure-four on the far side. Elbows tight.

Step a leg over their back and roll toward the choking-arm side—the gator roll. The roll is there to remove their posting base and cinch the loop, not to dump them on their head.

Once on your side, squeeze elbows, pull the choking elbow to your hip, and expand your chest. Walk toward their head if space remains.

If they widen the far elbow and kill the thread, return to front-headlock control, spin behind, or switch to a darce / guillotine rather than forcing a loose hug.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar control on the sprawl can help the snap-down; the anaconda finish is still a naked thread.

No-gi. The default. Expect more athletic rolls—drill them slowly.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex sprawls a double, seats front headlock, threads the anaconda, and gator-rolls to a tap.

Jordan cannot clear the far armpit, keeps the front headlock, and switches to a darce on the next underhook.

Types / Variations

Gator-roll anaconda

The picture most people mean from front headlock.

Static / no-roll finish

If they are already on their side, you can squeeze without rolling.

Turtle anaconda

Same thread when they are on all fours after a stuffed shot.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: sprawl, snap-down, turtle front headlock. Counters: widen the far elbow, hide the neck, stand up into you. Escapes: peel the thread before the roll; after the roll, tap to the choke and protect the neck.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult anacondas are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Gator rolls can dump partners onto their necks if you rip them. Drill slow, keep shoulders on the mat, and tap early to the choke.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling without a connected grip.
  • Threading so shallow that you only have a headlock.
  • Abandoning front-headlock control when the first thread fails.
  • Confusing darce and anaconda inserts mid-scramble.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Do I have to gator-roll?

No, but the roll is how most people tighten a front-headlock anaconda.

Darce or anaconda after a sprawl?

Whichever insert is open. Neck-side thread = anaconda; armpit-side = darce.

Is this legal in gi IBJJF?

Adult chokes of this type are standard—verify your current book.

Can beginners drill the roll?

Yes, slowly, on a crash pad or with a coach watching the neck.

What if they stand up?

Ride the front headlock, snap them down again, or abandon into a takedown defense.

Same as an arm-triangle?

Same head-and-arm compression idea; the anaconda uses your threaded arm from front headlock rather than a side-control walk.

The Bottom Line

Anaconda from front headlock is sprawl control turned into a thread: neck to far armpit, connect, roll, squeeze. Keep the front headlock if the first insert fails.
