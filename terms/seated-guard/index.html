Seated Guard

Seated guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard posture where you sit upright facing the opponent—managing distance with hands and feet while choosing entries into butterfly, DLR, shin-to-shin, SLX, or K-guard.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Upright sit = seated guard (a posture, not one technique).
  • Primary job: retain and choose the next open-guard system.
  • Gi and no-gi both use it; grips differ.
  • Hands and feet work together—don't become a static target.
  • Parent open guard.

What Is Seated Guard?

Seated guard is the upright open-guard sit: hips down, torso relatively vertical, legs free to frame, hook, or connect shin-to-shin. It is the default "home base" for many modern open-guard players before they commit to a specific system. It is a key branch of open guard.

How Seated Guard Works

From the sit you grip fight, frame against their hands, and pick a path: butterfly elevation, DLR/RDLR hooks, shin-to-shin, or entanglement entries. Good seated guard is active—static sitting gets smashed or toreando'd.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar-sleeve and pant grips from the sit.

No-gi. Wrist/ankle control, underhooks, and faster SLX/K paths.

What Does Seated Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex pulls to a sit, frames Jordan's hands, connects shin-to-shin, then enters reverse DLR with collar-sleeve (gi) or K-guard (no-gi) based on Jordan's pass attempt.

Types / Variations

Basic seated posture

Upright hips, active hands, ready feet.

Retention frames

Hand and shin frames that stop toreando and long-step passes.

Attack entries

Butterfly, DLR/RDLR, STS, SLX, K-guard—chosen from the sit.

Passing seated guard

Force them flat, strip grips, toreando or body-lock; don't stall in their frames.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Guard pulls; recovering from supine open guard; after failed half.

Bottom threats. Whatever system you enter next—plus sit-up sweeps and wrestling-ups.

Top counters. Flatten, grip break, pass with pace.

Seated vs Supine Open Guard

Factor Seated Supine Open
Torso Upright On back
Primary use Grip fight + choose system Hooks / frames / inversion
Pass look Toreando / smash sit Toreando / stack / knee cut

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Stall calls can appear if both athletes freeze—keep working grips and entries.

Safety

Standard open-guard safety: control partners on elevations and inversions.

Common mistakes

  • Sitting still with no frames—easy toreando.
  • Never committing to an attack system.
  • Hands only, feet dead (or vice versa).

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is seated guard a technique or a posture?

A posture/category—the sit from which you launch specific open-guard systems.

Seated or supine for beginners?

Learn both. Seated teaches grip fighting; supine teaches hooks and frames.

Best next system from seated?

Depends on gym and rules: butterfly, DLR/RDLR, STS→SLX, or K-guard are all common.

How do I pass seated guard?

Break postures/grips, force them flat or circle past feet, toreando or body-lock with pace.

Does seated guard work in MMA?

With caution—upright sits can eat punches. Many MMA players prefer supine frames or wrestling-ups.

Why do some coaches hate seated guard?

Usually because students sit still. Active seated guard with clear entries is widely used at high levels.

The Bottom Line

Seated guard is the upright open-guard home base: retain, grip fight, then enter butterfly, DLR, STS, SLX, or K-guard. See open guard.
