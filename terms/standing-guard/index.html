Standing Guard Pull

A standing guard pull is the Brazilian jiu-jitsu skill of sitting or jumping to guard from the feet while controlling grips and distance—so you enter closed, open, or specialty guards without eating an instant pass or takedown.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Pull with connection—do not flop to your back empty-handed.
  • Control a sleeve/collar (gi) or wrist/head (no-gi) before sitting.
  • Land in a guard you actually play (closed, butterfly, DLR, SLX).
  • Bad pulls get toreandoed or stacked—angle and frames on the way down.
  • Related: closed guard, open guard, guard retention.

What Is Standing Guard Pull?

A standing guard pull is the intentional choice to become the bottom player from the feet by sitting or jumping into a guard configuration. Done well, it is a strategic entry into your guard system. Done poorly, it is a free pass for the opponent.

Sport BJJ often rewards skilled pulls; self-defense and MMA contexts may prefer takedowns—know your goal.

How Standing Guard Pull Works

Establish connection (grips or clinch), break their posture or control a limb, then sit or circle to your preferred guard while keeping hooks and frames ready. The pull succeeds when you land with active guard—not flat with them already past your knees.

Opponent's job. Follow you down into a pass (toreando, double-under, knee cut), sprawl if you shoot as a fake, or force a takedown before you sit.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar and sleeve grips make pulls safer and more controlled; classic pull to closed guard is a gi staple.

No-gi. Wrist, elbow, and head control replace cloth; many athletes pull to butterfly, seated, or SLX rather than closed.

MMA context. Pulling guard under strikes is generally a last resort—prefer takedowns or clinch control.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex grips Jordan's sleeve and collar, sits to a hip, and locks closed guard before Jordan can toreando.

Gi. Dual sleeve/collar connection on the way down.

No-gi. Alex controls the neck/elbows and sits to butterfly with underhooks ready.

Types / Variations

Sit-down pull

Controlled sit to a hip or butt while keeping grips—safest learning pattern.

Jump pull (controlled)

Athletic jump to closed or wrapped guards; high reward, high pass risk if grips fail.

Pull to closed guard

Classic sport entry into closed guard offense.

Pull to open / butterfly / DLR

Modern entries into butterfly, De La Riva, or seated open guard.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

When to pull. Opponent is a stronger wrestler; you want your guard game; rule set rewards guard play.

Counters to pulls. Follow into toreando/leg drag, snatch a takedown as they sit, or sprawl and pass.

Fixing bad pulls. If you land flat, immediately frame and recover using guard retention mechanics.

Guard Pull vs Takedown

Factor Guard Pull Takedown
Goal Become bottom with your guard Become top with control
Risk Instant pass if empty Sprawl / counter takedown
Sport bias Common in IBJJF Common in ADCC / wrestling-heavy
MMA bias Usually discouraged Preferred

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Pulling guard is legal. Some events discourage stalling after a pull—engage offense. ADCC often favors wrestle-ups and top pressure.

Safety

Never pull into a slam-prone position in places that allow slams. Protect your neck and lower back on jump pulls.

Common mistakes

  • Butt-flopping with no grips.
  • Landing flat with knees wide—instant toreando.
  • Pulling closed when you only know open-guard retention.
  • In no-gi: hunting collar habits.
  • Pulling under punches in MMA without a plan.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a guard pull?

Intentionally sitting or jumping from standing into a bottom guard while keeping connection so you are not immediately passed.

Is pulling guard bad?

In sport BJJ it is a valid strategy. In self-defense/MMA it is usually worse than taking the other person down.

How do I pull guard without getting passed?

Establish grips first, sit to a hip with frames ready, and land in a guard you can actually play.

Gi vs no-gi guard pulls?

Gi uses collar/sleeve; no-gi uses wrists/head and often pulls to butterfly or seated open guard.

Jump pull vs sit pull?

Sit pulls are safer for beginners; jump pulls are faster but easier to pass if grips fail.

What if they follow me down into a pass?

Treat it as a retention problem—frames, hip movement, and recovering hooks per your guard retention system.

Do I lose points for pulling guard?

Generally no points for the pull itself; you can lose the positional battle if they pass immediately.

The Bottom Line

Pull guard with connection and land in a guard you own—empty butt-flops gift passes. See guard, closed guard, and guard retention.
