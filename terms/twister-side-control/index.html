Twister Side Control

Twister side control is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu side-control variation popularized by Eddie Bravo—angling toward the opponent's hips with far-leg control to enter the truck and finish the twister (or related back attacks).

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Angled toward the hips—not standard chest-to-chest side control.
  • Far-leg control (fishnet / hooks) sets truck and twister entries.
  • Twister itself is illegal in many IBJJF divisions—know your rules.
  • Soft angle gets reversed; keep the near hip heavy and the far knee hooked.
  • Related: truck, side control, crab ride.

What Is Twister Side Control?

Twister side control is a specialized side pin from the 10th Planet system: instead of classic chest-to-chest side control, you angle toward the opponent's hips, control the head/near shoulder, and hook or underhook the far leg. The position exists primarily to enter the truck and finish the twister—or to force back exposure.

How Twister Side Control Works

By facing the hips and owning the far leg, you prevent them from flattening and reclaiming frames. The “fishnet” (threading an arm behind/under the far leg) freezes leg recovery. From there you hook the near leg, roll into truck, and attack the twister, calf slicers, or back takes depending on rules and reactions.

Bottom player's job. Flatten early, clear the far-leg control, and reclaim underhooks before the fishnet locks. Do not let both legs get entangled while your spine is twisted.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips help control the far leg; many IBJJF gi divisions ban the twister—use the angle for truck-to-back or positional control instead.

No-gi. Home environment for 10th Planet entries; twister and truck games are more often legal in ADCC-style and submission-only shows—confirm event rules.

MMA context. The angle can create back exposure and control, but spinal twisters are high-risk—train only under qualified coaches.

What Does Twister Side Control Look Like in Practice?

Alex shifts from standard side control, posts, swims the near arm, angles toward Jordan's hips, and hooks the far knee. As Jordan turns, Alex fishnets the far leg and rolls into truck.

Gi. Alex may use pants grips and abandon the twister finish for a back take under IBJJF rules.

No-gi. Alex completes the truck and threatens the twister where legal.

Types / Variations

Standard twister side control

Angled hips, head/shoulder control, far-knee hook—Bravo’s base shape.

Fishnet / far-leg control

Arm threaded behind the far leg to stop flattening and set the roll.

Twister side control to truck

Hook the near leg and roll into truck for twister / back attacks.

Other attacks from the angle

Darce/anaconda opportunities, back takes without truck, and returns to standard side control if the twister path dies.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From side control shifts; after passes when they turn to their side; from truck when you rotate laterally.

Top threats. Truck, twister (where legal), back takes, front headlock chokes.

Bottom counters. Flatten, clear fishnet, underhook and recover guard, or force them back to flat side control you can shrimp from.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

The twister (spinal lock) is illegal in many IBJJF divisions. Truck entries and back takes may still be legal—always check the rulebook. ADCC and many no-gi events allow more spinal/twisting attacks for adults—still coach-supervised.

Safety

Spinal rotational locks are serious. Drill slowly, tap early, never crank. Train only under qualified coaches familiar with twister mechanics.

Common mistakes

  • Angling without far-leg control—getting reversed.
  • Hunting twisters in divisions where they are illegal.
  • Soft fishnet that lets them flatten.
  • Ignoring back-take options when the twister is defended.
  • High posture that gives up the back during the shift.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is twister side control?

An angled side-control position from Eddie Bravo’s system that faces the hips and controls the far leg to enter truck/twister.

Is the twister legal in IBJJF?

Often no—many IBJJF divisions ban the twister. Use the position for legal back takes and confirm your division’s rules.

How do you get to truck from twister side control?

Control the far leg (fishnet), hook the near leg, and roll into truck while keeping upper-body control.

Does twister side control work in the gi?

The angle works; pants grips help. Finish with legal attacks if the twister is banned.

How do you defend twister side control?

Flatten your back, clear far-leg control early, and reclaim underhooks before the fishnet locks.

Is twister side control the same as scarf hold?

No. Scarf hold faces the head with head-and-arm wrap; twister side control angles to the hips for leg entanglements.

Do I need 10th Planet to use this?

No, but learning from coaches who understand truck/twister safety is essential before applying spinal locks.

The Bottom Line

Twister side control angles toward the hips to set truck and twister entries. Know your rule set—twisters are often IBJJF-illegal—and train spinal locks carefully. See truck and side control.
