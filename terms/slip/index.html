Slip

A slip moves your head just off the center line—knees soft, hands up—so straights miss by inches and counters become available.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Slips are small and covered.
  • Outside first for safety.
  • Counters must be planned.

What Is Slip?

Primary defense against jabs and crosses. Sibling to Bob and Weave; cousin to Parry and Pull Counter.

Martial Index files Slip under Boxing so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Slip behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Slip Works

Bend knees/hips slightly, shift head left or right off the line, keep eyes on the opponent, hands remain in guard, return to stance or fire a Counterpunch.

Outside slip (away from their rear hand) is generally safer for beginners than deep inside slips.

Mechanically, successful slip work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, slip lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill slip daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Slip outside the jab → Cross. Slip inside → Lead Hook or Lead Uppercut.

Fight picture: slip appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply slip after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Outside slip

Head moves away from their power hand; strong for cross counters.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Inside slip

Toward their rear-hand lane—higher risk, opens lead uppercuts/hooks.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Bending only the neck.
  • Eyes closing / turning away.
  • Hands dropping during the slip.
  • Slipping too early and getting timed.
  • Treating slip as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox slip inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so slip connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether slip appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe slip as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put slip behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep slip scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on slip, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for slip under your next card (glove size, round length, amateur vs pro judging) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Slip vs bob-and-weave?

Slip is mostly lateral/offline; bob-and-weave adds a deeper U-shaped level change—see Bob and Weave.

Should beginners slip or block first?

Learn high-guard comfort and Catch/Parry alongside early slip drills—don’t skip cover.

How do I counter off a slip?

Decide the counter before the drill: outside slip-cross is the standard starter.

The Bottom Line

Slip inches, not feet—then punish the miss.
