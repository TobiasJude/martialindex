Slip Right

Slip right moves your head just right of the center line—small, covered, and timed—so straights miss and counters open.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Right slips clear many jabs for orthodox boxers.
  • Keep the lead shoulder and chin relationship honest.
  • Rebound into a planned counter; do not freeze offline.

What Is Slip Right?

Slip right is the rightward sibling of Slip Left under the parent Slip: head and torso ease right of the center line so a straight punch passes your left side.

Orthodox fighters often slip right outside the opponent's Jab, then fire a Cross—the textbook slip-and-cross pattern.

It belongs with Head Movement, Duck, and Weave Out as evasion tools that keep you in Boxing Range instead of bailing to the ropes.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Slip Right behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Slip Right Works

Soften knees, tuck the chin (Chin Tuck), shift head right a few inches, eyes forward, hands in Guard Hand Position.

Slight weight toward the rear (right) leg for orthodox is common, but stay springy—do not plant so hard you cannot pivot or fire.

Outside slip right vs their jab is the high-percentage beginner drill; inside slips toward their rear hand demand better reads and a ready Cover-Up.

Mechanically, successful slip right work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair this tool with the rest of the defensive layer: Slip/Parry for straights, Bob and Weave literacy for hooks, and honest Guard Hand Position always.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, slip right lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows as weapons—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill slip right daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Partner jabs; you slip right outside, return the Cross, reset to Boxing Stance.

Combination picture: jab–slip right–cross, or slip right–Lead Hook when their rear hand is late.

Fight picture: slip right appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply slip right after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Outside slip right

Clears their lead straight; classic gateway to the slip-cross Slip Counter.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Inside slip right

Moves toward their power lane—use sparingly until timing and Defensive Responsibility are solid.

Common Mistakes

  • Over-slipping into a bent-over posture.
  • Pulling the lead hand down as you slip right.
  • Looking away from the jab.
  • Slipping every jab on habit and eating the follow-up right.
  • Treating slip right as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Slip-line rounds: only right slips for two minutes, then add counters.

Pads: jab feeds only → slip right → cross; then mix feints so you read, not guess.

Shadowbox slip right inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so slip right connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether slip right appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, head movement, and counters.

Study Notes

Film sparring: mark every successful slip right that produced a clean counter versus slips that only delayed damage.

Read beside Distance Management—if range is wrong, slips become leans into punches.

Coaches often describe slip right as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents around slip right. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Slip right vs lean back?

Lean Back and Fade Away create depth; slip right creates lateral offline. Different lines, different counters.

Best beginner counter?

Cross after outside slip right.

Southpaw note?

Geometry flips—map slips to their lead/rear hands, not only left/right.

Hands up or hands low?

Hands stay in guard. Low-hand styles still cover the open line somehow—beginners keep classic High Guard (Boxing) height.

When to weave instead?

Against hooks and looping punches prefer Bob and Weave / Roll Under.

The Bottom Line

Slip right just enough—then own the center line again with a punch or a reset.
