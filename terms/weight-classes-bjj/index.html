Weight Classes (BJJ)

Weight classes group competitors by body weight so brackets stay fair. Exact cutoffs change by organization—always verify the current IBJJF or ADCC documents for your event.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Weight classes sort athletes into mass-based divisions for safer, fairer brackets.
  • Cutoffs and weigh-in procedures differ by federation and event—never memorize old flyers as law.
  • Absolute/open weight sits beside classed divisions—see absolute division.
  • Unsafe extreme cutting is not required to compete—seek professional guidance if cutting at all.
  • This page is high-level only; verify the current official rulebook for numbers.

What Are Weight Classes in BJJ?

Weight classes are published mass brackets that determine who can enter which division at a tournament. They sit inside the broader competition pathway alongside belt and age categories.

How It Works

Organizers publish a chart (often in kilograms with apparel rules for weigh-in). Athletes make weight at the stated time, then compete in that bracket—or register up a class. Some events allow open/absolute entries separately.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi and no-gi events may use different charts and weigh-in clothing rules. ADCC-style and IBJJF-style events are not interchangeable—read the packet for that weekend.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You check the event page weeks ahead, decide whether to compete near walk-around weight, weigh in under the stated protocol, then warm up for your bracket.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Adult classed divisions; juvenile/kids charts; master age + weight stacks; open weight alongside classed.

Gym / sport variants

Local promotions sometimes invent house charts. Always prefer the written guide for that event over gym lore.

Beginner notes

First competitors: pick a class close to healthy walk-around weight. Skill beats a drained cut.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Screenshot the official chart. Confirm scale units. Plan travel so you are not dehydrating in a parking lot. Pair with competition prep and sane recovery.

How It Compares

Weight classes are not the same as belt divisions or age divisions—those stack. Absolute removes the weight constraint while keeping other eligibility rules.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

High-level only: Martial Index does not publish a complete weight table here because federations update charts. Verify the current IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules (and your event’s athlete guide) before registering.

Safety

Aggressive dehydration cuts carry medical risk. This encyclopedia does not provide cutting protocols. Consult qualified professionals; skip cutting when unsure.

Common mistakes

Using last year’s chart; missing weigh-in clothing rules; cutting into illness; forgetting absolute registration deadlines.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Where do I find official weight classes?

In the current federation rulebook and your event’s athlete guide—not from memory.

Are IBJJF and ADCC the same?

No—charts and procedures differ; verify each.

Should beginners cut weight?

Usually better to compete near healthy walk-around weight for your first events.

What is absolute?

An open-weight style division—see absolute division.

Does this page list every cutoff?

No—high-level only; numbers go stale; check official docs.

Gi weigh-in clothes?

Event-specific—read that weekend’s guide.

Related pages?

Competition, brackets, match duration, IBJJF/ADCC rules.

The Bottom Line

Pick a weight class from the current official chart, stay healthy, and let skill—not a dangerous cut—do the talking.
