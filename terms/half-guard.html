Half Guard

Half guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu position where you trap one of the opponent's legs between yours—used as a recovery, a sweep engine, and a bridge into back takes or leg entanglements, with different grips in gi versus no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • One leg trapped between yours: half guard is a full offensive system, not a mistake.
  • Core branches: knee shield, underhook/dogfight, deep half, lockdown.
  • Gi favors shields and lapels; no-gi favors underhooks, lockdown, and SLX transitions.
  • Top players flatten, crossface, and free the knee to pass.
  • Often the honest recovery when closed or open guard fails.

What Is Half Guard?

Half guard is a bottom (or occasionally top) position where one of the opponent's legs is trapped between your legs while the other is free. In modern Brazilian jiu-jitsu it is far more than a "failed full guard"—it is a primary offensive system built on underhooks, knee shields, deep half, and dogfight entries.

It sits inside the parent family of guard, between closed guard and the open-guard systems, and it is often the recovery home when open guards collapse.

How Half Guard Works

By owning one leg, you limit their ability to take full mount and create a platform for sweeps and back takes. The classic offensive sequence fights for an underhook, comes up on the elbow/hand, and drives into a dogfight or sweep. Knee-shield half uses a framed shin across their chest to manage distance (especially in the gi). Deep half pulls their trapped leg across your centerline for waiter's sweeps and back takes.

Top half. The passer wants to flatten you, win the crossface, free the trapped knee, and cut to side control or mount—Lachlan Giles's half-guard passing material is a common modern reference.

Gi vs No-Gi

Bottom offense. Gi half guard uses lapel feeds, sleeve control, and pants grips to hold the knee shield. No-gi half guard emphasizes underhooks, head position, lockdown variations, and faster dogfight scrambles—plus entries into SLX and other entanglements.

Passing. Gi passers grip pants and collars to pin the shield. No-gi passers smash, body-lock, and backstep more freely.

Deep half. Famous in gi (Bernardo Faria) and still viable in no-gi with adjusted grips.

What Does Half Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex's closed guard is opened; rather than lose everything, Alex catches one of Jordan's legs in half guard, frames a knee shield, then pummels an underhook, comes up to dogfight, and takes Jordan's back.

Gi. Alex adds a lapel grip to freeze Jordan's posture while the shield holds.

No-gi. Alex skips the lapel, wins the underhook race earlier, and may transition to single-leg X if Jordan stands.

Types / Variations

Knee-shield half

Shin across their chest; distance management and frame-based sweeps.

Underhook / dogfight half

Classic offensive half: underhook, get to elbow, sweep or back take.

Deep half

Their trapped leg across your body; waiter's sweeps and back exposures.

Lockdown

Figure-four on their trapped leg (10th Planet flavor); control and electric chair paths—more no-gi native.

Half butterfly

Butterfly hook inside half structures—elevation into X-guard or sweeps.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Recovering from side control, failed closed/open guard, pulling half deliberately, top players sometimes forcing half to pass.

Bottom threats. Underhook sweeps, back takes, deep-half sweeps, entries to SLX / X-guard.

Top passes. Flatten and crossface, trap the bottom arm, free the knee, knee cut or backstep; smash the shield in the gi with pants control.

Half Guard vs Closed Guard

Factor Half Guard Closed Guard
Legs One of theirs trapped Both legs around waist
Mobility Higher lateral mobility Higher connection, lower mobility
Modern use Primary system for many athletes Foundation + recovery / gi classic
No-gi fit Excellent Useful but often temporary

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Half guard is fully legal. It does not score by itself from bottom; sweeps from half score like any sweep. Top players who clear half and stabilize a pin score the pass. Lockdown and certain leg configurations are legal in most rules but know your event's heel-hook and reaping policies when you transition to entanglements.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Lockdown can stress knees—communicate with partners. Tap early to kneebars and heel hooks if your half game enters the legs.

Common mistakes

  • Playing half without fighting for the underhook—getting smashed flat.
  • Knee shield with no frames, inviting the smash pass.
  • In no-gi: hunting lapel tricks instead of underhooks and head position.
  • In the gi: ignoring pants and lapel tools that hold the shield.
  • Recovering half and then freezing instead of sweeping or improving.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is half guard only for when I fail closed guard?

No. Many high-level athletes pull half on purpose because the underhook and deep-half games are so strong.

Knee shield or underhook first?

Use the shield to manage distance and create the underhook. Living forever on a soft shield without an underhook gets you smashed.

Does half guard work in no-gi?

Extremely well. Expect less lapel play and more underhooks, dogfights, lockdown, and transitions to SLX.

How do I pass half guard?

Flatten their shoulders, win the crossface, trap their underhook, free your knee, then knee-cut or backstep. Do not leave your posture broken over their shield.

What is deep half for?

Sweeping taller pressure passers and creating back exposure by controlling their trapped leg across your centerline.

Half guard vs closed guard for beginners?

Closed guard teaches posture breaking; half guard teaches underhooks and recovery. Learn both—half often saves you when closed opens.

The Bottom Line

Half guard traps one leg and turns recovery into offense: shields, underhooks, deep half, and lockdown. Gi athletes add lapels; no-gi athletes race to dogfights and SLX. See closed guard and open guard for the neighboring bottom games.
