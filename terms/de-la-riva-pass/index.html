De La Riva Pass

The De La Riva pass clears the outside hook of De La Riva then finishes with long-step, knee smash, or circling pressure—clustered with RDLR sweeps, berimbolo, and open guard pass.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Kill the DLR hook before you celebrate the pass.
  • Long-step and knee-smash are classic finishes.
  • Watch invert/berimbolo when you clear carelessly.
  • Gi pants grips help; no-gi uses ankle rides.
  • Hub: open guard pass.

What Is De La Riva Pass?

De La Riva pass is the top menu against the outside DLR hook. Parent: De La Riva. Related bottom: RDLR. Across: long-step, knee-cut, toreando, leg drag.

How It Works

Control the hooked pant/ankle, posture, clear or smash the hook, step offline (long-step or circular), pin hips, and settle side control while blocking rehooks and inversions.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grip on the hooked leg is standard.

No-gi. Ankle control and hip connection replace pants; same clear-then-circle logic.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

DLR hook in → you smash knee, long-step to the back side → side control before they berimbolo.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Long-step DLR pass.

Competition variation

Knee smash / knee slice from DLR.

Related option

Leg drag or toreando when the hook is light; vs RDLR see also reverse-DLR clearing (HQ paths).

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Vs established DLR. Counters. DLR sweeps, berimbolo, reinversion.

How It Compares

Vs spider pass: hook on leg vs feet on arms. Vs X pass: different elevation threat.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Central in IBJJF gi; also common no-gi with different grips.

Safety

Pin hips carefully—do not knee-on-neck while clearing.

Common mistakes

  • Turning back to a live hook (invite berimbolo).
  • No pants/ankle control.
  • Passing without blocking the free hook.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How do you pass De La Riva?

Control the hooked leg, clear the hook, long-step or smash, pin hips, block reinversion.

What about reverse DLR?

Related clearing problem—often HQ/knee-cut after the inside hook dies.

Gi or no-gi?

Both; grips change.

What next?

Side control or mount; watch the back exposure either way.

Common mistake?

Clearing while giving them an invert angle.

Beginner-friendly?

Long-step drills yes; live berimbolo defense is harder.

Martial Index home?

Under De La Riva in the open-guard mesh.

The Bottom Line

De La Riva pass is clear the hook, then finish—always account for berimbolo and RDLR twins.
