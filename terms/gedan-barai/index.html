Gedan-Barai

Gedan-barai is the low sweeping block: chamber high, cut downward across the line, finish with kime—often the first block in beginner kata.

What Is Gedan-Barai?

Gedan-barai (下段払い) is the downward block. It opens Heian Shodan and appears constantly in Kihon.

Compare with Age-Uke (rising) and mid blocks Soto-Uke/Uchi-Uke.

Used against low punches and kicks such as Mae-Geri in drill contexts.

Martial Index files Gedan-Barai under Karate so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Karate style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Gedan-Barai behaves in kihon, kata, and kumite.

How Gedan-Barai Works

Chamber (often fist near the opposite ear/shoulder as taught), then sweep the forearm downward across the body, finishing low with Hikite on the other hand and Kime.

Usually lands in Zenkutsu-Dachi. Hips contribute; do not flap only the arm.

After the block, be ready to counter with Oi-Zuki or Gyaku-Zuki.

Mechanically, successful gedan-barai work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Karate Stance with Kamae alive.

If distance (maai) is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: punches need Hikite and Kime; kicks need chamber and retract; blocks need a counter path into Gyaku-Zuki or Oi-Zuki.

Dojo vs Competition / Rules Notes

In traditional dojo training, gedan-barai lives inside the kihon–kata–kumite triangle—form quality matters as much as fight utility.

Under WKF sport karate, scoring and contact limits reshape how gedan-barai appears in matches versus full-contact styles such as Kyokushin.

Gym culture may drill gedan-barai daily; grading and competition may shrink the toolkit based on syllabus and ruleset.

Confirm the dojo syllabus and bout agreement before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

In kumite, modern checks may replace classical barai—still drill the syllabus block.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Kata: first move of Heian Shodan.

Drill: defend stepping mae-geri with gedan-barai, counter gyaku-zuki.

Dojo picture: gedan-barai appears when Karate Stance and timing say so, then yields to the next technique in a combination or to a reset of Kamae.

Gym test: can you apply gedan-barai after a feint or Kizami-Zuki setup and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Common Mistakes

  • Blocking with a bent, weak wrist.
  • Stopping short so the low line stays open.
  • No hikite.
  • Rising the shoulder into the ear.
  • Looking down at the kick.
  • Treating gedan-barai as a highlight instead of a distance-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down and no hikite.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Line-drill gedan-barai inside full kihon sets; return to Karate Stance and Kamae after every attempt.

Pads or makiwara: technical → moving → forced follow-up so gedan-barai connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat solo fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film kata and kumite weekly and ask whether gedan-barai appears with honest Kime.

Progress intensity with a sensei—especially for kicks, spins, and full-power Oi-Zuki.

Slow chamber-to-finish path in front of a mirror focusing on forearm angle.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe gedan-barai as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor maai, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare ryuha, you will see stylistic accents: some put gedan-barai behind heavy Kihon repetition; others behind application-minded Kata or sport Kumite. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Karate Stance, Kihon, and Kata into siblings, then return here after class to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for kicks, joint stress in deep stances, and impact training.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “stance,” and “power.” Martial Index’s Karate entries keep gedan-barai scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Boxing or BJJ articles that share casual language.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does gedan-barai mean?

Low-level sweep/block.

Is it only against kicks?

Classically low attacks—including low punches and kicks.

Why start Heian Shodan with it?

Teaches turn, stance, and low-line defense early—see Heian Shodan.

Age-uke vs gedan-barai?

High rising vs low sweeping—see Age-Uke.

Can it be an attack?

Some bunkai treat the motion offensively; learn syllabus block first.

The Bottom Line

Cut the low line cleanly—Gedan-Barai with stance and Kime, then punish with Oi-Zuki or Gyaku-Zuki.
