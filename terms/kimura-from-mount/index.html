What Is Kimura from Mount?

Kimura from Mount is a figure-four shoulder lock finished from mount by isolating one arm, clamping the kimura grip, and rotating the shoulder. In gym language you may also hear mounted kimura / ude-garami from mount. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

From mount, force an elbow away from the body, lace the figure-four, put their wrist on the mat, slide high or toward north-south, and turn the shoulder toward their head. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Same mechanics; sleeve grips can help pry the elbow free before you lock the figure-four.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, kimura from mount usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard kimura from mount as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (Mount). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

Mount finish sibling to Americana from Mount and cousin of Kimura Trap.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck, spinal, and knee pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck, shoulders, and knees. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing kimura from mount with a nearby sibling name.
