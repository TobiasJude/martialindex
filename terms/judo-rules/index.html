What are judo rules (IJF/Kodokan frame)?

‘Judo rules’ on Martial Index means the contest framework athletes actually meet: primarily IJF Sport & Organisation Rules for Olympics/World Tour, grounded in Kodokan technique classification.

This hub covers scores (Ippon, Waza-Ari, yuko), pins (Osaekomi), penalties (shido/hansoku-make), time, and golden score—without pretending every national federation matches IJF day one.

Beginners search this phrase; specialists later get a dedicated ijf-rules child page in a future batch.

How scoring works (2026–28)

Hierarchy: ippon > waza-ari > yuko. Ippon ends the contest. Two waza-ari = ippon. Yuko counts but never sums into waza-ari.

Under IJF 2026–28 rules, osaekomi scores yuko at 5–9s, waza-ari at 10–19s, and ippon at 20s. Yuko is live again and does not add up to waza-ari; two waza-ari make ippon.

Regulation: typically four minutes at senior/junior/cadet championships. Level on scores → golden score (unlimited) until a technical score or decisive penalty.

Three shido = hansoku-make. Shido does not add a point to the opponent; it is a penalty track.

Judogi vs no-gi

IJF contests are judogi contests with grip and jacket rules. No-gi judo events and BJJ use other rule sheets—label them clearly when you cross-train.

The Judogi itself must meet size/fit standards at official events.

In practice

Athlete A has one Waza-Ari. Athlete B scores two yuko. A still leads. If both have one waza-ari and B has more yuko, B wins at the buzzer—exactly why yuko returned.

Rule topics in this batch

Scores: Ippon, Waza-Ari; pins: Osaekomi; training context: Randori, Uchi-Komi; equipment: Judogi.

Practical coaching notes

Coach athletes to attack in ways that read as real techniques—false attacks draw shido.

Ne-waza activity counts toward combativity expectations in the modern cycle—do not treat the ground as a nap.

Compare with Kodokan traditional contests

Kodokan culture preserves technique and kata; IJF standardises global sport. Both matter; your shiai entry form tells you which sheet you’re under.

Safety & legality

Illegal: most direct leg grabs in senior IJF, dangerous joint locks outside the elbow, diving head-first, etc.

Safety: Ukemi, tap early, qualified referees/coaches.

Common mistakes

  • Saying ‘yuko was abolished’ as if it were still 2018—yuko is live for 2026–28.
  • Believing yukos add into waza-ari.
  • Confusing IBJJF/ADCC incentives with IJF shido logic.

Rule literacy is competitive hygiene. Athletes who train only “gym rules” meet surprise shido in their first open tournament. Spend one evening a season reading the IJF refereeing explanation that matches your cycle—then ask your coach how your club interprets gray areas.

Yuko’s return reduces pure golden-score lotteries by rewarding lesser landings and short pins. That should change training: do not discard partial throws as failures; finish them with control and learn to hold pins through the 5- and 10-second gates.

Penalties: non-combativity, false attacks, stepping out, illegal grips, and defensive postures all appear in real contests. The cure is usually technical (better kumi-kata and attack chains), not complaining about referees.

Kodokan classification still matters because scores expect recognisable judo techniques. A scramble that merely trips someone without a technique story may score poorly compared with a clear osoto or seoi variation.

Equipment and medical rules sit beside scoring: judogi measurements, bleeding stops, and rest between contests. Read judogi before you buy a “shiai special” that fails inspection.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is yuko abolished?

No—reinstated for 2026–28.

Do yukos add to waza-ari?

No.

Pin ippon?

20 seconds.

Golden score length?

Unlimited until a score/decisive penalty.

Leg grabs?

Heavily restricted in modern IJF—verify current text.

Kodokan vs IJF?

Technique roots vs sport standardisation.

Phone rules?

Irrelevant—train the sheet you’re entered under.
