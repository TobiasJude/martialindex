Osoto Gari

Osoto gari (major outer reap) breaks balance backward/sideways and reaps the outside leg—one of the highest-percentage judo throws in BJJ gi, linked with uchi mata and seoi nage under takedown.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Put their weight on the reaped leg.
  • Upper body drives as much as the reap.
  • Back-step variants help stubborn bases.
  • Chain to uchi mata when they step out.
  • Follow to knee-on-belly or pass.

What Is Osoto Gari?

Osoto gari is classic judo: kuzushi toward the corner, step, and reap the far/outside leg while the hands put them over that missing base. Modern BJJ often uses back-step or drop variants that fit shorter grip fights.

No-gi osoto appears from collar ties and underhooks with a shorter reap. See grip fighting and hand fighting.

Sport incentives: clean osoto to stable top can score; landing in closed guard softens the win.

How Osoto Gari Works

Off-balance, enter, reap the outside line, drive with chest/hands, follow the fall.

If they hop or step out, switch direction to uchi mata or foot sweeps.

Uchikomi first; live throws second.

Drill both classic and back-step entries.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel/sleeve osoto is a staple.

No-Gi. Head-and-arm or underhook osoto/otoshi hybrids.

Both: upper-body weight distribution makes the reap honest.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex wins a sleeve-lapel, steps, reaps osoto, and settles knee-on-belly.

Jordan defends by stepping; Alex switches to uchi mata.

No-gi: Alex’s collar tie and reap dump Jordan to a pass.

Types / Variations

Classic osoto gari

Step-and-reap textbook.

Back-step osoto

Modern angle for stubborn bases.

Drop / otoshi flavors

Kneeling entries when upright fails.

Osoto–uchi chains

Direction change system.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: forward pressure reactions, grip dominance, and failed seoi.

Counters: hop, lean in, block the reap thigh, and turn the corner.

Failed: do not stay on one leg—reset or chain.

Osoto Gari vs Seoi Nage

Factor Osoto Gari Seoi Nage
Direction Back/outside reap Forward shoulder throw
Space Often more linear Requires turn-in space
BJJ popularity Very high in gi High with drop variants
Chain To uchi mata To osoto when stuffed

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Verify IBJJF takedown scoring for your event. Dangerous throws landing opponents on their neck/head are unacceptable and may be penalized—coach-supervised training only.

Safety

Reaps twist knees. Control speed; partners breakfall. No competition ego in the gym.

Common mistakes

  • Reaping an unweighted leg.
  • Arms only, no chest drive.
  • No chain when stuffed.
  • Celebrating without passing.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is osoto gari?

Judo’s major outer reap throw widely used in BJJ.

Why is it popular in gi?

Grips make the backward off-balance reliable.

Back-step variant?

A modern entry that helps against wide bases.

No-gi possible?

Yes—with head/arm or underhook control.

Best chain?

Osoto threats into uchi mata.

Safety?

Protect knees; progressive resistance.

Next?

Uchi mata and seoi nage.

The Bottom Line

Osoto gari is the outer-reap workhorse of gi BJJ standing. Load the leg, drive with the upper body, and chain when they hop.
