Gi Sizing

Gi sizing is matching jacket, pants, and sleeve/pant lengths to your body—and to any competition measurement rules such as IBJJF gi requirements.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Brand size charts differ—use measurements, not ego letters (A2 vs A3 myths).
  • Competition gis may need stricter lengths/overlaps than gym beaters.
  • Wash/shrink expectations matter—read care labels.
  • See gi, belt tying, and IBJJF requirements.
  • Verify current federation measurement guidance before fight week.

What Is Gi Sizing?

Gi sizing is selecting a kimono that allows movement, survives grips, and meets house or federation presentation standards. Poor fit hampers technique and can fail inspection.

How It Works

Measure height/weight, consult the brand chart, try on if possible, and account for shrink. Competition athletes also check sleeve/pant lengths and jacket overlap against the current official guide.

Gi vs No-Gi

Sizing is a gi problem. No-gi uses rashguard/shorts fit instead—see spats and no-gi shorts.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You try an A2 that binds when you invert and an A3 that balloons—then pick the cut that moves and still passes a friendly measuring stick.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Standard cuts; slim/competition cuts; pearl/ultra-light weaves (preferences vary).

Gym / sport variants

Some rooms require white/blue/black only for class; competition colors follow event rules.

Beginner notes

Borrow before buying when possible. A durable midweight trainer beats a fragile fashion gi.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Keep a “comp gi” that you know passes measurements. Re-check after washes. Pair with legal patches if competing.

How It Compares

Related to—but broader than—IBJJF measurement specifics. Sizing includes comfort and durability for daily class.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

For IBJJF events, verify current IBJJF gi requirements and IBJJF Rules—do not invent measurements here.

Safety

Overly long sleeves/pants can snag; overly tight jackets restrict breathing/movement.

Common mistakes

Buying only by Instagram model height; ignoring shrink; one gi for both filthy training and inspection day.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How do I choose a gi size?

Use brand charts plus try-on when possible; account for shrink and competition rules.

Are A2/A3 universal?

No—cuts differ by brand.

Comp gi vs trainer?

Many athletes keep a cleaner, known-fit gi for inspection days.

Where are official measurements?

Current IBJJF documentation—see IBJJF gi requirements.

Color rules?

Event and gym dependent—verify before buying only fashion colors.

Kids sizing?

Youth charts differ—ask the academy.

Related pages?

Gi hub, belt tying, IBJJF requirements.

The Bottom Line

Size the gi for movement first, then confirm it still satisfies any competition measuring stick you will face.
