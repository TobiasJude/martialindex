X-Pass

The X-pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard pass that controls a leg (often with crossed grips or shin pressure) and clears the hips past the hooks—frequently chained with toreando and speed passing.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Win the X-structure on one leg, then clear the free hip—do not stall in the X.
  • Lives next to toreando and long-step in modern open-guard passing.
  • Gi pants/sleeve grips stabilize the X; no-gi uses wrist control and shin pressure.
  • If the bottom player sits up into you, abandon float and reconnect or knee-cut.
  • Common vs De La Riva, collar-sleeve, and standing open guard.

What Is X-Pass?

The X-pass is an open-guard pass named for the crossed control on a single leg—grips, shin trap, or both—that lets the passer step or circle past the remaining hook. It is not one universal choreography; gyms teach Mendes-style X concepts, shin-trap clears, and “X” as a grip geometry that feeds toreando or knee-cut finishes.

It belongs with speed passing under guard passing against open guard: posture, grip fighting, and decisive clears rather than long chest smashes. White belts meet a simplified X as “control the ankle and step past”; higher belts treat it as a headquarters and shin-trap system.

How X-Pass Works

Control one leg so it cannot recover a strong hook, keep posture against collar and sleeve pulls, and clear the free side before the bottom player re-squares hips.

Make the X. Typical pictures include crossing grips on a pant and sleeve, trapping a shin across your thigh, or pinning an ankle while your near shin blocks re-guard.

Clear. Step, toreando-circle, or long-step the free hip. The X is a momentary structure—not a place to camp while they climb butterfly hooks.

Bottom player’s job. Break the X early, invert or sit into the passer’s hip, recover De La Riva or collar-sleeve, and punish floating posture with sweeps.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeve grips make the X sticky. Collar posture fights are constant. Spider and lasso answers try to freeze the passer before the clear.

No-gi. Wrist control, underhooks, and shin traps replace fabric. The clear must be faster because hip escapes and K-guard entries appear the moment you stall.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Against standing De La Riva, the passer clears the hook, establishes crossed control on the near leg, and steps the free hip past into side control or knee-on-belly—often after a toreando fake.

From collar-sleeve, an X on the sleeve-side leg can strip the collar frame and open a knee-cut or long-step finish when the bottom player overcommits to the pull.

Types / Variations

Grip X (pant + sleeve)

Crossed fabric control that freezes one leg while you circle or step the other side.

Shin-trap X

Trap their shin across your thigh/hip line, then clear—common in Mendes-influenced open-guard passing.

X into knee cut / long step

Use the X only long enough to win headquarters, then finish with a splitting or outside pass.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: toreando grips that stall into a single-leg control, failed knee cut that leaves an ankle, and standing open-guard grip breaks that isolate one leg.

Counters: strip the X, elevate into butterfly, invert under the clear, or sit up into a single-leg. Top contingencies include switching to leg drag or body lock if they invert hard.

X-Pass vs Toreando Pass

Factor X-Pass Toreando
Primary idea Crossed control on one leg, then clear Circle both legs with grip pressure
Feel Trap then step Bullfighter circles and redirects
Common chain X → knee cut / long step Toreando → HQ / knee cut
Best vs Sticky single-leg hooks Mobile open guard feet

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Fully legal in IBJJF gi and no-gi. No special restrictions. In ADCC and submission-only, X-structures often precede body locks or, where legal, lower-body entries if the pass stalls into entanglement.

Safety

Do not yank the knee sideways while trapping the shin. Keep clears controlled in beginner rooms—jumping past an X into uncontrolled landings risks knee and ankle collisions.

Common mistakes

  • Camping in the X while they climb sleeves and butterfly hooks.
  • Clearing without posture—getting pulled into closed or rubber structures.
  • Ignoring the free hook until it becomes De La Riva again.
  • Forcing the X against a committed invert instead of changing passes.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the X-pass in BJJ?

An open-guard pass that uses crossed control on one leg, then clears the free hip past the remaining hooks.

Is the X-pass only a Mendes thing?

Mendes-influenced schools popularized certain shin-trap and X concepts, but many gyms teach grip-X clears under the same name.

X-pass vs toreando—which should I learn first?

Learn toreando footwork and grips first; add X when you can isolate one leg without losing posture.

Does X-pass work in no-gi?

Yes, with wrist control and shin traps replacing pants grips. Timing must be sharper.

Why does my X-pass turn into their butterfly?

You stalled. Clear immediately or switch to a pressure pass once both hooks elevate.

Can I X-pass closed guard?

Not as a first action. Open the closed guard, establish posture and grips, then apply open-guard X mechanics.

What comes after a successful X clear?

Side control, knee-on-belly, or a north-south transition—stabilize the near underhook or crossface before hunting submissions.

The Bottom Line

The X-pass is a momentary crossed control that must become a clear. Pair it with toreando and knee-cut threats so the bottom player cannot solve only one problem.
