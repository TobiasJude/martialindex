Stack Escape

The stack escape is late-stage survival when a stack pass (often via double unders) folds your hips over your shoulders. Create a side, relieve cervical pressure, and limp-leg or roll to recover. Pair with double-under escape and hip escape.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Parent threat: stack pass. Early prevention lives on double-under escape.
  • Once folded: angle off—do not stay flat on the neck.
  • Limp-leg, hip twist, or granby families appear in high-level instruction.
  • Movement base: shrimp / hip escape.
  • Protect the neck first; guard second.

What Is Stack Escape?

Escapes from being stacked—hips elevated, weight on upper back/neck—during a pass. Up: escape. Across: double-under escape, triangle defense (when stacked in a triangle).

How It Works

Turn to one shoulder to unload the neck, frame their hips/shoulders, limp the legs to slide down their body, then pummel knees and feet back in. If they commit hard to one side, some athletes granby or twist the hips to reverse the pressure.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and belt grips can help you pull yourself down the stack or attack elbows.

No-gi. Rely on frames and limp-leg friction management.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Fully stacked from double unders → turn to a shoulder → limp heels up and slide → free knees → insert butterfly/half → recover.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Angle + limp-leg recovery.

Competition variation

Hip-twist defenses popularized in pressure-pass counter systems.

Related option

Granby / roll escapes when space and timing allow.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by successful stacking. Prevention: double-under escape. Top answers: keep the fold, walk the legs, settle side control.

How It Compares

Vs double-under escape: stack escape assumes the fold already happened.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal.

Safety

Defense pages prioritize survival: protect neck/joints first, create frames, then escape. Tap early in live training; do not ego through chokes or joint locks. Cervical safety is non-negotiable—tap or call freeze if the neck is compromised.

Common mistakes

  • Staying centered on the neck.
  • Wild kicks that help them pass.
  • Skipping prevention until every pass becomes a stack emergency.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a stack escape?

How to survive and recover when a passer folds your hips over your shoulders.

First priority?

Get off the centerline of your neck—create a side.

Limp leg?

Relaxing/sliding the legs to free the knees so you can pummel back inside.

Prevention page?

Double-under escape—stop the stack earlier.

Gi help?

Pants/belt grips can help you climb down the fold.

Danger?

Cervical stress—protect the neck above all.

Related notes?

Stack pass, double-under escape, shrimp, hip escape.

The Bottom Line

Stack escape is neck-first recovery from the fold—angle off, limp free, and treat double-under escape as prevention.
