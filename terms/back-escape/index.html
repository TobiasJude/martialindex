Back Escape

A back escape clears hooks and fights the seatbelt from back control so you can recover guard or scramble—always racing the rear naked choke and gi collar chokes like the bow and arrow.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Hands to choke first, then peel hooks—hips escape the line of the back.
  • Pillar: back control. Siblings: turtle escape, granby, crucifix awareness.
  • Gi adds bow-and-arrow and collar feeds; no-gi centers on RNC and body triangle.
  • Do not roll belly-down into a worse choke without a plan.
  • Shoulder escapes, hip escapes, and corkscrews are variations—not one choreography.

What Is Back Escape?

Back escape is the family of techniques that remove hooks (or a body triangle), neutralize the seatbelt, and exit back control into guard, top, or a scramble. It is the defensive twin of back-taking systems.

Under back control, survival beats flash: hand fighting against the choking arm comes before ambitious hip escapes.

How Back Escape Works

Address the choking threat immediately—two-on-one the choking arm or keep the chin and hands in the path. Then peel a hook, slide the hips off the centerline, and put shoulders to the mat or turn into them carefully.

Hook clear. Push the top hook down, free the hip, and move over their hips or into half/closed guard depending on the school’s method (e.g., Wardziński-style hip-over exits).

Top. Maintain chest-to-back connection, reinsert hooks, and finish RNC or bow-and-arrow before the escape completes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar feeds enable bow-and-arrow and clock-style chokes; defend collars as seriously as the seatbelt. Pants grips help top maintain hooks.

No-Gi. Rear naked choke and body triangle dominate. Hand fighting and peeling the underhook side of the seatbelt are primary.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Hooks in, seatbelt on: defend the choke, clear the top hook, scoot hips out, and either recover guard or scramble on top as they chase.

If they lock a body triangle, address the triangle mechanics first—pure hook peels may not apply.

Types / Variations

Hook peel to guard

Clear one hook, hip out, rebuild guard facing them.

Shoulder / hip-over escape

Slide shoulders and hop hips across to exit the back line.

Standing back escape

Stand to change levels, then peel hooks—common when space allows.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

You are already behind—entries are “the moment hooks land.” Train from settled back control and from incomplete hooks.

Counters: re-hook, flatten, RNC, bow-and-arrow, body triangle, or trap an arm into armbar.

If escape fails mid-way, return to tight defensive posture—do not panic-roll into the choke.

Back Escape vs RNC Defense

Factor Back Escape (positional) RNC Defense (submission)
Goal Leave back control Survive the choke now
Order After/alongside choke defense Immediate hand fight
Gi twin threat Bow and arrow Collar chokes + RNC analogs
Success look Guard or scramble Chin/hands blocking finish

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Escaping back control stops back points from continuing and avoids advantages/submissions. Know belt-level legality for related upper-body attacks in your division.

Safety

Never ignore a tightening choke to chase a fancy escape. Tap early. Drill peels with controlled choking pressure only.

Common mistakes

  • Both hands peeling hooks while the choke finishes.
  • Rolling stomach-down without hand control.
  • Leaving a single hook in while turning the wrong way.
  • Ignoring gi collar feeds.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a back escape in BJJ?

Any sequence that clears hooks/seatbelt from back control and returns you to guard or a scramble.

What do I defend first?

The choke—hands to the choking arm—then peel hooks.

Gi vs no-gi back escapes?

Same hip ideas; gi adds bow and arrow defense, no-gi centers on RNC.

What if they have a body triangle?

You need triangle-specific escapes; standard hook peels may fail.

Can I granby from the back?

Sometimes from incomplete back/turtle, not as a default under locked hooks and a finishing choke.

How do I drill back escapes?

Positional from settled back control with increasing choke threats; always start with hand fighting.

Related positions?

Turtle often precedes back takes—turtle escape prevents the back from forming.

The Bottom Line

Back escapes are a race: kill the choke threat, clear a hook, move the hips off the back line. Everything else is detail inside that order.
