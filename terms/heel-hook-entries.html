Heel Hook Entries

Heel hook entries are the paths into a heel hook—not the twist itself. You arrive through entanglements such as saddle / honey hole / 411, single-leg X, K-guard, and 50/50, then expose the heel. Legality depends on the current IBJJF or ADCC book—not gym lore.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • This is an entries concept: how you get to a heel-hookable position, not a finish manual.
  • Primary highways: saddle/honeyhole/411, SLX, K-guard, 50/50, outside ashi.
  • Inside vs outside heel hooks are different finishes from related entries—see those pages.
  • IBJJF and ADCC treat rotational attacks and reaping differently; verify the current PDFs.
  • No invented illegal lists on this page—rulebooks change by year, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi.

What Are Heel Hook Entries?

A heel hook without an entry is just a hand on a foot. The modern no-gi game is an entries game: you win an ashi garami family position, control the knee line, then collect the heel. This page maps those on-ramps and sends you to the position pages for details.

The finish pages—heel hook, inside heel hook, outside heel hook—cover rotation and grips. Do not treat an entries article as permission to crank knees in the wrong rule set or the wrong gym culture.

Martial Index keeps the tone of the reaping page: orientation, not a fake rulebook. If your coach or event says a configuration is restricted, that is the source of truth for that mat.

How Heel Hook Entries Work

Think in highways, not highlights. From guard, K-guard and SLX are common first attachments; from there you can back-step, reverse-X, or roll into a saddle. From passing, a back-step or knee-cut leftover can drop you into top saddle.

Once the entanglement is real, the “entry” to the heel is exposing it—clearing the heel from hiding, controlling the secondary leg (double trouble), and only then collecting the foot. Catching a heel with no knee-line control is how people get swept or countered.

Outside ashi and 50/50 are different highways. They still feed heel hooks when the rule set allows, but the control problems (and the reaping conversation) change. Read those position pages rather than forcing every entry into saddle.

If the rule set does not allow the finish you wanted, the same entries often still give sweeps, back takes, or legal ankle-locks—verify before you invent a workaround on the mat.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Many athletes still enter SLX and saddle for control and sweeps even when a heel hook is not the plan. Fabric changes grips; the entanglement map is the same. What you may finish is book-dependent.

No-gi. Entries are the meta. ADCC-style events generally expect a live heel-hook game; still read the event PDF.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex plays K-guard, back-steps to saddle, controls the secondary leg, then collects the heel—entry first, finish last.

Jordan is in an IBJJF gi match and uses the same SLX entry to sweep rather than to rotate a heel—because the current book, not a blog, decides the finish.

Types / Variations

Saddle / honey hole / 411 highway

Inside-sankaku family. Synonyms, not three unrelated positions—see saddle, honey hole, and 411 for naming.

SLX and K-guard highway

First attachments from open guard that connect to saddle or outside ashi.

50/50 and outside-ashi highway

Different mechanics, still heel-hook entries when allowed. See 50/50 and outside ashi garami.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

“Entries to the entries”: failed passes, half-butterfly, seated guard, and stuffed singles all spill into ashi. Counters: hide the heel, clear the knee line, stand out with posture. Escapes: leave the entanglement before the heel is collected—late heel-hook escapes are taps, not heroics.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Rotational leg attacks and reaping are among the fastest-changing lines in sport grappling. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for your exact division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. This page does not publish an illegal-move list—those lists go stale and get people hurt or disqualified.

Safety

Heel hooks can injure a knee faster than you can process a tap. Train entries and finishes only under qualified coaches, with informed partners, slow intensity, and instant releases. If your gym forbids live heel hooks at your rank, respect that.

Common mistakes

  • Hunting the heel before the entanglement is real.
  • Copying a social-media “illegal list” instead of opening the current PDF.
  • Treating honeyhole, 411, and saddle as unrelated tricks.
  • Exploding on a heel in the gym the way people explode in super-fights.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is this a how-to heel hook?

No. It is a map of entries. Finish mechanics live on the heel-hook pages.

Are heel hooks illegal in IBJJF?

It depends on the current book, your division, and gi vs no-gi. Do not memorize a blog list—open the PDF.

Does ADCC allow them?

Adult ADCC-style rules generally feature a live leg-lock game, including heel hooks. Still verify the event PDF.

Saddle vs honey hole vs 411?

Largely the same inside-sankaku family with different gym names. See those pages.

Where should beginners start?

With your coach’s rules. Many gyms teach positions first and delay live heel hooks.

What is reaping doing on this page?

Many entries intersect the reaping conversation. Read the reaping page and the current rulebook together.

Inside or outside heel hook from these entries?

Saddle family more often inside; outside ashi more often outside. See the specific finish pages.

The Bottom Line

Heel-hook entries are a map: attach (SLX, K-guard, ashi), win an entanglement (saddle, 50/50, outside ashi), then decide whether the current rulebook and your gym allow the finish. Link reaping, saddle, and the parent heel hook—and open the PDF.
