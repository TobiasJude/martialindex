Judogi

The judogi is judo’s uniform—jacket, trousers, and belt—built for gripping and throwing, and regulated at official events for fair handling.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Gi is a handle kit.
  • IJF fit rules matter for shiai.
  • Weave and cut change feel.
  • No-gi removes collar tools.
  • Keep it clean and intact.
  • Ties into Judo Rules gripping.

What is a judogi?

The judogi (柔道衣) is judo’s uniform—jacket, trousers, and belt—built to survive gripping, throwing, and IJF measurement checks.

It is both equipment and rule surface: sleeves and lapels are legal handles that define Nage-Waza setups.

How it works

Jacket weave resists tearing; fit rules limit how short sleeves/trousers can be so grips remain fair.

Belt rank culture varies by country; the belt’s contest job is to keep the jacket closed, not to win arguments online.

Gripping strategy (kumi-kata) lives on the gi—future batches will expand dedicated grip pages.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Remove the gi and you remove collar chokes like Okuri Eri Jime and classic sleeve kuzushi. Mechanics of hips/legs remain; handles change.

BJJ gis differ in cut and rules; do not assume an IJF judogi passes IBJJF or vice versa without checking.

In practice

Before shiai, athletes check sleeve length and jacket overlap. In the dojo, a too-tight fashion gi changes how Seoi Nage (Judo) hooks the arm.

Types / variations

Training gi vs competition IJF-approved gi; white vs blue for contests; lightweight vs heavyweight weaves.

Care & practicality

Wash, dry, spare belt. Torn gis stop training.

Learn to grip fight without constantly adjusting a loose jacket—that’s on you and the fit.

Compare with rules

Judo Rules tell you what you may grab and when; the judogi is what you’re grabbing.

IJF notes & safety

Official events enforce measurements. Unofficial dojos should still demand clean, safe uniforms.

No jewelry; control infections; don’t train in a rotting gi.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a fashion ‘slim’ gi that fails inspection.
  • Thinking no-gi skill will automatically appear without grip-less drilling.
  • Using the belt as a primary offensive handle illegally in ways your ruleset bans—know your sheet.

Fit changes technique. Oversized sleeves gift easy grips to opponents; undersized sleeves may be illegal and also alter how you insert arms for seoi. Try throws in the gi you will actually fight in.

Maintenance is performance: salt-crusted collars spread skin issues and make gripping miserable. Dry thoroughly; rotate at least two training gis if you train daily.

Belt folklore wastes time; belt function does not. A belt that constantly comes loose mid-randori is a safety and stalling issue—tie it properly and keep a spare.

For no-gi crossover, schedule explicit no-gi sessions rather than only “forgetting the jacket.” You need reps where lapel options do not exist so underhooks and head position develop on purpose.

Buying tip: if you plan IJF events, start from an approved model and measure. If you only train recreationally, prioritise durability and partner comfort over Instagram cut.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

IJF approved needed for class?

Usually not; yes for many official events.

BJJ gi OK?

Sometimes for training; measure for judo shiai.

White vs blue?

Contest assignment.

Wash how often?

After every sweaty session.

Belt = skill?

Culture varies; don’t outsource judgment to color.

Sleeve grips illegal?

Context-dependent—read current Judo Rules.

No-gi instead?

Complement, don’t replace, if you want IJF judo.

Shrunk gi?

May fail inspection.

The Bottom Line

Treat the Judogi as equipment and rule surface, then learn grips that feed Nage-Waza and Okuri Eri Jime.
