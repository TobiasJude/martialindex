Yeop Chagi (Side Kick)

Yeop chagi is Taekwondo’s side kick: a chambered, heel-driven line kick that trains hip strength and appears across Poomsae and breaking demos—distinct from Ap Chagi (Front Kick).

What Is Yeop Chagi (Side Kick)?

Yeop chagi (옆차기) is the side kick. It is a signature power and line tool in many traditional classes.

Impact surfaces are typically the heel or knife-edge of the foot, toes pulled back.

Compare with Ap Chagi (Front Kick): front kick stays more square; side kick turns the hip over and presents a narrower profile.

In modern WT Kyorugi (Sparring), pure side kicks appear less than turning and cutting tools—but the hip literacy still transfers.

How Yeop Chagi (Side Kick) Works

Chamber knee up, pivot supporting foot, open hip so the heel points at the target, drive through, retract without collapsing the standing knee.

Shoulders stack over hips; avoid banana-shaped spines.

Height comes from hip flexibility and chamber quality, not from leaning away until you tip over.

Land back into Taekwondo Stance or the form stance required by the pattern.

Mechanically, successful yeop chagi (side kick) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, yeop chagi (side kick) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill yeop chagi (side kick) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Breaking demo: held board at chest height; clean yeop chagi through with heel.

Forms: a mid-height side kick punctuates a direction change with Juchum Seogi (Horse Stance) or forward stance work nearby.

Fight picture: yeop chagi (side kick) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply yeop chagi (side kick) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Pointing toes into the target (sprain risk).
  • Failing to pivot the base foot—knee torque injury risk.
  • Chambering too low then muscling the kick.
  • Looking away from the target line.
  • Dropping the guard hand on the kicking side every time.
  • Treating yeop chagi (side kick) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick yeop chagi (side kick) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so yeop chagi (side kick) connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether yeop chagi (side kick) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, axe kicks, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) work.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Heel or blade?

Both are taught; heel is common for power. Follow school standard for tests.

Why does my supporting knee hurt?

Often incomplete pivot or locked knee—coach check required.

Side kick in Olympic sparring?

Less common than Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) and cutters, but hip turn skills still matter.

Yeop vs dollyo?

Side kick is a linear heel drive; turning kick is a rotational impact—see Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick).

How to get it higher?

Dynamic hip mobility + slower perfect chambers beat forced height.

The Bottom Line

Build yeop chagi for hip power and line integrity—even if your Kyorugi (Sparring) menu prefers other kicks.
