What is Te-Waza?

Te-waza (手技 / hand techniques) is one of the classical standing throw groupings inside Kodokan judo. Where koshi-waza emphasises hip platforms and ashi-waza emphasises feet/legs, te-waza emphasises hand levers, pulls, and timing—though real throws blend categories. Martial Index parents this hub under Tachi-Waza within Nage-Waza.

Important linking rule for this encyclopedia: we do not link missing future hubs. Koshi-waza and ashi-waza are conceptual neighbours discussed in prose without live pages yet. This te-waza page therefore lists only techniques that already exist as Martial Index terms—Batch 1 and Batch 2 live members.

Live te-waza-oriented members you can open now include Tai Otoshi, Seoi Otoshi, Uki Otoshi, Sumi Otoshi, Kata Guruma, Obi Otoshi, Sukui Nage, and related seoi-family pages under Seoi Nage (Judo) when they function as hand-driven loads (including Morote Seoi Nage and Ippon Seoi Nage). Classification debates exist in teaching charts; Martial Index navigates by useful hubs and live links.

Search intent for “te waza judo” is family definition plus a roster. This page is that roster—with honesty about bans affecting some classical members.

How it works

Hand techniques still need whole-body judo. Kuzushi, tsukuri, and kake apply. The difference is emphasis: sleeves and lapels pull; timing breaks floating balance; blocking legs or knee drops may appear without turning the throw into a pure koshi story.

Training te-waza well means drilling timing throws slowly enough to feel balance, then testing them in Randori without abandoning them because they are less Instagram-friendly than uchi-mata.

Some classical te-waza historically used lower grips that modern IJF rules restrict. The hub’s job is to point you at member pages that explain those collisions—especially Kata Guruma, Sukui Nage, and the leg-grab history trio Morote Gari, Kuchiki Taoshi, Kibisu Gaeshi (often discussed beside te-waza politics even when classified among shinmeisho techniques).

Score literacy remains global: throws score Ippon or Waza-Ari; pins use yuko/waza-ari/ippon clocks under 2026–28 rules.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Te-waza loves the Judogi. Sleeve and lapel are the primary levers. No-gi hand throws become wrist, elbow, and head-position games—related, renamed.

Gripping rules are part of te-waza reality. Read Judo Rules beside any below-belt classical form.

What it looks like in practice

A healthy dojo te-waza block might rotate tai-otoshi, uki-otoshi, seoi-otoshi, and one rare form (obi or yama-arashi literacy) across a month—while seoi family volume continues separately under its hub.

Film study: mute commentary and watch only hand paths for ten attempts of a chosen te-waza. Count how often success begins with a step uke did not want.

After throws, pursue Ne-Waza—especially the transition pages Seoi to Osaekomi and Ippon Seoi to Juji Gatame for seoi-driven hand loads.

Types / variations

Timing drops (uki / sumi)

Floating and corner hand drops—kata literacy engines.

Body-drop / shoulder-drop (tai / seoi-otoshi)

Hand levers with leg block or knee drop.

Wheels / scoops / belt drops

Kata-guruma, sukui-nage, obi-otoshi—watch modern legality notes.

Seoi family hand loads

Morote, ippon, drop, eri, reverse—navigate via the seoi hub.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter te-waza from sleeve-dominated grip fights and forward balance breaks. Counters vary by member—see each child page. Combinations across te-waza members beat isolated move-of-the-week culture.

Uchi-komi should include stationary and moving forms; timing dies in purely static drills.

Compare

Conceptually compare koshi-waza (hip techniques) and ashi-waza (foot/leg techniques)—discussed here without live hub links yet. On Martial Index today, compare this hub’s live members to hip throws like Harai Goshi and O Goshi, and to ashi examples like Deashi Harai, Ouchi Gari, and Kouchi Gari.

IJF notes & safety

Te-waza as a category is legal; individual members must comply with gripping and safety rules. Direct leg grabs remain banned in modern IJF senior shiai—see member pages for details. Osaekomi scoring under 2026–28: yuko 5–9s, waza-ari 10–19s, ippon 20s.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Treating te-waza as “arm only” without legs/hips.
  • Ignoring legality notes on classical scoops/wheels.
  • Linking or inventing missing koshi-waza pages in your notes as if live here.
  • Abandoning timing throws because they rarely trend.
  • No follow-up into ne-waza.

Training notes

Program a weekly te-waza emphasis day. Rotate live members so the hub stays a living map. Revisit Tachi-Waza and Uchi-Komi planning monthly.

When cross-training BJJ, keep Japanese throw names and art labels honest—BJJ seoi-nage is a cousin page, not a replacement for this hub.

Study notes

A complete month of te-waza might look like: week 1 tai-otoshi; week 2 uki/sumi timing; week 3 seoi-otoshi + drop literacy; week 4 rare forms (obi, yama) plus a rules quiz on leg grabs. That month will not trend on social media—and will produce better judoka.

Hand-technique athletes still need ashi and koshi tools; categories are lenses, not prisons. Use this hub to organise links, not to forbid hip throws.

When writing your own notes, do not invent live URLs for koshi-waza or ashi-waza until those Martial Index pages exist. Prose discussion is fine; fake hrefs are not.
