Finger Tape

Finger tape is athletic tape used to support sore or hypermobile digits during gi gripping—an adjunct, not a cure. See a clinician for injuries.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Tape can reduce painful end-range stress during gripping—technique and volume management still matter.
  • Clean, dry skin; avoid cutting circulation; remove after training.
  • Persistent pain needs professional medical advice—not thicker tape forever.
  • More common in gi than no-gi, but used in both.
  • Educational only—no dangerous medical protocols.

What Is Finger Tape in BJJ?

Finger tape is narrow athletic tape wrapped to buddy-support or limit painful motion in fingers stressed by collar and sleeve grips.

How It Works

Athletes tape before class, train, then remove tape to let skin breathe. Methods vary (single-digit, buddy tape). Learn a simple method from a coach or sports clinician—do not cut off blood flow.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi gripping drives most taping. No-gi still sees tape after prior gi seasons or for general joint support.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Before a collar-heavy class you lightly support two cranky fingers, avoid death grips when possible, and ice/recover after—see recovery.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Buddy taping; figure wraps; pre-cut finger tape brands.

Gym / sport variants

Some competitions restrict excessive taping or colors—check event gear rules if relevant.

Beginner notes

You may not need tape early. Grip smarter before taping denser.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Trim excess. Watch for numbness/tingling—rewrap looser. Manage weekly gi volume. Strengthen hands gradually via strength for BJJ guidance without ego loading.

How It Compares

Different from knee sleeves or spats. Tape is local support; recovery and load management are systemic.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Gear rules vary—verify event policies if you compete heavily taped.

Safety

Not medical advice. Seek care for swelling, deformity, numbness, or persistent pain. Do not tape over infected skin.

Common mistakes

Tourniquet wraps; leaving tape on for days; ignoring technique that rips fingers.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why tape fingers for BJJ?

To support sore or stressed digits during gripping—volume and technique still matter.

Is tape a cure?

No—see a clinician for injuries; tape is temporary support.

Gi only?

Mostly gi-driven, but used in no-gi too.

How tight?

Supportive, never numb—rewrap if tingling.

Medical advice?

This page is educational only.

Competition taping rules?

Check your event gear policy.

Related recovery?

See recovery for BJJ and strength for BJJ.

The Bottom Line

Use finger tape as light support while you fix grip habits and training load—not as armor over an untreated injury.
