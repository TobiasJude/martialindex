Fast Kick (Bal Bal)

Bal bal is Taekwondo’s fast kick: short Chamber (Bal Deung), small pivot, many valid impacts. Compare with Double Kick and read it through Kyorugi (Sparring), not through a power-breaking lens.

What Is Fast Kick (Bal Bal)?

Fast kick (bal bal / 발발) is a sport nickname for rapid turning-kick volume—often lead-leg, often to Palding (Trunk Scoring).

The mechanic shortens the Chamber (Bal Deung) and reduces hip over-rotation so the foot can bounce back to the floor and fire again.

Compare with Double Kick: a double is a two-count phrase; fast kick is a flurry identity that may include doubles.

PSS changed the job. Light-fast legal impacts can outscore prettier single kicks that miss the sensor.

Martial Index files Fast Kick (Bal Bal) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Fast Kick (Bal Bal) behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Fast Kick (Bal Bal) Works

Keep the kicking knee close, pivot about a quarter turn, and snap the instep or sensor sock into the Hogu.

The upper body does not fully reset between kicks. That is the speed trick—and the reason your hands must still guard.

Use Skip Step or a tiny bounce to feed the next kick without hopping out of range.

If the beeps stop, you are slapping. Return to a cleaner Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) path, then speed it up again.

Mechanically, successful fast kick (bal bal) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, fast kick (bal bal) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill fast kick (bal bal) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Point values still follow target and technique type under World Taekwondo Rules; speed does not invent a new scoring zone.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Open-stance rally: two athletes trade lead-leg fast kicks to the trunk until someone cuts angle.

Coach holds a hogu and counts legal beeps in ten seconds—not pretty kicks.

Fight picture: fast kick (bal bal) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply fast kick (bal bal) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Slapping with a floppy foot and calling it speed.
  • Dropping both hands to windmill.
  • Standing square so every fast kick is a counter magnet.
  • Never checking whether {electronic-scoring} actually registered.
  • Training only on air, never on a {hogu}.
  • Treating fast kick (bal bal) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick fast kick (bal bal) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so fast kick (bal bal) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether fast kick (bal bal) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe fast kick (bal bal) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put fast kick (bal bal) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does bal bal mean?

Gym slang for fast, repeated kicking—usually turning kicks to the trunk in Kyorugi (Sparring).

Is it just sloppy dollyo?

Done well it is a shortened, legal Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick). Done poorly it is slapping.

Why it scores now?

Electronic Scoring (PSS) rewards valid force and surface, which fast legal kicks can stack.

Double or fast?

Double Kick is two-count; fast kick is the broader speed identity.

ITF?

ITF Taekwondo sparring values different hand/foot mixes; do not paste bal-bal volume onto every ITF match.

The Bottom Line

Speed is a scoring tool only if each kick could stand alone as a legal Body Kick (Momtong Chagi) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi).
