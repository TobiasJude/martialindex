Anaconda Choke

The anaconda choke is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu front-headlock submission that wraps the head and arm and is frequently finished with a rolling motion to tighten the strangle—closely paired with the darce in modern no-gi and fully usable in the gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Anaconda wraps head-and-arm from the front headlock tree—often finished with a roll.
  • Learn it next to the darce; defenses to one open the other.
  • No-gi staple; gi valid.
  • Control first, roll second—do not cartwheel without the lock.
  • Legal adult IBJJF; tap to the choke.

What Is the Anaconda?

The anaconda is a head-and-arm choke from the front headlock family, famous for a rolling finish that uses your body weight to tighten the strangle as you come over the shoulder.

How Anaconda Works

Secure the head-and-arm wrap, connect your hands, then roll (when appropriate) so the opponent’s weight and your shoulder pressure finish the choke. Without a deep wrap, the roll is just a scramble.

Gi vs No-Gi

Same story as the darce: culturally huge in no-gi, fully legal and useful in gi. Fabric can slow the roll slightly; connection still decides the finish.

Types / Variations

Rolling finish

The iconic completion—only after the wrap is real.

From sprawl / front headlock

Primary entry after stuffing a shot or seated posture.

Chains

Switch to darce/guillotine when the wrap side is wrong.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter on sprawls and turtled fronts. Escape early by clearing the wrap; late—tap.

When not to roll. If the wrap is shallow or their posture is already escaping, rolling just resets the scramble. Improve the head-and-arm connection first; the roll is a tightening tool, not a magic move.

Mat awareness. Rolling finishes need space. In crowded open mats, finish without the roll or move to the edge—do not cartwheel into another pair.

After failed anacondas. Keep front headlock control and switch to darce or guillotine rather than abandoning the neck entirely. Chase-the-neck systems exist because single finishes get defended.

Naming and imagery. “Anaconda” is sport slang for coiling around the head and arm; it is not a classical judo catalogue name. That does not make it less real—it means instructional vocabulary varies, so match the visual (wrap + optional roll) rather than arguing labels.

Connection to body-lock passing. Athletes who dive on body locks sometimes gift front headlocks; the anaconda is a common punishment when the passer’s head sits wrong. Passers should read the body lock pass entry alongside this choke family.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal adult IBJJF/ADCC.

Safety

Tap to chokes. Roll carefully in crowded mats.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling without a lock.
  • Shallow wrap.
  • Ignoring darce when the armpit is the open path.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why is it called anaconda?

Imagery of coiling around the head/arm—sport nickname, not a formal judo name.

Do I have to roll?

No. The roll is common, not mandatory if the choke is already tight.

Anaconda or darce first?

Learn recognition of both; many courses teach them as a pair.

Gi okay?

Yes.

They defend the roll—what then?

Reset depth, switch to darce/guillotine, or keep front headlock control.

Safe for beginners?

With coaching and early taps, yes—rolling adds mat awareness needs.

Related to guillotine?

Same neighborhood. See guillotine.

The Bottom Line

The anaconda coils the head and arm from a front headlock—often finishing with a roll. Train it beside the darce so their defensive turns still end in a choke.
