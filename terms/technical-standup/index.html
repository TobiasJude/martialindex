Technical Stand-Up

The technical stand-up is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu fundamental for rising from the ground to the feet while protecting the face and hips - bridging guard, open guard, and standing with sweeps like tripod and scissor.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Post, kick through, stand behind a frame - never turn your back blindly.
  • Pillars: guard / open guard / standing transitions.
  • Cluster bridges: tripod, tomoe, hip bump, butterfly when scrambles stand both up.
  • Gi and no-gi identical skeleton; grips change hand fighting.
  • Self-defense and sport both require clean technical stand-ups.

What Is Technical Stand-Up?

Technical stand-up (technical get-up) is the default safe way to stand: one hand posts behind, the opposite-side foot plants, you kick the near leg through and rise while framing. It belongs in every beginner curriculum and reappears in MMA/self-defense.

Catalog links: up to guard and open guard; across Batch 4 sweeps that create stand-up scrambles (tripod, tomoe, hip bump) and passing when the passer stands (toreando, X-pass).

How Technical Stand-Up Works

From seated or supine: post the hand opposite the kick-through leg, place the far foot, kick the near leg back/through while framing with the free hand, and stand into a wrestling stance.

Opponent job: close distance, catch a single/double, or pass before you rise. Your frames and kick-through deny the easy shoot.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve grips can stall your post hand - strip or stand behind a collar frame.

No-gi. Same skeleton; wrist control and underhooks replace fabric.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a failed closed-guard sweep, technical stand-up to reset standing instead of turning away. After a tripod scramble, both athletes may technical stand-up into a clean wrestling exchange.

Types / Variations

Classic technical stand-up

Post + kick-through to feet.

Combat / self-defense stand-up

Emphasize face frames and creating distance.

Scramble stand-up

Partial posts during open-guard scrambles.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: anytime you need feet from bottom without turning the back. Counters against you: shoot, grab head, smash before the kick-through. Bridge to open-guard retention if standing is not available.

Technical Stand-Up vs Rolling to Knees

Factor Technical Stand-Up Rolling to Knees / Turtle
Goal Feet with frames Knees / turtle structures
Risk Shot in if frames late Back exposure if rushed
Pillar Guard to standing Turtle / guard retention
When Space to stand No space / heavy pressure

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Movement is legal and universal. No special restrictions.

Safety

Do not post with fingers back under heavy load. Keep neck safe when framing a charging opponent.

Common mistakes

  • Turning the back to stand.
  • No frame - eating a double leg.
  • Standing into toreando without open-guard retention skills.
  • Treating it as optional for sport-only athletes.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a technical stand-up?

A framed rise from the mat to the feet that protects the face and hips instead of turning the back.

Why does every gym teach it?

It is foundational self-defense and sport movement - standing safely from bottom.

Gi vs no-gi?

Same skeleton; grips change. Practice both.

When should I stand vs recover guard?

Stand when you have space and frames; recover open guard when they are too close.

Link to sweeps?

Tripod/tomoe scrambles often end in mutual stand-ups - see tripod.

Common mistake?

Turning the back or standing without a frame into a shot.

Catalog placement?

Concept under guard/open guard; bridges to standing guard and open-guard sweeps.

The Bottom Line

Technical stand-up is non-negotiable literacy: post, kick through, frame, stand. Link it to guard and open-guard pillars, and to the sweeps that create stand-up scrambles.
