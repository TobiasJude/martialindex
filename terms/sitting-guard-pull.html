Sitting Guard Pull

A sitting guard pull parks the hips on the mat into seated / butterfly structures—often the “grip and sit” pattern—detailed beside the broader guard pull concept.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Sit with grips and feet ready.
  • Immediate hooks or elevation—no vacation.
  • Double pulls create unique races.
  • Butt scooting empty is a different (worse) habit.
  • Know IBJJF double-pull procedures at a high level—verify book.

What Is Sitting Guard Pull?

Sitting pulls trade jump risk for a stable seat into butterfly, seated collar-sleeve, or Ashi entries. Elite guard players often touch fabric and sit before the opponent can shoot cleanly.

Double guard pulls (both sit) create ankle-control races and crab-ride/leg-drag scrambles—see competition film study patterns.

Rules: IBJJF has addressed simultaneous pulls with countdowns/penalties in published books—confirm the version for your event. ADCC culture usually prefers wrestling over mutual sits.

How Sitting Guard Pull Works

Win a grip, sit on the hip, plant hands as needed, bring feet up for hooks/frames, and engage.

Against a standing passer, threaten elevation or collar drag immediately.

Against a mutual sit, fight for inside pant/ankle position first.

Drill: sit → butterfly sweep; sit → retention vs toreando.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar-sleeve sits into classic open guards.

No-Gi. 2-on-1 sits into SLX/Ashi or butterfly.

Both: empty sitting invites the pass.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex grips and sits to butterfly, elevates, and sweeps.

Both sit; Alex wins cross-ankle control and starts a pass race.

No-gi: Alex’s wrist drag sit enters SLX.

Types / Variations

Butterfly sit

Elevation-ready seat.

Collar-sleeve sit

Gi open-guard opener.

Ashi / SLX sit

Leg-entanglement openers.

Double-pull scenarios

Mutual sits and races.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: planned pulls and counters to timid stand-ups.

Counters: pass on the sit, throw before the hips land, or force them to stand.

If passed on the sit, shrimp and recover—do not go flat.

Sitting Pull vs Jumping Pull

Factor Sitting Pull Jumping Pull
Risk to knees Generally lower Higher if mishandled
Control on landing Hips already down Must lock or adjust mid-air
Rules sensitivity Grip requirements still apply Extra division limits possible—verify
Best for Most athletes Specialists with coaching

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

High-level: simultaneous pulls and grip-before-sit norms appear in IBJJF publications—read the current PDF for countdown/penalty language rather than trusting memory. This page does not invent procedures. Contrast ADCC: mutual sits are less central to winning culture.

Safety

Even sitting pulls can tweak knees if legs tangle oddly. Keep toes safe; agree on intensity.

Common mistakes

  • Sitting with no grips.
  • Butt scooting without engagement.
  • Ignoring double-pull ankle races.
  • Lying flat after the sit.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a sitting guard pull?

A pull that seats the hips on the mat into a seated/butterfly-style guard.

Better than jumping?

Often safer for knees; still needs grips and a plan.

What is a double pull?

Both athletes sit—creating a unique open-guard race.

IBJJF notes?

Grip and simultaneous-pull procedures exist—verify the current book.

No-gi sit goals?

Often SLX/butterfly elevation or 2-on-1 control.

Main mistake?

Sitting empty and waiting.

Next?

Guard pull overview and seated guard.

The Bottom Line

Sitting pulls are the controlled way to start on bottom. Grip, sit, threaten immediately—and know how your rulebook treats mutual sits.
