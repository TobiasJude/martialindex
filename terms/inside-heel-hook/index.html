Inside Heel Hook

The inside heel hook is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu rotational leg lock finished from inside entanglements (saddle / inside sankaku / “honey hole”)—a modern no-gi staple documented under the live heel hook hub, with mandatory IBJJF vs ADCC verification.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Inside triangle on the legs precedes the finish.
  • Heel seat + controlled rotation; no snaps.
  • Often illegal in IBJJF gi—confirm current no-gi rules too.
  • Entries via reverse X, butterfly, 50/50, K-guard.
  • Sister page: outside heel hook.

What Is Inside Heel Hook?

Inside heel hooks come from configurations where your legs triangle the opponent’s leg from the inside (saddle). You isolate the heel and rotate to load the knee. Craig Jones–style reverse-X entries popularized one high-percentage path into the saddle.

See outside heel hook, 50/50, kneebar.

How Inside Heel Hook Works

Enter inside sankaku/saddle, close the triangle on the trapped leg, win the heel with a tight grip against the ribs/bone, remove slack, then rotate through the body while keeping the knee line trapped.

Hand-fighting their defensive grips is half the finish.

If they punch the heel free, re-close control before turning.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi / IBJJF. Expect heel hooks to be illegal in standard gi—verify.

No-Gi / ADCC-style. Inside saddle games are central.

Never import no-gi finishing habits into a banned bracket.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From reverse-X butterfly hooks, Alex elevates, rolls into saddle, seats Jordan’s heel, and finishes an inside heel hook under no-gi rules.

In IBJJF gi, Alex would abandon the rotational finish for legal alternatives.

Types / Variations

Saddle / honey-hole

Primary modern finish position.

Reverse-X / butterfly entry

Jones-style elevation into inside sankaku.

50/50 to inside

Transitions when the inside heel appears.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: reverse X, butterfly, K-guard, 50/50 misdirections.

Counters: keep heels hidden, frame the triangle opening, hand-fight aggressively.

Escapes: extract heel and knee together—tap if rotation loads.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Heel hooks and related knee-line controls differ by organization. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Reaping definitions also vary—do not invent legality from gym lore.

Safety

Same as all heel hooks: laggy pain vs ligament risk. Slow, supervised, instant release.

Common mistakes

  • Rotating before the saddle is closed.
  • Explosive snaps.
  • Competing illegal hooks.
  • Skipping ankle-lock literacy for banned brackets.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an inside heel hook?

A rotational knee attack from saddle/inside sankaku that traps the heel.

Same as outside?

Same family, different entanglement.

IBJJF legal?

Generally not in gi—verify current books for all divisions.

Famous entry?

Reverse-X to saddle is a modern classic.

Defense?

Hide heels; fight the triangle opening.

Related to 50/50?

Yes—entanglement highways overlap.

Safety rule #1?

Tap early; never snap.

The Bottom Line

Inside heel hook is saddle rotation: close the inside triangle, seat the heel, turn as a unit. Cross-read heel hook and outside, and verify IBJJF vs ADCC every time.
