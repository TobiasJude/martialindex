What Is Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu?

Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) is a grappling art that prioritizes positional control and submissions—especially from the guard—over striking. It descended from Kodokan judo via the Gracie and Fadda lineages in Brazil, then diversified into sport gi, no-gi, and MMA contexts. On Martial Index, this page is the head-term hub: a map into positions, passes, sweeps, and finishes rather than a single technique.

How BJJ Works

Live rounds cycle through stand-up, takedowns or pulls, then a positional chess game. Bottom players build offense from closed guard, open guard, and half guard. Top players advance via guard passing into side control, mount, or back control, then hunt a submission. A sweep flips bottom to top without abandoning connection.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi BJJ adds collar, sleeve, and pant grips—fuel for spider guard, lasso guard, and collar chokes like the cross-collar choke or Ezekiel choke. No-gi leans on underhooks, wrist control, body locks, and leg locks (heel hooks, saddle, 50/50). Concepts transfer; grips change the menu.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

A white-belt class might drill hip bump, scissor sweep, and an armbar from closed guard, then spar. Advanced rooms chain De La Riva into berimbolo, or pass through headquarters into a knee cut. Competition scoring (IBJJF, ADCC) shapes which risks are worth taking.

Core Map on Martial Index

Positions & guards

Start with Guard, then deepen into closed, open, half, X-guard, single-leg X, butterfly, and deep half.

Passing & top control

Guard passing hubs pressure and speed systems—toreando, pressure, body lock, Saulo-style pressure—then pins like side control, knee on belly, and mount.

Offense categories

Use Sweep for bottom-to-top reversals, Submission for finishes (chokes and joint locks), and Leg locks for the modern lower-body game.

How to Study This Hub

Pick one pillar, learn two cluster pages under it, then one chain (for example triangle → triangle to armbar). Avoid collecting named moves without a positional home.

How It Compares

Related arts: judo (throws and pins), wrestling (takedowns and top control), sambo (leg locks and jackets). BJJ’s distinctive emphasis is prolonged ground fighting with a rich bottom game.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Gi sport, no-gi sport, and MMA self-defense curricula diverge on legal submissions and engagement rules. Always check the rule set before drilling heel hooks or neck cranks.

Safety

Tap early, especially on necks and knees. Encyclopedia pages are reference—not a substitute for qualified coaching.

Common mistakes

Chasing submissions without position; ignoring guard retention; treating every YouTube move as equal priority.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is Brazilian jiu-jitsu?

A grappling art focused on positional control and submissions, especially from the guard.

Is BJJ the same as judo?

Related roots, different modern emphasis—BJJ developed a deeper ground and guard game.

Gi or no-gi first?

Either works; pick the room you’ll attend consistently, then cross-train grips later.

What should beginners learn first?

Frames, hip escape, closed-guard posture break, two sweeps, two submissions, and basic side-control survival.

Where do leg locks fit?

Under the leg locks hub and entanglement pages—rule-set dependent.

Is this page a technique?

No—it is the head-term encyclopedia hub that links out to pillars and clusters.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Top-level BJJ hub linking guard, passing, submissions, and sweeps.

The Bottom Line

Use Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu as the map: pick a pillar (guard, passing, submission, sweep), then drill two live cluster pages underneath it.
