Octopus Guard

Octopus guard—also called the reach-around—is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu bottom entanglement where you wrap an arm around their upper body/shoulder line from half or open structures, hunting front headlocks, guillotines, and back takes (popularized in modern no-gi by Craig Jones).

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Reach-around arm + bottom structure = octopus.
  • Pays off in front headlocks, guillotines, and back exposure.
  • Thrives in no-gi; gi versions exist with sleeve help.
  • Often starts from half guard or seated when they posture in.
  • Related: half, guillotine.

What Is Octopus Guard?

Octopus guard is a bottom control that prioritizes an upper-body wrap—your arm snaking around their shoulder/torso—while your legs still manage half or open-guard connection. Craig Jones's "reach-around" instructional language brought the position into mainstream no-gi conversation; older gyms used similar overhooks and body wraps without the name. It is a key branch of open guard.

How Octopus Guard Works

The wrap kills their posture and creates a path to front headlock seats, guillotines, and back takes when they try to extract. Legs prevent the easy knee-cut while the arm does the trapping. If the wrap is shallow, they simply stand and leave.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. Primary home—rashguard friction and modern front-headlock games fit octopus well.

Gi. Sleeve and lapel can help the wrap, but many gi athletes prefer traditional half underhooks instead.

What Does Octopus Guard Look Like in Practice?

Jordan drives into Alex's half. Alex snakes the near arm around Jordan's upper body (octopus), sits to a front headlock when Jordan posts, and finishes a guillotine—or takes the back if Jordan turns away.

Types / Variations

Basic reach-around

Arm wrap from half/open with legs still connected.

Front headlock / guillotine path

Convert the wrap into seated front headlock attacks.

Back-take path

When they turn away from the choke, climb to rear mount.

Escaping octopus (top)

Posture, peel the wrap, free the head, and pass before the front headlock seats.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From half when they lean in; from seated when they reach; off failed passes.

Bottom threats. Guillotine, front headlock series, back takes.

Top counters. Don't dive head-first into the wrap; peel and posture; extract early.

Octopus vs Standard Half

Factor Octopus Standard Half

No-gi Excellent Excellent

Naming Modern / Jones-era popular Universal

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Guillotines and back takes score/finish normally. No special restrictions on the wrap itself.

Safety

Front headlocks can crank necks—tap early and drill with control.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow wrap with no leg connection—they simply stand up.
  • Hunting guillotine without sealing the front headlock.
  • On top: driving headfirst into the octopus.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is octopus guard the same as the reach-around?

Yes—reach-around is the common instructional synonym popularized with Craig Jones material.

Octopus from half or open?

Both. Half is a common start; seated/open entries appear when they reach in.

Best finish from octopus?

Front headlock series (guillotine variants) and back takes when they turn out.

Does it work in the gi?

Yes, but no-gi is where it shines culturally and mechanically for many athletes.

How do I escape octopus on top?

Peel the wrapping arm, posture, free your head, and pass before they seat a front headlock.

Octopus vs lockdown?

Octopus wraps the upper body; lockdown figure-fours the leg.

The Bottom Line

Octopus / reach-around wraps their upper body from bottom half or open structures to hunt front headlocks and backs—modern no-gi favorite. See half guard and guillotine.
