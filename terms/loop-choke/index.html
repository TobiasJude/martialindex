Loop Choke

The loop choke is a gi-only strangle that feeds a deep far collar across the neck and closes the “loop” with head control—often as opponents drive into closed guard or half guard.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Deep far collar + hand behind the head; pull head down while you curl.
  • Gi-only. Sibling of cross collar, clock, and sliding lapel ideas.
  • Best on forward pressure—use their pass energy.
  • Without head control the loop stays open.
  • Chain to front chokes, triangles, and collar drags when the head pops up.

What Is Loop Choke?

The loop choke uses one deep collar grip as a noose while the other hand (or elbow geometry) pulls the head into the loop. It thrives in transitions—guard pulls, smash passes, and half-guard underhooks—more than in static dual-collar fights.

It sits in the collar-choke cluster with cross collar and clock choke cousins.

How Loop Choke Works

Reach deep to the far collar (fingers in, thumb out is common), get the forearm on the neck, control the head, and close by pulling the skull down while curling your body. Elbows pinch.

Passer. Posture, clear the collar, and back out of the loop before the head is trapped.

Timing. Catch them moving forward—static loop attempts against good posture fail often.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi — required.

No-Gi. Not available. Use guillotines, front headlocks, and anaconda/darce as transitional analogs.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Guard pull with collar already set; as they follow down, hand behind head, close the loop. Or from bottom half as they drive: high collar, elbow high, catch the loop when the head dips.

Types / Variations

Guard-pull loop

Collar set standing/pulling, finish as they land.

Half-guard loop

Against smash pressure when the head is low.

Pass-rush loop

Punish toreando/knee-cut drives that dive the head.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: closed-guard collar fights, half-guard sit-ups, standing collar grips into pulls, failed triangles that leave deep collars.

Counters: posture, peel the collar, turn the head out, jump to north-south carefully, or free the head and continue the pass.

If they posture hard, switch to collar drag, scissor, or cross collar.

Loop Choke vs Cross Collar

Factor Loop Choke Cross Collar Choke
Second tool Head control Second collar grip
Best energy Forward pressure Stable pin or broken posture
Feel Noose closing Dual-lapel tourniquet
Common fail No head control Shallow first grip

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi. Absent in no-gi. Follow collar grip rules.

Safety

Close gradually; the loop can finish fast when they drive in. Tap early in positional sparring.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow collar.
  • Forgetting head control.
  • Attacking against perfect posture without an angle.
  • Letting them clear the grip mid-finish without a backup.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a loop choke?

A gi choke using a deep far collar and head control to close a lapel noose.

Is the loop choke gi-only?

Yes.

When does it work best?

When they drive forward into you—pass rushes, smash half, guard pulls.

Loop vs clock choke?

Clock is typically from top turtle with a walking finish; loop is often bottom/transitional. See clock choke.

Why does my loop fail?

Usually shallow grip or no head control—posture wins.

No-gi replacement?

Guillotine and front-headlock family, not lapels.

Related collar attacks?

Cross collar, bow and arrow, baseball, paper cutter—see collar choke hub.

The Bottom Line

The loop choke is a gi noose: deep collar, head trapped, close on their forward energy. Without the head, it is only a grip.
