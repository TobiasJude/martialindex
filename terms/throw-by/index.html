Throw-By

A throw-by clears an arm past you to open a Go-Behind—tie clearance, not a high-amplitude belly-to-back throw.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Throw-by clears an arm to the back.
  • Collar and under-elbow entries.
  • Sibling of slide-by/duck-under.
  • Step off-line.
  • Finish the go-behind.

What Is Throw-By?

A throw-by is a clearance: you pull, snap, or guide an arm across so the opponent’s shoulder passes you, then you attack the back or far hip.

Common from Collar Tie or under-elbow control (Cary Kolat–style under-elbow throw-bys).

Related to Slide-By and Duck Under in the “get to the back” family.

In Wrestling, throw-bys punish rigid postures in the pocket.

Martial Index files Throw-By under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Throw-By behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Throw-By Works

Control a wrist/elbow, create a yank or circular throw of that arm past your center line, step off-line, and latch a Go-Behind or short drag.

Keep your head safe—don’t throw yourself under a punch path in MMA cross-training without adjusting.

If they stiff-arm, combine with a Snap Down or level change.

Finish on the mat with control before looking for turns.

Mechanically, successful throw-by work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, throw-by is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how throw-by is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of throw-by either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

In folkstyle, complete control for the takedown/go-behind award.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Collar tie → throw-by the far arm → go-behind for two.

Under-elbow throw-by when they over-extend a reach.

Match picture: throw-by appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply throw-by after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Yank without stepping—staying in front.
  • Losing the wrist and getting underhooked.
  • No finish after the clear.
  • Telegraphing against elite hand-fighters.
  • Treating throw-by as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle throw-by inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so throw-by connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether throw-by appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Partner reaches on command; you throw-by to go-behind.

Live collar-tie only scoring via throw-by.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe throw-by as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of throw-by stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Freestyle Wrestling, Folkstyle Wrestling, and Wrestling Stance into siblings, then return here after live rounds to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations where mistiming injures partners.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Throw-by vs duck-under?

Duck-under goes under the arm; throw-by clears the arm past you.

Throw-by vs slide-by?

Sibling clearances—slide-by often emphasizes sliding past the underhook/shoulder.

Good for beginners?

Yes with collar-tie basics first.

Freestyle useful?

Very—path to go-behinds and exposure setups.

MMA note?

Watch strikes when clearing arms.

Best finish?

Go-Behind then ride or turn.

The Bottom Line

Practice throw-bys as hand-fight reactions—not as cooperative arm gifts.
