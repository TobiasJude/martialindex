Triangle Choke

The triangle choke is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu submission that forms a figure-four with the legs around the opponent’s neck and one arm, compressing the carotids—high percentage from closed and open guard when angle and shoulder control beat raw flexibility.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Triangle = figure-four with the legs around neck + one arm; typically a blood choke.
  • Angle and eliminating the far shoulder matter more than “having long legs.”
  • Gi sleeve/collar helps entries; no-gi uses wrist control and overhooks.
  • Converts constantly with the armbar and sometimes omoplata.
  • Legal in standard adult IBJJF; tap to the choke before you go out.

What Is the Triangle Choke?

The triangle (sankaku jime in judo lineage) locks your legs in a figure-four around the opponent’s neck and one arm so your thigh and calf compress the carotids. It is one of BJJ’s most iconic submissions from guard—and a family of positions (front, side, rear, reverse) at higher levels.

Short-legged athletes finish triangles regularly when mechanics are right: offline angle, controlled head, and a bite that excludes the far shoulder’s bulk.

How Triangle Works

Enter. Control a wrist or sleeve, break posture, throw a leg over the shoulder, lock the figure-four.

Adjust. Pinch the knee, pull the head, cut an angle so only neck and one arm remain inside.

Finish. Squeeze with the legs and elevate the hips; optional arm pull deepens the strangle.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar-and-sleeve entries are standard; pants grips help when they try to stand out.

No-gi. Wrist control, overhooks, and high-elbow closed guard replace fabric. Locking may feel tighter without gi bulk—or harder without sleeve anchors.

Standing passers. No-gi athletes stand out of triangles faster; angle and hip control must be earlier.

Gi-only extras. Some lapel-assisted triangles exist; they do not travel to no-gi.

>What Does the Triangle Look Like in Practice?

From closed guard, Alex pins Jordan’s right wrist, climbs the left leg over Jordan’s shoulder, locks the triangle, shrimps to an angle, and finishes as Jordan postures.

No-gi: same climb from an overhook instead of a sleeve.

Types / Variations

Front triangle from guard

The default: legs around neck and one arm, facing the opponent.

Angle and shoulder elimination

Get offline; do not stay square. Exclude the far shoulder so the lock diameter shrinks.

Side, rear, and reverse triangles

Advanced family members from passing, turtle, and mount transitions—same figure-four idea, different orientation.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Closed/open guard, failed armbars, overhooks, mount transitions.

Escapes. Posture and stack early; hide the arm; turn toward the locked hip. Late: tap—do not wait to go unconscious.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in adult IBJJF gi/no-gi. Confirm kids’ rules. Legal in ADCC. Going unconscious loses the match—tap earlier in training.

Safety

Tap to chokes. Partners should release immediately on tap. Do not crank the neck as a fake “finish.”

Common mistakes

  • Staying square.
  • Crossing ankles far from the neck instead of locking a tight figure-four.
  • No posture break before the climb.
  • Forcing against a stacked posture without angle.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Can short people hit triangles?

Yes. Mechanics and angle beat limb-length myths. See instruction that addresses shoulder elimination and offline hips.

Blood choke or air choke?

Proper triangles are primarily blood chokes via the carotids. Poor angles can feel like neck pain—fix the lock rather than crank.

Why does my triangle always get stacked?

You climbed without angle or head control. Shrimp offline and pull the head before you squeeze.

Gi sleeves required?

No. Helpful for entries, not required for the finish.

Triangle from mount?

Yes—front and side triangles appear from top as well as bottom.

How do I convert to armbar?

When they posture to relieve the choke, their arm often becomes available—swing to juji. See armbar.

Is the triangle good in MMA?

Yes, with caveats for punches; sport BJJ entries still transfer when timed safely.

The Bottom Line

The triangle choke strangles with a leg figure-four around the neck and one arm. Prioritize angle and shoulder control over flexibility myths, and keep the armbar ready when they defend.
