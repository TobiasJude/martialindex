What Is Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick)?

Dollyo chagi (돌려차기) is the turning or roundhouse-style kick in Korean curricula.

It is central to Kyorugi (Sparring) offense toward Palding (Trunk Scoring) and the head, and appears throughout basics and many patterns.

Compare with Roundhouse Kick: Muay Thai often emphasizes shin impact and body kick power under eight limbs; TKD sport often emphasizes speed, retraction, and sensor-legal foot surfaces.

Spinning and jumping relatives include Tornado Kick and jump turning kicks (later batches).

How Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) Works

Chamber knee across, pivot base foot, rotate hip so the foot travels on an arc into the target, strike with instep or ball per coaching, retract to guard.

Lead-leg dollyo is faster; rear-leg dollyo usually heavier.

For PSS, angle and surface must wake sensors—not merely “look hard.”

Recover to Taekwondo Stance immediately—posing invites counters like Dwi Chagi (Back Kick).

Mechanically, successful dollyo chagi (turning kick) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, dollyo chagi (turning kick) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill dollyo chagi (turning kick) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Under World Taekwondo Rules, turning kicks to trunk and head carry defined point values that have shifted across cycles.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Olympic exchange: double lead-leg feeder into a rear Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) on the Hogu.

Pad round: coach calls head/body randomly; you adjust height without dropping hands.

Fight picture: dollyo chagi (turning kick) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply dollyo chagi (turning kick) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Lead-leg dollyo

Speed, cut setups, and rapid re-chambers.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Rear-leg dollyo

Power turning kick; bigger tell if telegraphed.

Common Mistakes

  • Swing without chamber (loopy, slow, telegraphed).
  • Planting the base heel with no pivot.
  • Hitting with the wrong surface for the target/rules.
  • Leaning so far you cannot retract.
  • Ignoring the Muay Thai comparison then copying shin checks unsafely without context.
  • Treating dollyo chagi (turning kick) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Shadowkick dollyo chagi (turning kick) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so dollyo chagi (turning kick) connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Dollyo vs roundhouse?

Same broad family. Martial Index keeps Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) for TKD and links to Roundhouse Kick for Muay Thai context.

Instep or ball of foot?

Both appear; sport gear and school tests dictate. Instep is common on pads; ball appears in many traditional cues.

Why no score on hogu?

Angle, surface, or force under Electronic Scoring (PSS)—film and adjust.

Lead or rear first?

Beginners often learn rear for power feel; sport athletes live on lead-leg volume.

Relation to narae and tornado?

Narae Chagi and Tornado Kick are advanced turning-family relatives.

The Bottom Line

Make dollyo chagi your reliable scoring whip—then branch into cuts, spins, and counters.
