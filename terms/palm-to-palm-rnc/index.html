Palm-to-Palm RNC

The palm-to-palm RNC (clasping-hands / gable finish) is a rear naked choke variant: after the choking arm wraps the neck from back control, you clasp palms (or gable) instead of the textbook bicep-and-hand-behind-head figure-four. It is faster to lock and easier to peel.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Use it when the figure-four will not close—short arms, scramble, or a half-second window.
  • More mixed air/blood than a perfect figure-four; hide the hands anyway.
  • Do not confuse this with the short choke, which is a shallower wrap.
  • Setup still matters: see RNC setup from back.
  • MMA history used this clasp often with gloves on.

What Is Palm-to-Palm RNC?

A palm-to-palm RNC keeps the rear naked structure—you are behind them, one arm under the chin—but the second hand simply meets the first. Wikipedia and gyms also call it the clasping-hands variation. You gain speed and squeeze options; you lose some of the head-control the figure-four’s behind-the-head hand provides.

It is not automatically “worse.” Thick necks, short limbs, and scramble finishes often cannot complete a clean bicep catch. The clasp lets you finish anyway. The cost is a grip that is easier to split and a choke that can drift onto the windpipe if the elbow is not deep.

Martial Index splits the grip so “palm to palm RNC” searchers are not dumped into a generic RNC essay, then cross-links figure-four and short choke so the three finishes stay a system.

How Palm-to-Palm RNC Works

Complete the same setup: seatbelt, hooks, chin window, choking arm in. Then clap the support palm to the choking palm behind or beside their head—gable (thumbs together) is a common strong variant.

Join the elbows and expand the chest. Because you lack a dedicated palm on the back of the skull, use your head (ear-to-ear) to keep theirs from slipping out.

If they peel a thumb, hide it or convert to figure-four the moment the bicep is available. If the wrap is too shallow for either, switch to a short choke.

Do not lace fingers into a full nelson or crank the head sideways; that is a different, uglier attack.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Cloth can make clasping clumsier; many gi players prefer figure-four or a collar. The clasp still works.

No-gi / MMA. The usual home, especially with gloves that block a tidy bicep catch.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex hits the chin in a scramble, clasps palms, and finishes before a figure-four could form.

Jordan’s clasp gets peeled; they slide the choking hand to the bicep and convert to a figure-four.

Types / Variations

Gable / palm-to-palm behind the head

Hands meet; elbows join.

Scramble clasp

One-second window off a back take—speed over elegance.

Clasp to figure-four conversion

Use the clasp as a waypoint, not always a destination.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Same back-control entries as any RNC. Choose this grip when the bicep is out of reach. Counters: split the hands, tuck the chin, slide out the back door. Escapes: peel thumbs before the elbows meet.

Palm-to-Palm vs Figure-Four RNC

Factor Palm-to-palm Figure-four
Speed Faster to lock One extra step (hand behind head)
Head control Less (use your head) Support hand owns the skull
Peel risk Higher Lower if hands are hidden
Feel Often mixed air/blood Cleaner bilateral blood choke

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult rear naked chokes are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

A sloppy clasp can become a windpipe crush. Keep the elbow under the chin and tap early. No neck twists.

Common mistakes

  • Clasping so far from the neck that you only have a hug.
  • Showing thumbs for them to peel.
  • Calling a short choke a palm-to-palm RNC (different depth).
  • Abandoning hooks while you fuss with the clasp.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is palm-to-palm “wrong”?

No. It is a legitimate finish, especially in scrambles. Figure-four is still the textbook.

Gable or flat palms?

Gable is usually stronger. Use what you can hide.

Same as short choke?

No. Short choke is a shallower wrap, often with the elbow on their back.

Works with short arms?

Often better than forcing a figure-four you cannot close.

Gi?

Yes, but collars may be higher percentage if the clasp is messy.

MMA useful?

Very—gloves make some figure-fours awkward.

Setup still required?

Yes. Read RNC setup from back.

The Bottom Line

Palm-to-palm RNC is the fast clasp: lock it when the figure-four will not close, hide the hands, keep the hooks. Convert to figure-four when you can; use short choke when you cannot get depth.
