50/50 Heel Hook

The 50/50 heel hook is the rotational knee attack finished from 50/50 entanglement—both athletes’ legs crossed in a shared ashi. High percentage in no-gi; heavily rule-dependent in gi. Pair with heel-hook defense.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Finish lives inside 50/50—control the heel, connect the grip, rotate through the knee line.
  • Parent finishes: heel hook. Sibling positions: saddle, inside ashi.
  • Verify IBJJF vs ADCC + reaping before competing.
  • Defense: shorten the leg, hide the heel, hand-fight—see heel-hook defense.
  • Tap early; 50/50 heel hooks injure quickly when ego intervenes.

What Is 50/50 Heel Hook?

A heel hook applied while both players share 50/50 entanglement. Unlike saddle finishes, both athletes often have similar access—so control, grip fighting, and hiding the heel decide who wins the exchange. Cluster: outside heel hook, saddle heel hook, ashigarami.

How It Works

Isolate the heel (often inside), connect hands or a figure-four, pinch knees, and rotate the foot to load the knee. Secondary goals: break their posture, clear their defensive cross-feet, and prevent them from matching your finish.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Frequently illegal or restricted for heel hooks—use 50/50 for sweeps/control and legal lower-leg attacks only as the book allows.

No-gi. Signature ADCC-era finish; train entries from failed passes, SLX, and saddle transitions into 50/50.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You enter 50/50 off a scramble, win the hand fight, expose the heel, and finish the inside heel hook before they hide it.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Inside heel hook from top or bottom 50/50.

Competition variation

Outside heel hook variants when the foot orientation flips.

Related option

Transition out to saddle / honey hole on the opposite leg when 50/50 stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

From 50/50 itself, or via inside ashi and saddle trees. Defense: heel-hook defense, ankle-line awareness, and early extraction.

How It Compares

Vs saddle heel hook: mutual risk vs dominant control. Vs straight ankle lock: rotation vs extension.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Cross-read reaping and heel hook.

Safety

Leg attacks load ligaments fast. Tap early, drill slowly, and never crank through pain. Partners should release immediately on tap or freeze.

Common mistakes

  • Turning the heel the wrong way into the finish.
  • Ignoring hand fighting and getting finished first.
  • Training cranks at full speed without gradual exposure.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a 50/50 heel hook?

A heel hook finished while both athletes are entangled in 50/50 guard.

Is it legal in IBJJF gi?

Generally heel hooks are restricted in standard adult gi—verify the current book.

Inside or outside?

Both appear; inside finishes are common when the heel is exposed toward you.

How do you defend?

Hide the heel, shorten the leg, cross the feet, and hand-fight—see heel-hook defense.

How is it different from a saddle heel hook?

50/50 is mutual; saddle usually offers clearer top-side dominance.

Safe training tips?

Slow drills, early taps, and clear verbal agreements on leg-lock intensity.

Related notes?

50/50, heel hook, saddle heel hook, reaping, IBJJF rules, ADCC rules.

The Bottom Line

The 50/50 heel hook is a high-stakes finish inside mutual entanglement—win the heel fight or leave the position, and always check the rulebooks.
