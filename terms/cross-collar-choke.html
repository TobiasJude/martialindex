Cross Collar Choke

The cross collar choke (cross choke / juji-jime family) finishes with opposing collar grips that cut the neck—most famously from mount and closed guard. It is a gi-only tool inside the collar choke family.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Depth first, then second grip; wrists rotate more than arms squeeze.
  • Mount and closed guard are flagship homes; side control variants exist.
  • Gi-only—contrast with rear naked choke for no-gi.
  • Cluster siblings: loop, bow-and-arrow, baseball, paper cutter.
  • High mount helps stop elbow escapes while you feed grips.

What Is Cross Collar Choke?

The cross collar choke is the iconic gi strangle: one hand deep in the far collar, the other in the near collar (or a supporting grip), wrists turning to compress the carotids. Roger Gracie’s mount version is the modern teaching reference for many academies.

It maps to the mount and closed-guard pillars and cross-links to other collar tools when the second grip fails.

How Cross Collar Choke Works

Insert the first lapel deep (often sliding the arm before locking fingers), stabilize so they cannot bridge free, then secure the second grip and rotate the wrists while dropping weight or curling from guard.

Mount. Stay high enough to kill elbow escapes; offset your base against the bridge on the trapped-arm side.

Guard. Break posture, get the first collar, create angle, second collar, curl elbows and pull their head in.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi — required. No lapels, no cross collar.

No-Gi. Switch to arm-triangle, mounted guillotine attempts, or back takes—do not invent imaginary collars.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Mount: slide the first arm deep to the neck, grip, shift base, second thumb/grip behind the ear line, wrists rotate as the head drops.

Closed guard: posture break, deep cross collar, second collar, hips angle, finish curl.

Types / Variations

Mount cross collar

Roger-style deep first lapel + second grip under pressure.

Closed-guard cross collar

Posture break + dual collars + curl.

Palm-up / palm-down mixes

Grip orientations vary by school; wrist blades matter more than labels.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: stabilized mount, closed-guard collar fights, failed armbars that leave collars open, side-to-mount transitions.

Counters: hand-fight the first grip, bridge and roll, elbow escape, posture in guard, turn into the choke.

If the second grip fails, transition to armbar, americana, or bow-and-arrow if they turn.

Cross Collar vs Loop Choke

Factor Cross Collar Choke Loop Choke
Grips Two collars (classic) One deep collar + head control
Common home Mount / closed guard Guard pulls / half / pass rushes
Feel Tourniquet with dual lapels Noose closing with head pull
Gi-only? Yes Yes

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi. Not available in no-gi divisions. Follow sleeve/collar grip regulations in your ruleset.

Safety

Increase pressure gradually. Watch partners’ necks in drilling. Tap to deep mounts early.

Common mistakes

  • Gripping shallow before the arm is deep.
  • Squeezing arms instead of rotating wrists.
  • Low mount that invites elbow escape mid-choke.
  • Leaving posture intact from closed guard.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the cross collar choke?

A gi strangle using opposing collar grips, classically from mount or closed guard.

Is it gi-only?

Yes. No-gi uses other neck attacks.

Mount or guard first?

Learn both; mount pressure versions often finish higher percentage for beginners once mount is stable.

Why do my cross collars fail?

Shallow first grip, no posture break (guard), or low mount allowing escapes.

How is it different from bow and arrow?

Bow and arrow is usually from the back with one collar and a pant grip plus leg push. See bow and arrow.

Wrist tip?

Rotate wrists so the bone blades dig; do not only hug with biceps.

Defense priority?

Stop the first deep collar; elbows in under mount; posture in guard.

The Bottom Line

Cross collar is depth, base, second grip, wrists. It defines gi mount and closed-guard strangling—and it vanishes the moment the kimono comes off.
