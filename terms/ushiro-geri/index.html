Ushiro-Geri

Ushiro-geri is the back kick: glance, chamber, drive the heel behind you—linear power when the threat is at your rear arc.

What Is Ushiro-Geri?

Ushiro-geri (後ろ蹴り) is the back kick. It differs from spinning Mawashi-Geri variants by emphasizing a rearward linear thrust.

Compare with Yoko-Geri (side) and Mae-Geri (front).

Used in Kihon, some Kata applications, and selectively in Kumite.

Read this beside the Karate style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Ushiro-Geri behaves in kihon, kata, and kumite.

How Ushiro-Geri Works

Look over the shoulder to sight the target, chamber the knee, drive the heel back, retract, and turn to recover Kamae.

Balance on the supporting leg is everything—arms help counterbalance without flailing.

Do not confuse a sloppy spin with a true ushiro chamber.

Mechanically, successful ushiro-geri work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Karate Stance with Kamae alive.

If distance (maai) is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: punches need Hikite and Kime; kicks need chamber and retract; blocks need a counter path into Gyaku-Zuki or Oi-Zuki.

Dojo vs Competition / Rules Notes

In traditional dojo training, ushiro-geri lives inside the kihon–kata–kumite triangle—form quality matters as much as fight utility.

Under WKF sport karate, scoring and contact limits reshape how ushiro-geri appears in matches versus full-contact styles such as Kyokushin.

Gym culture may drill ushiro-geri daily; grading and competition may shrink the toolkit based on syllabus and ruleset.

Confirm the dojo syllabus and bout agreement before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Blind techniques can be unsafe in crowded dojo lines—space matters.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Kihon: slow ushiro-geri to a pad held behind the line.

Tactical: after a turn in kata or when a partner circles to your back.

Dojo picture: ushiro-geri appears when Karate Stance and timing say so, then yields to the next technique in a combination or to a reset of Kamae.

Gym test: can you apply ushiro-geri after a feint or Kizami-Zuki setup and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Floor ushiro-geri

Standard kihon back kick without excess spin.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives kumite pressure, not only line kihon.

Turn-in entries

Common Mistakes

  • Kicking blind without looking.
  • Spinning out of control.
  • Leaning so far you fall after impact.
  • Bent wrist-like soft ankle on impact.
  • No retract.
  • Treating ushiro-geri as a highlight instead of a distance-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down and no hikite.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Line-drill ushiro-geri inside full kihon sets; return to Karate Stance and Kamae after every attempt.

Pads or makiwara: technical → moving → forced follow-up so ushiro-geri connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat solo fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film kata and kumite weekly and ask whether ushiro-geri appears with honest Kime.

Progress intensity with a sensei—especially for kicks, spins, and full-power Oi-Zuki.

Practice the look-and-chamber without kicking until orientation is automatic.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe ushiro-geri as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor maai, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare ryuha, you will see stylistic accents: some put ushiro-geri behind heavy Kihon repetition; others behind application-minded Kata or sport Kumite. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Karate Stance, Kihon, and Kata into siblings, then return here after class to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for kicks, joint stress in deep stances, and impact training.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “stance,” and “power.” Martial Index’s Karate entries keep ushiro-geri scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Boxing or BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on ushiro-geri, one situational kumite theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Heel strike?

Typically yes for ushiro-geri.

Ushiro vs spinning hook?

Different paths—ushiro is back linear thrust; do not mix names casually.

Safe learning tip?

Slow sighting + pad, then speed.

Used in sport?

Less common than mawashi; still valuable.

Related?

Yoko-Geri for hip lessons.

The Bottom Line

Sight, chamber, heel drive—keep Ushiro-Geri honest so it is a weapon, not a blind spin.
