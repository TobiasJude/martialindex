Full Nelson

A full nelson locks both arms under the armpits to the neck—usually illegal in Folkstyle Wrestling; use the Half Nelson instead.

  • Illegal in folkstyle.
  • Use half nelson / power half.
  • Know penalties.

What Is Full Nelson?

The full nelson is a classical wrestling hold: both of your arms go under the opponent’s armpits and hands meet on the neck/head.

In modern Folkstyle Wrestling (NFHS/NCAA), the full nelson is illegal because of neck danger.

The legal sibling is the Half Nelson (and Power Half)—one arm under, hand on the head.

Martial Index covers the term so searchers understand legality—not so they hunt illegal pins.

Martial Index files Full Nelson under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Full Nelson behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Full Nelson Works

Historically and in some exhibition/catch contexts, the full nelson was used to force the head down and shoulders toward the mat.

In legal amateur folkstyle today, if you find yourself with both arms under, release and convert to a half nelson or another legal Pinning Combination.

Greco/freestyle have their own illegal-hold lists—never assume a catch-wrestling highlight is legal in your bout.

Coaches teach recognition: know it to avoid it, and to call it when an opponent drifts illegal.

Mechanically, successful full nelson work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, full nelson is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how full nelson is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of full nelson either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Always verify current NFHS/NCAA illegal-hold language for your season.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Youth wrestler accidentally double-unders—referee stops and penalizes; coach shows the half nelson fix.

Archive photos show old catch holds that would draw illegal calls today.

Match picture: full nelson appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply full nelson after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Copying old catch footage into folkstyle duals.
  • “Just a little full nelson” to finish a pin.
  • Not knowing the penalty progression.
  • Confusing body locks with nelsons.
  • Treating full nelson as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle full nelson inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so full nelson connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether full nelson appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe full nelson as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of full nelson stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Freestyle Wrestling, Folkstyle Wrestling, and Wrestling Stance into siblings, then return here after live rounds to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “ride,” “tilt,” and “headlock.” Martial Index’s Wrestling entries keep full nelson scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language—compare pages are labeled when useful.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on full nelson, one situational live theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing or hip position failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for full nelson under your next event (UWW freestyle, NCAA folkstyle, or Greco) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is the full nelson illegal?

In folkstyle scholastic/collegiate rules, yes—typically.

What should I use instead?

Half Nelson or Power Half.

Why illegal?

Neck safety.

Is it illegal in freestyle/Greco?

Check current UWW illegal-hold lists—do not assume.

Penalty?

Illegal hold penalties escalate—know your rule book.

Why have a page?

So searchers learn legality and the legal alternative.

The Bottom Line

Learn the full nelson as a boundary—train the half nelson as the craft.
