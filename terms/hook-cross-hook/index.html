Hook-Cross-Hook

Lead Hook, Cross, lead hook again—mid-range Combination rhythm that attacks around a high guard.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Mid-range tool.
  • Cross reloads the second hook.
  • Tight arcs, high opposite hand.

What Is Hook-Cross-Hook?

Unlike jab-first strings, this opens with a Lead Hook, so you need Boxing Range that already supports hooks—not long jab range.

The middle Cross re-centers the Power Line so the second hook is not a floppy arm punch.

Parent: Combination. Compare with Lead Hook and Lead Hook Cross.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Hook-Cross-Hook behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Hook-Cross-Hook Works

Start with a tight Short Hook or mid Hook. Rotate into the Cross. Reload the lead hook immediately.

Keep elbows honest—hooks that start as long swings get timed. Rear hand returns on each lead hook.

Pair with Bob and Weave entries or after a Slip so you are not hooking into a ready cross.

Mechanically, successful work with Hook-Cross-Hook is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, hook-cross-hook lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill hook-cross-hook daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Inside: after a clinch break, hook-cross-hook before they reset distance.

Bag call: “three-two-three” in number talk (Boxing Number System).

Fight picture: hook-cross-hook appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply hook-cross-hook after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Common Mistakes

  • Starting the combo from jab range.
  • Wide baseball hooks.
  • No cross—just three arm hooks.
  • Treating hook-cross-hook as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox hook-cross-hook inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so hook-cross-hook connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether hook-cross-hook appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe hook-cross-hook as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put hook-cross-hook behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep hook-cross-hook scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on hook-cross-hook, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for hook-cross-hook under your next card (glove size, round length, amateur vs pro judging) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

When not to throw it?

When you are too far—start with a jab or close range first.

Numbers?

Often 3-2-3 in the standard system.

Southpaw?

Mirror: lead (right) hook, rear (left) cross, lead hook.

The Bottom Line

If you can already hook, hook-cross-hook teaches you to straighten the middle without losing whip.
