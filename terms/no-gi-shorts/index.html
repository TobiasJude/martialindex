No-Gi Shorts

No-gi shorts are grappling shorts without dangerous pockets or hardware—standard lower-body wear for no-gi training, often over spats.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Choose shorts without pockets, zippers, or metal that can injure partners.
  • Length and logo rules vary by gym and event—ask before fight week.
  • Often worn over spats for coverage.
  • Different from board shorts with open pockets.
  • Verify competition attire in the current event guide / federation notes.

What Are No-Gi Shorts?

No-gi shorts are athletic shorts designed for grappling: secure fit, minimal hardware, and cuts that allow guard play without trapping fingers in pockets.

How It Works

Athletes wear them with a rashguard (and often spats). Coaches may refuse unsafe street shorts. Competition packets specify allowed lengths and markings.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi classes use kimono pants; no-gi classes use these shorts. Do not mix metal-laden gym shorts into live training.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You change into grappling shorts, check for drawstring safety, and decline a friend’s cargo shorts invitation to open mat.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Vale tudo-style shorts; longer board-short grappling cuts; compression shorts.

Gym / sport variants

Some rooms mandate shorts over spats; some allow spats-only. Events differ—read the guide.

Beginner notes

Buy one durable pair without pockets. See first BJJ class attire tips.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Double-knot drawstrings tucked. Wash after every session. Keep a competition pair clean.

How It Compares

Complement to spats; contrast with gi pants and with IBJJF gi measurement culture.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

High-level: attire rules are published per event/federation—verify current IBJJF / ADCC attire notes rather than inventing a complete gear list here.

Safety

No zippers, no metal eyelets that cut, no open pockets that catch toes/fingers.

Common mistakes

Wearing pocketed gym shorts; overly baggy lengths that trap grips illegally or unsafely; unwashed gear.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Can I wear regular gym shorts?

Only if they have no dangerous pockets/hardware—grappling shorts are safer.

Shorts over spats?

Common requirement—ask your gym.

Competition length rules?

Event-specific—verify the attire guide.

Gi pants instead?

For gi class yes; for no-gi use no-gi attire.

Beginner recommendation?

One durable pocketless pair + rashguard.

Related pages?

Spats and no-gi hub.

Safety non-negotiable?

No metal, no open pockets that catch fingers/toes.

The Bottom Line

Train no-gi in shorts that cannot injure your partners—then confirm event attire before competition day.
