What Is Knifehand Strike (Sonnal)?

Sonnal (손날) is the knifehand. A strike (chigi) uses the ulnar edge, thumb tucked, fingers together.

Neck-line versions (sonnal mok chigi) appear in patterns and self-defense. This page is the strike, not every knifehand block.

WT Kyorugi (Sparring) will not build your medal on knifehands. Poomsae and ITF Taekwondo will still grade them.

Compare with Jirugi (Punch) and Elbow Strike (Palkup) for range: punch long, knifehand mid, elbow close.

How Knifehand Strike (Sonnal) Works

Form the blade: fingers glued, thumb off the highway, wrist slightly aligned—not a floppy chop.

Chamber across the body, rotate the hips, and strike on a downward or inward diagonal per the pattern.

Leave a hint of elbow bend. Locked elbows hyperextend.

In Dwi Kubi (Back Stance) the knifehand often pairs with a block on the other arm—do not let the stance die to “reach.”

Mechanically, successful knifehand strike (sonnal) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, knifehand strike (sonnal) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill knifehand strike (sonnal) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Poomsae: knifehand neck strike after a stance change.

Pad: a soft target at neck/collarbone height, slow then sharp.

Fight picture: knifehand strike (sonnal) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply knifehand strike (sonnal) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Fingers splayed.
  • Thumb sticking out.
  • Hitting with the palm or fingers.
  • A huge telegraphed windmill.
  • Treating every sonnal makki as this strike.
  • Treating knifehand strike (sonnal) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick knifehand strike (sonnal) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so knifehand strike (sonnal) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether knifehand strike (sonnal) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe knifehand strike (sonnal) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put knifehand strike (sonnal) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Strike vs block?

This page is the strike. Knifehand blocks are related shapes with a different job.

Sonnal vs jirugi?

Jirugi (Punch) uses knuckles. Sonnal uses the blade.

Olympic?

Not a kyorugi staple. Learn it for Poomsae and self-defense literacy.

ITF?

Yes—knifehand attacks appear in tul and sparring menus.
