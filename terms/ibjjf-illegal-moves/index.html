IBJJF Illegal Moves

IBJJF illegal moves are techniques restricted or banned under International Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Federation competition rules—often by belt. This page is high-level only; verify the current IBJJF Rules (and compare ADCC Rules when relevant).

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Legality is belt-, age-, and ruleset-dependent—not universal gym lore.
  • Some attacks legal in ADCC-style events are restricted under IBJJF at lower belts (and vice versa contexts differ).
  • Martial Index does not publish a complete illegal catalog here—lists go stale and get people DQ’d.
  • Always read the current official rulebook before competing.
  • Related: disqualification, penalties, heel-hook culture vs federation rules.

What Are IBJJF Illegal Moves?

Under IBJJF competition, certain techniques are illegal outright or illegal until a given belt/age. The phrase “IBJJF illegal moves” is shorthand athletes use for that restricted set. Because the federation updates guidance, Martial Index keeps this entry conceptual rather than listing every technique as if complete.

How It Works

Referees enforce the published book: illegal attacks can draw penalties or disqualification depending on severity and context. Coaches and athletes must study the current PDF/app guidance for their division—not a friend’s summary from 2019.

Gi vs No-Gi

IBJJF gi and no-gi programs can differ in legal attack sets. ADCC and other promotions publish separate legality maps. Train the rulebook you will fight under.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

A lower-belt athlete starts a restricted leg entanglement in an IBJJF gi match; the referee stops the action under the current guidance—study ahead so your A-game is legal.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Belt-gated submissions; uniform/conduct fouls adjacent to “illegal”; drastic fouls vs minor.

Gym / sport variants

Gym sparring legality ≠ tournament legality. Agree training rules separately from competition rules.

Beginner notes

Before your first IBJJF entry, sit with a coach and the current book. Do not learn legality from highlight clips alone.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Print or save the current illegal/technical guidance for your belt. Drill legal chains. Tell your corner your legal A-game.

How It Compares

Different from penalties for stalling or lack of combativeness, though both are referee tools. Different from ADCC legality culture—cross-check ADCC Rules.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Verify current rulebook. Do not treat this encyclopedia page as a complete illegal list or point table. Official source: current IBJJF Rules documentation for your season.

Safety

Restricted techniques are often restricted for injury risk at developmental ranks. Respect gym consent even when a move is “legal somewhere.”

Common mistakes

Memorizing outdated lists; applying ADCC legality in IBJJF; arguing with the referee mid-match instead of adapting.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is there one universal illegal list?

No—legality depends on organization, belt, and the current published book.

Where should I verify?

Current IBJJF official rules for your event—plus the athlete guide.

Are heel hooks always illegal in IBJJF?

Treat heel-hook and other leg attacks as belt/ruleset-gated—verify the current book rather than gym myth.

IBJJF vs ADCC?

Different legality maps—read both if you compete in both.

Does this page list every illegal move?

No—intentionally high-level to avoid stale complete lists.

What if I hit an illegal move?

Referee may penalize or DQ depending on context—see disqualification.

Training tip?

Agree sparring rules separately; compete only with legal chains for that book.

The Bottom Line

Treat IBJJF illegal moves as a living official document problem: verify the current rulebook, then build a legal A-game.
