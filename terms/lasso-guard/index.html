Lasso Guard

Lasso guard is a gi open-guard entanglement that wraps your leg around the opponent's sleeved arm—stickier than spider, excellent against grip fighters, and not available in true form without a jacket.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Sleeve grip + leg wrap around the arm = lasso.
  • Stickier than spider; great when they fight grips.
  • Gi-only in its classic form.
  • Shallow and deep lasso change control vs mobility.
  • Naturally pairs with spider guard.

What Is Lasso Guard?

Lasso guard is a gi open guard where you grip a sleeve and wrap your same-side (or opposite) leg around that arm so the foot hooks behind their triceps/back—lassoing the arm. It is stickier than spider and famous for shutting down aggressive passers who try to free a hand.

How Lasso Works

The wrapped leg controls their arm and posture while your other foot frames hip or bicep. You can sweep by off-balancing toward the lasso side, attack omoplatas, or transition into spider-lasso hybrids. Shallow vs deep lasso changes how hard they can posture and how available certain sweeps are.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lasso is at home. Sleeve grip is required for the classic wrap.

No-gi. Without sleeves, true lasso disappears. Some athletes mimic with overhooks and leg wraps, but it is a different guard. Train lasso for gi sport; do not force it in no-gi.

What Does Lasso Look Like in Practice?

From spider, Jordan circles a hand. Alex keeps the sleeve, threads the leg into a lasso, frames the far hip, and sweeps Jordan into side control—or attacks an omoplata when Jordan posts.

Types / Variations

Deep lasso

Foot deep behind the shoulder/triceps—maximum control.

Shallow lasso

Higher on the arm—more mobility, different sweep timing.

Spider-lasso combo

One side each—modern gi tangle.

Reverse / special lassos

Less common wraps used by specialists.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. From spider when they circle; from collar-sleeve; deliberately pulling a sleeve into the wrap.

Passes. Long step, force grip breaks, stack carefully, backstep—clearing a deep lasso is a project.

Counters. Keep the sleeve at all costs; switch sides; go to worm or DLR if the lasso fails.

Lasso Guard vs Spider Guard

Factor Lasso Spider
Stickiness High Medium
Learning curve Steeper wrap mechanics More intuitive feet-on-biceps
Best when They fight sleeves aggressively You need posture break and mobility

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Lasso is legal in IBJJF gi. It is not a no-gi tool. Maintain active play—holding a dead lasso without advancing can look like stalling.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Do not crank shoulders with reckless lasso twists; apply sweeps and omoplatas with control.

Common mistakes

  • Losing the sleeve grip mid-lasso.
  • Soft wrap that they swim out of.
  • Forcing lasso concepts in no-gi.
  • Never using the free foot to frame.
  • Abandoning spider transitions too early.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is lasso guard usable in no-gi?

Classic lasso needs a sleeve. Skip it for no-gi and use overhooks, butterfly, or SLX instead.

Shallow or deep lasso?

Deep for maximum control; shallow when you need to move and sweep faster. Learn both.

How do I enter lasso from spider?

When they circle a hand, keep the sleeve and thread your leg around that arm while your other foot keeps a frame.

Why is my lasso getting passed easily?

You lost the sleeve or your free foot is idle. Re-grip and frame the hip; consider switching to spider-lasso.

Lasso vs worm guard?

Lasso wraps an arm; worm wraps a lapel around a leg. Both are gi puzzles—different problems for the passer.

What sweeps should I learn first?

Basic off-balance to the lasso side and the omoplata threat when they post. Add specialty sweeps later.

The Bottom Line

Lasso wraps a sleeved arm with your leg for sticky gi control and sweeps. Pair it with spider; for lapel-leg puzzles see worm guard.
