Arm-In Guillotine

The arm-in guillotine is a guillotine where their near arm stays inside the choking loop with the neck. It is easier to catch from shots and front headlock scrambles than a clean arm-out, and harder to finish without structure—hips, high elbow, and a tight near-arm squeeze.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Easier entry, more technical finish: the trapped arm fills one carotid side.
  • Do not pull the head into a crank; lift the elbow and squeeze the near arm to your ribs.
  • Closed or half guard kills their rotation—see guillotine from guard.
  • Sibling schools: high-elbow and marcelotine (usually arm-out).
  • Chains to darce / anaconda when they hide or roll.

What Is Arm-In Guillotine?

An arm-in guillotine traps the head and the near arm inside your choking arm. The trapped shoulder becomes part of the compression—similar in spirit to an arm-triangle—so you do not need a perfectly empty neck to start the attack.

That is why wrestlers and no-gi players catch it constantly on singles and body locks: the shooter’s arm is already there. The tradeoff is finish quality. A lazy squeeze becomes a painful neck crank; a structured one is a fast blood choke.

Martial Index files it under the parent guillotine and the front-headlock position so both “arm in guillotine” and “guillotine on a shot” land here.

How Arm-In Guillotine Works

Wrap the neck so the near arm is pinched to your ribcage. If that arm is loose, the choke leaks. Connect a gable, chin-strap-to-high-elbow, or support grip that lets you lift rather than yank.

Fall to the choking-arm hip, close closed or half guard, and crunch. Drive your wrist through the neck line while the trapped arm stays glued—hips extend, elbow rises.

If you end up on top, the finish is usually worse (poor hip angle). Roll or sit to guard rather than arching from mount unless you have a one-arm finish you actually own.

When they extract the arm, you may already have a high-elbow / marcelotine. When they roll, hunt darce or anaconda instead of restarting a dead chin strap.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Still fully viable; cloth just adds extra frames. Some players mix a collar grip on the support hand.

No-gi. The default shot-counter guillotine. Wrist control and hip angle decide everything.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex stuffs a single, sits to closed guard with the arm in, and finishes by crunching the near arm tight instead of ripping the head.

Jordan cannot finish on top, rolls to half guard, and the same grip suddenly bites.

Types / Variations

Shot / wrestle-up arm-in

They change levels; you catch neck plus the attacking arm.

Guard arm-in

They drive a pass or hug into closed/half; you close the legs.

Arm-in to arm-out conversion

If they extract, convert to high elbow rather than resetting.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: singles, doubles, body-lock shots, and low passes into guard. Counters: hide the chin, extract the near arm, rotate toward the choking side. Escapes: peel the grip and circle before the legs close; once locked, tap to the choke—not the crank.

Arm-In vs High-Elbow Guillotine

Factor Arm-in High-elbow / arm-out
Catch difficulty Easier on shots Needs a cleaner neck
Finish difficulty More technical Cleaner blood choke if locked
Crank risk Higher if sloppy Lower when elbow is truly high
Common platform Half / closed after a shot Front headlock or guard sit-up

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult guillotines are standard legal chokes; crank-only applications can be treated differently. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Arm-in finishes can flex the neck laterally. Drill the near-arm squeeze and high-elbow direction at low intensity. Tap early.

Common mistakes

  • Ripping the head toward your chest (crank).
  • Leaving the near arm loose against your ribs.
  • Trying to finish from a square top position with no hip angle.
  • Forgetting to close guard so they roll out.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why is arm-in harder to finish?

The extra arm changes the angle. You must squeeze that arm and lift the elbow, not just curl the biceps.

Is it a blood choke?

It can be, when the trapped shoulder and forearm compress the carotids. A bad one is mostly crank.

Should I prefer arm-out?

If you can get the arm out, high-elbow is often cleaner. Take the arm-in when that is what the shot gives you.

Closed guard or half?

Closed is best against rotation; half is what you usually get on a shot.

Top or bottom finish?

Bottom (guard) is more reliable for most people.

Gi difference?

Little. The mechanics are no-gi native.

Next choke if they roll?

Darce or anaconda—stay in the front-headlock family.

The Bottom Line

Arm-in guillotine is the shot-catch school: trap the near arm, close the legs, lift the elbow. Compare high-elbow and the parent guillotine so you are not married to one grip.
