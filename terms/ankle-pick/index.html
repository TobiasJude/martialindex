Ankle Pick

An ankle pick breaks posture with upper-body control, then cups the heel/ankle to collapse the base—a low-risk wrestling borrow favored in BJJ, linked from the takedown hub and often chained with arm drags.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Upper body first—then the pick.
  • Pull the heel while driving the head/weight opposite.
  • Low risk vs deep doubles if you stay connected.
  • Fake pulls set ankle picks.
  • Gi collar + pant variants are common.

What Is Ankle Pick?

The ankle pick is kuzushi plus a hand on the foot: collar tie or sleeve control loads the lead leg, then the free hand cups the heel as you steer them over that foot. Unlike a deep double, your head often stays safer from guillotines.

Wrestling film study (Taylor/Snyder-type tie-ups) shows level-change timing; BJJ adds fake guard pulls that become picks when they step in.

Rules: completed picks that stabilize top may score under IBJJF norms—verify. ADCC values the ensuing control.

How Ankle Pick Works

Establish collar/sleeve or head control, load the lead leg, drop your level slightly, cup the heel, and pull while driving their torso past the base.

If they step back, follow to double or drag.

If they sprawl, keep the upper-body control and switch attacks.

Drill: static collar tie → pick → settle top, both sides.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar + pant cuff picks are high percentage.

No-Gi. Collar tie / wrist control + heel cup.

Both: do not reach for the ankle before the weight is on it.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex snaps a collar tie, feels Jordan’s weight on the lead foot, picks the heel, and follows to knee-cut.

Fake pull: Alex sits as if pulling; Jordan steps in; Alex comes up on the ankle.

Arm drag creates the angle; Alex picks the far ankle.

Types / Variations

Collar-tie ankle pick

Classic wrestling pattern.

Gi collar-and-pant

Fabric-assisted heel control.

Arm-drag to pick

Angle first, then far/near ankle.

Fake guard-pull pick

Sit bait into step-in.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: ties, snaps, drags, fake pulls.

Counters: keep weight off the lead foot, hop, stuff the head, peel the cup.

Escapes mid-pick: base on the free leg and circle.

Ankle Pick vs Single-Leg

Factor Ankle Pick Single-Leg
Depth Shallow Often deeper
Risk profile Often lower neck risk More sprawl/guillotine stories
Key timing Weight on the foot Capture then finish
Chain Pick → double / drag Single → double

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Scoring depends on event rules for takedown control. Confirm IBJJF/ADCC books. No invented point values beyond general sport literacy.

Safety

Finger jams in heel cups and unexpected dumps—control the landing. Tap to ankle torsional surprises in training.

Common mistakes

  • Grabbing the ankle before loading the leg.
  • Bending at the waist with no upper-body control.
  • Abandoning connection after a miss.
  • Picking the unweighted foot.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an ankle pick?

A takedown that collapses a loaded lead leg by cupping the heel while controlling the upper body.

Why do BJJ players like it?

Lower amplitude and often safer neck position than deep shots.

Gi setup?

Collar plus pant cuff is classic.

What if they step back?

Follow to another shot or drag.

Fake pull?

Sit bait can draw a step-in for the pick.

Main mistake?

Reaching for an unweighted foot.

Next?

Arm drag and single-leg pages.

The Bottom Line

Ankle picks win by timing, not by diving. Load the foot, cup the heel, and steer—then pass like you meant it.
