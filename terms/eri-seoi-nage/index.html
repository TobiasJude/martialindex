Eri Seoi Nage

Eri seoi nage is a seoi entry that emphasises eri (collar/lapel) control on the loading side—related to Ippon Seoi Nage and Morote Seoi Nage, not a separate Kodokan gokyo row.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Grip-emphasis variant under Seoi Nage (Judo).
  • Compare Ippon Seoi Nage (one-arm trap) and Morote Seoi Nage (sleeve+lapel).
  • Needs forward Kuzushi like all seoi.
  • Gi-dependent—eri means cloth.
  • Chain to Kouchi Gari when posted.
  • Follow to Ne-Waza pins.

What is Eri Seoi Nage?

Eri seoi nage is coaching and competition language for a shoulder-throw entry that keeps strong eri (collar/lapel) involvement on the loading side rather than relying only on a deep one-arm trap or a balanced sleeve-and-lapel morote. It is not a separate classical gokyo title with its own Kodokan row; Martial Index files it as a living grip-emphasis child of the Seoi Nage (Judo) hub so searchers can find mechanics without inventing fake catalogue numbers.

Compare constantly with Ippon Seoi Nage, where the throwing arm typically slots under uke’s armpit, and with Morote Seoi Nage, where both hands stay on cloth in sleeve-and-lapel roles. Eri-seoi conversations usually start when athletes ask, “Can I keep the lapel and still throw seoi?”—yes, with trade-offs in trapping power and steering.

BJJ readers may recognise collar-heavy seoi patterns on the BJJ-labelled seoi-nage page; keep art labels honest. This article stays on judo: Judogi rules, IJF scoring, and Tachi-Waza entry culture on the judo hub.

Because eri means collar, the throw is inherently gi-shaped. No-gi approximations exist as head-and-arm or underhook turns, but they are not “eri” in any literal sense—do not force the Japanese grip name onto bare skin.

How it works

Win a usable lapel (or cross-lapel) while the hikite sleeve still creates forward Kuzushi. The loading turn looks like other seoi: hips under, elbows in, back as platform. The eri hand steers and lifts; it should not become a decorative hold while the body turns empty.

Trade-offs versus ippon-seoi: less complete arm trap, more continuous cloth steering, sometimes easier to re-grip if the first turn fails. Trade-offs versus morote: the eri emphasis may free the sleeve hand for a different pull angle—or keep both hands high on cloth depending on the gym’s definition. Ask your coach which local meaning they use; Martial Index describes the shared idea, not every club dialect.

If uke posts, do not crank the collar into a neck crank disguised as nage-waza. Switch to Kouchi Gari, Ouchi Gari, or drop into coached Seoi Otoshi / Drop Seoi Nage patterns. Scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude rules.

On partial throws, keep chest contact for Osaekomi. Collar control that survives the fall can also set sliding-collar strangle conversations in ne-waza—without confusing a throw page for a choke curriculum.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Eri-seoi is a Judogi story. Lapel thickness, jacket fit, and legal gripping heights all matter. Grips that stall without attacking draw shido under modern Judo Rules—eri control is a means to kuzushi and tsukuri, not a parking brake.

No-gi: keep the seoi geometry if you wish, but rename the tool honestly (arm wrap, head position, underhook). Transfers: timing and hip depth. Does not transfer: the word eri.

What it looks like in practice

In Randori, eri-seoi shows up in grip fights where tori refuses to release a hard-won lapel. You see a short snap, a turn that keeps the collar, and either a standing finish or a drop. Against stiff postures, the eri hand often becomes the difference between a dead turn and a steered load.

After landing, hunt Kesa Gatame, Yoko Shiho Gatame, or Kami Shiho Gatame. See Seoi to Osaekomi.

Uchi-komi should include re-gripping under motion—static collar holds without footwork teach the wrong lesson.

Types / variations

Lapel-heavy morote shape

Both hands on cloth with eri emphasis on the tsurite—close to morote with a higher or tighter collar story.

Collar retained into ippon-like turn

Athletes keep eri involvement while approximating one-arm loading—clarify with your coach whether this is still “eri-seoi” locally.

Eri into drop finish

Same grip story with knees down—cross-read Drop Seoi Nage and Seoi Otoshi.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter on forward weight created by sleeve snap, lapel lift, or a foot reap that loads the toes. Counters: peel the lapel, sprawl, circle off. Combinations: eri threat → Tai Otoshi; eri seoi → kouchi when uke steps wide.

Moving Uchi-Komi beats collar-only isometric holding. If the grip fight lasts longer than the attack intent, you are stalling, not setting.

Compare

Versus Ippon Seoi Nage: one-arm trap under the armpit versus collar-emphasized loading. Versus Morote Seoi Nage: balanced sleeve+lapel classic versus eri-weighted coaching language. Versus Reverse Seoi Nage: opposite-side modern entry rather than grip emphasis on the standard side.

IJF notes & safety

Eri-seoi as ordinary seoi-nage is legal when performed as controlled throwing. Illegal are dangerous neck actions disguised as grips, and gripping stalls without positive attacking purpose under Judo Rules. Cross-collar strangling is a separate ne-waza conversation—not a standing throw license.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Holding eri without kuzushi.
  • Turning while the collar hand goes slack.
  • Using the lapel as a neck crank.
  • Confusing club nicknames with Kodokan catalogue rows.
  • Stalling in grips instead of attacking.
  • Ignoring sleeve hikite entirely.

Training notes

Drill eri retention under moving partners, then immediately throw or release—no endless collar hugging. Pair with Morote Seoi Nage and Ippon Seoi Nage days so athletes feel grip trade-offs.

Film whether the lapel hand still steers after the turn begins. If not, you are wearing cloth, not throwing with it.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is eri-seoi a Kodokan gokyo throw?

Grip-emphasis language inside the seoi family—see Seoi Nage (Judo).

Eri vs ippon-seoi?

Collar emphasis vs one-arm trap—Ippon Seoi Nage.

Eri vs morote?

See Morote Seoi Nage.

Does it work no-gi?

Geometry can transfer; the eri name needs Judogi.

Common follow-up?

Osaekomi pins such as Kesa Gatame.

Illegal gripping?

See Judo Rules—stalls and dangerous neck actions are not “eri-seoi.”
