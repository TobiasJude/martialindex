Knee Pick

A knee pick snatches or blocks the lead knee from a tie—cousin of the Ankle Pick (Wrestling), sibling of trips.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Knee pick attacks the lead knee.
  • Collar-tie staple.
  • Needs level change.
  • Chain to ankle pick.
  • Finish in control.

What Is Knee Pick?

A knee pick is a takedown that targets the knee—grabbing, blocking, or lifting it while upper-body pressure forces the dump.

Common from Collar Tie hand-fighting when the lead leg is heavy.

Related to Ankle Pick (Wrestling) (lower) and to Inside Trip/Outside Trip (leg attacks).

In Wrestling, knee picks punish upright, reachy postures.

Martial Index files Knee Pick under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Knee Pick behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Knee Pick Works

Win a collar or wrist, change levels slightly, pick the lead knee, and drive through while circling the head/shoulder.

Do not bend solely at the waist—use a real Level Change.

If they hop the knee free, chain to an ankle pick or snap-down.

Finish with control; don’t leave yourself under a sprawling front head.

Mechanically, successful knee pick work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, knee pick is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how knee pick is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of knee pick either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Same takedown control standards as other shots.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Collar tie → knee pick the lead leg → dump for two.

Fake ankle pick; finish knee pick when they hop.

Match picture: knee pick appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply knee pick after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Waist bend only—get sprawled.
  • Picking without head pressure.
  • Telegraphing against elite sprawls.
  • Losing the tie mid-pick.
  • Treating knee pick as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle knee pick inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so knee pick connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether knee pick appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Collar live: only knee/ankle picks score.

Shadow level-change into pick.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe knee pick as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of knee pick stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Knee pick vs ankle pick?

Target height—knee vs ankle.

Freestyle useful?

Yes—common scoring attack.

Setup?

Collar Tie and hand-fighting.

Defense?

Light feet, sprawl, clear collar, punch knee back.

Related trips?

Inside/outside trips attack with your leg; knee pick uses the hand.

Beginner tip?

Level-change drills before live picks.

The Bottom Line

Treat the knee pick as a hand-fight score, not a dive—ties first.
