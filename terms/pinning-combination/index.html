Pinning Combination

A pinning combination is the turn toolkit—Cradle, Half Nelson, Power Half, and cousins that manufacture falls and Near Fall points.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Umbrella term for legal finishing turns.
  • Cradle and half nelson are pillars.
  • Purpose: fall or near fall.
  • Breakdown first, turn second.
  • Know what is illegal in your rule set.

What Is Pinning Combination?

Pinning combination is a coaching umbrella for legal turns that threaten a fall or create back points in Folkstyle Wrestling.

It is not one move; it is the set: Cradle, Half Nelson, Power Half, Crossface-to-cradle chains, and more.

Searchers want the concept that links rides to scores—why top wrestling exists beyond stalling.

In Wrestling, pinning-combination literacy is how folkstyle athletes convert control into dual-meet bonus.

Martial Index files Pinning Combination under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Pinning Combination behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Pinning Combination Works

Break the opponent down, remove posts, then apply a turn that drives a shoulder toward the mat while you keep hips heavy.

If the fall is there, finish; if not, hold the angle through the Near Fall count and re-ride.

Chain: Spiral Ride → Crossface → cradle or half nelson.

Always have an exit when the bridge is too strong—do not get reversed chasing glory.

Mechanically, successful pinning combination work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, pinning combination is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how pinning combination is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of pinning combination either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Illegal holds (e.g., full nelson in folkstyle) are not “just another combination.”

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Far-side cradle locks and the referee’s count runs into a fall.

Power half turns the near shoulder; you collect near-fall points when the pin stalls.

Match picture: pinning combination appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply pinning combination after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Turning before the breakdown is secure.
  • Leaving the far arm free to post.
  • No plan if the pin fails—losing the near fall too.
  • Illegal pressure (full nelson in folkstyle) when frustrated.
  • Treating pinning combination as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle pinning combination inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so pinning combination connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether pinning combination appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Top-only live with mandatory turn attempts each 30 seconds.

Referee-count drills for near-fall holds.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe pinning combination as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of pinning combination stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Freestyle Wrestling, Folkstyle Wrestling, and Wrestling Stance into siblings, then return here after live rounds to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “ride,” “tilt,” and “headlock.” Martial Index’s Wrestling entries keep pinning combination scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language—compare pages are labeled when useful.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on pinning combination, one situational live theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing or hip position failed.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is a cradle a pinning combination?

Yes—one of the flagship examples.

Do freestyle wrestlers say pinning combination?

Less often; they speak in exposure turns and throws.

Best beginner combination?

Tight waist breakdown to Half Nelson.

How does this relate to near fall?

Combinations create the exposure angles near falls require.

Is crossface a pinning combination?

Often a setup that becomes one when chained to a cradle or nelson.

Safety?

Coach-supervised turning only—neck and shoulders are involved.

The Bottom Line

Collect three pinning combinations you trust under fatigue—depth beats a highlight reel of one.
