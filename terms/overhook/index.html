BJJ · Concept

Overhook

An overhook (including the wrestling whizzer) wraps over the opponent’s arm/shoulder—fueling closed-guard triangles, omoplatas, and sweeps, and defending underhooks in clinch and half guard.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Closed-guard overhook opens triangle/omoplata highways.
  • Get an angle—do not stay flat under the overhook.
  • Whizzer counters underhooks on top/clinch.
  • Gi lapel assists are common; no-gi uses head frames.
  • Bridge to underhook and wrist control concepts.

What Is Overhook?

Overhooks control an arm from above, pinning it to your body or creating a whizzer curve that stops their underhook elevation. In closed guard, a tight overhook plus angle is a submission factory; in passing/clinch, the whizzer denies their underhook.

See triangle, omoplata, underhook.

Overhooks also appear in front-headlock battles and necktie setups when an arm is trapped high—another reason Batch 6 control concepts cross-link submissions.

How Overhook Works

Clamp the overhooked arm to your ribs, control their head or far arm with the free hand, and hip out to an angle before attacking.

As a whizzer: curve the arm, keep chest posture, and deny their head position.

If the overhook is loose, they will pummel free—retighten or switch controls.

Drill: closed-guard overhook to angle to triangle entry without finishing the choke—then reset. Add lapel in gi rounds; remove it in no-gi rounds.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Overhook + opposite lapel is a classic closed-guard pair.

No-Gi. Head and wrist frames replace lapels.

Same isolation idea.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Closed guard, Alex overhooks Jordan’s right arm, angles out, and climbs to triangle.

In half guard top, Alex whizzers Alex’s underhook attempt and crossfaces to flatten.

Standing, Alex whizzers a shot, forces a front headlock scramble, and chooses guillotine vs pass—overhook as the bridge, not the trophy.

Types / Variations

Closed-guard overhook

Attack platform.

Whizzer

Anti-underhook tool.

Clinch overhook

Takedown and front-headlock bridges.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Create via collar-drag style pulls, failed underhooks, and posture breaks.

Counters: pummel to underhook; straighten posture; peel the elbow.

Escapes: free the elbow and reconnect inside position.

Overhook vs Underhook

Factor Overhook Underhook
Arm path Over the arm Under the armpit
Guard offense High (triangles) Medium
Half-guard top Whizzer defense Bottom’s prize
Feel Arm isolation Shoulder elevation

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Universal legal control. Follow-on submissions follow normal legality.

Safety

Do not crank necks while “holding” overhooks with head control on injured partners.

Common mistakes

  • Flat overhook with no angle.
  • Loose clamp.
  • Forgetting the free-hand job.
  • Ignoring triangle when they posture into it.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an overhook?

Wrapping over their arm/shoulder to isolate or whizzer.

Best use from guard?

Angle + triangle/omoplata chains.

Whizzer means?

Overhook used to stop an underhook.

Gi tip?

Add opposite lapel.

No-gi tip?

Head frame + tight clamp.

Counter?

Pummel to underhook.

Related?

Underhook, wrist control, head control.

The Bottom Line

Overhook isolates the arm: clamp, angle, attack—or whizzer to kill their underhook. Keep triangle and underhook in the same study block.
