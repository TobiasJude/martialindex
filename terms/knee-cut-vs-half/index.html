Knee Cut vs Half Guard

Knee cut vs half guard is the battle between the knee-cut / knee-slice pass and bottom half guard (including knee shield). It sits under guard passing and often chains to the body-lock system.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Knee cut thrives when you control the bottom leg and clear frames.
  • Knee shield is the main roadblock—address it early.
  • Underhook vs crossface choices define the finish.
  • If smash fails, switch to body lock rather than forcing.
  • Sibling: knee-cut-pass and knee-slice pages.

What Is Knee Cut vs Half Guard?

This page is the matchup article: top knee-cut against half-guard retention—not a second clone of knee-cut-pass.

Half guard gives the bottom player an underhook race, knee shield, and deep-half exits. The passer must win legs first, then upper body.

Cross-link force half and back step as alternate answers.

How Knee Cut vs Half Guard Works

Pin the bottom thigh with your cutting knee/shin while controlling the top leg so it cannot rebuild a shield.

Win a crossface or underhook; keep them flat with chest pressure and frame clearing.

Vs knee shield: walk to the end of the knee, smash the hand, or abandon into body lock.

Finish by extracting the trapped foot and settling into side control—don’t leave a quarter-guard souvenir.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Crossface lapel and pant grips help pin the shield arm.

No-gi. Head position and underhook racing matter more; expect more body-lock switches.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex starts a knee cut, meets a strong shield, walks off the knee, and flattens half guard before extracting the foot.

Jordan loses the underhook race and switches to a low body lock instead of forcing the slice.

Types / Variations

Crossface knee cut

Classic smash path vs flat half.

Underhook knee cut

When crossface is framed away.

Abandon to body lock

Smart exit when shield + frames win—see body-lock system.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from headquarters, RDLR clears, and forced half. Counters: lockdown, deep half, butterfly half. Escapes for bottom: shrimp to recover full guard or take the back.

Knee Cut vs Body Lock Against Half

Factor Knee cut path Body lock path
Best when Shield manageable Shield + frames sticky
Upper-body tool Crossface / underhook Hands connected around torso
Risk Underhook loss / lockdown Balance / guillotine threats
Pillar Guard passing Guard passing

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Half-guard top is not a scored pass until the legs are cleared and the position stabilizes—verify current criteria.

Safety

Don’t crank necks in the crossface; clear frames patiently.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting without controlling the top leg.
  • Ignoring the knee shield.
  • No abandon plan to body lock.
  • Leaving the foot trapped in quarter guard.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why does my knee cut fail in half guard?

Usually an unchecked knee shield or lost underhook race.

Is knee cut the same as knee slice?

Same family—see both live pages for naming.

When do I switch to body lock?

When frames and shield make the slice high-risk.

Gi tip?

Use lapel crossface to pin the shield hand.

No-gi tip?

Win head position early; be ready to connect hands.

Bottom player’s best counter?

Knee shield, underhook, and deep-half exits.

Related pillar?

Guard passing, with half guard as the opposing pillar.

The Bottom Line

Knee cut vs half is a matchup: control the shield, win the upper body, or abandon wisely into the body-lock system.
