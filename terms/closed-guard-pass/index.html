Closed Guard Pass

Closed guard pass is the Martial Index category hub for getting past closed guard: open the ankles, then choose a named pass—stack, double-under, single-under, standing, knee-cut, toreando, and more.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Step 1 is almost always breaking / opening closed guard with posture.
  • Step 2 is a named pass—do not invent a vague smash.
  • Pressure family: stack, double-under, single-under, over-under, smash.
  • Speed family: standing, toreando, bullfighter.
  • Knee-cut family often starts the moment the guard opens—see knee-cut and HQ.

What Is Closed Guard Pass?

This page is an Investopedia-style category hub, not a single technique. Closed guard pass means the whole problem of leaving closed guard for a pin. You almost always (1) open the ankles with posture—see breaking closed guard—then (2) pick a named pass. Parent pillar: closed guard. Concept umbrella: guard passing, pressure passing, modern passing.

How Passing Works Here

Win posture (head up, elbows in), create hip distance, wedge a knee or stand to unlock the feet, immediately control both legs, then branch: stack/pressure if they stay supine and heavy; circle/toreando if they open light; knee-cut if you can split the thigh line. Stabilize side control or mount before hunting submissions.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar/sleeve fights dominate opening. Classic pressure: stack, double-under, Saulo pass. Standing opens into toreando.

No-gi. Same opening logic with underhooks and head position; body-lock and knee-cut metas are huge—see body lock and knee-cut.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Inside closed → posture → open → double-under stack to side, or stand into toreando, or split into knee-cut toward mount (knee-cut to mount).

Named Pass Menu

Pressure / stack family

Pressure / stack family (live pages): stack pass, double-under pass, single-under pass, over-under pass, smash pass, Saulo pass, body-lock pass.

Speed / circling family

Speed / circling family: standing pass, toreando pass, bullfighter pass, cartwheel pass, jumping pass, X pass.

Knee-cut / HQ family

Knee-cut / HQ family: after the ankles open, knee-cut pass, HQ pass, headquarters, leg drag, company pass.

When to Choose Which Pass

Choose pressure when they are heavy and supine; choose standing/toreando when they frame light; choose knee-cut when you can win the thigh line. If they recover half, switch to the half guard pass hub. If they open fully, see open guard pass.

How This Hub Compares

This hub organizes closed-guard exits. Technique depth lives on child pages. Vs open hub: closed always starts with an opening problem. Vs half hub: half is often the intentional or accidental next station.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Opening and passing closed is foundational IBJJF/ADCC skill; verify illegal neck cranks and youth rules on IBJJF rules / ADCC rules.

Safety

Open with control—do not blast knees into their tailbone recklessly; stack without cervical dumping.

Common mistakes

  • Trying to pass before the guard is open.
  • Leaving elbows wide (armbar/triangle food).
  • No named follow-up after the ankles unlock.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a closed guard pass?

The category of opening closed guard and finishing with a named pass to a pin—not one single move.

What should I learn first?

Posture + breaking closed guard, then stack/double-under and a standing pass.

Knee-cut from closed?

Usually after the ankles open—then knee-cut becomes available.

Gi or no-gi differences?

Gi emphasizes collar/sleeve; no-gi emphasizes underhooks and body locks.

Common mistake?

Diving into a pass while their feet are still locked.

How does this hub relate to other hubs?

Closed → often half or open; use those hubs when the position changes.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Under closed guard as the pass category hub with dense child links.

The Bottom Line

Treat closed guard pass as a map: open safely, then click into a named child—stack, double-under, single-under, standing, knee-cut, toreando—instead of a vague smash.
