Berimbolo

The berimbolo is a modern De La Riva-driven sweep/back-take that inverts under a standing passer, spins to expose their back, and finishes in back control—popularized by the Mendes brothers and still central to gi open-guard games.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Berimbolo = invert + spin under from DLR-family control to take the back.
  • Grip discipline (belt/pants/sleeves) and head placement protect the neck during the roll.
  • Gi is the classic habitat; no-gi adaptations exist but differ.
  • Sibling: kiss of the dragon from reverse De La Riva.
  • End product is a back take—train seatbelt/hooks immediately after.

What Is Berimbolo?

The berimbolo transformed sport BJJ guard by making inversion a structured path to the back rather than a circus move. From De La Riva (or related open guards), you invert, keep critical grips, and roll through so the passer’s hips turn away—leaving you attached to the back.

Martial Index nests it under open guard with heavy links to DLR, reverse DLR, and back-take literacy. It is a sweep in scoring language and a back-take engine in strategic language.

How Berimbolo Works

Establish DLR hook and dominant grips (often pants + sleeve/belt in gi). Off-balance the passer forward/up, invert safely (shoulder/hip awareness), and spin under while keeping the far grip that prevents them from free-turning to face you.

As you come out behind, seat the seatbelt and insert hooks—do not celebrate the spin before upper-body control.

If they sit or counter-spin, chain to knee-bar angles, 50/50, or secondary bollos rather than forcing a broken roll.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Classic Mendes-era berimbolo with cloth grips—highest percentage habitat.

No-gi. Harder without pants/belt; athletes adapt with wrist control, body triangles later, or prefer other back takes.

Types / Variations

Standard DLR berimbolo

Textbook invert-to-back from De La Riva.

Waiter’s / variation bollos

Grip and angle variants for different passer reactions.

Counter berimbolo chains

When they sit, smash, or reverse-roll.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: DLR, some collar-sleeve transitions. Counters: free the trapped grip, step over carefully, force them to 50/50, or knee-cut before the invert. Escapes (for the passer): keep hips heavy facing them, peel hooks early, reclaim DLR defense posture.

Berimbolo vs Kiss of the Dragon

Factor Berimbolo Kiss of the Dragon
Typical start De La Riva Reverse De La Riva
Motion Invert spin under Invert / slide-under back exposure
Gi dependency High High (also drilled no-gi)
Shared end Back take Back take

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal sport technique when performed without illegal spinal/neck pressure. Inversion itself is not banned—dangerous landings can still draw stoppages. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Protect the neck during inversions; drill progressive rolling on mats with coaching. Never yank partners onto their heads.

Common mistakes

  • Inverting without grips—neck first.
  • Spinning then forgetting seatbelt/hooks.
  • Forcing bolo against a stacked passer with no off-balance.
  • Ignoring kiss-of-the-dragon as a sibling tool.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does berimbolo mean?

Brazilian slang vibe—“scramble/roll” energy—now a technical proper noun.

Is it only gi?

Classic in gi; no-gi versions exist but are less grip-rich.

Points or back take?

Often scores as sweep then back—verify scoring; strategically it is a back engine.

Hard on the neck?

It can be—learn head placement and progress slowly.

Mendes vs Miyao?

Both lineages shaped modern bolo culture—study multiple sources.

Vs kiss of the dragon?

Different preferred starting guards; same family of invert-to-back ideas.

Beginner ready?

After solid DLR and basic inversion drills with a coach.

The Bottom Line

Berimbolo is structured inversion from De La Riva to the back. Win grips, protect the neck, finish with seatbelt and hooks—and keep kiss of the dragon in the same folder.
