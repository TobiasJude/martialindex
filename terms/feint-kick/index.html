What Is Feint Kick?

A feint kick is a deliberately incomplete kick cue: Chamber (Bal Deung), hip, eyes, or Skip Step without the full strike—or with a tiny tap that is not the real attack.

The job is to move the opponent’s guard, stance, or timing. The payoff is usually a different kick, a Cut Angle (Kyorugi), or a Counter Kick.

This is not the Boxing feint encyclopedia page. The Boxing term lives at a different slug; do not merge them.

In PSS Kyorugi (Sparring), a feint that accidentally slaps the Hogu may or may not beep—and may leave you standing on one leg.

Martial Index files Feint Kick under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Feint Kick behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Feint Kick Works

Show a believable Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) or Cut Kick (Taekwondo) chamber, then change height, leg, or direction.

Keep the Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) alive so you can still fire or flee.

Hands stay in a real guard. A feint with both arms down is just a pose.

If they do not buy it, do not feint twice in a row from the same picture.

Mechanically, successful feint kick work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, feint kick is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill feint kick daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Lead-leg body feint, rear Head Kick (Olgul Chagi).

Skip as if cutting, then Sliding Step the other way and score the open side.

Fight picture: feint kick appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply feint kick after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Chamber feint

Lift the knee for Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) or Yeop Chagi (Side Kick), then change.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Skip / cut feint

Sell Skip Step or Cut Kick (Taekwondo), then exit or switch.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Height feint

Show body, go head—or the reverse.

Common Mistakes

  • Feinting with a chamber nobody believes.
  • Feinting every kick so nothing is real.
  • Standing frozen after the fake.
  • Linking this page to Boxing feint as if they were one article.
  • Accidental slaps that wreck your balance.
  • Treating feint kick as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick feint kick inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so feint kick connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether feint kick appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe feint kick as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put feint kick behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep feint kick scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on feint kick, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for feint kick under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related reading inside this batch should feel like a cluster, not a random walk: return to Chamber (Bal Deung) and Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) when mechanics fail, and to Kyorugi (Sparring) when techniques “stop working” for mysterious reasons.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is this the Boxing feint?

No. Boxing feint is a different term page. This slug is Taekwondo’s kick fake.

Feint vs combination?

A feint is the lie. Kick Combination is the sentence that may include the lie.

Do feints score?

Only if contact meets Electronic Scoring (PSS). Most feints should not be scored attempts.

Lead-leg feints?

Common because Lead-Leg Kicking chambers are cheap to show.

ITF?

ITF Taekwondo sparring also uses fakes; sine-wave timing changes how obvious a chamber looks.

The Bottom Line

Feint only what you can actually throw—otherwise athletes stop believing you.
