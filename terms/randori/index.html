Randori

Randori is judo’s free practice: live gripping, throwing, and groundwork with a partner under dojo constraints, distinct from kata and formal shiai.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Free practice ≠ street fight.
  • Needs solid Ukemi.
  • Constraints create skill.
  • Gi and no-gi both useful.
  • Follows Uchi-Komi.
  • Culture matters as much as intensity.

What is randori?

Randori (乱取り) is free practice—open attacking and defending with a partner under dojo constraints, not a scripted drill.

Kano distinguished it from shiai (contest) and kata (forms). It is where Uchi-Komi skills meet chaos.

r/judo beginners ask how hard to go; the honest answer is: hard enough to learn, safe enough to return tomorrow—mediated by Ukemi and culture.

How it works

Agree intensity, then fight for grips, Kuzushi, throws, and Ne-Waza without needing a referee—unless your club runs shiai-style randori.

Goals vary: only ashi-waza rounds, only follow-ups to pins, left-side only. Constraints create skill.

Randori without a technical aim becomes scramble theatre. Carry one tokui theme from Nage-Waza.

Judogi vs no-gi

Gi randori teaches lapel-sleeve wars. No-gi randori teaches underhooks and head position. Both belong if your targets include BJJ/MMA.

In practice

After uchi-komi on Seoi Nage (Judo), partners do three-minute randori where seoi attempts earn praise even when they fail, as long as Kuzushi was real.

Types / variations

Light technical randori; competitive randori; newaza-only; tachiwaza-only; open open.

Etiquette & programming

Bow, communicate injuries, protect partners’ ukemi.

Program hard days and technical days so nervous systems recover.

Compare with uchi-komi

Uchi-Komi repeats entries; Randori tests them against a moving, thinking partner.

Safety

No randori without reliable Ukemi.

Tap early; avoid hero landings; coaches should stop unsafe mismatches.

Common mistakes

  • Going 100% with beginners.
  • Never doing newaza in randori.
  • Using randori to bully instead of learn.
  • Skipping bow/communication.

Randori is a laboratory. Treat each round as a question: Can I get my grip? Can I create kuzushi on the strong side? Can I follow a throw into a pin without pausing to admire myself?

Intensity ladders help mixed-level rooms: technical randori where anyone may freeze to fix a foot; medium randori with continuous movement; hard randori with coaches watching for unsafe mismatches. Ego ladders help nobody.

Newaza randori deserves scheduled minutes. If every round ends the moment someone touches a knee, your osaekomi and shime will never mature. Agree on “stay down until tap, pin escape, or coach call.”

Cross-training athletes should label rounds: “IJF gripping rules tonight” versus “no-gi clinch into throw.” Mixing unspoken rule sets causes conflicts that look like personality problems but are actually contract problems.

Recovery is part of the skill. If your ukemi degrades, you end the session—even if your cardio wants one more round. Read ukemi and uchi-komi as the bookends of sustainable randori.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How hard?

Learn tomorrow’s lesson—ask your coach.

Newaza allowed?

Usually yes; do not skip it.

Beginners?

After basic Ukemi and a few throws.

Vs shiai?

No official scoreboard required.

No-gi randori?

Valuable for cross-training.

Tokui focus?

Yes—theme rounds.

Injuries?

Communicate; sit out.

Etiquette?

Bow, protect partners, tap early.

The Bottom Line

Earn Randori with Ukemi and Uchi-Komi, then hunt real Kuzushi under club culture.
