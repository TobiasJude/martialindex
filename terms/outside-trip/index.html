Outside Trip

An outside trip blocks the outside leg from a strong tie—Russian Tie and collar entries to the mat.

What Is Outside Trip?

An outside trip attacks the far/outside leg while you control the upper body or a Russian Tie/Collar Tie.

Cary Kolat–style teaching often pairs outside trips with two-on-one control.

Sibling of Inside Trip; cousin of Knee Pick.

In Wrestling, outside trips appear whenever feet get lazy in the pocket.

Martial Index files Outside Trip under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Outside Trip behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Outside Trip Works

Establish the tie, step to create angle, place your tripping leg outside theirs, and drive them over the blocked foot while pulling the tie.

Keep chest contact; a trip without the pull becomes a shin bump.

If they hop out, re-angle or switch to a throw-by/Slide-By.

Finish in control for the takedown award.

Mechanically, successful outside trip work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, outside trip is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how outside trip is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of outside trip either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Confirm leg-attack legality by style.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Russian tie → outside trip for two.

Collar snap into outside trip when they step wide.

Match picture: outside trip appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply outside trip after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • No tie pull—only the foot.
  • Tripping into their underhook pressure.
  • Losing angle and getting knee-picked yourself.
  • Incomplete finish on the mat.
  • Treating outside trip as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle outside trip inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so outside trip connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether outside trip appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Russian-tie live: only outside trips score.

Footwork ladders into trip entries.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe outside trip as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of outside trip stays stable even when accents change.
