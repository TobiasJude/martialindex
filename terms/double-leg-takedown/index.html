Double-Leg Takedown

The double-leg takedown levels and drives through both legs to put the opponent on the mat—wrestling’s blast entry adapted for BJJ with corner finishes and guillotine awareness, linked from the takedown hub.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Range check before you shoot.
  • Level-change under the hands.
  • Penetration step between their feet.
  • Finish by turning the corner—not only linear blasting.
  • Chain from singles and snap-downs.

What Is Double-Leg Takedown?

Mechanically: change level, penetrate, secure behind the knees/thighs, keep head up/hips under, then drive and turn. Lachlan-style fundamentals stress range and controlled landing into top control rather than heroic lifts.

BJJ context adds: do not land neck-down into a guillotine; expect fence/wall in MMA; gi grips can set or stop the shot. See single-leg chains and hand fighting.

Scoring depends on ruleset—verify IBJJF vs ADCC rather than assuming.

How Double-Leg Takedown Works

Hands clear or snap, drop level, step deep, collect both legs, posture head/hips, cut the corner to side control.

Against sprawl: keep driving angles, peak out, or switch to single.

Drill: shoot to hold without finishing, then add corner finishes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar ties and sleeve clears help; pants can also block your hands—strip first.

No-Gi. Classic wrestling double; hand fighting is everything.

Both: a double into closed guard without pass plan is incomplete.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex snaps Jordan down, times the posture rise, shoots, and turns to side control.

From a failed single, Alex connects to both legs and finishes the double.

Gi: Alex clears sleeves, levels, and drives to a knee-cut-ready landing.

Types / Variations

Blast double

Linear drive through both legs.

Corner / angle finish

Turning off the centerline to dump.

Snap-down setup

Force posture rise into the shot.

Lift variants

Higher amplitude—use cautiously in BJJ training.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: hand clears, snaps, feints, and single-leg transitions.

Counters: hard sprawl, underhooks, and whizzer with hip pressure.

Escapes mid-shot: widen base, stuff head, peel hands, circle.

Double-Leg vs Single-Leg

Factor Double-Leg Single-Leg
Capture Both legs One leg
Power path Penetration + drive Angle + dump/pipe
Sprawl answer Corner / peak / to single Elevate / to double
Training caution Slams / head position Knee torque

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF/ADCC scoring for completed doubles depends on control criteria in the current book—verify. Slams are illegal/dangerous in sport BJJ training and competition contexts.

Safety

Never slam. Control partners to the mat. Progressive resistance only until breakfalls are solid.

Common mistakes

  • Shooting from too far.
  • Head down, hips behind.
  • Linear blast with no corner plan.
  • Ignoring sprawl cues.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a double-leg?

A shot that attacks both legs to put them down.

Key beginner cue?

Range, level-change, penetration, corner finish.

Gi differences?

Grip clears matter more before the shot.

Main defensive answer?

Sprawl plus head control / underhooks.

Safe training?

No slams; mat returns with control.

Chain ideas?

Snap → double; single → double.

Next?

Single-leg and takedown hub.

The Bottom Line

The double-leg is simple to describe and hard to finish clean. Earn the range, stay under the hands, and turn the corner into real top control.
