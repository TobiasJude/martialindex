Eight Limbs

Eight Limbs is the art’s full toolkit: two fists, two elbows, two knees, two feet—a range system, not a trick list.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Eight Limbs = fists, elbows, knees, feet/shins as one system.
  • Range chooses the live limb.
  • Stadium scoring valued kicks and clinch control.
  • Defense spans long guard, checks, and clinch posture.
  • Train the full set; fight the card’s rules.

What Is Eight Limbs?

Eight Limbs is Martial Index’s name for Muay Thai’s complete striking map: two fists, two elbows, two knees, and two feet (shins as roundhouse surface). Pad rounds mixing a Jab, Horizontal Elbow, Straight Knee, and Teep (Push Kick) teach one system.

Unlike boxing’s two fists or kickboxing that bans elbows, traditional Muay Thai keeps all eight live—reshaping Muay Thai Stance, Range, and pacing.

Learning order: stance and teep first, Thai roundhouse next, then clinch/knees, then elbows, punches as connective tissue.

Defensive stack: Kick Check, Long Guard, plum posture so knees are not free.

Martial Index files Eight Limbs under Muay Thai so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Eight Limbs map when you need the full weapon system; keep the focus on how Eight Limbs behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Eight Limbs Works

Distance bands: long = Teep (Push Kick)/Roundhouse Kick; mid = Jab/Cross/body kicks vs checks; close = elbows/knees/Thai Clinch (Plum).

Timing picks the live limb—meet the step with a teep; hide elbows on clinch breaks.

Stadium judging rewarded clear kicks and clinch dominance—hence shin work and plum control.

Chains beat highlights: jab → teep → Body Kick → clinch knee.

Mechanically, successful eight limbs work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Muay Thai Stance with hands alive.

If Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into elbows or clinch threats you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Kick Check awareness; pocket strikes need Long Guard or tight covers; clinch tools need posture.

Stadium vs Gym / Rules Notes

Under traditional stadium Muay Thai, eight limbs lives inside a full Eight Limbs rule set—elbows, knees, and clinch time change how often you invest in this tool.

Amateur cards, kickboxing crossovers, and some promotions restrict elbows or clinch duration. Gym culture may still drill eight limbs daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit.

Confirm the bout agreement before importing stadium frequency into restricted rules or MMA, where wrestling reframes clinch incentives.

Scoring emphasis varies. Some judges still reward hard kicks and balance; others read more like kickboxing. Let the card’s eyes shape volume.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Round builder: Jab, Teep (Push Kick), Body Kick, Thai Clinch (Plum), Clinch Knee.

Counter: Catch Kick, Horizontal Elbow, reset teep.

Pad constraint: teep + roundhouse + punches + knee + elbow each round.

Fight picture: eight limbs appears when Range and Timing say so, then yields to the next limb in the Eight Limbs chain.

Gym test: can you apply eight limbs after a Jab feint (or a teep feint) and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Fists (Chok)

Jab, Cross, Hook, Uppercut, Overhand—framed for kicks/clinch, not pure boxing.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Elbows (Sok)

Horizontal Elbow, Upward Elbow, Spinning Elbow where punches lose leverage.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Knees (Khao)

Straight Knee, Clinch Knee, Flying Knee when hips close.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Feet / shins

Teep (Push Kick) for space; Thai Roundhouse family with shin, long arc—not karate/TKD snap.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Isolating weapons.
  • Boxing without teep/clinch awareness.
  • Elbow spam.
  • No checks.
  • Sparring rules ≠ stadium rules.
  • Treating eight limbs as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox eight limbs inside full rounds; return to Muay Thai Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up (punch, kick, or clinch) so eight limbs connects to Eight Limbs.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether eight limbs appears in the right Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for elbows, flying knees, and catch-heavy games.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe eight limbs as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Range, or a stance that collapses when kicks and clinch enter the round.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put eight limbs behind heavy boxing; others behind teeps and body kicks; stadium-oriented rooms may emphasize clinch delivery. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Teep (Push Kick), Thai Clinch (Plum), and Muay Thai Stance into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for elbows, knees, and catch-kick dumps where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “clinch,” “guard,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Muay Thai entries keep eight limbs scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on eight limbs, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where Timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for eight limbs under your next card (elbows allowed? clinch time? scoring bias?) so training intensity matches the bout, not a generic Instagram Muay Thai.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What are the eight limbs?

Two fists, two elbows, two knees, two feet including shins.

Is clinch a ninth weapon?

Clinch delivers knees/elbows; classic frame is eight limbs.

Why so many kicks?

Scoring history and gym culture.

Need elbows?

Train yes; bout rules may ban.

Beginner order?

Stance/teep → roundhouse/checks → punches → clinch knees → elbows.

Shin conditioning?

Progressive exposure; do not rush.

The Bottom Line

Eight Limbs is Muay Thai’s OS: pick the weapon for the range and train transitions.
