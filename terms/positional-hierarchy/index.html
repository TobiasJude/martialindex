Positional Hierarchy

The positional hierarchy ranks positions by control and finishing power—typically guard → pass → side → mount / back. Climb with control before submit; defend with the escape hierarchy.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Know the ladder you’re climbing.
  • Stabilize each rung before the next.
  • Back and mount beat side for most finishes.
  • Don’t trade high positions for bad submissions.
  • Bottom uses escape hierarchy in reverse.

What Is Positional Hierarchy?

A strategic map popularized across sport BJJ coaching—not a law of physics.

Top goals: pass, settle, advance, finish. Bottom goals: retain/recover guard, then reverse the ladder.

Connects pass-to-side, pass-to-mount, take-the-back, and the maintain-* pages.

How Positional Hierarchy Works

From top: clear guard → settle side → climb mount or expose back.

Use knee-on-belly as a hub to force movement when pins stall.

Maintain each pin against the matching escapes.

Submit when control is real—not as a substitute for position.

From bottom: survive → escape → recover → sweep/pass of your own.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Points ladders (IBJJF-style) explicitly reward hierarchy climbs.

No-gi / ADCC. Fewer points incentives; hierarchy still guides high-percentage control.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a pass, Pat settles side for three breaths, then mounts—no early armbar dive.

Jordan on bottom escapes mount to half, recovers closed, then sweeps to reverse the ladder.

Positional Hierarchy vs Escape Hierarchy

Factor Positional hierarchy Escape hierarchy
Owner Usually top player Bottom player
Direction Climb Descend out of danger
Success Higher control + finish Safer position + guard
Shared Order over chaos Order over chaos

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF-style scoring encodes a hierarchy; ADCC differs—always verify current IBJJF / ADCC packets.

Safety

Forcing climbs with reckless knee pressure injures partners—control the head.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping settle for highlight submissions.
  • Treating hierarchy as dogma in every rule set.
  • Ignoring maintain skills.
  • Bottom attacking before escaping.

Universal?

Why settle?

Hub to force movement between pins.

The Bottom Line

Positional hierarchy is the climb map—earn each rung, maintain it, then finish.
