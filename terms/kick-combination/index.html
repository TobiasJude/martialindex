Kick Combination

A kick combination is a purposeful string—not random flurry. Train the idea here; build the reps on Combination Kicking Drills.

What Is Kick Combination?

A kick combination links two or more kicks (and often a Feint Kick or step) so the second action is better than the first.

It is a tactical object, not a fitness circuit. The circuit lives on Combination Kicking Drills.

Good strings use Lead-Leg Kicking to probe and Rear-Leg Kicking or a cut to finish—or the reverse.

In Kyorugi (Sparring), combinations exist to change height, side, or timing after the opponent covers the first look.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Kick Combination behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Kick Combination Works

Start from a honest Taekwondo Stance. First kick must be real enough to be respected—or a committed Feint Kick.

Do not drop the Chamber (Bal Deung) into a vacation between kicks unless the combination includes a planted reset on purpose.

Exit on a Cut Angle (Kyorugi) or recover guard. Combinations that end posing are singles with extra cardio.

Call the string in Korean or English consistently so partners feed the right cover.

Mechanically, successful kick combination work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, kick combination is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill kick combination daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Lead cutter, skip Fast Kick (Bal Bal) body, rear Head Kick (Olgul Chagi).

Class: three assigned strings, thirty seconds each, technique over count.

Fight picture: kick combination appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply kick combination after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Height-change strings

Body then head, or axe then turning kick.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Side-change strings

Open to closed via Switching Stance (TKD).

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Feint-to-real strings

Feint Kick then the kick you actually wanted.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Stringing kicks that all need the same cover.
  • No footwork between kicks.
  • Counting reps instead of reading the partner.
  • Forgetting hands.
  • Confusing this page with a workout plan.
  • Treating kick combination as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick kick combination inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so kick combination connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether kick combination appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe kick combination as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put kick combination behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep kick combination scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on kick combination, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for kick combination under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related reading inside this batch should feel like a cluster, not a random walk: return to Chamber (Bal Deung) and Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) when mechanics fail, and to Kyorugi (Sparring) when techniques “stop working” for mysterious reasons.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Combination vs drills?

This page is the match concept. Combination Kicking Drills is how you train the strings.

How many kicks?

Two honest kicks beat five slaps. Double Kick is a special two-beat case.

Must they all score?

No. The first kick can be a probe or Feint Kick.

WT vs ITF?

WT strings chase PSS zones; ITF Taekwondo may include more hand techniques in the same phrase.

Where to start?

One height-change and one side-change string you can recover from.

The Bottom Line

Write combinations that change a variable—height, side, or timing—then stop adding kicks for decoration.
