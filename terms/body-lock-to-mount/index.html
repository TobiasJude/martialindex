What Is Body Lock to Mount?

Body Lock to Mount is the finishing path that turns a locked body-lock pass into mount by clearing the hips and climbing once the legs are neutralized. In gym language you may also hear bodylock pass finish to mount. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

Secure a tight body lock around the torso, smash or walk the legs aside, keep chest-to-chest, then hop or slide into mount without freeing their frames. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Over-under or double under body locks with lapel help; no-gi uses hands locked palm-to-palm or gable behind the back.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, body lock to mount usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard body lock to mount as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (Guard Passing). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

Finish branch of Body Lock Pass; lands in Mount beside side control to mount.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck, spinal, and knee pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck, shoulders, and knees. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing body lock to mount with a nearby sibling name.
