Gift Wrap

Gift wrap is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu upper-body control where you figure-four the opponent's arm around their own head—like wrapping a present—freezing the near shoulder for armbars, back takes, and short chokes from technical mount or the back.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Kimura grip that includes the head—not a plain figure-four on the arm alone.
  • Pairs with technical-mount legs or back hooks; freezes the near shoulder.
  • Gi sleeves and collars make the wrap stickier; no-gi needs tighter wrist and head control.
  • Soft wraps get hand-fought—connect elbow to ribs and ride the turn.
  • Lives next to technical mount and back control.

What Is Gift Wrap?

Gift wrap is an upper-body control position: you lock a kimura (figure-four) grip on one of the opponent's arms so that their arm and head are trapped together in the loop. Your torso often sits between mount and back—especially when your legs are in technical mount.

The wrap removes the near arm as a frame and exposes the neck and far arm. It is a control first, submission platform second.

How Gift Wrap Works

By pinning their wrist behind or beside their head with a figure-four, you immobilize the near shoulder. Legs can stay in technical mount, switch to hooks for the back, or threaten an armbar on the free arm. In the gi, collar feeds add short chokes; in no-gi, the path often goes to the back and RNC.

Bottom player's job. Never let the wrist travel behind the head. Hand-fight early, keep the elbow glued, and if wrapped, protect the neck and free the trapped wrist before they insert the second hook.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar grips make gift wrap extremely sticky; paper-cutter and bow-and-arrow style finishes appear quickly.

No-gi. Relies on wrist control and head position; expect faster transitions to back hooks and RNC rather than collar chokes.

MMA context. Gift wrap limits their near arm for defense while creating punching angles and back-take opportunities.

What Does Gift Wrap Look Like in Practice?

From technical mount, Alex threads a kimura on Jordan's near arm, includes the head in the loop, and freezes Jordan's shoulder. When Jordan turns, Alex inserts hooks and finishes.

Gi. Alex feeds a collar with the free hand for a short choke while the wrap holds.

No-gi. Alex abandons collar ideas, takes the back, and hunts the RNC.

Types / Variations

Mounted / technical-mount gift wrap

Classic: technical-mount legs + kimura-through-head on the near arm.

Gift wrap from the back

Seatbelt upgrades into a wrap to isolate an arm for armbar or to clear hands from the neck.

Gift wrap from side control

Trap the near arm from side control, then step over into technical mount or the back.

Submission cluster

Armbar on the free or wrapped arm, short chokes (gi), RNC (no-gi), and shoulder locks as secondary pressure.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Technical mount when they frame with the near arm; side control near-arm traps; turtle when they leave a wrist high.

Top threats. Back take, armbar, gi chokes, shoulder locks.

Bottom counters. Elbows in, wrist never behind the head, explode the figure-four early, and recover frames before hooks land.

Gift Wrap vs Seatbelt

Factor Gift Wrap Seatbelt
Grip Kimura including the head Over/under around the torso
Primary goal Freeze near arm + open finishes Chest-to-back control
Best with Technical mount or one hook Full back hooks
Gi vs no-gi Gi chokes shine Universal; RNC path

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal control. Scores come from mount/back criteria and submissions—not from the wrap itself.

Safety

Do not crank the neck while wrapping. Control shoulder rotation; tap early to arm and shoulder pressure.

Common mistakes

  • Figure-four on the arm without including the head—losing the wrap.
  • No leg structure—wrap without technical mount or hooks.
  • Parking without attacking—letting them hand-fight free.
  • In no-gi: soft wrists and no head connection.
  • Leaving the far arm free to frame while you admire the wrap.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the gift wrap in BJJ?

A kimura-style figure-four that includes the opponent's head, freezing the near arm for submissions and back takes.

Is gift wrap a position or a submission?

Primarily a control position that feeds submissions—armbars, chokes, and back takes.

How do you set up gift wrap?

Trap the near arm from technical mount, side control, or turtle, lock a kimura that includes the head, and secure your legs.

Does gift wrap work in no-gi?

Yes, but expect fewer collar chokes and more back-take / RNC finishes.

How do you escape gift wrap?

Prevent the wrist from traveling behind the head. If caught, explode the figure-four and protect the neck while clearing hooks.

Gift wrap vs seatbelt—which is better?

Different jobs: gift wrap freezes an arm; seatbelt owns chest-to-back. Many sequences use both.

Can beginners use gift wrap?

Yes as a control concept, but drill the kimura grip and technical-mount base carefully to avoid neck strain on partners.

The Bottom Line

Gift wrap freezes the near arm with a kimura-through-the-head grip, pairing with technical mount and the back. Gi adds choke options; no-gi races to hooks and the RNC. See technical mount and back control.
