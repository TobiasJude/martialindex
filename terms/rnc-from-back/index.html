RNC Setup from Back

The RNC setup from back is the sequence that turns back control into a rear naked choke: keep the seatbelt and hooks (or body triangle), win the hand fight, slide the choking arm under the chin, then lock a finish grip.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Control first, chin second—chasing the neck without a harness loses the back.
  • This page is the setup path; finish grips live on palm-to-palm, figure-four, and short-choke pages.
  • Strip or misdirect their hands; do not muscle past a tight chin tuck.
  • Parent: rear naked choke. Platform: back control.
  • Gi adds collar options (bow-and-arrow) when the chin stays hidden.

What Is RNC Setup from Back?

Most failed RNCs are failed setups. People have the back, abandon the seatbelt, and poke at a tucked chin until the opponent spins face-to-face. The setup page exists so “how do I get the RNC from the back?” has a home that is not just “squeeze harder.”

The job is sequential: stay on the back, hide your own hands, create a window under the chin, insert the choking arm, then choose a finish grip. Those finish grips are detailed on figure-four RNC, palm-to-palm RNC, and short choke.

Martial Index also links rear triangle and armbar from back as the usual punishes when the neck stays sealed.

How RNC Setup from Back Works

Lock a seatbelt (over-shoulder arm on top) and at least one hook or a body triangle. Ear-to-ear head position stops them creating space.

Use the underhook hand to peel or trap a defending wrist. When the chin lifts a centimeter, slide the choking arm palm-down along the neck—do not punch it over a clenched jaw.

Once the elbow line is under the chin, hide the choking hand (bicep catch or clasp) and bring the support hand to its finish role. Expand the chest and join the elbows; see the grip pages for details.

If the chin never opens, switch: short choke from below, rear triangle, or armbar. Staying in a five-minute hand fight is how you lose the back on points.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar chokes (bow-and-arrow, clock from turtle) are the usual plan B. You can still finish a naked RNC in the gi.

No-gi. This setup is the entire offense. Hand fighting is the skill block.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex keeps the seatbelt, peels a wrist, slides under the chin, and only then locks a figure-four.

Jordan cannot win the chin, switches to a short choke / rear triangle instead of ripping the jaw.

Types / Variations

Seatbelt to chin slide

The standard classroom setup.

Wrist-trap / misdirection setup

Trap a hand so the chin window opens without a strength battle.

One-hook or body-triangle setup

Lower-body control changes; the upper-body sequence stays the same.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

You “enter” the setup only after a back take—turtle, mount transition, or scramble. Counters (for them): two-on-one the choking arm, chin down, slide toward the underhook side. Your counters to that: short choke, rear triangle, armbar.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult rear naked chokes are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Do not wrench jaws or crank necks to “open the chin.” Create a window; tap early in drilling.

Common mistakes

  • Abandoning the seatbelt to chase the neck.
  • No hooks—riding high and getting shaken off.
  • Forcing the arm over a fully tucked chin.
  • Never switching to short choke or rear triangle.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Should I hook first or choke first?

Harness and at least one hook first. The choke without control is a scramble.

What if they tuck the chin forever?

Short choke, rear triangle, armbar, or bow-and-arrow in the gi.

Figure-four or palm-to-palm?

Figure-four is the textbook blood choke; palm-to-palm is the faster clasp. See those pages.

Need both hooks?

Ideal, not mandatory. Body triangle is an alternative.

Gi difference?

Collars become plan B; the setup sequence is the same.

Is this different from the parent RNC page?

Yes—this is the setup path. The parent covers the whole choke.

Legal?

Standard adult choke—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

RNC from the back is a setup problem first: seatbelt, hooks, hand fight, then chin. Pick a finish on the figure-four, palm-to-palm, or short choke pages.
