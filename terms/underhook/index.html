Underhook

An underhook is a foundational Brazilian jiu-jitsu and wrestling control: your arm under their armpit elevates the shoulder—deciding half-guard battles, body-lock passes, and clinch takedowns, and bridging to overhook / head control.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Deep underhook beats shallow.
  • Half guard: underhook often decides who is “winning.”
  • Chain to double unders and body locks.
  • Whizzer (overhook) is the common counter.
  • Cross-cluster bridge concept.

What Is Underhook?

The underhook puts your arm through the armpit to lift and connect to the back, breaking their shoulder line and opening backs, sweeps, and passes. In half guard it is the classic dogfight currency; in standing it starts body-lock offense.

See half guard, overhook, body-lock passing.

As a cross-cluster bridge, underhooks appear in body-lock passing, butterfly elevation, and front-headlock scrambles whenever a shoulder line must be won or denied.

How Underhook Works

Pummel the hand through the armpit, connect high on the back (not only the waist), keep your head on the correct side, and move your hips behind the grip.

Shallow underhooks get whizzered—climb deeper or off-balance first.

Escalate: single under → double unders → body lock when available.

Drill pummeling rounds: single under to double unders without finishing—feel the difference between a decorative underhook and one that actually moves their spine.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Underhooks plus lapel/belt amplify control.

No-Gi. Pure pummeling and head position.

Same shoulder-elevation idea.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Bottom half guard, Alex wins a deep underhook, comes to dogfight, and takes Jordan’s back.

Top half, Alex blocks the underhook with head position and crossface, then knee-cuts.

In a clinch, Alex wins double unders, locks a body lock, and hits an inside trip—same underhook literacy as half-guard dogfights, different endgame.

Types / Variations

Half-guard bottom underhook

Dogfight / sweep / back-take engine.

Top underhook passing

Body lock and smash pathways.

Standing underhook

Clinch and takedown chain.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Win via pummeling, off-balances, and knee-shield conversions.

Counters: whizzer/overhook, head on the outside of their elbow, crossface.

“Escapes”: pummel back; do not stay flat under their underhook.

Underhook vs Overhook

Factor Underhook Overhook
Arm path Under armpit Over shoulder/arm
Primary gift Shoulder elevation / back access Arm isolation / whizzer
Half-guard role Bottom offense king Top counter tool
Guard attack role Less central than overhook triangles Closed-guard attack platform

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Universal legal control concept. Submissions you reach afterward follow normal charts.

Safety

Avoid cranking shoulders while “just holding” underhooks on injured partners.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow underhook at the waist only.
  • Head on the wrong side.
  • No hip connection behind the grip.
  • Ignoring the whizzer fight.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an underhook?

Your arm under their armpit controlling the shoulder/back.

Why in half guard?

It often decides offense vs getting flattened.

Counter?

Whizzer/overhook and head position.

Gi or no-gi?

Both.

Standing use?

Yes—body locks and trips.

Deep vs shallow?

Deep to the back beats waist-only.

Related concepts?

Overhook, head control, wrist control.

The Bottom Line

Underhook elevates the shoulder and opens the back: pummel deep, put hips behind it, escalate to body lock. Cross-link half guard and overhook.
