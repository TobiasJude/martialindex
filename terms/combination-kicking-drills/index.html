What Is Combination Kicking Drills?

Combination kicking drills are planned sequences on pads, Hogu, or open floor that force more than one kick plus a recovery.

They exist so Kick Combination is not only a whiteboard idea. The drill must include movement, a cover, or a timing cue.

Good drills change one variable: height, side, Feint Kick, or Cut Angle (Kyorugi). Bad drills are just “do more kicks.”

Sport drills should sometimes wear PSS or at least a Hogu so Electronic Scoring (PSS) honesty is part of the rep.

Martial Index files Combination Kicking Drills under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Combination Kicking Drills behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Combination Kicking Drills Works

Write the string. Example: lead body, lead body, rear head. Then add a Skip Step or Sliding Step entry.

Start technical, then add a moving feeder, then add a counter threat so Counter Kick defense is in the same round.

Count quality, not only volume. A thirty-second round of sloppy Fast Kick (Bal Bal) is conditioning, not a combination drill.

Film one round a week. If the last kick never lands in Taekwondo Stance, the drill failed.

Mechanically, successful combination kicking drills work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, combination kicking drills is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill combination kicking drills daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Three beginner strings (front-roundhouse-slide; roundhouse-axe-crescent; cut-fast-rear) on a clock.

Hogu partner: only score if the second kick is a different height than the first.

Fight picture: combination kicking drills appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply combination kicking drills after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Drilling only in the air.
  • Never changing height or side.
  • No recovery standard.
  • Confusing this page with the {kick-combination} concept page.
  • Fatigue rounds with zero technical standard.
  • Treating combination kicking drills as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick combination kicking drills inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so combination kicking drills connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether combination kicking drills appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

End every drill with a mandatory exit step so posing never gets reps.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe combination kicking drills as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put combination kicking drills behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep combination kicking drills scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on combination kicking drills, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for combination kicking drills under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related reading inside this batch should feel like a cluster, not a random walk: return to Chamber (Bal Deung) and Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) when mechanics fail, and to Kyorugi (Sparring) when techniques “stop working” for mysterious reasons.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Drills vs combination?

Kick Combination is the match idea. This page is the practice method.

How many strings?

Two or three you can recover from beat ten you cannot.

Need PSS?

Not always. A Hogu already changes honesty.

ITF?

Build strings that include hands if your card includes hands.

Weekly plan?

Two technical rounds, one situational, one film review.

The Bottom Line

If a combination only works on a quiet line, you do not have a combination—you have a dance. These drills exist to fix that.
