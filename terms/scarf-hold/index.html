Scarf Hold (Kesa Gatame)

Scarf hold (kesa gatame) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu / judo side-control pin where you wrap the opponent's head and near arm while sitting with your hips toward their head—a brutal pin that feeds armbars, chokes, and transitions to modified scarf.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Hips toward their head + head-and-near-arm wrap = classic kesa.
  • Great pin and unique armbar/choke angles; watch the bridge-and-roll escape.
  • Gi grips (sleeve, collar, lapel) stabilize; no-gi needs tight headlocks and underhooks.
  • High hips get rolled—keep weight low and the near arm trapped.
  • Parent: side control; cousin: modified scarf hold.

What Is Scarf Hold (Kesa Gatame)?

Scarf hold—Japanese kesa gatame—is a side pin where the top player sits beside the opponent, wraps the head and near arm, and orients the hips toward the opponent's head. In BJJ it is a specialized branch of side control, inherited from judo osaekomi (hold-down) systems.

Unlike chest-to-chest side control, kesa turns your body so your ribs and hip pressure the near shoulder while the wrapped arm cannot frame.

How Scarf Hold Works

Trapping the near arm under your armpit and controlling the head removes their primary escape frames. A wide base (legs splayed toward the head) resists bridges. From here you can pin for points/advantages in some rules, attack armbars by stepping over the head, shoulder-lock, or transition to modified scarf, mount, or north-south.

Bottom player's job. Free the near arm, get to a bridge-and-roll (upa) before their base widens, or shrimp and reclaim underhooks. Never leave the near arm dead under their armpit.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar grips lock the wrap; lapel feeds create paper-cutter and short-choke options uncommon in no-gi.

No-gi. Classic headlock and underhook mechanics; expect more bridge-and-roll attempts and faster transitions to north-south or mount.

MMA context. Kesa creates punching lanes while trapping an arm—but a high headlock can be rolled. Keep hips low.

What Does Scarf Hold Look Like in Practice?

Alex passes to side control, sits through toward Jordan's head, wraps the head and near arm, and sprawls the legs for a kesa pin. When Jordan bridges, Alex adjusts the base and steps over for an armbar.

Gi. Alex grips Jordan's near sleeve under the armpit and a collar behind the head.

No-gi. Alex locks a gable or figure-four behind the head and keeps the near arm pinched.

Types / Variations

Hon kesa gatame (classic)

Head wrap + near-arm trap, hips facing the head—standard scarf hold.

Armbar paths

Step the free leg over the head into juji-gatame style finishes when they extend or bridge poorly.

Chokes and shoulder locks

Gi collar attacks, arm-triangles from adjusted angles, and Americana-style shoulder pressure.

Transitions to mount / north-south

When the pin stalls, circle to mount or north-south rather than dying in a static headlock.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From side control sit-throughs; after throws (judo); from knee-cut residuals when the near arm is trapped.

Top threats. Pin control, armbars, shoulder locks, gi chokes, mount transitions.

Bottom counters. Bridge-and-roll toward the trapped arm, free the elbow, shrimp to guard, or force them into a high headlock you can roll.

Scarf Hold vs Standard Side Control

Factor Scarf Hold Side Control
Hip orientation Toward their head More parallel / chest-to-chest
Near arm Trapped under armpit Often framed or underhooked
Escape bias Bridge-and-roll Shrimp / underhook recoveries
Submission bias Armbar over the head, shoulder locks Kimura, Americana, mount path

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. In IBJJF, side control / kesa can score the pass when stabilized. Judo scores osaekomi differently—know your sport.

Safety

Avoid cranking the neck in the head wrap. Control armbar falls; tap early to shoulder pressure.

Common mistakes

  • High hips and loose near-arm trap—getting rolled.
  • Pure headlock without trapping the near arm.
  • Parking forever without attacking or transitioning.
  • In no-gi: soft gable grips behind the head.
  • Ignoring modified scarf when they free the head wrap.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is kesa gatame / scarf hold?

A side pin wrapping the head and near arm with your hips facing their head—classic judo hold adopted in BJJ.

Is scarf hold good for BJJ or only judo?

Both. BJJ uses it for pins, armbars, and transitions; just respect the bridge-and-roll escape more than in pure osaekomi contests.

How do you escape scarf hold?

Free the near arm and bridge-and-roll, or shrimp to reclaim frames before their base widens.

Scarf hold vs modified scarf hold?

Classic kesa wraps the head; modified (kuzure) often uses a deep underhook instead of the head wrap.

Does scarf hold work in no-gi?

Yes—tighten the head-and-arm connection and expect faster rolls if your hips rise.

What submissions come from scarf hold?

Armbars over the head, shoulder locks, some chokes in the gi, and transitions to mount finishes.

Why do I keep getting rolled from kesa?

Usually high hips and a free near arm. Trap the arm, lower your base, and adjust when they bridge.

The Bottom Line

Scarf hold (kesa gatame) pins with a head-and-near-arm wrap and hips toward the head. Attack armbars and transitions; respect the bridge-and-roll. See side control and modified scarf hold.
