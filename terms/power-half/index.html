Power Half

A power half is a driven Half Nelson turn—hips and drive that invent Near Fall points and pin threats.

What Is Power Half?

The power half belongs to the Half Nelson family: you thread an arm under the near armpit, grab the head, and drive with hips to turn the opponent.

“Power” describes the drive and body weight—not a different illegal hold.

It is a staple Pinning Combination in Folkstyle Wrestling rooms.

Parent: Half Nelson. Often set by Crossface or breakdown rides.

Martial Index files Power Half under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Power Half behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Power Half Works

Break the opponent down, clear the near arm, insert the half, and step your hips up the mat while driving the head toward the far side.

Keep chest pressure so they cannot roll through you into a Peterson Roll.

If the pin is close, finish; if they bridge hard, hold the angle for near-fall counts.

Release and re-ride when the neck position gets dangerous or the referee warns.

Mechanically, successful power half work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, power half is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how power half is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of power half either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Officials protect the neck—dangerous pressure will be stopped.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Spiral breakdown → power half → long near-fall count.

Crossface clears the arm; power half finishes the turn.

Match picture: power half appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply power half after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Half without hips—arm-only crank.
  • Getting peterson-rolled by staying too high.
  • Leaving the far arm free to post.
  • Confusing with illegal {full-nelson}.
  • Treating power half as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle power half inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so power half connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether power half appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Ten power halves with a partner bridging realistically.

Live top: only nelson-family turns score.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe power half as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of power half stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Power half vs half nelson?

Power half is the driven finishing application of the half nelson family.

Is it legal?

Yes when applied as a half nelson—not a full nelson.

Best setup?

Breakdown + Crossface to clear the arm.

Defense?

Don’t let the half in; peel and Sit-Out; if in, bridge and look Peterson Roll.

Freestyle?

Nelson-like turns appear differently; know UWW legality.

Youth tip?

Teach hip drive before “cranking the neck.”

The Bottom Line

Train the power half as a hip skill first—arms only get you stalled and reversed.
