What Is 360 Dollyo?

360 dollyo is a jumping turning kick that rotates through a full turn before impact.

Most US gyms call the same family Tornado Kick. This page exists because athletes search “360” as often as “tornado.”

It sits on Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) mechanics plus a spinning entry, not on a new impact surface.

Compare with Twio Dollyo Chagi (Jumping Turning Kick): a jumping turning kick may hop without the full 360 setup.

Martial Index files 360 Dollyo under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how 360 Dollyo behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How 360 Dollyo Works

Step or swing the lead to create rotation, look over the shoulder, jump, and chamber the kicking knee.

The kicking foot travels a Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) arc at the end of the spin. Retract and land facing the target.

The Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) and eyes do the aiming. The kicking leg is the last thing that decides.

Recover to Taekwondo Stance. A 360 that ends in a spin-out is a highlight, not a skill.

Mechanically, successful 360 dollyo work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, 360 dollyo is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill 360 dollyo daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Sport: a lead-leg cutter draws a check; you 360 to the open Hogu or head.

Class: slow 180 turns, then 360 without a kick, then add a mid pad.

Fight picture: 360 dollyo appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply 360 dollyo after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Sport / short 360

Compact spin used as a Kyorugi (Sparring) surprise. Less air time, more recovery.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Stepping so large you telegraph a week early.
  • Kicking before the hips finish turning.
  • Landing with the back to the opponent.
  • Treating {tornado-kick} and 360 as unrelated techniques.
  • Throwing it on every tired exchange.
  • Treating 360 dollyo as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick 360 dollyo inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so 360 dollyo connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether 360 dollyo appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe 360 dollyo as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put 360 dollyo behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

360 vs tornado?

Same family. Martial Index keeps 360 Dollyo for the degree name and Tornado Kick for the common English sport name.

360 vs 540?

540 Kick adds another half-turn and usually a hook or extra kick finish.

Does WT bonus the spin?

Spinning kicks can carry higher point values when they meet World Taekwondo Rules definitions—confirm the current chart.

Prerequisite?

Reliable Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), a safe jump, and the ability to spot the target mid-spin.

Safe for kids?

Only with mats, progressions, and a coach. Encyclopedia pages are not a license to freestyle on hardwood.

The Bottom Line

If you already trained Tornado Kick, treat 360 dollyo as the same mechanic under a search-friendly name—then decide when the spin is worth the risk.
