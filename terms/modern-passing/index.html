Modern Passing

Modern passing names the contemporary top-game metas that blend pressure, speed, and connection ideas—body locks, headquarters control, and chained toreando / knee-cut paths—to clear today's retention and leg-entanglement guards.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Not one technique—an umbrella for post-2010s passing systems answering advanced guards.
  • Body-lock / connection pressure rose partly to deny leg-lock entries.
  • Speed and movement passing still matter against seated and hook guards.
  • Pillar: guard passing; siblings pressure / speed / connection.
  • Co-evolves with guard evolution—study both sides.

What Is Modern Passing?

Modern passing is a concept label for how elite and instructional culture now teach guard passing: as interconnected decision trees rather than a folder of unrelated moves. It answers evolved guards—seated retention, reverse De La Riva, K-guard, and ashi threats—with connection, pressure, and movement used together.

Gordon Ryan / Danaher-influenced body-lock passing is one famous no-gi branch; gi rooms still run heavy knee-cut, over-under, and stack games inside the same umbrella.

How Modern Passing Works

Win a chest-to-chest or hip-to-hip connection, constrain the opponent's hips, then clear legs with switches rather than leaping into submissions from broken posture. Headquarters / J-point concepts park safely outside dangerous hooks before committing to knee cuts, toreandos, or smash passes.

Against leg hunters, denying space matters as much as clearing the knees—body locks shrink the room for saddle entries.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeves enable long toreando chains and sleeve-control knee cuts; lapel guards demand specific strip skills.

No-gi. Body locks, underhooks, and head position replace cloth; float and smash hybrids are common.

What Does Modern Passing Look Like in Practice?

Alex cannot blast through Jordan's seated guard. Alex forces a body lock, walks the hips down, shelves a leg, and settles in side control without ever giving Jordan a clean ashi angle.

Gi. Alex might pant-grip toreando into the same pin.

No-gi. Expect longer body-lock rides and hip switches.

Types / Variations

Connection & body lock

See connection passing and body-lock pass—deny distance, then clear.

Pressure & smash

Pressure passing, over-under, and half-smash families still decide many gi matches.

Speed & movement

Speed passing, toreando, and leg drag chains punish static hooks.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From standing grips, failed guard pulls, or after opening closed guard.

Counters (bottom). Recompose frames, invert to ashi, or wrestle up before the lock settles.

Passer mistakes. Hovering without connection invites triangles and heel hooks.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Passing that ignores heel hooks fails in ADCC; passing that ignores lapels fails in IBJJF gi. Train the event.

Safety

Do not dump weight on necks while smashing. Watch partner knees when extracting from entanglements.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any knee cut "modern" without a system around it.
  • Body-locking without a clearing plan—stalls invite escapes.
  • Abandoning pressure entirely for highlight toreandos.
  • Never drilling against live retention.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is modern passing just body locks?

No. Body locks are a prominent no-gi branch; see also speed and pressure.

Do I need New Wave instructionals to learn it?

No. Many coaches teach connection and headquarters ideas without that brand.

What is headquarters / J-point?

A family of outside-the-hooks holding positions used to choose the next pass safely.

How do I pass K-guard?

Deny the underhook/angle early, keep connection, and clear before they climb to ashi—see K-guard.

Is toreando outdated?

No. It remains a core movement pass—see toreando.

Gi or no-gi first?

Learn concepts in the uniform you train most; translate deliberately.

Where does this sit on Martial Index?

Under the guard-passing pillar, cross-linked to guard evolution.

The Bottom Line

Modern passing is systems-first top game: connect, constrain hips, clear legs, and account for entanglements. Use the guard-passing pillar and sibling pressure/speed/connection pages as your map.
