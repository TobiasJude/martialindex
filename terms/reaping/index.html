Reaping

Reaping is the grappling concept (and rule keyword) about placing a heel or calf across certain knee lines during leg entanglements—central to how ashigarami, saddle, and heel hook games are policed in IBJJF versus freer ADCC-style contexts.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Reaping is about knee-line geometry—not a single named submission.
  • IBJJF traditionally restricts many reaping configurations; ADCC-style rulesets are typically freer—verify current books.
  • Learn definitions from official PDFs, not only social media clips.
  • Cross-link to heel hook, outside heel hook, and saddle/honeyhole pages for mechanics.
  • Training reaping-heavy games requires partner consent and clear rule-set goals.

What Is Reaping?

In modern BJJ discourse, reaping refers to crossing the attacking heel or calf across the opponent’s knee line in ways many federation rulebooks treat as illegal or restricted—especially in gi. Mechanically it often appears when you rotate a trapped leg for heel hooks from outside or inside entanglements.

Martial Index does not publish a homemade illegal-move checklist. Instead we explain why coaches care: reaping language shapes which ashigarami entries are “sport legal” in your next tournament versus what you drill for ADCC trials.

How Reaping Works

Conceptually: if your heel crosses outside the line of their thigh/knee while you control the far hip, many IBJJF interpretations flag the position—details live in the official definition. Train the geometry slowly with a coach who knows your target rule set.

In freer no-gi rulesets, the same geometry may be foundational to outside heel-hook systems. Your job as an athlete is to label drills “IBJJF gi Monday” vs “ADCC Friday,” not to invent bans.

When refereeing or competing, ask: am I crossing the knee line as defined this year? If unsure, do not guess—read the PDF.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi / IBJJF culture. Reaping restrictions have historically shaped legal ankle locks vs banned heel-hook pathways—confirm the current book for your belt and age.

No-gi / ADCC culture. Reaping-heavy entanglements are often expected; still verify event-specific bans.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Taylor trains saddle heel hooks for ADCC, then switches to IBJJF-legal ankle-lock finishing angles the week of a gi Open—same ashi entry, different knee-line discipline.

A referee stops a match for reaping; both athletes learn the year’s diagram rather than arguing gym folklore.

Types / Variations

Outside-line geometry

Commonly discussed with outside ashi / outside heel-hook pathways.

Inside-entanglement debates

Saddle/honeyhole configurations may interact with definitions differently by year—verify.

False-reap style entries

Modern entry jargon—study under coaches; still subject to rulebook language.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

You “enter” reaping discussions whenever you play outside ashi, saddle, or 50/50 with heel exposure. Counters in restricted rule sets: straighten the knee line, abandon the illegal angle, attack legal straight ankle locks. Escapes: clear the crossing heel and reclaim posture before the rotation finishes.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. See also heel hook and outside heel hook for related mechanics.

Safety

Rotational knee attacks injure quickly. Only train heel-hook/reaping geometry with informed partners, gradual intensity, and instant taps.

Common mistakes

  • Memorizing last year’s Instagram “illegal list.”
  • Training only ADCC legs then freezing in IBJJF weigh-ins.
  • Explaining reaping without pointing athletes to the rulebook diagram.
  • Cranking through a tap while “showing the reap.”

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is reaping a submission?

No—it is a positional/rule concept about heel/knee-line geometry that often appears during leg attacks.

Illegal everywhere?

No. Restrictions are organization-specific—verify IBJJF vs ADCC vs local rules.

How do I learn the definition?

Read the current official rulebook diagrams; ask your coach to walk the lines on the mat.

Related to honeyhole?

Honeyhole/saddle control can intersect reaping debates depending on year and federation—cross-check, don’t assume.

Safe to drill?

Yes with consent, slow rotational pressure, and clear rule-set intent.

Gi white belts?

Prioritize legal fundamentals; introduce reaping literacy before competition week.

Where next?

Study ashigarami mechanics, then rule pages.

The Bottom Line

Reaping is knee-line literacy for the leg game. Learn the geometry, then verify the current rulebook before you compete—pair this page with heel hook and IBJJF rules.
