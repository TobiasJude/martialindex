Quarter Guard

Quarter guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu bottom position where you trap only the passer's ankle or lower leg—often after a knee cut almost succeeds—turning a failed pass into back takes, sweeps, and reverse half entries.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • You own their ankle/foot—not a full half-guard thigh trap.
  • Appears when knee cuts nearly finish; bottom's last entanglement before the pass.
  • Gi pants grips help; no-gi uses wrist control and hip heists.
  • Soft ankle traps get stomped free—pinch and attack immediately.
  • Parent: half guard; passer threat: knee cut.

What Is Quarter Guard?

Quarter guard is a thin bottom entanglement: instead of trapping the passer's thigh in full half guard, you catch only the ankle, heel, or lower calf—often the trailing foot left behind during a knee cut. It is the last guard-ish connection before a completed pass.

Modern athletes treat quarter guard as an offensive scramble platform—especially for back takes when the passer back-steps or posts.

How Quarter Guard Works

Pinching the ankle stops them from freeing the foot and finishing the pass cleanly. From there you can back-take as they turn, sweep if they overcommit, dive into reverse half, or recover full half if they stall. Timing beats strength—attack while their weight is still splitting.

Top player's job. Free the trapped ankle immediately, stabilize the pass, or switch to a smash before they take the back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips on the trapped ankle make quarter guard sticky; sleeve control helps back takes.

No-gi. Faster ankle escapes—pinch harder and commit to back takes or wrestle-ups sooner.

MMA context. Thin traps are risky under strikes; prefer recovering full guard or standing rather than parking in quarter.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Jordan's knee cut nearly clears Alex's half guard, but Alex clamps Jordan's trailing ankle. As Jordan back-steps, Alex rolls and takes the back.

Gi. Alex adds a pants grip to freeze the ankle.

No-gi. Alex heists the hips and races to the seatbelt without cloth.

Types / Variations

Classic ankle-trap quarter

Feet or ankles pinched on their trailing foot after a near pass.

Back takes

Follow their turn or back-step; insert hooks and seatbelt.

Sweeps and wrestle-ups

When they post heavily, elevate and come up on top.

Into reverse half / lockdown

If the pass stalls, deepen into reverse half or related traps rather than releasing.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Residual ankle traps from knee cuts; failed smash passes; intentional thin traps from half.

Bottom threats. Back take, sweep, reverse half, recovery to full half.

Top counters. Kick the ankle free, pin the hips, complete the pass to side control, or carefully back-step without giving the back.

Quarter Guard vs Half Guard

Factor Quarter Guard Half Guard
Trap depth Ankle / lower leg Thigh / hip line
Stability Thin—must attack fast More stable retention
Common origin Nearly passed knee cut Intentional half pull
Top priority Free the foot now Smash or knee-cut free

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. If the passer clears and stabilizes, the pass scores. Sweeps and back takes from quarter score normally.

Safety

Do not twist partners' ankles while fighting the trap. Tap early to kneebars if the scramble enters the legs.

Common mistakes

  • Parking in quarter without attacking—getting stomped free.
  • Loose ankle pinch.
  • Giving up the trap instead of recovering full half when the back take dies.
  • In no-gi: slow hand fighting on the seatbelt.
  • As passer: back-stepping blindly into the back take.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is quarter guard?

A thin bottom trap on the passer's ankle or lower leg—often after a knee cut almost passes.

Is quarter guard the same as half guard?

No. Half guard traps more of the thigh/hip; quarter is a shallower ankle-level entanglement.

How do you take the back from quarter guard?

When they turn or back-step, follow with a seatbelt and insert hooks while keeping the ankle trap until hooks land.

How do you pass quarter guard?

Free the trapped ankle immediately, pin their hips, and stabilize side control—do not leisurely back-step into a back take.

Does quarter guard work in no-gi?

Yes, but you must attack faster because ankles escape more easily without pants grips.

Should beginners learn quarter guard?

Learn to recognize it so you do not panic when almost passed—and so you do not gift the back when passing.

Can you sweep from quarter guard?

Yes—especially when they overpost or back-step poorly. Timing and hip heists matter more than strength.

The Bottom Line

Quarter guard traps the passer's ankle after a near pass—attack the back or deepen the entanglement immediately. See half guard and knee cut.
