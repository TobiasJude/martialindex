Slide-By

A slide-by slips past a shoulder or underhook to the back—clearance cousin of the Throw-By and Duck Under.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Slide-by clears past the shoulder.
  • Answers underhooks.
  • Finish with go-behind.
  • Sibling of throw-by/duck-under.
  • Keep moving through the clear.

What Is Slide-By?

A slide-by is a clearance/takedown path: you slide your body past their underhook or collar pressure and arrive behind them.

It answers stiff underhooks and overextended ties.

Related: Throw-By, Duck Under, Arm Drag (Wrestling).

In Wrestling, slide-bys keep pocket fighters from getting stuck underhooks-down forever.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Slide-By behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Slide-By Works

When they clamp an underhook or reach, control the wrist/elbow, drop slightly, slide your head and shoulder past, and take the back with a Go-Behind.

Keep moving—pausing mid-slide invites a whizzer counter.

Chain with snap-downs if they posture up during the slide.

Finish control before hunting turns.

Mechanically, successful slide-by work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, slide-by is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how slide-by is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of slide-by either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Control criteria for scoring the go-behind still apply.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

They dig an underhook; you slide-by to the back for two.

Collar pressure → slide-by when they overcommit the head.

Match picture: slide-by appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply slide-by after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Sliding without wrist control—getting snapped down yourself.
  • Stopping in front.
  • No go-behind finish.
  • Diving under without a level plan.
  • Treating slide-by as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle slide-by inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so slide-by connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether slide-by appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Sticky underhook live: only slide-bys score.

Shadow slide footwork.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe slide-by as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of slide-by stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Freestyle Wrestling, Folkstyle Wrestling, and Wrestling Stance into siblings, then return here after live rounds to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “ride,” “tilt,” and “headlock.” Martial Index’s Wrestling entries keep slide-by scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language—compare pages are labeled when useful.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on slide-by, one situational live theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing or hip position failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for slide-by under your next event (UWW freestyle, NCAA folkstyle, or Greco) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Slide-by vs throw-by?

Both clear to the back; mechanics differ by which arm/shoulder path you use.

Slide-by vs duck-under?

Duck-under goes under the arm; slide-by passes the shoulder line.

Good counters to underhooks?

Yes—primary classroom answer.

Freestyle?

Excellent go-behind path.

Defense?

Don’t overcommit the underhook; keep hip connection.

Beginner tip?

Drill slowly with a sticky underhook partner.

The Bottom Line

When underhooks stall your offense, slide-by is the door—practice the door daily.
