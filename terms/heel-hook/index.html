Heel Hook

The heel hook is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu leg lock that traps the heel and rotates the foot to torque the knee—central to modern no-gi and ADCC-style grappling, with important IBJJF legality limits, reaping rules, and safety requirements that every practitioner must understand.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Heel hooks rotate through the heel to attack the knee—tap early; pain can lag injury.
  • Inside and outside variants matter for control and legality discussions.
  • Huge in no-gi/ADCC; many IBJJF gi divisions prohibit heel hooks (and related reaping)—always check the current rulebook.
  • Compare with kneebar (straight knee extension vs rotation).
  • Only train under qualified coaches with clear safety culture.

What Is the Heel Hook?

A heel hook traps the opponent’s heel (typically in your armpit/elbow crease) while your legs control their leg, then rotates the foot to transmit torque into the knee. It is not an ankle twist for its own sake—the knee is the vulnerable structure.

How Heel Hook Works

Win an entanglement (SLX, 50/50, saddle/inside ashi, K-guard entries, etc.), isolate the heel, control the knee line so they cannot relieve rotation, then apply controlled rotational pressure. Grip quality on the heel decides many finishes.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi / ADCC-style. Primary home of modern heel-hook games.

IBJJF gi. Heel hooks are generally illegal in IBJJF gi competition across standard adult divisions; reaping rules also restrict many entanglements. Always verify the current IBJJF rulebook—do not rely on gym lore alone.

IBJJF no-gi. Adult no-gi has expanded legal lower-body attacks in recent rule eras, but details differ by year and division—confirm before you compete.

Training culture. Even where competition-illegal, some gi rooms drill heel hooks for knowledge; many do not. Follow your coach’s policy.

Types / Variations

Inside vs outside heel hook

Orientation of the trapped foot/knee line changes control and finishing details—learn both names from your coach’s system.

Entries from SLX, 50/50, K-guard

Positions first; finish second. Entanglement quality beats raw cranking.

Reaping and rule context

Reaping (crossing the heel across certain knee lines) is tightly policed in IBJJF. Understand definitions before you play legs in that rule set.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter from modern open-guard and scramble entanglements. Escape by relieving the knee line early and clearing the heel—late stages are dangerous; tap.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Critical: Heel hooks are a defining legality split. ADCC and many no-gi shows allow them. Standard IBJJF gi competition does not. IBJJF no-gi adult rules have changed over time—read the current book for your division. Reaping penalties in IBJJF can DQ or penalize even without a finish.

Safety

Train only with coaches who teach heel-hook safety. Tap at the first sign of trouble—do not “tough out” knee pressure. Partners must release instantly on tap. This is not a white-belt ego move.

Common mistakes

  • Cranking without knee-line control.
  • Training illegal techniques in a competition room that forbids them.
  • Ignoring reaping definitions in IBJJF.
  • Late taps.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Are heel hooks legal in IBJJF gi?

Generally no in standard adult gi divisions. Confirm the current official rulebook—rules evolve.

What about IBJJF no-gi?

Adult no-gi legality has expanded in recent years, but specifics depend on the active rules and division. Verify before competing.

Why are they considered dangerous?

Rotational stress on the knee can injure ligaments quickly, sometimes before pain peaks. Early taps and good coaching mitigate risk.

Inside vs outside—what’s the difference?

Foot/knee orientation and control mechanics differ; your coach’s system will define the entries.

Can beginners learn heel hooks?

Only under coaches who emphasize positions, control, and tapping culture—not as a crank to try on day one.

Heel hook or kneebar for no-gi?

Both. Heel hooks dominate modern no-gi metas; kneebars remain essential. See kneebar.

What is reaping?

A rule concept about heel position across the knee line—central to IBJJF legality. Learn the definition from the rulebook, not only from social media.

The Bottom Line

The heel hook rotates the foot to attack the knee from modern entanglements—core to no-gi, heavily restricted in standard IBJJF gi. Know the rules, tap early, and compare with the kneebar for straight-knee attacks.
