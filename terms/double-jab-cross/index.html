What Is Double Jab Cross?

This is how coaches upgrade a single One-Two: the extra jab disrupts Parry timing and closes Boxing Range.

Builds directly on Batch 2’s Double Jab page; here the emphasis is the finished string with the cross.

Parent: Combination. Compare with Double Jab and One-Two.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Double Jab Cross behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Double Jab Cross Works

Throw two complete jabs—each extends and recovers. Vary rhythm (equal, light-hard, pause-second).

Step on one or both jabs if you need space. Fire the Cross on the Power Line as the second jab returns.

Optional: Feint the first jab so the second and cross arrive against a committed reaction.

Mechanically, successful work with Double Jab Cross is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, double jab cross lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill double jab cross daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Classic pad call: “one-one-two.”

Fight: double jab to the gloves, cross down the pipe when they lift the lead hand.

Fight picture: double jab cross appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply double jab cross after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Stepping double jab cross

Both jabs advance; cross lands as you settle mid-range.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Pause before the cross

Tiny hitch after jab two so they open—then rear hand.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Second jab to body

Common Mistakes

  • Lazy first jab that never recovers.
  • Walking onto a {counterpunch} chin-up.
  • Never throwing the cross after the double.
  • Treating double jab cross as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox double jab cross inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so double jab cross connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether double jab cross appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe double jab cross as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put double jab cross behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep double jab cross scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on double jab cross, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Double jab without cross?

Useful, but the cross is why you stole the range—see double-jab for the jab-only skill.

Triple jab cross?

Valid longer string; master double first.

Power on which jab?

Usually second jab or the cross—avoid equal max power on all three.

The Bottom Line

Double jab cross is range theft with a receipt—two looks, then the rear hand collects.
