Rashguard

A rashguard is a tight athletic top that protects skin, manages sweat, and serves as the standard upper garment for no-gi—and often as an underlayer beneath the gi.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Primary jobs: reduce mat burn, limit skin-to-skin abrasion, wick sweat.
  • Default no-gi top; common under the gi jacket.
  • Competition legality of logos/underlayers depends on the event book.
  • Fit should be compressive without choking mobility.
  • Hygiene: wash every session—same as a gi.

What Is Rashguard?

Born from surf culture, rashguards entered grappling because shared mats and skin contact spread irritation and infections when people train bare-chested or in cotton tees. Compression fabric is harder for fingers to accidentally grip than loose cotton.

In no-gi, the rashguard is part of the uniform language with shorts or spats. In gi rooms, wearing one under the jacket reduces collar burn and keeps the kimono cleaner against skin.

It does not replace skill—but it removes unnecessary friction injuries so you can focus on rolling and drilling.

How Rashguard Works

Choose long-sleeve for maximum abrasion coverage or short-sleeve for heat; both are common.

Pair with board shorts or spats per gym rules; avoid pockets and zippers that injure partners.

For tournaments, confirm whether under-gi shirts are allowed and how patches are treated.

Rotate at least two so one can dry fully.

Gi vs No-Gi

Under gi: reduces burn; some federations regulate visible underlayers—verify.

No-gi: the rashguard is the jacket substitute.

Never substitute a baggy cotton shirt in serious no-gi rooms.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex stops getting shoulder mat burn after switching to long-sleeve rashguards for open-mat marathons.

Jordan’s loose tee bunches and gives accidental handles—coach bans cotton tops for no-gi.

Sam checks IBJJF attire notes before wearing a ranked rashguard on competition day.

Types / Variations

Short sleeve

Cooler; more forearm skin exposure.

Long sleeve

Max coverage for passes and turtle scrambles.

Ranked designs

Display belt color cues in no-gi—house dependent.

Competition cut

Minimal seams/logos aimed at event checks.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries to good gear habits: buy fitted grappling-specific shirts, not random compression tees with huge seams.

Counters to skin issues: wash gear, shower, and skip training with active infections.

Escapes from gear drama: read the gym’s dress code once and follow it.

Rashguard vs Gi Jacket

Factor Rashguard Gi jacket
Grip handles Minimal (by design) Abundant fabric grips
Protection Abrasion / hygiene Abrasion + structure
Rank display Optional prints Belt on kimono
Primary context No-gi (+ under gi) Gi training & many tournaments

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Uniform rules specify what may be worn under or instead of the kimono. Check the current book for your division. ADCC-style events mandate no-gi attire norms of their own.

Safety

Clean gear lowers infection risk. Do not share sweaty rashguards. Tap early regardless of brand.

Common mistakes

  • Training sick with open skin wounds.
  • Wearing rashguards with abrasive screen prints that sandpaper partners.
  • Assuming any MMA shirt passes every federation check.
  • Never washing “just one more round” gear.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why wear a rashguard?

Skin protection, hygiene, and sweat management—especially in no-gi.

Under the gi?

Common and often encouraged for training.

Long or short sleeve?

Preference and temperature; long covers more skin.

Are logos OK in competition?

Depends on the event—verify.

Spats required?

Gym-dependent; useful for leg friction and modesty.

Can partners grip my rashguard?

Usually discouraged/illegal to hold clothing in no-gi—follow the rule set.

How often to wash?

After every session.

The Bottom Line

A rashguard is small gear with outsized hygiene and friction benefits. Wear clean ones, especially when the gi comes off.
