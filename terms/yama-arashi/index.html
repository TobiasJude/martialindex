Yama Arashi

Yama arashi is the mountain storm: a rare Kodokan throw combining a distinctive grip with a harai-like sweeping action—related to, but not identical with, Harai Goshi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

What is Yama Arashi?

Yama arashi (山嵐 / mountain storm) is a rare Kodokan throwing technique remembered for a strong gripping structure paired with a sweeping hip/leg action reminiscent of harai-goshi. Martial Index includes it because catalogue judo includes rare storms—not only weekly contest engines.

Primary compare is Harai Goshi: both sweep, but yama-arashi’s classical identity emphasises a particular same-side/cross gripping story that teachers use to distinguish it from ordinary harai. Parent: Nage-Waza. Also see Te-Waza discussions depending on classification emphasis in your syllabus—follow your federation’s teaching charts while using this page for mechanics literacy.

Searchers usually want “is this just harai-goshi with a fancy name?”—no. The grip and action package is the point of the classical name.

On the judo hub, rare throws keep the art from collapsing into five techniques.

How it works

Secure the characteristic grips your coach uses for yama-arashi (classical descriptions emphasise strong sleeve/lapel relationships that freeze uke’s posture). Break balance, then execute a harai-like sweep with the hip/leg while the grips keep uke from circling out.

If grips are merely “normal harai grips,” you may be throwing harai-goshi—name it honestly. Yama-arashi literacy is partly about recognising the gripping difference on film and in kata-oriented teaching.

Finish through the sweep to a clear back landing for scoring criteria. On failure, do not hold illegal stalls; switch to another attack such as O Goshi or Tai Otoshi.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

Gi grips are the identity. No-gi “mountain storm” is not a standard name—train harai-like sweeps under other labels. Transfers: sweep timing. Does not transfer: the classical grip name without a jacket.

What it looks like in practice

Yama-arashi appears more in classical study than in every Grand Slam final. Treat that as distribution, not dismissal. Film comparison with Harai Goshi is the best teacher after a live coach.

In Randori, attempt only when grips truly match the technique you are naming.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter from the grip structure, not from a random harai entry. Counters: break the grips, posture, circle inside the sweep. Combinations: threaten yama grips → harai or tai-otoshi if uke over-defends one line.

Compare

Versus Harai Goshi: shared sweeping flavour, different classical grip identity. Versus O Goshi: major hip load without the same storm grip story. Versus Uchi Mata (Judo): inner-thigh reap family rather than this rare storm.

IJF notes & safety

Yama-arashi as ordinary nage-waza is legal when grips and actions comply with current rules. No special ban attaches to the name itself. Follow Judo Rules for gripping legality and safety.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every harai “yama-arashi.”
  • Ignoring the grip distinction.
  • Forcing rare throws as weekly tokui without fundamentals.
  • Poor ukemi on sweeping amplitudes.

Training notes

Study yama-arashi beside harai-goshi in a compare-and-contrast block. Film grips first, sweep second.

Keep rare throws in quarterly literacy sessions even if they never become your shiai engine.

Study notes

Rare throws test whether a dojo is an art school or only a contest factory. You do not need yama-arashi to win locals; you may need it to understand why Kodokan catalogues are wide.

Grip literacy from yama-arashi study often improves ordinary Harai Goshi because athletes finally watch how sleeves freeze posture before the sweep.

Demo teams sometimes overplay mountain-storm drama. Prefer clarity of grips over theatrical spinning that confuses students watching from the wall.

Deep notes

Archival and Kodokan teaching clips (including the official mountain-storm demonstration) repay slow watching: freeze on the grips before the sweep. Most mislabels happen because viewers only watch the leg.

Comparative uchi-komi: five grips of yama-arashi intent, five ordinary harai grips, spoken labels each time. Athletes leave knowing whether they can feel a difference—if they cannot, they are not ready to claim the rare name in randori.

Tournament reality check: if your tokui-waza list starts with yama-arashi and skips harai, reorder priorities. Literacy first; specialty later.

Related hip/sweep map: Harai Goshi, O Goshi, Uchi Mata (Judo). Keep yama-arashi as the rare storm in that weather system.
