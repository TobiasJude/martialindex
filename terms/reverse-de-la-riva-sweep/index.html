Reverse De La Riva Sweep

The reverse De La Riva sweep is a reverse from reverse De La Riva that uses the inside hook and a hip foot to tripod-dump or invert—clustered with kiss of the dragon, DLR sweep, and DLR pass.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • RDLR sweep = inside hook + off-balance from RDLR.
  • Tripod is the high-percentage white-to-blue pattern; invert/back takes are the modern branch.
  • Works gi and no-gi; gi adds pants/sleeve, no-gi emphasizes underhooks.
  • Top answers: DLR/RDLR passing, long-step, knee-cut after clearing the hook.
  • Failed sweep → kiss of the dragon or recompose open guard.

What Is Reverse De La Riva Sweep?

Reverse De La Riva sweep covers tripod dumps, dummy sweeps, and invert-to-back chains from the RDLR hook (your foot inside their near thigh while you control the ankle/pants). Parent: reverse De La Riva. Across: DLR sweep, tripod, berimbolo.

How It Works

Secure the RDLR hook and ankle control, place the free foot on the hip, elevate and hook the far ankle if needed, then kick them back. Reinsert the hook as they fall so you can come up or spin to the back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grip on the hooked leg + sleeve/collar assists the dump.

No-gi. Underhook/ankle rides replace pants; same tripod geometry.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Passer steps in for knee-cut → you catch RDLR → hip foot → tripod dump → you rise into their former half or take the back if they turn.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Classic RDLR tripod sweep.

Competition variation

Dummy sweep / sit-up wrestle-up when they overcommit weight forward.

Related option

Kiss of the dragon / invert to back when the dump stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Vs knee-cut; from open guard. Counters. Clear the hook early—see DLR pass and long-step families.

How It Compares

Vs orthodax DLR sweep: RDLR hooks inside the thigh line; DLR hooks outside. Vs berimbolo: berimbolo is a dedicated invert system often entered from DLR/RDLR.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Legal IBJJF and ADCC at adult levels; verify youth inversion rules on IBJJF rules / ADCC rules.

Safety

Control the fall—do not dump partners onto their head/neck in drilling.

Common mistakes

  • Flat on your back without a hip foot.
  • Releasing the ankle too early.
  • Ignoring the back-take branch when they turn.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a reverse De La Riva sweep?

A reverse from RDLR using the inside hook and hip foot—often a tripod dump or invert to back.

Does it work no-gi?

Yes—ankle/underhook control replaces pants grips.

How do you set it up?

Catch RDLR vs their step-in, add hip foot, elevate and dump or invert.

What should you chain next?

Come up on top, or kiss-of-the-dragon / back take.

Common mistake?

No hip foot, or releasing the hook before you reverse.

Is it beginner-friendly?

Tripod yes; invert branches are intermediate.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Under reverse De La Riva next to DLR and berimbolo.

The Bottom Line

Reverse De La Riva sweep is the RDLR offense tree—tripod first, then invert/back. Pair it with pass study so both roles improve.
