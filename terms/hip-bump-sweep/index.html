Hip Bump Sweep

The hip bump sweep is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu closed-guard reverse where you sit up, bump your hip into the top player’s chest/shoulder line, and roll them over—classically chained with kimura and guillotine.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Sit up, bump, come to mount—posture break first.
  • Pillar: closed guard. Cluster: scissor, flower, pendulum, elevator.
  • Kimura and guillotine are mandatory sibling attacks when they post.
  • Works gi and no-gi; no-gi underhooks help.
  • Do not sit up into a stacked pass without frames.

What Is Hip Bump Sweep?

Hip bump (upa-style from closed) sits you up into their posture, uses a hip bump to off-balance, and finishes in mount. It is a closed-guard pillar technique and a gateway to upper-body submissions. It is a key branch of closed guard.

Across Batch 4: scissor, flower, pendulum; submissions kimura, guillotine.

How Hip Bump Sweep Works

Open guard with control, sit up toward one hip, bump through their chest line while blocking a post, and roll through to mount.

Top answers: posture hard, sprawl weight, catch the underhook, or pass as they sit (toreando/knee cut opportunities).

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control helps stop posts.

No-gi. Underhook and wrist control; head position critical.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Hip bump to mount; if they post the arm, kimura. If they duck the head, guillotine. If bump fails, return to scissor/flower grips.

Types / Variations

Classic hip bump to mount

Sit-up bump reverse.

Hip bump to kimura

Post creates the figure-four.

Hip bump to guillotine

Head duck creates the choke.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: broken posture closed guard, failed scissor. Counters: heavy posture, smash pass as they sit. Always train submission branches.

Hip Bump vs Scissor Sweep

Factor Hip Bump Scissor
Body shape Sit-up Back flat / shin across
Attack chain Kimura / guillotine Triangle / armbar often
Pillar Closed guard Closed guard
Risk Getting stacked/passed while sitting Getting posture-passed

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Fundamental in IBJJF curricula.

Safety

Do not bump into a stacked neck. Control your base when sitting up so you are not slammed in rooms that allow it—sport context matters.

Common mistakes

  • Sitting up with no post control.
  • No submission branch.
  • Ignoring closed-guard pillar links.
  • Bumping into a ready passer without frames.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the hip bump sweep?

A BJJ sweep that reverses top/bottom using the hip bump sweep mechanics described above.

Gi or no-gi?

Both, with different grips. Gi uses sleeves/pants/collar; no-gi uses underhooks, head control, and hooks.

Where does it sit in the catalog?

Under closed guard; across to scissor, flower, kimura.

Good for white belts?

Often yes when drilled slowly with a clear hip path—confirm with your coach for your body type.

What if it fails?

Chain to a sibling sweep or attack (armbar/triangle/kimura) instead of forcing one path.

Hip bump to kimura?

Yes—the post that stops the sweep often gives the kimura.

Works in no-gi?

Yes; underhooks replace sleeve grips.

The Bottom Line

Hip bump is the sit-up engine of closed guard: sweep, kimura, or guillotine. Keep it wired to the closed-guard pillar and scissor/flower cluster.
