Folding Pass

The folding pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu pressure pass that collapses the bottom player’s legs toward their chest, removes framing space, and walks the hips past into side control or north-south.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Fold the knees + glue the chest + walk—do not hover over frames.
  • Flagship cousin of the smash pass and pressure passing family.
  • Gi pants and sleeve grips slow the shrimp; no-gi needs underhooks and head position.
  • High chest invites inversion—stay heavy and low through the fold.
  • Chain with headquarters, knee cut, and body locks when the fold stalls.

What Is Folding Pass?

The folding pass is a pressure guard pass: you force the opponent’s knees together or toward their chest, remove the space that makes frames and butterfly hooks dangerous, and walk your hips past the legs into a pin. It sits in the same family as the smash pass—many gyms use the names interchangeably—but “folding” emphasizes the deliberate collapse of the shin line before the walk. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

Pedagogically it is taught after students can hold chest-to-chest weight: white and blue belts learn a simple fold from open or butterfly; black belts treat folding as a system entry from headquarters, over-under, and body-lock failures.

How Folding Pass Works

Mechanically the fold solves three problems: control the legs, remove framing height, and clear the knee line while shoulders stay flat.

Leg control. Pants grips, ankle pins, or shin traps gather both legs. The goal is a single package the chest can sit on—not two independent frames.

Chest glue. Once the package is made, drive your sternum onto the shins or the near hip so the bottom player cannot shrimp into space. Incremental hip walks beat one explosive shove.

Bottom player’s job. Frame early, create knee space, invert or shrimp before the glue sets, and threaten underhooks the moment the passer floats.

Gi vs No-Gi

The skeletal motion is shared. The handles and timing are not.

Gi. Pants cuffs and sleeve grips let you glue ankles and stall shrimp attempts. Collar drives help flatten the near shoulder once you clear. Retention answers include lasso, spider, and collar-sleeve frames that stop the fold before it starts.

No-gi. Without fabric you win underhooks, head position, and hip heaviness. Knee shields and butterfly hooks return faster, so the fold must be decisive—hovering mid-fold is how you get swept or entangled.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From butterfly or seated open guard, the passer pins both ankles, drives the chest onto the shins, folds the knees toward the chest, and walks the near hip past until the far knee clears. Side control or north-south follows when the near underhook (or crossface) is won.

Against a knee shield, the fold may start as a smash on the shield or a hip switch that clears the frame before the legs are packaged. Against reverse de la Riva, clearing the hook usually comes before any honest fold.

Types / Variations

Fold from butterfly / seated

Pin ankles or shins, remove hook elevation, and fold both knees under the chest before walking past.

Over-under into fold

Win the over-under structure, then collapse the near knee line and walk—common when a pure over-under stalls.

Headquarters fold

From headquarters, gather the inside leg and outside ankle into one package, then fold and clear rather than cutting.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: failed toreando that meets stiff feet, butterfly smash setups, body-lock failures that leave the legs free to package, and half-guard smash into a full fold.

Counters (bottom): early frames on hips and biceps, invert before the chest lands, underhook the floating hip, and recover closed or butterfly the moment space appears.

Top contingencies: if they invert, do not chase the fold into an unplanned leg entanglement; if they lock half, treat it as half-guard passing; if they underhook, flatten or switch to a knee cut or long step.

Folding Pass vs Smash Pass

Factor Folding Pass Smash Pass
Emphasis Collapse / package the shin line first Chest pressure and walk, often named interchangeably
Typical start Butterfly, seated, HQ package Open, half, or HQ with heavy chest
Failure mode Float mid-fold → underhook / invert High chest → shrimp or invert
Cousin links Pressure passing, over-under Stack, body lock, pressure passing

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi and no-gi with no special belt restrictions. In ADCC-style rules the fold remains a high-percentage pressure option; stalling while hovering over the package can draw referee attention in some no-gi events.

Safety

Drive weight through the hips and chest, not the neck. Do not crank the spine while stacking a folded opponent—especially in youth and hobbyist rooms. Tap early if trapped under a fold with no frame.

Common mistakes

  • Hovering over the shins without chest glue—frames return instantly.
  • Folding only one leg while the other keeps a butterfly hook.
  • Walking past before the near shoulder is flattened.
  • Chasing the fold after a successful invert instead of resetting posture.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the folding pass in BJJ?

A pressure pass that collapses the opponent’s legs toward the chest, removes framing space, and walks the hips past into a pin.

Is the folding pass the same as the smash pass?

Often yes in gym slang. Martial Index treats folding as the collapse-first emphasis inside the smash/pressure family—same geometry, slightly different teaching accent.

Does the folding pass work in no-gi?

Yes. Without pants grips you need underhooks, head position, and decisive hip heaviness so knee shields cannot reopen.

Why do I get swept when I try to fold?

Usually you floated mid-fold. Glue the chest, deny the underhook, and walk incrementally—or abandon into a safer pass.

What guard is folding best against?

Butterfly and seated open guards where both legs can be packaged; also useful after a knee shield is smashed flat.

How is folding different from a stack pass?

A stack lifts hips toward the head more vertically; a fold packages the legs under chest pressure and walks laterally or diagonally past. See stack pass.

Should beginners learn folding before toreando?

Learn both. Toreando teaches circling and grips; folding teaches pressure and connection.

The Bottom Line

The folding pass is pressure made literal: package the legs, glue the chest, and walk past. When it stalls, chain into smash, knee cut, or body-lock structures—do not hover.
