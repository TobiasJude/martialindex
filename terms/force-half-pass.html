Force Half Guard Pass

The force half guard pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu strategy that deliberately collapses open or butterfly into a half-guard configuration the top player can smash, weave, or slice through—under the guard passing pillar.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Force a half you like, then pass it—do not gift them deep half or lockdown.
  • Pillars: half guard top + guard passing / pressure passing.
  • Cluster: smash, leg weave, hip switch, knee slice, back step.
  • Clear the “line of feet” before forcing—classic Giles framing language.
  • Gi pants help pin the forced half; no-gi needs head pressure and underhooks.

What Is Force Half Guard Pass?

Forcing half means you choose half guard as an intermediate destination: you pin one of their legs between yours (or smash both into a half-like package) so explosive open-guard movement dies, then you pass with your half-guard top toolkit.

It is a strategy more than a single technique—linked up to guard passing and half guard, across to the Batch 4 half cluster (weave, hip switch, knee slice, folding) and live siblings smash / back-step.

How Force Half Guard Pass Works

Clear or control the line of feet so both hooks cannot elevate, drive connection into their hip line, trap one leg in a half you can pressure, then finish with smash, weave, switch, slice, or back-step.

Bottom player’s job. Keep both feet free, enter deep half on purpose, lockdown, or recover butterfly/closed before the trap sets.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeve grips help freeze one leg while you step into half. Collar drives flatten after the force.

No-gi. Underhooks, head position, and body locks replace fabric. Forcing half without upper-body connection gifts underhooks and sweeps.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Against butterfly, the passer pins ankles, clears elevation, forces a half-like trap on one leg, and smashes or weaves through—same pressure language as folding and smash.

Against standing open guard, after toreando fails, forcing half can be safer than another jump—then finish with knee cut / knee slice from the resulting headquarters.

Types / Variations

Clear line of feet → force half

Remove double-hook elevation, then trap one leg.

Body-lock force half

Use body lock to collapse into a passable half.

Force half into knee slice / smash

The force is only the entry; finish with the half-passing cluster.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: stalled toreando/X, butterfly smash setups, body-lock failures that leave one free leg. Counters: deep half, lockdown, invert, knee shield reset. Contingency: if they win deep half, abandon force-half ego and fight the deep half top game.

Force Half vs Smash Pass

Factor Force Half Smash Pass
Idea Create a passable half first Chest-smash legs and walk
Pillar Half + guard passing Pressure + guard passing
Often sequenced? Force → smash/weave/slice Smash may skip named half
Failure Deep half / lockdown Invert / underhook

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Forcing half is ubiquitous in IBJJF and ADCC-style grappling; watch stalling if you sit in forced half without progress.

Safety

Do not crank the trapped knee while forcing. Avoid dumping weight onto a twisted ankle during the collapse.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing half without clearing feet—getting butterfly-swept.
  • Gifting deep half by diving the wrong hip.
  • No finish plan after the force (smash/weave/slice).
  • Ignoring pillar exits back to toreando when half is losing.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does force half mean?

Deliberately collapsing open or butterfly into a half-guard trap you can pass with your half top game.

Is force half a pass by itself?

It is an entry strategy. The pass finishes via smash, weave, hip switch, knee slice, or back-step.

Force half vs knee cut?

Force half creates the half/HQ; knee cut/slice often finishes once the line is there.

Gi or no-gi?

Both. Gi grips help the trap; no-gi needs stronger upper-body connection.

Why do I get deep-halved?

You forced the wrong hip or lost head position. See deep half.

Good against closed guard?

Not until open. See closed guard opening first.

Catalog placement?

Under guard passing / half guard; cluster with weave, hip switch, knee slice, smash, folding.

The Bottom Line

Force half is intentional geography: make a half you can pass, then use the half-passing cluster. Link up to half guard and guard passing—never orphan the force without a finish.
