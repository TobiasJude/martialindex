Kimura Defense

Kimura defense protects the shoulder against kimura attacks from guard, side, and north-south. Elbow to ribs, grip breaks, and careful rotations beat panic pulls. Sibling: americana defense.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Parent: kimura. Rule one: elbow glued to ribs.
  • Break or neutralize the figure-four before they separate the arm.
  • Related: americana defense, armbar defense.
  • Do not straighten into their torque.
  • Tap to shoulder pain—rotator injuries linger.

What Is Kimura Defense?

Defenses versus kimura (ude-garami family, palm-down figure-four). Up: escape. Across: americana, americana defense.

How It Works

Keep the threatened elbow tight to the body. Clasp hands or grab your own clothing/leg. If the figure-four locks, walk toward the grip, straighten carefully to weaken their bend, or use a knee/hip post to create a counter wrist threat that forces a release—then extract.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Belt and lapel grips help you connect; their sleeve grips fuel the kimura—strip when possible.

No-gi. Wrist control and body connection replace fabric; expect kimura traps into back takes.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Kimura from closed guard → elbow in + clasp → posture and stack → extract arm → pass.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Elbow-in prevention.

Competition variation

Grip-break / walk-to-the-hands escapes.

Related option

Kimura-trap awareness when they abandon the finish for the back.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by kimura attempts. Offense answers: better hip position, figure-four tightness, switch to armbar or back take.

How It Compares

Vs americana defense: opposite shoulder rotation (kimura typically attacks the near arm with palm down behind the back).

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal.

Safety

Defense pages prioritize survival: protect neck/joints first, create frames, then escape. Tap early in live training; do not ego through chokes or joint locks. Shoulder ligaments: tap early.

Common mistakes

  • Extending the arm to “power out.”
  • Ignoring the back-take branch of the kimura trap.
  • Fighting only the hands while hips are already under you.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is kimura defense?

Habits and escapes that stop figure-four shoulder locks.

First rule?

Elbow stays glued to the ribs.

How do I break the grip?

Clasp hands, walk toward their figure-four, and remove the bend that fuels torque.

Kimura trap?

They may skip the finish to take the back—defend both.

Vs americana?

Opposite rotational direction on the shoulder.

Tap cue?

Shoulder/rotator pain—do not wait for a pop.

Related notes?

Kimura, americana defense, armbar defense.

The Bottom Line

Kimura defense is shoulder survival—elbow in, break the figure-four, and never straighten into their torque.
