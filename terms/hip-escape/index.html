Hip Escape

The hip escape is the fundamental bottom movement that creates space by driving the hips away from pressure—commonly drilled as the shrimp. This page treats hip escape as the applied escape concept and leans on shrimp for the drill/pattern details.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Heavily overlaps shrimp—read that page for the drill mechanics.
  • Hip escape = using the hips to make frames work in live pins and passes.
  • Appears inside knee-on-belly escape, elbow escape, double-under escape.
  • Bridge slightly, push off a foot, hips out, re-square—then frame.
  • Do not confuse “hip escape” marketing names with a totally different move.

What Is Hip Escape?

In most gyms, hip escape and shrimp name the same family of movements: create distance on your side by moving the hips away from the opponent. Martial Index keeps both terms—shrimp for the foundational drill/pattern, hip escape for the concept as it appears inside named escapes. Up: escape, frame and shrimp.

How It Works

From your back or side, plant a foot, load the opposite shoulder, and push the hips laterally away while elbows stay tight. Reconnect the feet, re-square, and repeat. In live rolling, each hip escape inserts frames (forearms, knee shield) into the space you just made.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeve frames fill the space the hips create.

No-gi. Underhooks and forehead postures fill the same space without cloth.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Side control pressure → frame → hip escape to insert the knee → elbow escape to half or full guard.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Technical shrimp drill (warmup pattern)—see shrimp.

Competition variation

Applied hip escape under side control / mount pressure.

Related option

Hip escape chains into sit-outs and technical stand-ups.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Anytime bottom pressure appears. Related named pages: elbow escape, frame escape, north-south escape.

How It Compares

Vs shrimp: shrimp emphasizes the drilled movement; hip escape emphasizes the live escape concept. They should cross-link, not compete.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal movement.

Safety

Defense pages prioritize survival: protect neck/joints first, create frames, then escape. Tap early in live training; do not ego through chokes or joint locks.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing with the arms only and leaving the hips stuck.
  • Shrimping without inserting a frame (space vanishes).
  • Treating shrimp and hip escape as unrelated techniques.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is hip escape the same as shrimp?

Almost always the same movement family—shrimp is the common drill name; hip escape is the applied concept.

Why two pages?

So searches for either label land cleanly, with heavy cross-links.

Where is it used?

Nearly every bottom escape: side, mount, knee-on-belly, stack prevention.

Key mistake?

Moving the hips without inserting a frame into the new space.

Gi vs no-gi?

Same hips; different frames.

Related drill page?

Shrimp—start there for mechanics.

Related escapes?

Elbow escape, frame escape, knee-on-belly escape.

The Bottom Line

Hip escape is space-making with the hips—train the pattern on shrimp, then apply it inside every named bottom escape.
