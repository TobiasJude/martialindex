Invisible Collar

Invisible collar is Eddie Bravo's no-gi rubber-guard concept: using wrist, head, and arm structures as a "collar" substitute so Mission Control and related positions still freeze posture without cloth.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Invisible collar = no-gi stand-in for collar grips in rubber.
  • Keeps Mission Control honest without a gi.
  • Part of 10th Planet vocabulary—mechanics over mystique.
  • Pairs with Mission Control, New York, and triangle paths.
  • Parent rubber guard.

What Is Invisible Collar?

Invisible collar is a 10th Planet / Eddie Bravo term for no-gi controls that mimic what a collar grip does in the gi: freeze the head/posture line so rubber-guard structures (especially Mission Control) stay sticky. It is a concept embodied in specific grips—not a single universal hand placement. It is a key branch of closed guard.

How Invisible Collar Works

By controlling the near wrist/head complex in lieu of cloth, you stop them from posturing out of your shin-and-overhook game. That lets you climb to New York, enter waiting room, or hold Mission Control under punches in MMA. Without some invisible-collar idea, no-gi rubber collapses when they stand their posture up.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. This is the point of the concept.

Gi. Use a real collar—invisible collar is the substitute, not the default.

What Does Invisible Collar Look Like in Practice?

Alex hits Mission Control in no-gi, uses an invisible-collar structure on Jordan's head/wrist line to stop the posture, then climbs to New York or locks a triangle when Jordan reaches in.

Types / Variations

Basic invisible collar idea

Head/wrist posture freeze without cloth.

With Mission Control

The most common pairing—keeps the hub alive in no-gi.

Attacks it enables

New York, waiting-room triangles, armbars off rubber.

Defending invisible collar (top)

Clear the head/wrist control, posture, peel the shin.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Anytime you play rubber in no-gi and need posture control.

Bottom threats. All rubber finishes that need a frozen posture.

Top counters. Hand fight the invisible collar early; don't leave your head free in their rubber.

Invisible Collar vs Gi Collar

Factor Invisible Collar Gi Collar
Tool Wrist/head structures Actual collar grip
Context No-gi rubber Gi open/closed games
Feel Conceptual + specific grips Literal cloth control

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Concept matters most in no-gi/ADCC/MMA where collars do not exist.

Safety

Avoid cranking necks while drilling head controls. Tap early to chokes set from the same postures.

Common mistakes

  • Treating "invisible collar" as magic words without a concrete grip.
  • Playing rubber no-gi with zero posture control.
  • Forcing gi collar habits when there is no collar.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is invisible collar a specific grip?

It is a concept realized through specific no-gi grips (head/wrist structures). Learn the concrete details from a rubber-guard coach or curriculum.

Do I need invisible collar in the gi?

No—use the real collar. Invisible collar exists because no-gi has no collar.

Where does Eddie Bravo teach it?

Inside 10th Planet rubber-guard material (including MTS sessions). Pair it with Mission Control practice.

Invisible collar vs overhook?

Overhook is arm control; invisible collar targets posture/head line like a collar would. They work together in Mission Control.

How do I stop it on top?

Hand fight, free your head, posture, clear the shin before they climb.

Is this useful outside rubber guard?

Posture-control ideas transfer, but the name is rubber/10th Planet vocabulary.

The Bottom Line

Invisible collar is the no-gi posture freeze that keeps rubber guard alive without cloth—learn it with Mission Control. See rubber guard.
