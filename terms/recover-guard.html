Recover Guard

Recover guard means reclaiming a playable guard after a pass threat or pin—using frames, the shrimp, and knee-elbow connections. It sits next to recompose guard (rebuild structure mid-pass) and feeds the escape hierarchy.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Outcome, not a single move: get legs between you again.
  • Frames + hip escape create the insert pocket.
  • Half guard counts as a recover if it is active.
  • Then neutralize—don’t attack from a broken shell.
  • Sibling: recompose guard for mid-pass rebuilds.

What Is Recover Guard?

Recover guard is the destination of many escapes: you leave a pin or near-pass and put a functional guard back online.

Distinct from pure guard retention (never lose it) and lightly distinct from recompose guard (rebuild while the pass is still forming).

Success looks like closed, open, half, or butterfly with inside space and a plan—not just “legs somewhere near.”

How Recover Guard Works

Survive first: protect neck and inside elbows per escape hierarchy.

Create space with frames and a bridge or turn; shrimp to free the hips.

Insert knee/shin and reconnect to elbows; extend or pummel to a preferred guard.

Win posture or grips so the passer cannot instantly re-pass.

Cross-train with frame and shrimp and knee-elbow escape as primary engines.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel frames and pant grips help re-anchor after the insert.

No-gi. Underhooks, wrist rides, and shin frames replace cloth—speed of hip escape matters more.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a knee-cut nearly clears, Casey frames the hip, shrimps, and recovers closed guard.

Morgan escapes side control to half guard, then pummel-recovers full butterfly.

Types / Variations

Recover from side control

Frame-shrimp-knee into half or closed.

Recover from mount

Elbow escape path into half/guard.

Recover from near-pass

Invert, hip heist, or scramble re-guard.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by failed or partial passes, settled pins, and scramble landings. Counters: immediate re-pass, knee-on-belly, back-take on the turn. Pair with recompose guard so you have both mid-pass and post-pin answers.

Recover Guard vs Recompose Guard

Factor Recover guard Recompose guard
Timing After pin or clear pass threat During the pass, before it settles
Feel Escape → rebuild Retention mid-fight
Tools Frame-shrimp, upa chains Frames, underhooks, inversions, sticky legs
Goal Playable guard again Never fully lose the guard layer

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Recovering guard can deny further scoring advances; pass points may already be awarded—verify current IBJJF / ADCC language.

Safety

Don’t invert blindly under heavy stack pressure; protect the neck while you shrimp.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any leg entanglement a recovery.
  • Attacking before the guard is stable.
  • Ignoring half guard as a valid recover.
  • Confusing recover with recompose and drilling only one.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does recover guard mean?

Reclaim a functional guard after a pass threat or pin.

Is half guard enough?

Yes if it is active and stops the pass from finishing.

Vs recompose?

Recover is post-threat rebuild; recompose is mid-pass rebuild.

Main tools?

Frames, shrimp, knee-elbow, occasional bridge-and-roll.

After recovery?

Neutralize posture/grips before attacking.

Related pillar?

Guard—and the pin pillars you escaped.

Sport note?

Pass points may already count; still recover to stop the climb.

The Bottom Line

Recover guard is the return ticket to the bottom game—escape the pin, insert structure, then play again.
