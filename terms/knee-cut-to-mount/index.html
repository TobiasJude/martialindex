Knee Cut to Mount

Knee cut to mount is the finish path that turns knee-cut / knee-slice into mount instead of stopping in soft side control—clustered with HQ, pass to mount, and half hub.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Knee-cut can end in side or mount—mount needs hip climb.
  • Underhook + head control before you free the back leg.
  • Avoid landing in lazy kesa that they reverse.
  • Works from HQ and half.
  • Gi and no-gi.

What Is Knee Cut to Mount?

Knee cut to mount names the transition that completes a knee-slice by settling in mount. Parent pass: knee-cut pass. Goals: mount, sometimes via side. Related: pass to mount, HQ pass.

How It Works

Win underhook/crossface, cut the shin across their thigh, free your trailing leg, then walk hips toward their head/shoulder line and sit into mount rather than collapsing beside them.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar underhook hybrids help the climb.

No-gi. Head pressure and underhook; same hip walk.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From HQ knee-cut → underhook → clear trail leg → climb to mount before they shrimp.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Direct knee-cut to mount.

Competition variation

Knee-cut to side, then immediate mount transition.

Related option

Knee-cut to modified scarf, then mount (higher risk).

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. HQ, half top, open after toreando. Counters. Knee-cut counters, frames, underhook recovers.

How It Compares

Vs stopping at side: mount scores/controls differently—see pass to mount. Vs body-lock to mount: different entry pressure.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Mount scores in IBJJF; verify current point values on IBJJF rules.

Safety

Climb without driving shoulder into throat as a crank.

Common mistakes

  • Lazy side landing.
  • No underhook.
  • Freeing the trail leg late.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is knee cut to mount?

Finishing a knee-slice pass by settling in mount instead of soft side.

Do I have to hit mount every knee-cut?

No—side is fine; this page is the mount branch.

How do I not get reversed?

Underhook + head control, then climb hips.

Gi or no-gi?

Both.

Common mistake?

Landing beside them with no climb.

Beginner-friendly?

After basic knee-cut mechanics.

Martial Index home?

Under knee-cut-pass as a finish transition.

The Bottom Line

Knee cut to mount is intentional climbing—pair it with solid knee-cut mechanics and mount maintenance.
