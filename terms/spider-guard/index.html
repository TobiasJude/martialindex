Spider Guard

Spider guard is a gi open-guard system that plants the feet on the opponent's biceps while gripping the sleeves—extending to break posture, sweep, and set triangles, with a natural transition into lasso when a hand circles.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Feet on biceps + sleeve grips = spider.
  • Extend to break posture; off-balance to sweep.
  • Gi-native; do not expect true spider in no-gi.
  • Pairs tightly with lasso guard.
  • Common finishes: sweeps, triangle, omoplata.

What Is Spider Guard?

Spider guard is a gi-forward open guard where you grip both sleeves (or one) and place the soles of your feet on the opponent's biceps, extending your legs to break their posture and control their hands. It is a staple of IBJJF sport gi and pairs naturally with lasso.

How Spider Guard Works

Sleeve grips freeze their hands; feet on biceps turn their arms into stiff posts you can bend by extending and retracting your legs. Off-balancing left-right creates sweep angles; pulling one arm while framing the other sets triangles and omoplatas. If they circle a hand, you can transition to lasso by wrapping the sleeve with your leg.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Spider is native here. Double sleeve grips + biceps feet are the reference. Combine with collar grips for extra posture breaks.

No-gi. True spider dies without sleeves. Athletes borrow the idea—feet on hips/shoulders and wrist control—but call it open guard or collar-tie variants, not spider. Do not expect sleeve spider mechanics in rashguards.

What Does Spider Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex grips both of Jordan's sleeves, plants both feet on the biceps, and extends to flatten Jordan's posture. Alex pulls the right sleeve while retracting the right leg, sweeps Jordan to that side, and comes up to mount.

Types / Variations

Double spider

Both feet on biceps, both sleeves—classic control.

One spider / one hook

One foot on a bicep, the other on a hip or in DLR—hybrid play.

Spider-lasso

One side spider, one side lasso—high-level gi tangle.

Sitting spider

More upright torso for different sweep angles.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. From closed guard by opening with sleeve grips; from seated guard; after a failed pass when sleeves are free.

Passes. Grip break, smash, knee cut after clearing a foot, torreando when feet float, pressure into half.

Counters. Re-establish a foot, switch to lasso, or invert under the pass.

Spider Guard vs Lasso Guard

Factor Spider Lasso
Foot position Sole on bicep Leg wrapped around their arm/sleeve
Control feel Posture break via extension Tighter entanglement, harder to clear
Mobility Higher foot mobility Lower; more sticky
Common pair Often combines with lasso Often combines with spider

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Spider guard is legal in IBJJF gi. It is largely irrelevant in no-gi competition. Sleeve grip breaks and stalling rules still apply—keep advancing.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Do not hyperextend partners' elbows by violently kicking the biceps; apply controlled pressure.

Common mistakes

  • Feet on biceps with loose sleeve grips—instant pass.
  • Static spider with no off-balancing.
  • Trying to force spider in no-gi.
  • Forgetting the lasso transition when they circle a hand.
  • Extending so hard you lose your own hips on the mat.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Can I play spider guard in no-gi?

Not in the true sense. Without sleeves the biceps-foot control disappears. Use wrist-and-hip open guard instead.

Why do my feet keep slipping off the biceps?

Your sleeve grips are loose or you are pointing toes wrong. Pinch the sleeve, keep the foot sticky on the bicep, and adjust angle when they turn the arm.

Spider or lasso?

Spider for posture breaking and mobility; lasso when you need a stickier tangle. High-level gi often mixes both.

What submissions come from spider?

Triangles, omoplatas, and armbars after a sweep or posture break are the standards.

How do people pass spider?

Break a sleeve, clear a foot, then knee cut or torreando. Do not lean into both feet with bent posture.

Is spider outdated?

Still excellent in IBJJF gi. Less central in no-gi metas—but the posture-breaking lesson remains.

The Bottom Line

Spider guard breaks posture with feet on biceps and sleeve grips—a gi open-guard classic that feeds sweeps and triangles. When a hand circles, graduate to lasso; for standing hooks without sleeves, see De La Riva.
