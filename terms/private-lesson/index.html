Private Lesson

A private lesson is scheduled 1:1 (or small-group) coaching time focused on your gaps—complementing group class, not replacing community rolling.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Privates accelerate troubleshooting when you arrive with a clear problem.
  • Best after you have base mat hours—so feedback sticks.
  • Bring questions, film (if allowed), and a notebook; leave with drills.
  • Complements open mat and class; does not replace live partners.
  • Price and coach availability vary—no affiliate spam, just practical framing.

What Is a Private Lesson?

A private lesson is paid instructional time with a coach focused on your game: escapes, passes, competition film, or fundamentals repair. It is academy culture infrastructure under Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

How It Works

You book a slot, state goals, drill with the coach or a training partner, and leave with homework. Some privates are technique-only; others include positional sparring.

Gi vs No-Gi

Specify uniform when booking so grips and legalities match your goals (and your competition book if relevant).

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You show three failed mount escapes on video; the coach installs two frames and a single drill; you repeat them at the next open mat.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Technique privates; competition film review; kids privates; semi-privates (2–3 students).

Gym / sport variants

Some schools restrict privates to members; drop-in privates exist at others. Policies differ—ask politely.

Beginner notes

New students often get more from consistent fundamentals classes first. A private can still help if anxiety or a specific fear blocks you—see first BJJ class.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Arrive with one primary goal. Record (with permission). Schedule open mat within 48 hours to install. Do not collect unused instructionals in your head.

How It Compares

More individualized than class; less live chaos than open mat. Different from watching online instructionals alone—feedback loops matter.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Competition privates should match the rulebook you will fight under (IBJJF vs ADCC).

Safety

Communicate injuries. Privates are still live training risk.

Common mistakes

No homework; shopping coaches weekly without installing anything; using privates to avoid hard rounds forever.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Are private lessons worth it?

They can be—when you bring a clear problem and install the homework on the mats.

Private vs group class?

Privates customize; classes build community curriculum and partner variety.

How often?

Many athletes do occasional privates around camps or plateaus—not daily forever.

What should I bring?

Goals, questions, notebook, and optional film if allowed.

Beginner privates?

Useful for confidence; fundamentals classes still matter most early.

Gi or no-gi private?

Match the uniform to your goal and competition ruleset.

Where does it fit?

Academy-culture coaching concept under Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

The Bottom Line

Book a private lesson with one problem, leave with drills, and prove them at open mat within days.
