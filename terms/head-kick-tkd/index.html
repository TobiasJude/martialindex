Head Kick (Olgul Chagi)

Olgul chagi is the head-target family, not one path. Most sport heads are Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick); axes and hooks also live here. Scoring is Electronic Scoring (PSS) plus judges.

What Is Head Kick (Olgul Chagi)?

Olgul chagi (얼굴차기) means a kick to the head. The path may be turning, downward (Naeryeo Chagi (Axe Kick)), hooking, or spinning.

WT Kyorugi (Sparring) pays extra for legal head contact compared with Body Kick (Momtong Chagi). That is why athletes hunt it even when the percentage is low.

This slug is not Muay Thai high-kick. Martial Index keeps a TKD compound so the arts stay distinct.

Headgear and PSS head sensors changed how “clean” looks. A pretty kick that misses the sensor is still zero.

Martial Index files Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) Works

Get hip turn and Pivot Foot (Taekwondo) honest at body height first, then climb.

Lean just enough to clear their shoulder—not so much you cannot retract.

Hands stay up. Head-kickers who drop the opposite hand get kicked on the way in.

If they are tall, use Lead-Leg Kicking and a Feint Kick body look rather than a hero rear-leg swing.

Mechanically, successful head kick (olgul chagi) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, head kick (olgul chagi) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill head kick (olgul chagi) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Head-kick point values and valid surfaces have shifted across World Taekwondo Rules cycles; coaches should quote the chart on the wall, not a memory from 2012.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Lead body Fast Kick (Bal Bal), then lead head when the guard drops.

Coach holds a head paddle at a realistic height—not a fantasy ceiling.

Fight picture: head kick (olgul chagi) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply head kick (olgul chagi) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Turning head kick

Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) to the head—the volume option.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Axe / downward head

Naeryeo Chagi (Axe Kick) to the skull or collarbone line.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Spinning head kick

Spinning Hook Kick, Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning), Tornado Kick.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Common Mistakes

  • Throwing head every time you are losing.
  • No body threat, so they wait for the head.
  • Kicking with a floppy foot that never meets the sensor.
  • Landing with the back turned.
  • Training only on air.
  • Treating head kick (olgul chagi) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick head kick (olgul chagi) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so head kick (olgul chagi) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether head kick (olgul chagi) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe head kick (olgul chagi) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put head kick (olgul chagi) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Olgul vs dollyo?

Olgul is the target. Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) is a common path to that target.

Why so many points?

World Taekwondo Rules assign higher values to legal head kicks; confirm the current chart.

PSS head?

Electronic Scoring (PSS) head sensors plus judge calls depending on the cycle.

Safe training?

Controlled paddles, headgear, and a coach. Encyclopedia pages are not live instruction.

ITF?

ITF head kicks exist under different contact and scoring conventions.

The Bottom Line

Hunt the head as a high-value target—not as a personality. Body threats make heads real.
