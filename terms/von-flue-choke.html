Von Flue Choke

The Von Flue choke is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu (and MMA) counter that finishes when an opponent keeps a guillotine after you clear their legs to side control—your shoulder and head weave cut the neck against their own arm.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Opposite-side pass is the rule of thumb when your head is trapped.
  • Gable grip + shoulder dig beats raw chest smash.
  • It punishes people who will not release a failed guillotine.
  • Works gi and no-gi; cage walls can amplify pressure in MMA.
  • Teach the “let go” habit as hard as the finish.

What Is Von Flue Choke?

Named after Jason Von Flue’s UFC finish, the Von Flue choke appears when the bottom player insists on squeezing a guillotine after the top player has passed to the far side. Their own biceps becomes half the choke; your shoulder and clasped hands supply the rest.

Catalog-wise it is a side-control sibling and a direct answer inside the guillotine cluster—including necktie mistakes that leave you stuck on top of a hung arm.

How Von Flue Choke Works

When caught in a guillotine, move away from the choking arm’s side (opposite-side pass). Clear the legs, trap their choking wrist/forearm with your far shoulder/body, weave a hand behind their head, clasp (often Gable), then dig the near shoulder into the neck while turning the hip toward their head.

Pass discipline. Passing to the same side as the choke tightens their guillotine—do not “help” them.

Finish. Settle weight toward the head; small rolls of the shoulder often matter more than frantic squeezing.

If they wisely release, you already own side control—take the pin.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Same geometry; lapels can assist the head weave but are optional.

No-Gi / MMA. Classic home. Cage or wall walking (sometimes called “Super Von Flue” themes) can add vertical pressure when available—train the mat version first.

Either way, the lesson is cultural: release failed guillotines once the pass is real.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Jordan hits an arm-in guillotine as Alex passes. Alex clears to the far side, clasps behind Jordan’s head, digs the shoulder, and Jordan taps—or should have let go two seconds earlier.

In training, coaches often pause here to rehearse the verbal “let go” habit before rewarding the finish.

Types / Variations

Classic Gable-grip Von Flue

Palm-to-palm behind the head with shoulder dig—the standard teaching model.

Wall / cage amplified pressure

Feet on a vertical surface to load more weight into the neck when rules and venue allow.

Half-guard stubborn variants

When they keep half guard but refuse to release, similar shoulder geometry can still appear after you free the hips.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: any guillotine they refuse to abandon after a far-side pass—closed guard jumps, arm-in from scrambles, or necktie failures that leave you on top.

Counters for the bottom player: release early, turn into them, recover guard, or fight the clasp before the shoulder settles.

Escapes late: create space under the shoulder, shrimp, and reclaim frames—tap if vision tunnels.

Von Flue vs Guillotine

Factor Von Flue Choke Guillotine (hung)
Role Counter finish Primary attack that failed
Position Top side control-ish Bottom trying to finish
Key mistake punished — Not releasing after the pass
Tool Shoulder + their arm Arms around neck

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal as a choke/strangle in typical IBJJF and most no-gi rules when applied as neck compression without illegal spinal cranks as defined by the event. Confirm your rulebook if referees are strict about “neck crank” labels on shoulder pressure.

Safety

Partners may struggle to hand-tap when arms are trapped—agree on foot taps and verbal taps. Apply gradually; release instantly.

Common mistakes

  • Passing to the same side as the choke.
  • Chest flopping without trapping the arm.
  • Neglecting to teach the release habit.
  • Cranking the cervical spine instead of compressing the neck.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a Von Flue choke?

A shoulder-pressure choke that counters a guillotine someone will not release after you pass to the far side.

Why opposite side?

Passing toward the choking arm usually tightens their guillotine; far side sets the trap.

Gi or no-gi?

Both; especially common in no-gi and MMA.

How do I avoid getting Von Flued?

Let go of failed guillotines once they clear your legs and settle on top.

Is it the same as an arm-triangle?

Related shoulder/neck pressure family, different arm trap geometry.

Can beginners learn it?

Yes as a defensive concept (release) and a simple finish—with supervised pressure.

What if they half guard?

Free the hips first; do not settle for a loose shoulder smash without the trap.

The Bottom Line

Von Flue is the tax on stubborn guillotines: far-side pass, trap the arm, dig the shoulder. Train the release as hard as the finish—and keep it linked to guillotine and side control.
