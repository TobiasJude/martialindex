Frame Escape

The frame escape uses skeletal frames on the neck and hip to hold space under side control (and related pins), then shrimps the hips out to recover guard.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Frames hold space; hips escape—do not push with soft arms.
  • Pillar: side control. Cluster with side-control escape, shrimp, elbow escape.
  • Near elbow to hip connection is the core unit.
  • Gi crossfaces fight frames; no-gi underhooks replace many collar tools on top.
  • Bridge briefly to unload, then shrimp on the way down.

What Is Frame Escape?

A frame escape is any bottom escape that prioritizes bone posts—forearms on neck/shoulder and hip—before hip movement. It is the conceptual parent of many side-control guard recoveries and the framing half of mount elbow escapes.

Martial Index files it under side control because that is where framing literacy is taught hardest, with bridges to mount and north-south.

How Frame Escape Works

Build a neck/shoulder frame and a hip frame with elbows tight. Bridge to shift weight, shrimp diagonally while frames hold the pocket, and insert the near knee for half or closed guard.

Structure over strength. Soft pushing arms get americana’d; stacked bones let weight rest while hips move.

Top. Crush frames by cutting the near hip, winning the crossface, and walking toward north-south before the knee inserts.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar crossfaces and pants grips attack your frames and escaping knee. Prefer forearm bones and early hip frames over grabbing fabric from bottom.

No-Gi. Same posts without collar anchors; top players use underhooks and head position. Frames must appear earlier because pins settle faster without fabric friction.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Settled side control: pillow/frame the crossface, post the hip, bridge, shrimp, knee shield in—classic frame-and-reguard.

If the far arm leaves, you may switch from pure frame escape to underhook-to-knees instead of forcing the shrimp.

Types / Variations

Frame and reguard

Neck + hip frames → shrimp → knee → guard.

Pillow / head-on-arm frame

Head on your own forearm to kill the crossface while you turn.

Frame into underhook escape

Use frames to create the pummel window, then come to knees.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: the moment a pass lands, before the crossface sets; any time you reclaim elbows inside under side control.

Counters: smash the near hip, walk to north-south, trap the framing arm, or knee-on-belly when frames get tall.

Do not abandon frames mid-shrimp—hold space through the movement.

Frame Escape vs Underhook Escape

Factor Frame Escape Underhook to Knees
Primary tool Bone frames + shrimp Far underhook + turn
Best when Crossface heavy / underhook denied Far arm light / crossface manageable
Finish Guard recovery Knees / dogfight / scramble
Risk Americana if arms float D’Arce / front headlock if head stays in

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Early frame escapes are how bottom players deny side-control points from stabilizing after a pass.

Safety

Elbows in—extended arms invite americana and arm-triangles. Do not crank necks while framing in drilling.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing with straight arms instead of stacking bones.
  • Shrimping without a hip frame—chest glues back instantly.
  • Abandoning the neck frame mid-escape.
  • Turning belly-down without protecting the neck (turtle risks).

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a frame escape?

An escape that builds skeletal frames to hold space, then uses hip escapes to recover guard—most often from side control.

Are frames the same as pushing?

No. Frames are posts that hold structure; pushing with soft arms burns energy and risks submissions.

When do I frame vs underhook?

Frame when the crossface is heavy; underhook when the far arm leaves and you can turn safely.

Does framing work in no-gi?

Yes—and earlier timing matters more without gi friction slowing the pin.

How does this relate to shrimp?

Frames create the pocket; the shrimp moves you through it.

What if they smash my frames?

Rebuild smaller, bridge to unload, or switch escapes—do not muscling-match a settled pin.

Is frame escape only side control?

Flagship yes, but the same framing literacy feeds mount elbow escapes and north-south survival.

The Bottom Line

Frame escapes turn bones into temporary walls so hips can move. Hold the posts, unload, shrimp, insert the knee—then choose guard or underhook paths.
