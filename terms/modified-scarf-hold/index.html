Modified Scarf Hold

Modified scarf hold (kuzure kesa gatame) is a side-control pin that keeps scarf-style hip orientation but swaps the classic head wrap for a deep underhook—often more secure against rolls and common after knee-cut passes.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Underhook instead of (or with less) head wrap—kuzure / broken scarf.
  • Often safer against bridge-and-roll than classic kesa; fewer headlock chokes.
  • Gi pants/sleeve grips help; no-gi needs a deep far underhook and low hips.
  • Leaving space under the armpit lets them reclaim the underhook—stay glued.
  • Parent: side control; cousin: scarf hold.

What Is Modified Scarf Hold?

Modified scarf hold—Japanese kuzure kesa gatame—is a scarf-style pin where the top player typically takes a deep underhook around the torso instead of (or in place of) the classic head wrap of hon kesa. Hips still tend toward the head or a diagonal sit, but the control emphasis shifts to the far underhook and near-arm trap. It lives in the side control pin family.

In modern BJJ it often appears after knee-cut passes when you sit through and deny their underhook.

How Modified Scarf Hold Works

The deep underhook prevents them from pummeling the escape underhook that fuels many side-control escapes and rolls. Near-arm control under your armpit plus low hips create a stable pin. Attacks include armbars, transitions to mount, and some shoulder locks—though classic headlock chokes are less available than in hon kesa.

Bottom player's job. Fight the underhook immediately, get to frames, and shrimp or invert before the sit-through solidifies.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeve grips freeze the near arm; belt control can glue the underhook side.

No-gi. Underhook depth and head position matter more; expect wrestle-up attempts if your hips rise.

MMA context. Excellent for controlling posture and landing short punches while denying underhooks.

What Does Modified Scarf Hold Look Like in Practice?

Alex finishes a knee cut, sits through, shoots a deep far underhook, traps Jordan's near arm, and pins with low hips. Jordan cannot find the underhook to roll.

Gi. Alex adds a pants grip on the near leg to stop shrimps.

No-gi. Alex keeps the underhook and crossface pressure without hunting collars.

Types / Variations

Kuzure kesa basics

Kodokan-style modified scarf: torso underhook + near-arm control with scarf hip orientation.

Deep underhook scarf

BJJ-favored version after passes—deny their underhook at all costs.

Attacks and transitions

Armbars, mount climbs, north-south circles, and return to standard side control.

Escape prevention

Low hips, glued underhook, near arm trapped—anti-roll checklist.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Knee-cut sit-throughs; switching from classic kesa when the head wrap fails; sprawling after failed guard recoveries.

Top threats. Pin, armbar, mount, north-south.

Bottom counters. Pummel the underhook, frame the hips, shrimp to half/guard, or force them high and roll.

Modified vs Classic Scarf Hold

Factor Modified Scarf Classic Scarf
Head wrap Often underhook instead Defining head-and-arm wrap
Roll resistance Often higher Vulnerable if hips rise
Choke access Fewer headlock chokes More headlock / collar options
Common entry Knee-cut sit-through Side control sit to head

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal pin. Scores as a stabilized pass/side control under IBJJF criteria when held.

Safety

Do not crank shoulders while fighting underhooks. Tap early to armbars from the sit-through.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow underhook—they pummel free and escape.
  • High hips inviting the roll.
  • Forgetting near-arm control while obsessing over the underhook.
  • No plan after the pin—stalling without mount or submission paths.
  • In the gi: ignoring pants grips that stop shrimps.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is modified scarf hold?

A scarf-style pin emphasizing a deep underhook instead of the classic head wrap—also called kuzure kesa gatame.

Why use modified instead of classic kesa?

Often harder to roll and better after modern passes; fewer headlock finishes but more underhook control.

How do you escape modified scarf hold?

Pummel the underhook, frame the hips, and shrimp to half or closed/open guard before they climb to mount.

Is kuzure kesa the same as broken scarf hold?

Yes—naming varies by gym and judo vs BJJ dialects; the underhook-based scarf idea is the same family.

Does it work in no-gi?

Yes. Deep underhooks and low hips matter more without cloth anchors.

What attacks work from modified scarf?

Armbars, mount transitions, north-south, and returns to standard side-control submissions.

When should I switch from classic to modified scarf?

When they threaten the bridge-and-roll or free their head from your wrap—secure the underhook and re-pin.

The Bottom Line

Modified scarf hold (kuzure kesa) pins with a deep underhook and scarf hips—often safer against rolls than classic kesa. See scarf hold and side control.
