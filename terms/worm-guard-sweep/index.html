Worm Guard Sweep

The worm guard sweep is a gi reverse from worm guard that uses a fed lapel under the near leg to off-balance a standing or kneeling passer—clustered with reverse De La Riva, lasso, and worm guard pass.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Worm sweep = lapel-fed leverage from worm to dump or invert past the passer.
  • Keep the lapel tight and the near knee over theirs so they cannot knee-cut free.
  • Chains into kiss of the dragon, mount, or back when they turn.
  • Top answers: worm guard pass, grip strips, and forced knee-cut.
  • Gi-native; no-gi has no true worm—use RDLR / SLX cousins.

What Is Worm Guard Sweep?

Worm guard sweep covers the high-percentage reverses once the far lapel is fed under your near calf and gripped across—Keenan-style worm. Train it as named offense from worm guard, often mixed with reverse De La Riva and polish-worm hybrids. Up: worm, open, De La Riva. Across: lasso sweep, RDLR sweep. Top: worm pass.

How It Works

Feed and tighten the lapel, pin their near knee with yours, stretch the wrap to break posture, then kick/invert to reverse into mount, side, or back. If they base hard, switch to kiss-of-the-dragon style back takes rather than muscling a stalled sweep.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Requires a usable lapel—home art for worm sweeps.

No-gi. No true worm; migrate to RDLR, single-leg X, and X-guard sweeps.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Standing passer over worm → you stretch lapel → they tip → you come up to mount or invert to the back when they turn away.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Basic worm sweep to mount with lapel stretch and knee pin.

Competition variation

Polish / reverse-DLR worm hybrids that invert toward the back.

Related option

Kiss of the dragon / crab-ride exits when the sweep stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From DLR by stripping a lapel; from seated open guard. Counters. Worm pass—strip lapel early, knee-cut, or force them off the wrap.

How It Compares

Vs lasso sweep: worm owns the lapel/leg line; lasso owns the arm. Vs RDLR sweep: RDLR can be no-gi; worm cannot.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

IBJJF gi legal; youth/master grip rules vary—verify IBJJF rules. Absent in ADCC.

Safety

Avoid yanking necks with the lapel; sweep the base, do not choke as a substitute mid-drill.

Common mistakes

  • Loose lapel that re-threads out mid-sweep.
  • Letting their knee float over yours (invite knee-cut).
  • Abandoning worm entirely after one failed kick.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a worm guard sweep?

A gi reverse from worm guard using a fed lapel under the near leg to dump or invert past the passer.

Is worm guard sweep gi-only?

Yes—true worm needs a lapel; no-gi uses RDLR/SLX cousins.

How do you set it up?

Strip and feed the far lapel under your calf, pin their near knee, stretch, then kick or invert.

What should you chain next?

Mount, side control, or kiss-of-the-dragon/back takes.

Common mistake?

Loose lapel or letting their knee float over yours.

Is it beginner-friendly?

Blue+ usually; white belts can drill entries after basic open guard.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Under worm guard, meshed with RDLR and lasso offense.

The Bottom Line

Worm guard sweep is lapel leverage plus knee pin—study worm entries and the pass answers so you know both sides of the exchange.
