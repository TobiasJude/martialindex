What Is Plan B Sweep?

Plan B Sweep is a half-guard backup sweep used when the old-school / underhook sweep is defended—often by whipping up and taking them the opposite direction or ducking the whizzer. In gym language you may also hear plan B half-guard sweep. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

From lockdown or underhook half, threaten old school; when they post or whizzer, cup the posted leg or duck under, reverse direction, and come up on top. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Pant grip on the posted ankle clarifies the cup; no-gi cups the thigh and uses lockdown whip-ups.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, plan b sweep usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard plan b sweep as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (Half Guard). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

Chains with Old School Sweep and Electric Chair Sweep under Half Guard Sweep.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck, spinal, and knee pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck, shoulders, and knees. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing plan b sweep with a nearby sibling name.
