Elbow Escape

The elbow escape (elbow-knee escape) recovers guard from mount by framing, power-shrimping, and inserting the knee—built on the shrimp rather than a full reversal.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Elbows in, frames on hips, power shrimp, insert knee—then sliding shrimps to close guard.
  • Pillar: mount. Sibling of upa and hub mount escape.
  • Toes toward the mat often helps the knee carve under tight knees.
  • Works when upa is dead against low, heavy mounts.
  • Gi and no-gi share movement; gi crossfaces and pants grips change the fight for frames.

What Is Elbow Escape?

The elbow escape is the highest-percentage guard recovery from mount for many gyms: instead of reversing the opponent, you create a pocket, connect elbow to knee, and rebuild half or closed guard underneath.

It is the primary applied form of the shrimp under mount, and it pairs with the upa as a two-escape system—bridge threats open posts; elbow escapes punish low mounts.

How Elbow Escape Works

Keep elbows glued to ribs. Frame the near hip (and often the far hip/chest). Hit a power shrimp (single-shoulder bridge + shrimp) to unload, turn the near knee in, and slide the knee across their thigh line.

Detail. Pointing toes toward the mat can remove the gap under a tight knee so your knee travels. Follow with sliding shrimps until half or closed guard locks.

Top. Tighten knees, grapevine, crossface, and float to technical mount when the knee threatens.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Crossfaces and collar pressure fight your frames; pants grips can pin the escaping knee. Use bone frames, not soft arms.

No-Gi. Underhooks and head position replace collar crossfaces; expect faster re-mounts if you leave space after a partial shrimp.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Low mount: frame hips, power shrimp to one shoulder, insert the near knee, shrimp again into half guard, then recover closed or butterfly.

If they post high during the shrimp, threaten upa on the posting side before returning to the elbow path.

Types / Variations

Classic elbow-knee

Frame, shrimp, knee through, close half/closed guard.

Power shrimp + sliding shrimps

Unload first, then incremental slides—Danaher-style sequencing.

Elbow escape to half guard

Stop at knee shield / half when full closed is unavailable.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: settled low mount, failed upas, mounts that sit on the hips, after defending americana/cross-collar with elbows in.

Counters: grapevine, move to high/technical mount, trap the escaping arm, or switch to side control if the knee wins too much space.

Chain with upa threats so they cannot sit still on both options.

Elbow Escape vs Upa Escape

Factor Elbow Escape Upa Escape
Result Your guard back You on top / in their guard
Best vs Low heavy mount High posting mount
Engine Shrimp + frames Bridge + traps
Teaching order Often second, used more Often first, flashier

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Recovering guard from mount prevents continued mount scoring once the escape is complete under IBJJF positional rules—escape early.

Safety

Do not push with extended arms into americana territory. Protect the neck while framing under a crossface.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with sliding shrimps while still fully weighted—no unload.
  • Elbows away from the body.
  • Toes up under a tight knee, leaving a gap they clamp.
  • Stopping in a loose half without frames.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the elbow escape?

A mount escape that uses frames and shrimps to insert the knee and recover half or closed guard.

Why is it called elbow escape?

Because the elbow stays connected to the hip/ribs and meets the knee as the primary framing unit while you shrimp.

Does it work against high mount?

Harder—survive and get to mid/low mount first, or use upa when they post.

Gi or no-gi differences?

Same skeleton; gi crossfaces and pants grips make frames and knee inserts fight harder.

How do I chain with upa?

Threaten the bridge to force a post, then elbow-escape into the opened hip line.

What if my knee will not go under?

Unload with a power shrimp, turn toes to the mat, and inch—do not muscle a flat knee.

Is elbow escape only from mount?

Mount is the flagship, but the same elbow–knee idea appears in frame escapes.

The Bottom Line

The elbow escape is shrimp under mount: frames on, weight unloaded, knee through, guard rebuilt. Pair it with the upa so neither answer sits alone.
