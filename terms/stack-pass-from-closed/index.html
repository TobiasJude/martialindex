Stack Pass from Closed Guard

The stack pass from closed guard opens closed guard, then folds the opponent with vertical pressure—a positional application of the stack pass. It follows breaking / opening closed guard under guard passing.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Stack after the ankles open—don’t stack a locked closed guard blindly.
  • Drive knees toward shoulders; walk hips forward.
  • Control at least one leg while you clear the other.
  • Sibling of double-under and pressure passing.
  • Watch neck safety under fold.

What Is Stack Pass from Closed Guard?

Closed guard top becomes a stack when you convert posture fights into vertical fold after the guard breaks.

Parent mechanic: stack-pass. Prerequisites: posture in guard and combat base.

Compare standing outside passes after the same break.

How Stack Pass from Closed Guard Works

Win posture, open the ankles (knee-pull or standing break), then grip pants/legs and drive forward.

Walk hips toward their head so shoulders carry weight—frames die.

Angle to one shoulder, clear the near leg, settle into side control.

If they invert, follow hips carefully or disengage rather than cranking the neck.

Pressure ethics: a legal stack folds hips toward shoulders; an illegal crank attacks the neck as the goal. Teach partners to tap to pressure early and teach passers to angle off the centerline so the pass completes without spinal drama.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips and double-unders make the fold sticky.

No-gi. Cup knees/ankles and use shoulder pressure.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex postures, knee-pulls open, stacks to the right shoulder, and passes.

Jordan’s stack meets an inversion; they abandon to a toreando.

Types / Variations

Double-under stack

Arms under thighs, clasp, walk forward.

Pant-grip stack

Gi grips at knees driving the fold.

Stack to side / mount

Choose finish based on shoulder angle.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Prerequisite: breaking closed guard. Counters: inversion, hip scoops. Escapes for bottom: create angle before hips fully elevate.

Stack from Closed vs Standing Outside Pass

Factor Stack path Standing outside path
Pressure Vertical fold Lateral circle
Best when Chest connection already won Legs extended after break
Risk Neck / inversion battles Recompose / DLR
Related stack-pass bullfighter / slash

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Pass criteria unchanged—clear legs and stabilize. Illegal neck cranks are a separate foul topic—verify current IBJJF book.

Safety

Never treat a stack as a neck crank. Train controlled folds.

Common mistakes

  • Stacking while ankles still locked.
  • Driving into the chin as a finish.
  • No angle—stalling on the centerline.
  • Ignoring inversion counters.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Can I stack while closed guard is locked?

You can pressure, but the true stack pass needs the ankles open.

Gi or no-gi?

Both—grips change, fold geometry stays.

Is stacking illegal?

Pressure passes are normal; illegal neck attacks are different—verify the PDF.

Main counter?

Inversion and creating an angle before hips elevate.

Relate to double-unders?

Double-unders often feed the stack.

After the pass?

Side control or mount with tight chest connection.

Pillar?

Guard passing, with closed guard as the starting pillar.

The Bottom Line

Stack from closed is break, then fold—open the ankles, elevate the hips safely, and clear a leg.
