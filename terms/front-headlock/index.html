Front Headlock

The front headlock is a head-and-arm control position from the front—typically after a sprawl or snap-down—where you clasp around the neck and near shoulder to break posture, circle, and attack guillotine, anaconda, or D’Arce chains.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Front headlock is a hub, not a single choke—control first, finish second.
  • Chest heaviness and elbow position beat pure arm strength.
  • Common after sprawls and failed shots; also from clinch snap-downs.
  • Gi adds collar options; no-gi emphasizes seat-belt-like neck clasps and arm isolation.
  • Safety: neck cranks vs blood chokes—tap early and coach-supervised drilling only.

What Is Front Headlock?

A front headlock seats your shoulder and arm around the opponent’s neck from the front while controlling an arm or posting on the mat. In wrestling it scores and rides; in BJJ it is the launchpad for front-choke families and back takes when they turn.

It sits between standing clinch work and ground chest-to-chest pressure. Martial Index treats it as the connective tissue after stuffed takedowns—the moment you decide whether to finish a choke, spin behind, or reset.

How Front Headlock Works

From a sprawl, walk your hips toward their head line, drop a chin strap or clasp, and keep your elbow tight so they cannot posture into your chest.

Use shoulder pressure to round their spine; circle toward the trapped arm for guillotine entries or toward the far arm for anaconda/D’Arce pathways.

If they turtle hard, keep the head locked and insert a hook or spin behind for back control rather than abandoning the neck entirely.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar grips amplify snap-downs and can convert into clock-choke or loop-choke style pressure from related angles.

No-gi. Focus on clasp mechanics, wrist control, and chest weight—guillotine and head-and-arm chokes are the high-percentage products.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Casey sprawls a double, seats the front headlock, and finishes a high-elbow guillotine as the shooter posts a hand.

Morgan loses the choke race but keeps the head, spins behind, and inserts hooks for points.

Types / Variations

Standing front headlock

Snap-down control before knees touch—great for circling and mat returns.

Knees / sprawl front headlock

After a stuffed shot—chest heavy, hunt chokes or back takes.

Cradle-adjacent rides

Mixing near-side cradle ideas to tilt them and enter legs or back.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: sprawl follow-ups, clinch snap-downs, failed guard passes that leave their head low. Counters: hand fight the clasp, posture, peel the elbow, or sit to guard with frames. Escapes: turn into them carefully or roll to guard without exposing the neck further.

Front Headlock Control vs Finished Guillotine

Factor Front headlock (control) Guillotine (finish)
Primary job Posture break + options Blood/air choke finish
Arm position Clasp + shoulder pressure Blade of wrist under neck
Exit ramps Back take, anaconda, reset Tap or escape
Beginner focus Chest heaviness Hand placement & angle

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Front headlock control is generally legal; some neck compressions and cranks may draw penalties depending on division. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC rulebooks—do not invent legality from gym lore.

Safety

Distinguish blood chokes from dangerous neck cranks. Partners must tap early; coaches should police cranking during drills.

Common mistakes

  • Only squeezing arms without chest weight.
  • Abandoning the head the moment the first choke fails.
  • Driving the crown of their head into the mat.
  • Ignoring the back-take when they turn away.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is front headlock a submission?

It is primarily a control position that produces submissions (guillotine family, head-and-arm chokes) and back takes.

How do I get there?

Most often from sprawling a shot or snapping down from clinch.

Gi vs no-gi difference?

Gi adds collar tools; no-gi emphasizes clasp mechanics and chest pressure.

What if they tuck their chin?

Improve angle and shoulder pressure; chain to anaconda/D’Arce or take the back.

Can beginners learn this early?

Yes as control—finish details come with coaching.

Relation to side control?

You may pass into north-south or side control if they flatten; keep neck threats alive.

Legal in IBJJF?

Control is typically fine; verify choke vs crank distinctions in the current book.

The Bottom Line

Front headlock is the sprawl’s paycheck: break posture, then choose choke chain or back take. Study it beside guillotine and sprawl.
