Head Control

Head control is a core Brazilian jiu-jitsu principle: steering or denying the opponent’s head to break posture, pin shoulders, and retain or pass guard—bridging side control, passing, and guard retention.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Deny free head control when retaining guard.
  • Passers graduate from legs to head.
  • Pins die without head/shoulder control.
  • Crossface and forehead pressure are tools—not only chokes.
  • Pairs with wrist, under-, and overhooks.

What Is Head Control?

Because the head heavily influences posture and balance, controlling it (or preventing theirs on you) shapes entire exchanges. Danaher-style retention teaching stresses not giving head control away; passers and pin players treat head position as the second graduation after clearing legs.

Universal bridge across Martial Index clusters.

How Head Control Works

Offensively: align their head with the pin you want (crossface, forehead in jaw, chest-to-face pressure) while hips follow.

Defensively: fight inside position on arms before they reach your head; keep their head and hips on the same side when possible during retention.

Do not confuse “head control” with illegal neck cranks—pressure with purpose and rules awareness.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar ties and crossfaces are potent head controls.

No-Gi. Shoulder pressure, forehead, and hands replace collars.

Same principle.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Passing, Alex clears the legs then immediately claims Jordan’s head with a crossface before the re-guard.

Retaining, Alex hand-fights to stop head control and recovers closed guard.

Types / Variations

Crossface

Pin and pass staple.

Retention denial

Stop them owning your head.

Clinch / collar tie

Standing posture battles.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Appears whenever distance closes past the knees.

Counters: frames, inside hand-fighting, shrimps that free the head.

Escapes from pins: clear the crossface first, then hips.

Head Control vs Wrist Control

Factor Head Control Wrist Control
Steers Posture & shoulders Hands & grips
Pass graduation Late (after legs) Early (frames/grips)
Pin role Essential Supportive
Guard retention Deny theirs Clear their hands

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal as control; illegal neck cranks are a separate referee question—apply pressure as taught for pins/passes, not as banned spinal attacks.

Safety

Be mindful of cervical discomfort. Partners with neck injuries need modified pressure.

Common mistakes

  • Passing legs then forgetting the head.
  • Giving head control free while retaining.
  • Cranking necks instead of pinning.
  • No connection between head and hip pressure.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is head control?

Steering or denying the head to manage posture and pins.

Why in passing?

After legs, head control stops re-guards.

Retention tip?

Do not gift head control; fight inside hands.

Gi tool?

Collar crossface.

No-gi tool?

Shoulder/forehead pressure.

Illegal?

Control is fine; illegal cranks are not—know the difference.

Related concepts?

Wrist, underhook, overhook.

The Bottom Line

Head control steers the body: claim it when passing and pinning; deny it when retaining. Study it beside wrist, underhook, and overhook.
