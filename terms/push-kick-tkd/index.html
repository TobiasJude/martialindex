What Is Push Kick (Mireo Chagi)?

Mireo chagi (밀어차기) is the push or thrust front kick. The intent is displacement, not a snap bruise.

It is the closest Taekwondo cousin to the Muay Thai Teep (Push Kick). Martial Index keeps a TKD slug so the MT page stays MT.

Compare with Ap Chagi (Front Kick): snap front kick recoils; push kick commits more hip weight through the target.

In WT Kyorugi (Sparring) a push kick may score poorly or not at all on Palding (Trunk Scoring) if it lacks snapping force—yet it still stops charges.

Martial Index files Push Kick (Mireo Chagi) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Push Kick (Mireo Chagi) Works

Chamber the knee, toes back or foot flat per coach, and drive the heel through the midsection or hip.

Hips stay squarer than Yeop Chagi (Side Kick). The supporting heel stays down longer than a snap kick.

Recover to Taekwondo Stance immediately. A planted push with dead hands is a Counter Kick invitation.

Use Sliding Step or Skip Step to set range rather than leaning the head in.

Mechanically, successful push kick (mireo chagi) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, push kick (mireo chagi) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill push kick (mireo chagi) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Confirm whether a pushing impact meets the current World Taekwondo Rules force and surface definitions before you build a game around it.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Opponent sprints; you push the Hogu to break rhythm, then fire a Fast Kick (Bal Bal).

Shield drill: coach walks in; you keep them at bay for a full round without snapping.

Fight picture: push kick (mireo chagi) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply push kick (mireo chagi) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Snapping when you meant to push—or pushing when you needed a snap score.
  • Leaning the torso back so far you fall.
  • Kicking with the toes.
  • Assuming every push beeps on {electronic-scoring}.
  • Copying {teep} clinch entries that do not exist in your rule set.
  • Treating push kick (mireo chagi) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick push kick (mireo chagi) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so push kick (mireo chagi) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether push kick (mireo chagi) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Mireo vs ap chagi?

Ap Chagi (Front Kick) is usually the snap front kick. Mireo chagi is the push/thrust version.

Mireo vs teep?

Related range kicks. Teep (Push Kick) is a Muay Thai staple under eight-limbs rules; this page stays in Taekwondo curricula.

Does it score in Olympic kyorugi?

Often a poor PSS scorer if it lacks impact quality. Still useful as a jam.

Lead or rear?

Both. Lead push is faster; rear push moves more mass.

ITF?

ITF Taekwondo teaches front piercing and thrusting kicks with their own sine-wave timing.

The Bottom Line

Use mireo chagi to steal rhythm—then score with tools Electronic Scoring (PSS) actually likes.
