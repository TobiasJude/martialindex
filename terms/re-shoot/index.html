Re-Shoot

Re-shoot means changing levels again right after a stuffed shot—often the attempt that actually scores—parented by Level Change and central to Chain Wrestling (MMA).

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Assume you need a second shot.
  • Change side or timing—do not dive the same hole.
  • Parent: {level-change}.
  • Clear safely from front headlocks first.
  • Fence re-shoots raise finish rates.

How Re-Shoot Works

Re-shoots punish opponents who sprawl tall with their hands down or who overcommit to a front headlock without control.

Bad re-shoots are desperate dives into the same stuffed hole; good ones change side, level, or timing.

Mechanically, successful re-shoot work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

Pairs with Turn the Corner and peek-outs when the first shot was a single.

Common Mistakes

  • Re-shooting into a locked front headlock.
  • Hands down after the first sprawl.
  • Telegraphing with a huge reset step.
  • Giving up after one stuff.
  • Treating re-shoot as a highlight instead of a position-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down or hips light.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe re-shoot as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor fence awareness, or grips that collapse when strikes enter the round.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put re-shoot behind heavy wrestling; others behind dirty boxing; submission-heavy rooms may leave GNP light. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Ground and Pound, Cage Wrestling, Dirty Boxing, and Get-Up into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for elbows, mat returns, and live striking on the ground.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “clinch,” “control,” and “get-up.” Martial Index’s MMA entries keep re-shoot scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated pure-BJJ articles that share casual language—though we cross-link those pages when mechanics overlap.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a re-shoot?

An immediate second takedown attempt after a stuff or sprawl.

Part of chain wrestling?

Yes—one of the core links.

Vs level change?

Level change is the drop; re-shoot is using another drop after failure.

Beginner tip?

Drill the clear from sprawl before the second shot.

The Bottom Line

Re-shoots turn stuffed shots into setups—change the side or timing and keep chaining.
