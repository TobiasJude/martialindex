Ashigarami

Ashigarami (literally “leg entanglement”) is the umbrella family of lower-body controls that trap one or both legs—parent concept behind saddle, outside ashigarami, honey hole/411, and many heel hook entries.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Ashigarami = family name; saddle/honeyhole/outside ashi are members.
  • Control the hip and knee line before hunting the finish.
  • Inside vs outside configurations change which heel hook is primary.
  • Rule sets dramatically change which ashi games you should prioritize.
  • Link reaping literacy before competing in IBJJF gi.

What Is Ashigarami?

Borrowed from judo terminology and popularized in modern no-gi, ashigarami means you have entangled the opponent’s leg(s) with your own so their hip cannot freely escape. It is the positional backbone of contemporary leg-lock systems.

Martial Index uses ashigarami as the parent hub: from here you specialize into inside sankaku (saddle/honeyhole/411 naming cluster) or outside ashi pathways, then finish with inside/outside heel hooks, kneebars, or ankle locks depending on rules.

How Ashigarami Works

Win an entry (SLX, RDLR backstep, K-guard, scramble) so one leg is trapped between yours with your hips controlling their knee line. Pinch knees, hide their heel or expose it intentionally, and decide inside vs outside configuration.

Do not yank the foot first—stabilize the entanglement, break their posture, then rotate as a unit for heel hooks when legal.

If they counter-entangle into 50/50, treat it as a sibling problem: hand fight, posture, and reclaim the better ashi rather than stalling in mutual danger.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Many deep ashi + heel-hook combinations collide with IBJJF restrictions—train legal ankle-lock finishes and verify reaping language.

No-gi. Primary habitat for ashi → heel-hook systems under ADCC-style rules.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Avery enters single-leg X, transitions to outside ashi, and threatens an outside heel hook in a no-gi ruleset.

Blake mirrors for IBJJF gi: same entry, finishes a straight ankle lock without crossing illegal knee lines.

Types / Variations

Inside ashigarami / path to saddle

Sets inside heel-hook games via honeyhole/saddle.

Outside ashigarami

Often pairs with outside heel-hook attacks.

Mutual / 50-50 adjacent

Both athletes entangled—high danger, high theory.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: single-leg X, K-guard, reverse De La Riva backsteps, turtle rolls. Counters: clear the knee line, stand in base, counter-entangle. Escapes: extract the trapped heel, turn the knee out, scramble to pass or reclaim open guard.

Ashigarami vs 50/50

Factor Ashigarami (generic) 50/50
Structure One-sided entanglement family Mutual inside-leg entanglement
Primary finishes Heel hooks / ankle locks (rules) Heel hooks both ways
Control clarity Often clearer top/bottom roles Symmetry = shared danger
Beginner advice Learn outside/inside ashi first Add later with safety culture

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Cross-read reaping and heel hook.

Safety

Ashigarami drills must include tap culture for knees. Never crank rotational finishes on uninformed partners.

Common mistakes

  • Yank the heel before the entanglement is stable.
  • Ignoring rule-set differences between gym nights.
  • Confusing every leg tangle as “the saddle.”
  • Stalling in mutual ashi without a plan.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is ashigarami one position?

It is a family; saddle and outside ashi are specific members.

Japanese meaning?

Roughly “leg entanglement”—used in BJJ as technical jargon.

Legal in IBJJF?

Many configurations interact with reaping/heel-hook rules—verify current book.

Best entry for beginners?

Coach-led SLX to outside ashi with ankle-lock finishes in restricted rules.

Relation to half guard?

Many ashi entries start from half, SLX, or K-guard butterflies.

Do I need heel hooks to use ashi?

No—control and sweeps/ankle locks exist; heel hooks are the modern finisher when legal.

Next read?

Saddle and outside ashigarami.

The Bottom Line

Ashigarami is the leg-entanglement umbrella. Specialize into saddle/honey hole or outside ashi, and keep reaping literacy beside the rulebooks.
