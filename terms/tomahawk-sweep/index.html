What Is Tomahawk Sweep?

Tomahawk Sweep is an X-guard sweep that chops the top hook diagonally while extending the bottom hook to dump a standing opponent into mount. In gym language you may also hear tomahawk / X-guard chop sweep. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

Control a sleeve or ankle, elevate into X-guard, chop the top hook across while the bottom hook lifts behind the knee, and follow hips into mount or technical mount. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Sleeve grip helps the chop; no-gi uses ankle and wrist rides.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, tomahawk sweep usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard tomahawk sweep as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (X-Guard). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

High-percentage X Guard finish alongside single-leg X entries; more direct-to-mount than many X sweeps.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check Ibjjf Rules and Adcc Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck and spinal pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck and shoulders. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing tomahawk sweep with a nearby sibling name.
