Kizami-Zuki

Kizami-zuki is the lead punch: a quick same-side hand shot from kamae that measures range and opens the door for gyaku-zuki.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Lead punch for range and setups.
  • Protect the rear hand.
  • Natural bridge to gyaku-zuki.
  • Sport kumite essential.

What Is Kizami-Zuki?

Kizami-zuki (刻み突き) is the lead punch. In sport Kumite it is a primary scoring and ranging tool.

Compare with Oi-Zuki (full step lunge) and Gyaku-Zuki (rear power hand).

In Karate, kizami often keeps more hanmi than a fully squared gyaku finish.

Martial Index files Kizami-Zuki under Karate so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Karate style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Kizami-Zuki behaves in kihon, kata, and kumite.

How Kizami-Zuki Works

From Kamae, extend the lead fist without collapsing the rear hand’s cover. Use a small hip/shoulder contribution; recover fast.

Feet may stay put or use a small slide. Overcommitting turns kizami into a lunging mistake.

Classic combo: kizami-zuki → Gyaku-Zuki.

Mechanically, successful kizami-zuki work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Karate Stance with Kamae alive.

If distance (maai) is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: punches need Hikite and Kime; kicks need chamber and retract; blocks need a counter path into Gyaku-Zuki or Oi-Zuki.

Dojo vs Competition / Rules Notes

In traditional dojo training, kizami-zuki lives inside the kihon–kata–kumite triangle—form quality matters as much as fight utility.

Under WKF sport karate, scoring and contact limits reshape how kizami-zuki appears in matches versus full-contact styles such as Kyokushin.

Gym culture may drill kizami-zuki daily; grading and competition may shrink the toolkit based on syllabus and ruleset.

Confirm the dojo syllabus and bout agreement before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Point fighters may spam kizami legally; knockdown formats demand heavier intent—train to your rules.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

WKF-style bout: repeated kizami to the body/head to rack points and disrupt rhythm.

Dojo drill: partner checks your kizami; you immediately fire gyaku on the opening.

Dojo picture: kizami-zuki appears when Karate Stance and timing say so, then yields to the next technique in a combination or to a reset of Kamae.

Gym test: can you apply kizami-zuki after a feint or Kizami-Zuki setup and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Snap kizami

Fast touch/score emphasis—recover to Kamae instantly.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives kumite pressure, not only line kihon.

Common Mistakes

  • Dropping the rear hand.
  • Overreaching and falling onto the lead leg.
  • Telegraphing with a huge chamber.
  • Never following up.
  • Using only arm flicks with zero stance.
  • Treating kizami-zuki as a highlight instead of a distance-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down and no hikite.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Line-drill kizami-zuki inside full kihon sets; return to Karate Stance and Kamae after every attempt.

Pads or makiwara: technical → moving → forced follow-up so kizami-zuki connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat solo fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film kata and kumite weekly and ask whether kizami-zuki appears with honest Kime.

Progress intensity with a sensei—especially for kicks, spins, and full-power Oi-Zuki.

Mirror drill: 50 kizami focusing on rear-hand stillness.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe kizami-zuki as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor maai, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare ryuha, you will see stylistic accents: some put kizami-zuki behind heavy Kihon repetition; others behind application-minded Kata or sport Kumite. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is kizami-zuki a jab?

Functionally similar as a lead punch; karate naming and chamber culture differ.

Kizami vs oi-zuki?

Lead punch without (or with minimal) full lunge vs committed stepping punch.

Do traditional kata use kizami?

Less as a named staple than in sport; related lead actions appear in drills.

Best follow-up?

Usually Gyaku-Zuki or a kick like Mae-Geri/Mawashi-Geri.

How do I stop telegraphing?

Smaller chamber, better Kamae, relaxed shoulders.

The Bottom Line

Own a sharp Kizami-Zuki from honest Kamae—then let Gyaku-Zuki punish whatever the lead hand reveals.
