BJJ · Concept

No-Gi

No-gi is Brazilian jiu-jitsu without the kimono: athletes wear a rashguard and shorts or spats, so control comes from underhooks, head position, and body locks instead of collar grips.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • No clothing grips—control the body, not the fabric.
  • Pace and sweat make scrambles more common.
  • Rule sets (IBJJF no-gi vs ADCC-style) change legal attacks—verify books.
  • Train both no-gi and gi for completeness.
  • Leg-lock literacy rises in importance culturally.

What Is No-Gi?

Remove the gi and every lapel choke and sleeve-dependent guard needs a translation. Butterfly, body locks, front headlocks, and wrestling top games step forward. Positions like mount and back control remain king; the path there changes.

No-gi is the dialect closest to MMA ground game attire, though MMA adds strikes. It is also the default look of many submission-only and ADCC pathways.

Equipment is light: rashguard, shorts/spats, mouthguard—plus optional groin protection per gym norms.

How No-Gi Works

Drill underhook pummeling, wrist control, and chest-to-chest connection passing.

Retune guards: hooks and frames over sleeve pulls—see butterfly and seated guards.

Study the legality of leg locks for your rule set before cranking heels in the gym.

Keep one day of gi if possible so collar awareness does not vanish forever.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi vs gi: same art family, different handles and friction.

Transfer: hip movement, frames, and positional hierarchy carry; lapel tricks do not.

Many competitors periodize camps toward the attire of their next event.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex’s collar choke A-game stalls; they rebuild finishes around arm-triangles and back takes.

Jordan’s sweaty arm slips free of a kimura that always stuck in the gi—elbow control must tighten.

Sam uses no-gi weeks to sharpen takedowns for ADCC trials.

Types / Variations

IBJJF no-gi

Federation no-gi divisions with their own legal lists—verify.

ADCC-style / trials

Submission-grappling incentives; open leg games culturally.

Submission-only variants

House rules vary widely—read the board.

MMA-adjacent

Shorts + rashguard under striking contexts—different sport.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: wrestling shots, body-lock passes, front-headlock chains.

Counters: strong frames, inside position, and early underhook fights.

Escapes: technical stand-ups and knee-elbow connections without pant grips.

No-Gi vs Gi

Factor No-gi Gi
Friction Low / slippery High / sticky
Guards Hooks, body locks Lapel & sleeve systems
Common finish culture RNC, guillotines, legs Collars + joint locks
Gear Rashguard + shorts Kimono + belt

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF publishes separate no-gi regulations (including evolving leg-lock permissions by belt). ADCC is another book entirely. Do not mix folklore—see IBJJF Rules and heel hook.

Safety

Sweat + speed = accidental cranking. Tap early; control finishes; mind fingers in tight no-gi entanglements.

Common mistakes

  • Reaching for collars that are not there.
  • Neglecting wrestling because “BJJ starts on the ground.”
  • Cranking legs without rule literacy.
  • Cotton T-shirts that shred and trap fingers.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What do you wear for no-gi?

Rashguard and fight shorts or spats; follow gym dress codes.

Is no-gi easier?

Different—often faster and more athletic-feeling, not automatically easier.

Gi first or no-gi first?

Either; consistency beats dogma.

Are heel hooks always legal?

No—depends on the rulebook and division.

Does no-gi use belts?

Rank still exists culturally; clothing may not show it.

Transfer to MMA?

Closer attire, but strikes change priorities—see MMA ground game.

Best bridge skill?

Underhooks, head position, and hip escape timing.

The Bottom Line

No-gi removes the handles and asks whether your jiu-jitsu controls bodies—or only fabric. Train it on purpose.
