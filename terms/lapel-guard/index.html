Lapel Guard

Lapel guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu gi open-guard family that feeds a lapel (yours or theirs) around legs or body as a tether—most famously Keenan Cornelius's worm guard and related lapel systems.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Lapel as a tether—not just a collar grip.
  • Worm guard is the famous lapel-guard child.
  • Almost entirely gi; no-gi has no true lapel analogue.
  • Control first, then sweep or back take.
  • Parent open guard; sibling worm guard.

What Is Lapel Guard?

Lapel guard is an umbrella for gi open-guard games that weave a lapel under a leg, around a hip, or through a hook to create a sticky tether. Worm guard is the best-known subtype; other feeds create similar off-balances and pass-killing connections. It is a key branch of open guard.

How Lapel Guard Works

Once the lapel is fed and gripped, their mobility drops and your hooks gain leverage. You can sweep, invert, or climb to the back while they fight the cloth. The tradeoff is setup time and grip-fight vulnerability while you feed.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi only for true lapel guard. No-gi athletes use body locks, K-guard, and SLX instead of cloth tethers.

What Does Lapel Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex pulls open guard, feeds a lapel under Jordan's near leg (worm-style), grips the end, and sweeps when Jordan tries to toreando—or climbs to the back when Jordan kneels into the tether.

Types / Variations

Basic lapel feed

Extract and thread a lapel to create a control tether.

Worm-guard family

Lapel under the leg with specific hook configurations—Keenan's signature.

Sweeps and back takes

Off-balance over the tether; invert or sit-up to rear mount.

Passing lapel guards

Strip or reverse the lapel feed early; do not play inside their tether.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From collar-sleeve, spider, or seated when a lapel is free; dedicated lapel pulls.

Bottom threats. Sweeps, back takes, pass prevention.

Top counters. Grip fight the lapel, back out, pass only after the tether dies.

Lapel Guard vs Collar-Sleeve

Factor Lapel Guard Collar-Sleeve
Cloth use Woven tether under/around legs Collar + sleeve grips
Setup time Higher Lower
Famous system Worm / Keenan family Mikey-style open guard
No-gi None None (grip analogues differ)

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in gi. Irrelevant in no-gi/ADCC. Some events regulate belt grabbing differently—lapels are generally fair game in IBJJF gi.

Safety

Do not crank necks with aggressive lapel wraps in drilling. Communicate during worm entries.

Common mistakes

  • Feeding the lapel without a plan—free pass while you weave.
  • Abandoning grips mid-feed.
  • Trying lapel tricks in no-gi by habit.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is lapel guard the same as worm guard?

Worm is the most famous lapel-guard system; lapel guard is the broader family. See worm guard.

Who popularized lapel guard?

Keenan Cornelius's worm-guard era brought lapel systems into mainstream sport BJJ; others expanded the family.

Does any of this work in no-gi?

Not as lapel guard. Train non-cloth open guards instead.

How do I pass lapel / worm?

Prevent or strip the feed early. Passing inside a live lapel tether is a trap.

Is lapel guard legal in IBJJF?

Yes in gi. Follow standard grip and reaping rules for whatever hooks you add.

Should white belts learn worm/lapel early?

Learn posture and basic open guard first; add lapel systems once grip fighting is solid.

The Bottom Line

Lapel guard tethers cloth through legs and hooks—worm guard is the star child. Pure gi. See worm guard and open guard.
