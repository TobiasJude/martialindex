What Is Elbow Strike (Palkup)?

Palkup (팔굽) is the elbow. Strikes use the point or the surrounding forearm depending on the direction.

Taegeuk and higher Poomsae include elbow target strikes (often into a palm). Self-defense drills use them for clinch distance.

Olympic Kyorugi (Sparring) does not reward elbows. Do not import Muay Thai elbow culture onto a WT card.

Compare with Knee Strike (Mureup): both are close-range; elbows live higher, knees lower.

Martial Index files Elbow Strike (Palkup) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Elbow Strike (Palkup) Works

Step or pull the opponent into elbow range. The hip turns; the elbow does not travel from another zip code.

Keep the hand of the striking arm tight—fist or palm per school—so the forearm is a unit.

Common pictures: inward elbow, upward elbow, downward elbow into a target hand in forms.

Recover a frame. Elbows that stay buried in a clinch you did not train become wrestling you did not want.

Mechanically, successful elbow strike (palkup) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, elbow strike (palkup) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill elbow strike (palkup) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Poomsae: crescent kick, elbow into the palm in horse stance.

Pad: a shield at chest height, two-step entry, elbow, exit.

Fight picture: elbow strike (palkup) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply elbow strike (palkup) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Throwing elbows from kicking range.
  • Flaring the hand so the elbow is weak.
  • No hip.
  • Using elbows in a WT match.
  • Missing the {knee-strike-tkd} option when the head is too high.
  • Treating elbow strike (palkup) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick elbow strike (palkup) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so elbow strike (palkup) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether elbow strike (palkup) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe elbow strike (palkup) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put elbow strike (palkup) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Legal in Olympic TKD?

No as a scoring identity. This is forms/self-defense/ITF close-range.

Palkup vs sonnal?

Knifehand Strike (Sonnal) is longer. Elbow is closer.

Forms target?

Often your own palm as a target strike.

ITF?

Elbows appear in self-defense and some sparring contexts—check the event.

Pair?

Knee Strike (Mureup).

The Bottom Line

File elbows as close-range literacy—not as a WT loophole.
