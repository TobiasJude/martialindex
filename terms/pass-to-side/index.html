Pass to Side Control

Pass to side control is the classic finish of many guard passes: clear the legs, land perpendicular, and settle a side control pin before you climb or attack. It pairs with maintain side control and control before submit.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Clear legs, then glue chest-to-chest.
  • Win the head (crossface) and near hip.
  • Knees off the mat—weight through the toes.
  • Stabilize before mount or submissions.
  • Expect frame-and-shrimp answers from bottom.

What Is Pass to Side Control?

The hub pin after passing: side control scores the pass under many rulesets and opens mount, knee-on-belly, and back takes.

Conceptually distinct from the pass mechanics themselves—this page is about arriving and owning the pin.

Sits beside pass to mount on the top ladder and opposite recover guard / frame and shrimp.

How Pass to Side Control Works

Complete the pass path (knee cut, toreando, over-under, body lock, etc.).

Drop hips, chest on chest, angle roughly 90°.

Crossface the head; block the near hip with knee or hand.

Kill near-side frames; don’t leave a pocket for the shrimp.

Only then choose advance (mount, KOB) or attack—see maintain side control.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Crossface collars and far underhooks lock the turn.

No-gi. Shoulder pressure and underhooks; expect slipperier shrimp battles.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a bullfighter clear, Lee lands side control, crossfaces, and kills the near hip before looking at the far arm.

Chris passes with a body lock, settles, then switches north-south when the bottom player bridges.

Types / Variations

Classic settle

Chest-to-chest, crossface, hip block.

Smash / over-under land

Heavy chest, dig the near elbow.

Mobile pin

Cycle side ↔ north-south ↔ KOB.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Follows any completed pass. Counters from bottom: frames, bridge-then-shrimp, underhook turns. Top answers live on maintain side control.

Pass to Side vs Pass to Mount

Factor Pass to side Pass to mount
Role Stabilize hub Climb the hierarchy
Safety Usually safer first stop Higher reward / higher reverse risk
Offense start Limited until elbows lose Both arms freer
Bottom answer Frame-and-shrimp Bridge-and-roll + elbow escape

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Pass points typically require stabilized control—side control is the usual proof. Verify current IBJJF / ADCC control-time wording.

Safety

Shoulder pressure can smother—give training partners air; tap early if breathing fails.

Common mistakes

  • Landing on knees with no weight.
  • Celebrating the pass before settling.
  • Leaving the near hip open.
  • Diving at submissions with no pin.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is pass to side?

Clear the guard and settle a side-control pin.

Why not mount immediately?

Side is often the safer hub while frames die.

Key controls?

Head and near hip.

Points?

Usually where pass points stabilize—verify rulebook.

Bottom’s job?

Frame and shrimp / recover guard.

Next climb?

Mount, KOB, or take the back.

Related principle?

Control before submit.

The Bottom Line

Pass to side control is the settle—clear the legs, glue the pin, then decide whether to climb or finish.
