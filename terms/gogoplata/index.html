Gogoplata

The gogoplata is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu choke that uses the top of the foot or shin across the opponent’s neck—most famously from rubber guard / high-guard structures, sibling to the omoplata pathway.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Posture break and knee-line control beat raw flexibility theater.
  • Rubber guard / New York / mission-control themes are classic entries.
  • Gi and no-gi both work; no-gi needs sticky hip connection.
  • Chain to omoplata, triangle, and armbar when the foot slips.
  • Tap early—shin on throat can spike.

What Is Gogoplata?

Gogoplata (sometimes grouped with “shin chokes”) finishes by placing the foot or shin across the neck and pulling the head into that blade while the hips and grips lock the structure. It is iconic in 10th Planet-influenced rubber guard but also appears from closed/high guard when the knee clears the shoulder line.

Catalog links: rubber guard, omoplata, triangle, and closed-guard attack cycles.

How Gogoplata Works

Break posture, elevate the knee past the shoulder (rubber-guard or high-guard path), place the foot/shin across the neck, hug the knee/foot, and pull the head down while extending the hips.

Angle. Slight side hip often helps more than square-on flexibility.

Finish. Compress neck against shin; do not just kick the foot into the face.

If the foot slips, pivot to omoplata or triangle rather than muscling a bad shin.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar helps hold posture for the setup.

No-Gi. Rubber-guard styles thrive; use underhooks/overhooks and head control to replace lapels.

Flexibility helps but framing and angle matter more than viral “foot on neck” clips.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From rubber guard, Alex clears Jordan’s elbow, brings the shin across Jordan’s neck, hugs the foot, and finishes as Jordan tries to stack.

If Jordan tucks the chin hard, Alex switches to omoplata on the trapped arm.

Types / Variations

Rubber-guard gogoplata

Classic 10th Planet pathway via mission control / New York style controls.

Omoplata branch

When the shoulder lock appears first, the foot can still migrate to the neck.

Top / mount adaptations

Less common teaching models place shin pressure from top after high mount or stack counters.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: rubber guard, high closed guard, failed omoplata, and triangle defenses that leave the foot high.

Counters: posture, clear the knee early, stack carefully, extract the head, or peel the foot.

Escapes: turn into the shin, free the trapped arm, and reclaim posture—tap if the neck loads.

Gogoplata vs Omoplata

Factor Gogoplata Omoplata
Target Neck (choke) Shoulder (joint)
Tool Foot/shin blade Legs isolating shoulder
Flexibility ask Often higher Moderate
Sibling chain Triangle / rubber guard Kimura / sweeps

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal as a choke in typical IBJJF and most no-gi rules when it compresses the neck without illegal spinal/neck crank definitions as applied by the referee. Confirm your event if finishes look cranky.

Safety

Shin-on-throat is sharp. Drill slow; partners with prior neck issues should opt out of hard finishes.

Common mistakes

  • Kicking the foot without hugging structure.
  • Forcing square-on flexibility instead of angling.
  • Ignoring omoplata/triangle branches.
  • Stacking so hard you injure your own neck.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a gogoplata?

A choke using the foot or shin across the neck, often from rubber/high guard.

Do I need crazy flexibility?

Helpful but angle and posture break matter more.

Gi or no-gi?

Both; rubber-guard culture is strong in no-gi.

How does it relate to omoplata?

Shared high-leg pathways; one attacks neck, one shoulder.

Is it common in IBJJF?

Less common than triangle/armbar but legal as a choke when clean.

Defense tip?

Clear the knee early and posture—do not wait for the shin to land.

What if they defend the neck?

Pivot to omoplata, triangle, or sweep.

The Bottom Line

Gogoplata is the shin-blade choke of high-guard systems: break posture, clear the knee, hug the foot, finish the neck. Keep omoplata and rubber guard one step away.
