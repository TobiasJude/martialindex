Outside Ashigarami

Outside ashigarami is the outside-line member of the ashigarami family—your legs entangle their leg without the inside sankaku triangle of the saddle—often feeding the outside heel hook when rules allow.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Outside ashi ≠ saddle: no inside triangle lock; more dynamic outside-line control.
  • Primary modern finish: outside heel hook (verify legality).
  • Common entries from SLX, RDLR, and scrambles.
  • Watch reaping definitions in IBJJF contexts.
  • Can transition into saddle/honeyhole when you thread the inside triangle.

What Is Outside Ashigarami?

Where the saddle triangles the leg on the inside, outside ashigarami keeps an outside configuration: you control the leg and hip line from the outside path that makes outside heel hooks natural. It is a workhorse of no-gi systems that want mobility before committing to inside sankaku.

Martial Index clusters it with ashigarami siblings and reminds athletes that the same position can be a legal ankle-lock platform or an illegal reap depending on the year’s federation text.

How Outside Ashigarami Works

Secure the trapped leg between your knees/hips with your body on the outside path—pinch, extend their knee line as your system teaches, and control the heel with a cup or two-on-one.

Rotate through the hip/knee as a unit for outside heel hooks when legal; otherwise attack straight ankle locks or use the entanglement to sweep/stand.

To upgrade, thread into inside sankaku / saddle when their defense opens the cross hook.

Think of outside ashigarami as the mobile workshop where you decide whether to finish outside, sweep, or transfer inside—decision-making under entanglement pressure is the real skill.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Emphasize legal finishes and non-reaping orientations per IBJJF.

No-gi. Outside heel-hook habitat under freer rules.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Dana hits SLX, falls to outside ashi, and finishes an outside heel hook in no-gi.

In gi season Dana uses the same control to sweep or ankle-lock without crossing banned lines.

Types / Variations

Standard outside ashi

Classic outside-line entanglement.

Dynamic / mobile outside ashi

Used to off-balance before committing finishes.

Outside-to-saddle transfer

Thread into inside sankaku when available.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: single-leg X, reverse De La Riva, butterfly entries. Counters: clear heel, straighten knee line, stand in base. Escapes: extract foot, turn knee out, scramble to pass.

Outside Ashigarami vs Saddle

Factor Outside ashigarami Saddle
Triangle lock Usually no Inside sankaku yes
Primary heel hook Outside Inside
Mobility Higher Lower / stickier
Upgrade path → saddle → double trouble

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. See reaping and outside heel hook.

Safety

Outside heel hooks injure knees quickly—slow partner drilling only.

Common mistakes

  • Yank heel before hip control.
  • Ignoring reaping in IBJJF weeks.
  • Never learning the saddle transfer.
  • Confusing outside ashi with 50/50.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Outside vs inside ashi?

Outside keeps outside-line geometry; inside/saddle uses inside triangle control.

Best finish?

Outside heel hook when legal.

IBJJF?

Often constrained—verify reaping/heel-hook language.

Entry for beginners?

SLX to outside ashi with coach supervision.

Same as outside sankaku?

Related discussion—see outside sankaku for triangle-named outside variants.

Can I sweep from here?

Yes—off-balancing is part of the position’s value.

Next?

The Bottom Line

Outside ashigarami is the mobile outside-line entanglement: control the leg, respect reaping rules, finish outside when legal, or transfer to the saddle.
