Collar-Sleeve Guard

Collar-sleeve guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu gi open-guard system built on one collar grip and one sleeve grip—controlling posture, setting hooks (DLR/RDLR), and feeding sweeps and berimbolos made famous by athletes like Mikey Musumeci.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Collar + sleeve = the posture-breaking grip pair of modern gi open guard.
  • Feeds DLR, reverse DLR, spider-ish frames, and berimbolo entries.
  • Primarily a gi game—no-gi needs wrist/ankle substitutes.
  • Break posture before hunting fancy hooks.
  • Parent: open guard.

What Is Collar-Sleeve Guard?

Collar-sleeve is a gi open-guard grip configuration: one hand on their collar (usually cross or same-side depending on system) and one hand on a sleeve, while your legs manage distance with frames, DLR hooks, or shin-to-shin structures. It is less a single "pose" than a control language for modern sport BJJ open guard.

How Collar-Sleeve Guard Works

The collar breaks and monitors posture; the sleeve freezes an arm and creates off-balancing. Together they let you insert DLR or reverse DLR hooks safely, sweep when they drive, or invert to the back. Lose either grip and the system softens fast.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. This is the home. Collar and sleeve are the point.

No-gi. Approximate with wrist control, head position, and ankle grips—expect a different game (seated, K-guard, SLX) rather than true collar-sleeve.

What Does Collar-Sleeve Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex pulls guard, establishes cross collar and near sleeve, inserts a reverse DLR hook, and either sweeps Jordan forward or berimbolos to the back when Jordan circles.

Types / Variations

Basic collar-sleeve control

Grip pair + hip mobility—foundation before hooks.

To DLR / reverse DLR

Add outside or inside hooks once posture is broken.

Sweeps and back takes

Off-balance sweeps, berimbolo, and sit-up style attacks.

Passing collar-sleeve

Strip grips first (especially collar), keep posture, toreando or knee-cut once hooks die.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Guard pulls, seated open guard, failed closed guard with collar retained.

Bottom threats. Sweeps, berimbolo, back takes, transitions to spider/lasso/lapel.

Top counters. Grip break, posture, clear hooks, pass.

Collar-Sleeve vs Spider

Factor Collar-Sleeve Spider
Primary grips Collar + sleeve Both sleeves + foot-on-biceps
Feel Posture + hook feeder Extension + sleeve tension
Famous users Mikey-style open guard Classic spider specialists
Hook pairing DLR / RDLR common Lasso / spider feet

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Gi-legal. Collar grips are restricted or absent in no-gi/ADCC—train the no-gi cousins separately.

Safety

Do not crank necks with collar grips in drilling. Tap early to gi chokes set from the same grips.

Common mistakes

  • Grips without hip movement—static collar-sleeve dies.
  • Hunting berimbolo before posture is broken.
  • Ignoring grip fighting on top—passing starts with sleeve/collar strips.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is collar-sleeve only for lightweight competitors?

No—mechanics scale—but elite lightweights popularized the modern look.

Collar-sleeve or spider first for beginners?

Collar-sleeve teaches posture control with simpler footwork; spider demands more sleeve tension literacy. Either works with good coaching.

Can I use collar-sleeve in no-gi?

Not literally. Train wrist/ankle and seated systems as the no-gi analogue.

How do I break collar-sleeve on top?

Strip the collar first when possible, posture up, clear hooks, then pass. Do not lean into their grips.

Does collar-sleeve feed worm guard?

It can transition into lapel-fed guards including worm variants when you start weaving lapels. See lapel guard.

Best hook with collar-sleeve?

Reverse DLR and classic DLR are the most common modern pairings.

The Bottom Line

Collar-sleeve is the gi open-guard grip language: break posture, insert DLR/RDLR hooks, sweep or berimbolo. No-gi needs different tools. See open guard and reverse DLR.
