New York (Rubber Guard)

New York is a rubber-guard position in Eddie Bravo's system: a higher climb from Mission Control that loads the shoulder for omoplatas, jiu-claw style controls, and related finishes.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • New York = higher rubber climb off Mission Control.
  • Signature threat family: omoplata / shoulder attacks.
  • Control still matters—don't climb empty.
  • No-gi native; gi optional.
  • Parent rubber guard; hub Mission Control.

What Is New York (Rubber Guard)?

New York is the rubber-guard position you climb to from Mission Control when you want more shoulder load for omoplata-family attacks. In Bravo's tree it sits above the home base—tighter on the upper body, more committed to the shoulder line. It is a key branch of closed guard.

How New York Works

By elevating the rubber structure, you isolate their shoulder and arm for omoplata finishes and related controls (including jiu-claw style grips in some curricula). If they defend the omoplata, you can often return to Mission Control or divert to triangles rather than abandoning rubber entirely.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. Standard rubber environment.

Gi. Sleeve help exists; many still prefer classic omoplata from closed/high guard in the gi.

What Does New York Look Like in Practice?

Alex locks Mission Control, climbs to New York, and spins to an omoplata when Jordan tries to posture out—finishing or sweeping off the shoulder lock.

Types / Variations

Basic New York

Higher rubber climb with shoulder load.

Omoplata / jiu-claw path

Primary finishing grid.

From Mission Control

The standard entry—don't skip control.

Escaping New York (top)

Peel early, posture, clear the threatening leg before the omoplata seats.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Almost always via Mission Control in the system; sometimes from related high climbs.

Bottom threats. Omoplata, sweeps, returns to triangle paths.

Top counters. Stop the climb from Mission Control; extract before New York settles.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Omoplatas finish or score advantages/points depending on rules when they roll.

Safety

Shoulder locks: tap early. Drill omoplata entries with control.

Common mistakes

  • Climbing to New York without Mission Control security.
  • Abandoning triangle options when omoplata is stuffed.
  • Over-forcing flexibility positions cold.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why is it called New York?

Eddie Bravo's rubber-guard naming convention—learn the mechanics under whatever label your gym uses.

New York before or after Mission Control?

After. Mission Control is the hub; New York is a climb.

Is New York only for omoplatas?

Omoplata is the signature threat; related controls and returns to other rubber attacks still apply.

Flexibility required?

Helpful. Progressive rubber drilling beats forcing the climb on day one.

How do I escape New York on top?

Clear the high leg and peel before the omoplata spins; posture beats panic stacking alone.

New York vs chill dog / zombie?

Sibling rubber positions in the same instructional tree—different grips and threats off the rubber structure.

The Bottom Line

New York is the higher rubber-guard climb from Mission Control aimed at omoplatas and shoulder attacks. Lock control first. See Mission Control and omoplata.
