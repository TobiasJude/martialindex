Smash Pass

The smash pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu pressure pass that folds the bottom player's legs—often pinning both knees—and drives chest pressure through to side control, embodying the pressure-passing family.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Fold the legs + chest pressure + walk—do not hover.
  • Flagship of pressure passing.
  • Gi pants grips glue ankles; no-gi uses underhooks and head drive.
  • High chest invites inversion—stay heavy and low.
  • Cousins: over-under, stack.

What Is Smash Pass?

The smash pass is a pressure guard pass: you force the opponent's knees together or pin them under your chest, remove space, and walk your hips past the legs into side control. It is simple, high-percentage, and scalable across body types when connection is honest. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

How Smash Pass Works

Control the legs (pants, ankles, or underhooks), drive your chest onto the shins or torso, flatten their shoulders as you clear, and stabilize. Incremental hip movement beats one explosive shove.

Bottom player's job. Frame early, create knee space, invert or shrimp before the glue sets.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips make the smash sticky and slow.

No-gi. Underhooks and head position replace pants; expect more body-lock hybrids.

MMA context. Smash limits their hip mobility for upkicks while you advance.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex pins Jordan's knees together with pants grips, drops chest pressure, and walks to side control.

Gi. Dual pants control throughout.

No-gi. Alex underhooks and drives the head as he folds the legs.

Types / Variations

Classic double-leg smash

Both legs controlled and smashed under the chest.

Smash from half / headquarters

Fold the free leg and clear half-guard residues.

Smash to mount

Continue the pressure climb after the pass.

Chaining to over-under

If they free a leg, switch to over-under pressure.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From combat base; after stuffing butterfly; from headquarters.

Counters. Early frames, underhooks, inversion, forcing you upright.

Failure mode. Chest high with no hip advance.

Smash vs Over-Under

Factor Smash Over-Under
Leg control Often both legs smashed One over / one under
Feel Fold and glue Asymmetric pressure
Common next Side control / mount Side control / mount
Skill ceiling Simple to start More grip specificity

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Stabilize for pass points. Avoid stalling under pure pressure without advance.

Safety

Allow partners to breathe in training. Do not dump onto the neck.

Common mistakes

  • Hovering instead of connecting.
  • No hip walk after the smash.
  • Ignoring underhook fights.
  • In no-gi: hunting pants habits.
  • Gassing by muscling instead of using bone structure.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the smash pass?

A pressure pass that folds the legs under chest pressure and walks to a pin.

Is smash pass only for heavyweights?

No—connection and angles matter more than mass.

Smash vs over-under?

Smash often folds both legs; over-under splits control one over and one under.

Does smash pass work in no-gi?

Yes—with underhooks and head drive replacing pants grips.

How do you stop a smash pass?

Frame early, create knee space, and invert or shrimp before the chest glues—see guard retention.

Best drills for smash passing?

Positional rounds from combat base with a goal to glue and walk—not to explode once.

Smash pass vs stack pass?

Smash folds/glues more horizontally; stack drives knees toward the face more vertically.

The Bottom Line

The smash pass is pressure passing made concrete—fold the legs, stay heavy, and walk to the pin. See pressure passing and over-under.
