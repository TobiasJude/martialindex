Paper Cutter Choke

The paper cutter choke drops a forearm blade across the neck from side control or north-south, usually with a collar assist—gi pin strangling next to the baseball bat and north-south choke.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Control the face/jaw line, feed the collar, drop the shoulder/forearm blade.
  • North-south and side control are primary homes.
  • Classic forms are gi; pure no-gi pressure differs (see north-south choke).
  • If they tuck the chin, contour and re-angle before forcing.
  • Cluster with baseball, ezekiel, and cross collar.

What Is Paper Cutter Choke?

The paper cutter is named for the slicing forearm across the neck—like a blade. Top players set it when the bottom athlete is flat under side or north-south and a collar can be fed under the neck line.

It belongs in the side-control cluster of submissions and contrasts with the no-gi-friendly north-south choke that uses the armpit/lat more than a lapel.

How Paper Cutter Choke Works

Pin arms/chest, turn the face if needed, grip a collar under/near the neck, and drop the near shoulder so the ulnar blade cuts across the throat while weight settles.

Detail. Chin tucks kill lazy paper cutters—use the back of the hand to turn the face, then climb to the shoulder cup before dropping.

Bottom. Frame early, turn to your side, and hand-fight collars before the blade drops.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar feed makes the classic paper cutter.

No-Gi. True lapel paper cutter disappears; use north-south choke, arm-triangle, and darce entries from similar pins.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

North-south: body on the face line, one arm over/under for control, slide a collar, drop the shoulder for the paper cutter. Side control: same blade with a shorter setup.

Types / Variations

North-south paper cutter

Common highlight finish from NS control.

Side-control paper cutter

Blade from classic side pin.

Face-turn setup

Contour the jaw when they hide the chin before the drop.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: settled side control, north-south transitions, failed mount attempts that land in NS, knee-on-belly resets to side.

Counters: frames, shrimp, turn away carefully, peel the collar, escape to turtle only with neck protection.

Chain to baseball bat, kimura, and mount when the blade is blocked.

Paper Cutter vs North-South Choke

Factor Paper Cutter Choke North-South Choke
Primary tool Forearm blade + collar Lat/armpit + head position
Gi needed? Classic yes No
Feel Slice across neck Smothering clamp
Same pin family? Yes (side/NS) Yes

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi. For no-gi, prefer north-south choke mechanics without illegal cranks.

Safety

Blade pressure can be abrupt—control in drilling. Avoid combined neck twists.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping without face control—chin hides the neck.
  • Losing the pin while chasing the collar.
  • Forcing a crank when the blade is not aligned.
  • Ignoring frames that are already reclaiming space.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the paper cutter choke?

A pin choke that drops a forearm blade across the neck, usually with a gi collar assist from side or north-south.

Is it gi-only?

Classic yes. No-gi uses related north-south choking without lapels.

Paper cutter vs baseball bat?

Paper cutter emphasizes the blade drop; baseball emphasizes bat grips and spin. See baseball bat.

Best position?

North-south and tight side control.

What if they tuck the chin?

Turn the face first, then re-apply the blade—do not stack a crank.

Defense?

Frames, side-control escapes, and collar peels before the shoulder drops.

Related no-gi finish?

North-south choke—see entry.

The Bottom Line

Paper cutter is pin + blade: control the face, feed the collar, drop the forearm. Keep baseball and north-south choke as sibling answers from the same pins.
