Takedown

A takedown puts a standing opponent to the mat with you landing in a controllable top relationship—fed by wrestling shots and judo throws, scored differently under IBJJF vs ADCC-style rules.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Takedown = control after the fall, not just the trip.
  • IBJJF often pays 2 after stabilization; guard pulls change math.
  • ADCC-style rulesets raise wrestling value.
  • Chain shots and throws; don’t marry one entry.
  • Hub links to singles, doubles, ankle picks, drags, and judo throws.

What Is Takedown?

In BJJ sport, takedowns inherit wrestling (single, double, ankle pick, snatch) and judo/sambo (osoto, uchi-mata, seoi, trips). Success is measured by landing where you can pass or submit—not by Olympic aesthetics.

Rule incentives matter: IBJJF awards takedown points with stabilization norms and interacts with guard pulls; many submission-only / ADCC-style events reward top control and wrestling differently. Always verify the book you are competing under.

This hub links the batch-7 cluster: single-leg, double-leg, ankle pick, arm drag, uchi mata, osoto gari, seoi nage.

How Takedown Works

Win hand fighting or grip fighting, create kuzushi/off-balance, level-change or turn-in, finish, then settle top control.

Train breakfalls and paced intensity before live shooting.

Pair every takedown with a pass plan (knee cut, body lock, etc.).

Drill chain wrestling: single→double→front headlock.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Throws and ankle picks shine with collar/sleeve; watch pants grips that award the opponent on your pull.

No-Gi. Shots, body locks, and trips dominate; hand fighting is the gateway.

Both: landing in their closed guard without a plan is a soft takedown.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex clears a wrist, shoots a double, turns the corner, and settles side control for the score/control.

Gi: Alex off-balances with a collar and hits osoto, following to knee-cut.

If Jordan pulls first, Alex’s late shot may not score the same—timing vs pull matters.

Types / Variations

Wrestling shots

Singles, doubles, high crotch, snatches.

Judo / jacket throws

Osoto, uchi-mata, seoi, foot sweeps.

Clinch trips & body locks

Inside trips, outside trips, body-lock takedowns.

Low-risk picks

Ankle picks and drag-downs.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: hand/grip wins, snap-downs, failed pulls, and fence/wall pressure in MMA contexts.

Counters: sprawls, whizzers, underhooks, and pulling guard as a strategic choice.

Escapes from bad shots: turn the corner or abandon early to avoid guillotines.

Takedown vs Guard Pull

Factor Takedown Guard Pull
Goal Top control from standing Bottom guard as preferred start
IBJJF trend 2 pts if stabilized per rules Legal with grip norms; timing vs shot matters
ADCC trend Often highly valued Less rewarded than top wrestling
Risk Guillotine / scramble Immediate pass pressure

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

High-level only: IBJJF commonly treats successful takedowns as two-point actions when control/stabilization criteria are met; interactions with guard pulls are specific—verify the current IBJJF rulebook. ADCC and other no-gi rulesets use different scoring/advantages. This encyclopedia does not invent rule quotations.

Safety

Takedowns cause more training injuries than many ground drills. Progressive resistance, clean mats, and breakfall literacy are mandatory. Never slam.

Common mistakes

  • Shooting without hand clearance.
  • Celebrating the fall in their guard.
  • Ignoring the sprawl/guillotine.
  • One takedown only—no chain.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What counts as a takedown in BJJ?

Putting them down from standing and securing a controllable top relationship per your ruleset.

Are takedowns required?

No—many athletes pull—but incentives differ by event.

Best beginner takedowns?

Ankle pick, basic double, and a simple trip/osoto entry.

Gi vs no-gi?

Throws/grips vs shots/hand fighting emphasis.

IBJJF vs ADCC?

Different scoring and pull incentives—read both books.

How do I train safely?

Breakfalls, progressive resistance, no slams.

Where next?

Pick one shot and one throw from this cluster and deep-dive.

The Bottom Line

Takedowns are how BJJ borrows wrestling and judo to start on top. Learn the incentive of your ruleset, chain entries, and always land with a pass plan.
