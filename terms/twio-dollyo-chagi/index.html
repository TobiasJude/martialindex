What Is Twio Dollyo Chagi (Jumping Turning Kick)?

Twio dollyo chagi (뛰어 돌려차기) is the jumping turning kick: a Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) delivered after both feet leave the floor.

It appears in breaking demos, promotion tests, and highlight Kyorugi (Sparring) finishes more than as a high-percentage Olympic opener.

Compare with Twio Ap Chagi (Jumping Front Kick): the jumping front kick stays linear; this kick rotates the hip onto a turning arc and usually strikes with instep or ball.

It is not automatically a Tornado Kick or 360 Dollyo. Those add a full spin setup. Twio dollyo can be a hop-up turning kick without a 360 entry.

How Twio Dollyo Chagi (Jumping Turning Kick) Works

Load a compact Taekwondo Stance, dip, and jump. Chamber the kicking knee across as if starting a standing Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick).

Pivot the hips in the air so the foot travels on an arc. Strike, retract toward a tight Chamber (Bal Deung), and spot the floor.

Land with knees soft and hands alive. Posing in the air is how you eat a Counter Kick or twist an ankle.

Height is optional; most sport versions are a short hop that clears the opponent’s check, not a demo-team leap.

Mechanically, successful twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

A jumping turning kick to the head is still a head kick under Electronic Scoring (PSS); the jump is cosmetics unless the sensor and judges agree.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Pad station: coach holds a mid or high target; you hop-turn, score, and recover without drifting off line.

Breaking picture: a run-up jumping turning kick through a board held at head height—athletics first, then control.

Fight picture: twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Hop / pop jumping turning kick

A short bounce into turning kick used to change height or beat a low check without a full 360.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Run-up jumping turning kick

Demo and breaking version: steps create lift. Rare as a first-choice Kyorugi (Sparring) opener.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Head-height jumping dollyo

Same family aimed at Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) scoring; risk and landing demand go up together.

Common Mistakes

  • Jumping first and deciding the kick later—chamber must start on takeoff.
  • Leaning the torso so far you cannot retract or land.
  • Confusing this with {tornado-kick} and adding an unneeded full spin.
  • Landing with a straight knee or dead hands.
  • Using a run-up in a ring where there is no runway.
  • Treating twio dollyo chagi (jumping turning kick) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is twio dollyo the same as a tornado kick?

No. Tornado Kick / 360 Dollyo use a spinning entry. Twio dollyo chagi is a jumping Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) that may hop without a full turn.

Instep or ball?

Same choice as standing Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick): instep is common on pads; ball appears in many traditional cues.

Is it scored extra in WT?

Jumping itself is not a bonus. Points come from valid trunk or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) impact under World Taekwondo Rules.

What should I learn first?

A clean standing Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), a safe Twio Ap Chagi (Jumping Front Kick) landing, and Chamber (Bal Deung) discipline.

ITF name?

ITF Taekwondo often says twimyo dollyo chagi for flying turning kick. Mechanics overlap; pattern context differs.

The Bottom Line

Own the standing Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick) first—then add hop height only if you can still land in Taekwondo Stance.
