Jumping Pass

The jumping pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard clear that jumps the hips past the bottom player’s legs into side control or north-south—clustered with cartwheel, toreando, and X-pass under speed passing.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Commit only when feet are light—sticky hooks punish mid-air.
  • Pillars: open guard + guard passing / speed passing.
  • Cluster siblings: cartwheel, toreando, X-pass, float pass, long step.
  • Gi: clear grips first; no-gi: timing and wrist control.
  • Landing connection matters more than the jump height.

What Is Jumping Pass?

Jumping passes leap your hips past open-guard feet instead of circling or cutting through. They are athletic options inside the speed-passing family—useful when legs are extended and light, dangerous when hooks are sticky or you have no landing pin.

Link up to guard passing and open guard; across to cartwheel, X-pass, toreando, and long step.

How Jumping Pass Works

Break or float their feet, jump the hips clear of the knee line, and land with chest/hip connection before they re-square. Without the landing, you often fall into closed or half.

Bottom player’s job. Keep feet heavy, catch a single as they jump, invert under the landing, or close the guard on impact.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants and sleeve grips must be managed before a clean jump; otherwise they pull you down mid-air.

No-gi. Faster, but wrist rides and underhooks on landing decide whether you keep the pass.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a toreando fake stretches their feet, the passer jumps past into side control and immediately wins a crossface—same setup language as cartwheel, different air path.

Against De La Riva, jumping without clearing the hook is a common failure; clear first, then jump, or choose X-pass / knee cut instead.

Types / Variations

Straight jump past both feet

Both legs light and extended; leap to side or north-south.

Jump after toreando fake

Use circling threats to extend feet, then jump.

Jump into HQ / knee cut

Land in headquarters and finish with the cut family rather than a pure pin.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: extended open-guard feet, grip breaks that float hooks, scramble moments. Prefer toreando/X when commitment is low. Cluster exit: if the jump fails into half, switch to force half or smash rather than resetting open.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Unsafe landings can still be coached out; referees care about stalling and illegal contact more than the jump itself when clean.

Safety

Control landings—do not jump onto a stacked neck. Progress from low hops with cooperative partners.

Common mistakes

  • Jumping into sticky De La Riva or butterfly hooks.
  • No crossface on landing.
  • Using jump as a first option instead of toreando/X.
  • Ignoring half-guard cluster exits after a failed landing.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a jumping pass?

An open-guard clear that jumps the hips past the legs into a pin past the hooks.

Is it beginner-friendly?

Usually no as a primary pass. Build toreando and X-pass first.

Jumping vs cartwheel?

Jump leaps; cartwheel rotates over the hands. Same cluster.

Gi vs no-gi?

Both work; gi requires better grip management before the leap.

Why do I land in their guard?

Feet were not light, or you landed without connection. Clear hooks first; pin on impact.

Good against closed guard?

Not until the guard is open. See closed guard opening first.

Pillar links?

Open guard + guard passing / speed passing; siblings cartwheel, toreando, X-pass, float.

The Bottom Line

Jumping pass is a commitment clear inside the open-guard speed cluster. Default to toreando and X-pass; jump when feet are light and your landing pin is rehearsed.
