Windshield Wiper Escape

The windshield wiper escape pivots a shin or leg in an arc—like a wiper blade—to clear weight, stop a mount transition, or recover guard. Pair with shrimp, knee-elbow escape, and frames. Also appears as a top transition (wiper to mount)—know which job you mean.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Name covers bottom escapes and some top transitions.
  • This page emphasizes the escape/clearing job.
  • Pivot at the knee; keep frames.
  • Chain into knee-elbow or shrimp.
  • Sibling of trap-and-roll for mount survival.

What Is Windshield Wiper Escape?

In escape language, windshield-wipering the free leg clears a hooking or pinning leg so you can insert a knee or turn to guard.

Top players also “wiper” a shin to mount from side control—related motion, opposite goal; see context.

Cross-link mount and side control escapes.

How Windshield Wiper Escape Works

From bottom mount or during a pass, frame, create a seam, and arc your shin across to clear their leg line.

Immediately insert the knee (toward knee-elbow) or hip escape out.

Don’t wiper without frames—empty arcs get smashed.

Vs grapevines, clear one side at a time.

Naming hygiene: if your coach says “windshield wiper to mount,” they mean the top transition. If they say “wiper escape,” they mean the bottom clear. This encyclopedia page prioritizes the escape meaning while acknowledging the shared geometry.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips can pin your wiper—peel fabric first.

No-gi. Cleaner pivots; expect faster weight shifts.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex frames under mount, windshield-wipers the right shin, and inserts a knee for half guard.

Jordan uses a wiper clear mid-pass to recover butterfly hooks.

Types / Variations

Mount windshield clear

Arc to create knee insertion space.

Pass retention wiper

Clear a slicing shin mid-pass.

Top transition note

Same geometry used to mount—opposite goal.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Used under mount and during passes. Counters from top: pin the ankle, grapevine, or shift weight. Chain with knee-elbow and shrimp.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Escape movements unrestricted; resulting positions score normally.

Safety

Don’t crank your own knee while pivoting under load—create space first.

Common mistakes

  • Wipering with no frames.
  • Leaving the cleared space empty.
  • Confusing top mount transition drills with escapes.
  • Telegraphing without a chain.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the windshield wiper escape?

Pivoting a shin/leg in an arc to clear weight and recover guard or half.

Is it only from mount?

Common there, also mid-pass.

Same as top wiper to mount?

Same geometry, opposite goal—context matters.

What next after the clear?

Insert knee (knee-elbow) or shrimp out.

Gi issue?

Pant grips can freeze the arc.

Relate to trap-and-roll?

Different mount answer—bridge vs clear-and-insert.

Key principle?

Frames first, then the arc.

The Bottom Line

Windshield wiper escapes clear a line so knee-elbow and shrimp can finish the recovery.
