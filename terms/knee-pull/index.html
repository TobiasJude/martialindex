Knee Pull

The knee pull opens closed guard by inserting a knee near the hip and driving the ankles apart while keeping posture. It is a primary tool inside breaking / opening closed guard, usually from combat base.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Insert knee, tall spine, push the far ankle.
  • Home platform: combat base.
  • Then pass—don’t sit in the open.
  • Gi pant grips help; no-gi uses shin pressure.
  • Sibling of standing breaks.

What Is Knee Pull?

Knee pull (knee drive / knee pry) is the kneeling classic for unlocking ankles without fully standing.

It belongs to the closed-guard break cluster and feeds knee-cut and stack paths.

Contrast with standing stretch breaks on the standing-pass page.

How Knee Pull Works

From combat base inside closed guard, control arms, place your knee in the middle, and push their far knee/ankle down your thigh.

Keep the raised foot posting for base; don’t let them flatten you forward.

As ankles open, pin one leg and begin a pass—knee-cut and stack are natural.

If the pull stalls, stand or switch sides rather than muscling blindly.

Detail that separates a real knee-pull from a stall: the posting foot must stay alive, the spine tall, and the pushing knee tracks their far leg down a straight rail—not into your own thigh. If the rail collapses, reset posture before you force another pry.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Grab the pant at the knee you’re pushing; collar posture helps.

No-gi. Use forearm/shin wedges and wrist control.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex hits combat base, knee-pulls, and knee-cuts through.

Jordan’s pull meets heavy ankles; they stand and finish a bullfighter.

Types / Variations

Combat-base knee pull

Default teaching method.

Both-knees variation

Less mobile but common in beginners.

Pull-to-pass chains

Directly into knee-cut or stack.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Used whenever closed guard is locked and posture is won. Counters: cross-grip posture breaks, triangle setups. Escapes for bottom: keep ankles glued and attack arms.

Knee Pull vs Standing Break

Factor Knee pull Standing break
Height Kneeling / combat base On the feet
Best when Stable and controlled Need stretch leverage
Risk Slow if posture dies Ankle picks
Follow-ups Knee-cut / stack Toreando / slash

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal mechanics; pass points only after a completed pass.

Safety

Drive steadily—don’t explode into their knee joint.

Common mistakes

  • Knee pull with broken posture.
  • No post foot.
  • Stopping after the open.
  • Hands on the mat.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a knee pull?

A kneeling/combat-base method to pry closed-guard ankles open.

Do I need to stand?

Not for this method—standing is an alternative.

Key detail?

Posture and a posting foot.

What pass next?

Knee-cut and stack are natural.

Gi grip?

Pant at the knee you’re driving.

Why does it fail?

Usually broken posture or no base.

Related pages?

Breaking/opening closed guard and combat base.

The Bottom Line

Knee pull is the workhorse break from combat base—insert, posture, pry, then pass.
