Submission

Submission means forcing a tap by choke or joint lock. This hub indexes the Martial Index finish clusters—from RNC to leg locks.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Submission = A technique that forces a tap by choke/strangle or joint lock after establishing control.
  • Mechanics: Isolate neck or limb from a dominant position or guard, remove frames, apply leverage until the opponent taps.
  • Gi vs no-gi: Collar chokes thrive in the gi; no-gi leans on RNC, guillotines, kimuras, and leg locks.
  • Category hub above armbar, kimura, triangle, and leg locks.
  • Drill both sides; tap early on neck, shoulder, and knee pressure.

What Is a Submission?

A submission in Brazilian jiu-jitsu is a technique that forces a tap (or referee stoppage) by threatening unconsciousness via choke/strangle or structural damage via a joint lock. It is the scoring climax of many matches and the practical end-state of positional control from mount, back control, side control, or the guard.

How Submissions Work

High-percentage finishes usually follow control: isolate a limb or neck, remove defensive frames, then apply leverage slowly enough to allow a tap. Categories stack—chokes (RNC, triangle, guillotine, arm triangle, Ezekiel, brabo) and joint locks (armbar, kimura, americana, omoplata, plus leg locks).

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi adds collar strangles and sleeve-control entries; no-gi emphasizes figure-fours, head-and-arm fronts, and a larger leg-lock diet under ADCC-style rules. Many finishes (kimura, armbar, triangle) are universal with grip changes.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From mount you might climb to S-mount for an armbar, attack a kimura from mount, or thread an Ezekiel from mount. From closed guard, classic chains include triangle ↔ armbar ↔ omoplata (triangle to armbar, omoplata to armbar).

Types / Variations

Chokes & strangles

Blood chokes restrict carotid flow; air chokes are rarer and riskier. Signature map: rear naked, triangle, guillotine, cross collar, Ezekiel, north-south chokes, brabo.

Upper-body joint locks

Armbar, kimura / kimura trap systems, americana, omoplata, tarikoplata, go-go plata.

Leg locks

See the leg locks hub: heel hook, straight ankle, toe hold, kneebar, saddle, 50/50.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries are positional. Counters are early frames, hand-fighting, and turning to a safer pin or guard recovery. Escapes after a lock is set are low-percentage—tap when appropriate.

How It Compares

A submission ends the match; a sweep only changes who is on top. Positional dominance without a finish still scores in many rule sets—but the catalog treats submissions as their own topic cluster.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Legal submissions vary by belt and organization—especially heel hooks and spinal locks. Confirm IBJJF vs ADCC before competing.

Safety

Apply slowly; never crank necks or knees ballistically. Reference only—train with coaches.

Common mistakes

Hunting submissions from poor position; skipping hand-fighting; ignoring the chain when the first finish fails.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is submission in BJJ?

A technique that forces a tap by choke/strangle or joint lock after establishing control.

Is submission gi-only?

Collar chokes thrive in the gi; no-gi leans on RNC, guillotines, kimuras, and leg locks.

How do you set it up?

Isolate neck or limb from a dominant position or guard, remove frames, apply leverage until the opponent taps.

Common mistake?

Fundamentals yes; timing against experienced partners takes structured drilling.
