Fence Takedown

A fence takedown is the MMA skill of completing a shot or clinch takedown after an opponent's hips hit the cage—lifting, turning, or dumping so the fence helps you finish instead of stalling you.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • The cage is a backstop: finish by lifting/turning, not by pushing them deeper into the fence.
  • Connect hands low under the hips before you try to elevate.
  • Shoulder pressure and head position keep them from circling the corner.
  • Chain to a fence pin or mat return if they stand the first dump.
  • No finger hooks in the fence—train legal grips.

How Fence Takedown Works

Typical loop: ride them to the fence → chest pressure → connect hands under the hips (S-grip or Gable Grip) → lift slightly off the fence → turn them away and dump.

If you only shove them into the cage you create a stalemate they can frame out of; elevation and rotation beat raw forward drive.

Mechanically, successful fence takedown work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

Common Mistakes

  • Pushing into the fence without connecting under the hips.
  • Grabbing fence fingers during the finish.
  • Hips light so they reverse.
  • Head in the pocket unprotected while finishing.
  • Treating fence takedown as a highlight instead of a position-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down or hips light.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a fence takedown in MMA?

Finishing a shot or clinch takedown after the opponent hits the cage, using the wall as a backstop while you lift, turn, or trip them to the mat.

Is grabbing the fence allowed?

Usually no under Unified Rules—do not grasp the fence to gain advantage.

Fence takedown vs mat return?

Fence takedowns often start from a shot/clinch drive; mat returns put a standing opponent back down. They overlap on the cage.

What first?

Open-space doubles and a basic fence clinch, then add lift-and-turn under a coach.

The Bottom Line

A fence takedown turns the cage from a stall into a finishing tool: connect under the hips, lift, turn, and chain to pins or returns when the first dump fails.
