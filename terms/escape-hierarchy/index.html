Escape Hierarchy

The escape hierarchy orders bottom priorities: survive the submission, neutralize damage, escape the pin, recover guard, then improve. It pairs with positional hierarchy and tools like frame and shrimp and bridge and roll.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Neck and limbs first—always.
  • Frames before desperate explosions.
  • Escape the pin before you attack.
  • Recover a real guard, then neutralize.
  • Patience beats panic under pins.

What Is Escape Hierarchy?

A decision ladder for inferior positions—not a single technique.

Borrowed from “survive → escape → improve” coaching language across sport BJJ.

Mirrors the top-side positional hierarchy from the bottom up.

How Escape Hierarchy Works

Protect neck and inside elbows immediately under mount/back/side.

Build frames; breathe—see breathing.

Choose the right engine: bridge-and-roll vs frame-and-shrimp vs hip heist.

Recover or recompose guard; only then sweep or submit.

If the top player relaxes control to attack, that is often your window—timing matters.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar threats raise survival priority; address chokes before hip escapes.

No-gi. Arm-in chokes and body triangles change the same ladder—still survive first.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Under mount, Sam blocks the cross collar, then runs knee-elbow instead of bridging into a choke.

On the back, Avery hand-fights the seatbelt before peeling hooks.

Types / Variations

Survive-first

Kill the submission threat.

Pin-escape focus

Frame-shrimp / upa once safe.

Improve-after

Recover → sweep / stand.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Applies under mount, side, back, and front headlocks. Counters from top: tighten control when you escape, or attack when you freeze—your hierarchy should stay intact either way.

Escape Hierarchy vs Positional Hierarchy

Factor Escape hierarchy Positional hierarchy
Viewpoint Bottom / survival Top / advancement
First job Don’t get finished Don’t give up the pin
Tools Frames, shrimp, bridge Pass, maintain, climb
Shared idea Order of operations Order of operations

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Hierarchy thinking works in points and sub-only—scoring may change urgency but not survival order. Verify event rules via IBJJF / ADCC.

Safety

Panic bridges under neck attacks cause injuries—address the choke first.

Common mistakes

  • Exploding into a choke.
  • Skipping frames.
  • Attacking from a broken pin.
  • Treating all bad positions as equal urgency.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is escape hierarchy?

An ordered priority list for escaping bad positions.

First step?

Survive the submission threat.

Then?

Neutralize, escape pin, recover guard, improve.

Vs positional hierarchy?

Bottom ladder vs top ladder.

Tools?

Frames, shrimp, bridge-and-roll, knee-elbow.

Breathing?

Part of survival—panic apnea fails the first rung.

Sport difference?

Points change incentives, not survival order.

The Bottom Line

Escape hierarchy is survive, then leave—neck first, frames next, guard after, offense last.
