What Is Naeryeo Chagi (Axe Kick)?

Naeryeo chagi (내려차기) is the downward “axe” kick common in Taekwondo demos, some self-defense curricula, and occasional sport moments.

The path goes up then down rather than straight forward like Ap Chagi (Front Kick).

Flexibility and control matter more than raw aggression—missed axes leave you exposed.

In Kyorugi (Sparring), it is situational compared with high-volume Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick).

How Naeryeo Chagi (Axe Kick) Works

Swing or chamber the leg upward, then accelerate the heel downward onto the target; supporting leg softens the landing.

Toes and ankle alignment protect the kicking foot; do not spike with toes.

Hands stay alive—axe kicks are famous for dropping guards.

Retract or step down into Taekwondo Stance without hopping off-balance.

Mechanically, successful naeryeo chagi (axe kick) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, naeryeo chagi (axe kick) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill naeryeo chagi (axe kick) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Pad drill: tall shield held vertically; axe onto the top edge with control.

Sparring cameo: opponent covers the body tightly; you chop toward the collar line then exit.

Fight picture: naeryeo chagi (axe kick) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply naeryeo chagi (axe kick) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Common Mistakes

  • Toe-stabbing the target.
  • Leaning so far you fall after the chop.
  • Telegraphing a huge wind-up every time.
  • Using it as your only head attack.
  • Skipping partner consent on contact axes.
  • Treating naeryeo chagi (axe kick) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Axe vs front kick?

Ap Chagi (Front Kick) drives forward; axe chops down.

Is it legal in WT?

Downward kicks have had rule nuances historically—confirm current World Taekwondo Rules for your division.

How to train safely?

Soft targets first; never surprise a partner with full-power neck-line axes.

Flexibility requirement?

Helpful but control beats circus height.

Sport frequency?

Lower than turning kicks for most Olympic athletes.

The Bottom Line

Use naeryeo chagi as a precision chop—own the recovery or it becomes a highlight reel for your opponent.
