Duck

A duck sits you briefly under a straight—knees bend, eyes up, hands covering—so the punch travels over you without a full weave.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Duck is a sit, not a dive.
  • Come up ready—uppercuts punish slow rises.
  • Use ducks selectively; weave for hooks.

What Is Duck?

A duck (sometimes called a sit) lowers the head under a straight Jab or Cross by bending the knees and hips while the torso stays relatively upright compared with a full Bob and Weave.

It lives in the Head Movement toolkit beside Slip Left, Slip Right, and Lean Back, and differs from Roll Under, which travels under looping punches with a U-shaped path.

Beginners often confuse ducking with covering; a true duck creates a miss, while Cover-Up accepts contact on the gloves.

Martial Index files Duck under Boxing so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Duck behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Duck Works

From Boxing Stance, bend knees enough that their straight passes over your crown/forearm line; chin stays tucked (Chin Tuck); eyes remain on the opponent.

Hands stay in Guard Hand Position or briefly reinforce a Defensive Shell as you drop—never both hands drop to the waist.

Rebound immediately to stance or into a Counterpunch; lingering low invites Uppercut and body shots.

Mechanically, successful duck work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair this tool with the rest of the defensive layer: Slip/Parry for straights, Bob and Weave literacy for hooks, and honest Guard Hand Position always.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, duck lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows as weapons—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill duck daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

Amateur headgear can encourage lazy ducks; still train as if an uppercut is waiting.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Partner jabs high; you sit under, then rise with a body Hook or straight.

Pad call: jab–duck–cross, then jab–duck–Lead Uppercut once the rise is clean.

Fight picture: duck appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply duck after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Sit duck

Minimal depth—just under the forearm—then reset. Best for beginners learning level change without weaving.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Duck to counter

Duck timed to their extension, then explode up into a planned shot. Requires Distance Management.

Common Mistakes

  • Diving the head forward into uppercuts.
  • Looking at the floor.
  • Staying low for multiple punches.
  • Confusing duck with a full weave under hooks.
  • Treating duck as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox sits under imaginary straights for rounds.

Partner holds a stick or mitt at jab height; duck under without crashing forward.

Film: check knee bend vs waist fold.

Shadowbox duck inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so duck connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether duck appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, head movement, and counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often sequence duck after slips: lateral first, vertical second, weave third.

Pair study with Defensive Responsibility—ducking every punch is not a plan.

Coaches often describe duck as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents around duck. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Duck vs bob-and-weave?

Duck is mostly a vertical sit under straights; Bob and Weave adds lateral U-motion under hooks.

Duck vs roll-under?

Roll Under emphasizes the under-hook travel path; duck is the compact sit.

Is ducking safe for beginners?

Yes at low intensity with eyes up; progress carefully because mistimed ducks eat uppercuts.

Can I duck hooks?

Prefer Bob and Weave or Roll Under for hooks; a pure sit may still be in the hook's arc.

How deep?

Just under the punch—if your hands touch the floor energy, you went too far.

The Bottom Line

Sit under the straight, eyes up—then stand into the counter before they reload.
