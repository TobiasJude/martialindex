Morote Seoi Nage

Morote seoi nage is the two-hand shoulder throw: sleeve and lapel control, forward kuzushi, and a turn that loads uke over tori’s back—distinct from one-arm Ippon Seoi Nage.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Child of the Seoi Nage (Judo) hub under Nage-Waza.
  • Two hands (sleeve + lapel) vs one-arm Ippon Seoi Nage.
  • Needs clear forward Kuzushi before the turn.
  • Drop-knee finishes often blur into Seoi Otoshi classification.
  • Chain to Kouchi Gari / Ouchi Gari when uke posts.
  • Follow messy landings into Ne-Waza pins.

What is Morote Seoi Nage?

Morote seoi nage (双手背負投 / two-hand shoulder throw) is the classic two-grip form of the seoi family. Tori typically holds uke’s sleeve with the hikite and the lapel with the tsurite, breaks uke forward and slightly up, turns in, and loads uke over the back or shoulder line. In Martial Index it lives under the Seoi Nage (Judo) hub—beside Ippon Seoi Nage, Seoi Otoshi, and modern drop forms—not as a free-floating “shoulder throw” synonym.

Search intent for “morote seoi nage judo” is almost always definitional: how it differs from ippon-seoi, which hand does what, and whether competition drop versions still count as morote. Kodokan teaching videos labelled simply “Seoi-nage” usually demonstrate this two-hand pattern; the one-arm form is catalogued separately as ippon-seoi-nage.

BJJ cousins keep the Japanese slug seoi-nage for the gi shoulder-throw idea—label that page BJJ when you cross-train. This article stays on the judo line: Judogi grips, IJF scoring, and Tachi-Waza entry culture.

Filed under Nage-Waza on the judo style hub. Morote is not “easier seoi”—it trades the trapping power of a deep one-arm slot for continuous two-hand steering and clearer kuzushi levers.

How it works

Start with posture and grip. In a standard right-handed attack, tori’s left hand (hikite) controls uke’s right sleeve near the wrist or forearm; the right hand (tsurite) takes a mid-to-high lapel. Forward-up Kuzushi puts uke onto the balls of the feet—without that break, the turn meets a flat, heavy partner and the throw stalls.

Tsukuri is the turn and fit. Tori pivots on the balls of the feet, drops the hips under uke’s center, and keeps elbows close so the back becomes a loading surface rather than a wall. Kake finishes the throw: a continuous pull-down and rotation that sends uke over the shoulder line onto their back. Amplitude and control decide whether referees see Ippon or Waza-Ari.

If uke posts a hand or stiffens the hips, do not muscle a second lift from a dead position. Switch to a linked ashi-waza—Kouchi Gari, Ouchi Gari, or a step into Tai Otoshi—or change levels into a drop finish that Kodokan may classify closer to Seoi Otoshi.

Mechanically, success is a chain: grip integrity → kuzushi direction → hip depth → rotational commitment → landing awareness for Ne-Waza. Film your elbow path; flared elbows leak the load and invite counters.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

In the Judogi, sleeve and lapel give morote its identity. The hikite sleeve pull creates the forward vector; the lapel tsurite lifts and steers. Competition jackets that are too long or grips that drift low change the lever arms—clean mid-sleeve and standard lapel are the textbook start.

No-gi “seoi” keeps the turn and hip geometry but replaces cloth with underhooks, wrist control, and head position. Expect lower success rates without the sleeve lever; many no-gi players prefer a one-arm style closer to ippon-seoi with a trapped arm. Transfers: timing and kuzushi direction. Does not transfer: stalling in grips that IJF would already shido in gi.

Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning. Martial Index is a map, not unsupervised curriculum—especially for drop entries that load the knees and shoulders.

What it looks like in practice

In Randori, morote often appears after a sleeve-and-lapel fight where tori wins a clear forward break. You see a snap, a tight turn, and either a standing finish or a knee drop as uke defends high. World Tour film shows lighter athletes using morote as a tempo weapon: threaten seoi, force a posture change, then punish with kouchi or ouchi.

In kata and formal Uchi-Komi, the emphasis is upright tsukuri and clean back contact—less dramatic than competition drops, more honest about whether your hips actually got under. Dojo programming that only drills knee drops without standing morote builds a one-speed athlete.

After a scoreless scramble, hunt pins: Kesa Gatame, Yoko Shiho Gatame, or Kami Shiho Gatame under the Osaekomi clock. See also the transition concept Seoi to Osaekomi.

Types / variations

Standing morote

Upright finish with hips under and a continuous pull. Best for teaching kuzushi and for partners who need clean Ukemi lines.

Drop / knee forms

One or both knees touch as tori pulls down. Kodokan distinguishes lift-up seoi-nage from pull-down seoi-otoshi when knees stay grounded at kake—see Drop Seoi Nage and Seoi Otoshi rather than treating every knee touch as “morote.”

Combination setups

Kouchi or ouchi into morote when uke’s weight shifts forward; morote feint into Tai Otoshi when uke sprawls the hips back.

Entries, counters, combinations

Enter on forward weight—never flat. A half-committed turn against a rooted uke is how shoulders get hurt. Counters include sprawling hips back, posting the free hand, and circling off the loading shoulder; keep elbows tight so you are not gift-wrapping yourself for a counter throw.

Combinations that survive pressure: sleeve snap → morote; morote → kouchi when uke steps wide; failed morote → side pin if you kept chest contact on the way down. Moving uchi-komi before hard nage-komi teaches the step pattern without thrashing partners.

Compare

Compare Ippon Seoi Nage: one arm traps under uke’s armpit; morote keeps two cloth grips and a broader steering surface. Compare Seoi Otoshi: pull-down / knee-contact classification vs lift-and-throw seoi-nage. Compare Tai Otoshi: hand technique with a blocking leg rather than a back load. IJF notes & safety

Morote itself is a legal classical throw. Problems arise from diving head-first without control, illegal gripping stalls, or reckless drops that deny ukemi. Coach level changes; do not copy highlight reels that ignore partner safety.

Under IJF 2026–28 scoring, osaekomi awards yuko at 5–9 seconds, waza-ari at 10–19 seconds, and ippon at 20 seconds. Yuko is live; it does not stack into waza-ari. Two waza-ari equal ippon. Standing scores still follow Ippon and Waza-Ari amplitude criteria in the Judo Rules.

Safety: solid Ukemi before hard nage-komi; tap early; train under qualified coaches. Build skill in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.

Common mistakes

  • Turning without forward kuzushi (hips meet a brick wall).
  • Elbows flared—load leaks off the back.
  • Hips too far from uke (no tsukuri depth).
  • Calling every knee-drop “morote” when it is functionally seoi-otoshi.
  • Abandoning chest contact after a partial throw—losing the pin.
  • Skipping ukemi culture before amplitude training.

Training notes

Separate standing morote uchi-komi from drop entries for at least a block of weeks so athletes feel the difference in kuzushi. Film the sleeve hand: if it stops pulling, the throw dies. Pair weekly work with one complementary foot sweep or reap and one pin so combinations are programmed, not hoped for.

Cross-trainers: keep BJJ seoi-nage labels honest. Shared shoulder geometry is real; scoring and gripping rules are not identical.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is morote the same as seoi-nage?

Morote is the two-hand form usually shown when Kodokan says seoi-nage; see the Seoi Nage (Judo) hub.

Morote vs ippon-seoi?

Two cloth grips (sleeve + lapel) vs one-arm trap—see Ippon Seoi Nage.

When is it seoi-otoshi instead?

When the concept is pull-down with knees grounded at execution—see Seoi Otoshi.

Best foot combination?

Kouchi Gari and Ouchi Gari into forward weight.

Pin after morote?

Kesa Gatame, Yoko Shiho Gatame, or Kami Shiho Gatame.

BJJ cousin page?

BJJ-labelled seoi-nage—keep the arts distinct.

Parent page?

Seoi Nage (Judo) under Nage-Waza.

The Bottom Line

Train morote as the two-hand seoi classic: kuzushi first, hips under, then specialise into Ippon Seoi Nage, Seoi Otoshi, and drop forms without conflating their names.
