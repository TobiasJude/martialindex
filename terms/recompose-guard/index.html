Recompose Guard

Recompose guard means rebuilding a functional guard while a pass is still forming—before the pin settles. It overlaps guard retention and is the mid-fight twin of recover guard. Heavy cross-links keep the pair clear.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Act during the pass, not after the pin.
  • Keep head, shoulders, and hips connected.
  • Frames and sticky legs buy the rebuild.
  • Half and open guards are valid recompositions.
  • If the pin settles, switch language to recover.

What Is Recompose Guard?

Recompose is a retention-under-fire concept: the passer has broken your first layer, but you rebuild before they earn a clean pin.

Martial Index differentiates lightly from recover guard: recover answers settled pins; recompose answers the scramble and near-pass.

Lives under the guard pillar next to guard retention, open guard, and half guard.

How Recompose Guard Works

Read the pass early: knee cut, toreando, over-under, long step.

Prioritize head position and shoulder line; deny chest-to-chest.

Use long-arm/forearm frames, knee shields, and outside-leg scissors to realign.

Pummel underhooks or re-attach hooks/feet to a preferred guard.

If they clear fully into side control, stop “recomposing” and run escape → recover.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and pant grips let you sticky-recompose after a toreando yank.

No-gi. Underhooks, wrist control, and shin frames replace cloth; hip heists and inversions appear more often.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

As a knee cut clears one leg, Avery scissors the outside leg and recomposes butterfly.

Taylor loses closed guard to a standing break, frames the biceps, and recomposes collar-sleeve before the pass lands.

Types / Variations

Outside-leg scissor recompose

Second leg rescues the first.

Knee-shield recompose

Force half/Z structure mid-cut.

Invert or hip-heist recompose

Emergency angle change vs speed passes.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries are failed or partial passes. Counters: clear the head, smash the shield, back-step. Drill next to recover guard so athletes know which map they are on.

Recompose Guard vs Recover Guard

Factor Recompose guard Recover guard
When Pass still forming Pin or pass largely complete
Mindset Retention under fire Escape then rebuild
Risk Late recompose becomes a pin Slow recover eats more damage
Sibling links Guard retention, open/half Frame-and-shrimp, escape hierarchy

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Successful recompose can prevent pass points from stabilizing; always verify current IBJJF / ADCC control-time language.

Safety

Inversions under stack pressure need coaching—protect the neck and don’t force circus moves in crowded rooms.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting until side control to “recompose.”
  • Only drilling recover and never mid-pass rebuilds.
  • Recomposing into a dead, inactive shell.
  • Ignoring head position while fighting legs.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is recompose guard?

Rebuild guard structure while a pass is still happening.

How is it different from recover?

Recover is after the pin/near-pin; recompose is mid-pass.

Same as retention?

Overlaps heavily—recompose is the rebuild moment inside retention.

Valid end states?

Closed, open, half, butterfly—anything playable.

If I’m already pinned?

Switch to escape hierarchy and recover guard.

Key body lines?

Head, shoulders, hips—keep them connected.

Gi vs no-gi?

Grips help in gi; underhooks and shin frames dominate no-gi.

The Bottom Line

Recompose guard is the mid-pass rebuild—fix the shell before the pin, and leave recover for when the pin already landed.
