Upa Escape

The upa escape (bridge and roll) reverses mount by trapping the opponent’s same-side arm and foot, then bridging diagonally over that shoulder—powered by the bridge concept.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Trap arm + same-side foot, clear the head, bridge diagonally—then follow the roll.
  • Pillar: mount. Cluster with elbow escape and mount escape.
  • Best when they are high or posting; low mount often needs elbow escape first.
  • Gi sleeve traps help; no-gi uses underhooks and wrists.
  • Failed upa is information—chain to shrimp/elbow escape immediately.

What Is Upa Escape?

The upa escape is the classic mount reversal taught early in nearly every Brazilian jiu-jitsu curriculum: remove one post (the arm) and one base (the foot), then bridge through that diagonal so the top player cannot catch themselves.

It belongs under mount escapes alongside the elbow escape. Martial Index treats “upa” as the full trap-and-roll sequence and “bridge” as the hip engine inside it.

How Upa Escape Works

Elbows in first so they cannot climb. Trap an arm to your chest (or control the wrist tightly), hook the same-side ankle with your foot, turn the head toward the roll, and bridge explosively over the trapped shoulder.

Angle. Think 12–1 o’clock, not straight up. Straight bridges lift without tipping.

Finish. Follow into their closed guard or scramble to top; settle before hunting submissions.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar help glue the trapped arm; pants grips can assist the foot trap. Watch for cross-collar threats while you set traps.

No-Gi. Underhook or wrist hug replaces the sleeve; expect them to free the foot faster—re-trap or abandon to elbow escape.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

They sit high hunting a collar choke; you bump to make a hand post, trap that arm and foot, clear your head, and roll them into your closed guard—then posture and pass.

If they step the free foot wide early, skip the upa and frame for the elbow escape.

Types / Variations

Classic trap-and-roll

Arm to chest + ankle hook + diagonal bridge into guard.

Wrist-and-elbow trap

More control on the posting arm before the roll—common competition detail.

Upa threat into elbow escape

Use the bridge to force a post, then shrimp the knee inside without completing the roll.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: high mount, one-hand posts, failed americana or cross-collar attempts, chest-to-chest mounts that lean forward.

Counters: free the foot, base the free hand early, technical mount, or grapevine the legs to kill bridges.

Your chain: upa → if blocked, elbow escape → half guard → full guard or stand.

Upa Escape vs Elbow Escape

Factor Upa Escape Elbow Escape
Goal Reverse to top / their guard Recover your guard
Best vs High / posting mount Low / heavy hip mount
Key trap Arm + same-side foot Elbow–knee frames + shrimp
Failure mode Free foot / no angle No space / toes wrong for insert

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal at all belts. Reversing mount into top position typically scores as a sweep when criteria are met; escaping to guard stops the mount points from sticking if done before stabilization windows close—know your rule set.

Safety

Control the roll in drilling so partners do not post awkwardly on the shoulder. Tap if a choke is already locked while you set traps.

Common mistakes

  • Bridging without trapping the foot.
  • Leaving the head in the way of the roll.
  • Muscling a vertical bridge against a wide base.
  • Repeating a dead upa instead of switching to elbow escape.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the upa escape?

A mount escape that traps the opponent’s arm and same-side foot, then bridges diagonally to reverse the position.

When should I use upa vs elbow escape?

Upa when they are high or posting; elbow escape when they are low and heavy on the hips.

Why does my upa fail?

Usually the free foot posts, the bridge is vertical, or the arm trap is loose. Re-trap or switch escapes.

Does upa work in no-gi?

Yes with underhooks/wrists instead of sleeves—expect faster foot frees.

Where do I land after a successful upa?

Usually in their closed guard; settle posture before passing or standing.

Is upa only for white belts?

No. Higher belts refine traps, angles, and upa-to-elbow chains under better mounts.

How does the bridge concept relate?

The bridge is the hip drive; the upa is the full escape that uses it with traps.

The Bottom Line

The upa escape is trap, angle, bridge, follow. When the foot frees or the mount sits low, stop forcing the roll and shrimp to the elbow escape.
