Downward Elbow (Spike)

Downward Elbow (Spike) chops from high to low—forehead, orbital, collarbone—or spikes a caught thigh.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Raise, then chop down like an axe.
  • Compare Chop Elbow and Diagonal Elbow.
  • Clinch and catch setups are common.
  • Some MMA rules ban 12–6; MT differs.
  • Sharp bone edge > soft forearm slap.

What Is Downward Elbow (Spike)?

Downward Elbow (Sok Tong / spike) travels high-to-low onto head or shoulders—beside Horizontal Elbow and Chop Elbow.

Searchers wanting “12-to-6 elbow Muay Thai” need stadium context—legality differs from some MMA rules.

How Downward Elbow (Spike) Works

Chamber elbow above, drive tip/edge downward, rotate shoulder, free hand controls or covers.

Step in or pull them into the path—pure arm chops lack power.

On a caught kick, thigh spike is classical—train safely. Recover to guard.

Mechanically, successful downward elbow (spike) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Muay Thai Stance with hands alive.

If Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into elbows or clinch threats you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Kick Check awareness; pocket strikes need Long Guard or tight covers; clinch tools need posture.

Stadium vs Gym / Rules Notes

Under traditional stadium Muay Thai, downward elbow (spike) lives inside a full Eight Limbs rule set—elbows, knees, and clinch time change how often you invest in this tool.

Amateur cards, kickboxing crossovers, and some promotions restrict elbows or clinch duration. Gym culture may still drill downward elbow (spike) daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit.

Confirm the bout agreement before importing stadium frequency into restricted rules or MMA, where wrestling reframes clinch incentives.

Scoring emphasis varies. Some judges still reward hard kicks and balance; others read more like kickboxing. Let the card’s eyes shape volume.

Amateur and some crossover cards restrict elbows—confirm before fight night even if the gym drills them daily.

Cuts change stadium dynamics; referees may pause for blood. Train clean technique and hygiene.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Clinch: pull posture down, downward elbow.

Catch kick → thigh spike (controlled).

Pad: elevated pad, chop down with footwork.

Fight picture: downward elbow (spike) appears when Range and Timing say so, then yields to the next limb in the Eight Limbs chain.

Gym test: can you apply downward elbow (spike) after a Jab feint (or a teep feint) and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Forearm slap instead of tip.
  • No control hand in clinch.
  • Huge wind-up.
  • From kicking range.
  • Ignoring rule differences.
  • Treating downward elbow (spike) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox downward elbow (spike) inside full rounds; return to Muay Thai Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up (punch, kick, or clinch) so downward elbow (spike) connects to Eight Limbs.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Timing is part of the skill.

Study Notes
