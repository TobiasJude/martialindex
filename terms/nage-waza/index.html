Nage-Waza

Nage-waza is the Kodokan classification for judo’s throwing techniques—standing and sacrifice throws that break balance and put uke on the mat for score or groundwork.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Nage-waza covers te/koshi/ashi/sutemi maps.
  • Clean throws chase Ippon; near-misses are Waza-Ari/yuko under Judo Rules.
  • Throws run on Kuzushi→tsukuri→kake.
  • Learn Ukemi before hard nage-komi; groove with Uchi-Komi.
  • Gi grips differ from no-gi handles; mechanics transfer.
  • Follow throws into Ne-Waza.

What is nage-waza?

Nage-waza (投げ技) is the Kodokan family of throwing techniques—the skills that put uke on the mat with control when judo looks like judo.

People search it because World Tour and Olympic contests still swing on throws: a clean Ippon ends the match; Waza-Ari and the reinstated yuko change the board under Judo Rules.

Kodokan maps throws into te-, koshi-, ashi-, and sutemi-waza. Tachi-Waza is the standing subset; sacrifice throws like Tomoe Nage still count as nage-waza.

This page is the Martial Index throws pillar under the judo style hub, linking live batch throws instead of a glossary stub.

If you remember one sentence: throws run on Kuzushi, not biceps.

How it works

Teach and drill kuzushi (off-balance) → tsukuri (entry/fit) → kake (finish). Miss step one and you collide with a rooted partner.

Hands set direction; hips/legs make the fulcrum; the finish rotates uke onto back or side for the referee’s landing read.

Combinations score at high levels: failed Ouchi Gari into Uchi Mata (Judo); blocked seoi into Kouchi Gari or Tai Otoshi.

After landing, think Ne-Waza immediately—Kesa Gatame, Yoko Shiho Gatame, or Juji Gatame if the arm is free.

Judogi vs no-gi judo

The Judogi gives sleeve and lapel levers that define classic IJF gripping. Seoi, uchi-mata, and sleeve-driven ashi thrive here.

No-gi keeps the same hip/leg stories with underhooks, wrist control, and head position instead of fabric.

Transfers to BJJ/MMA: timing and off-balancing. Does not transfer: stalling in a collar grip the rules no longer reward.

What it looks like in practice

Right-vs-right grips: tori pulls uke onto the toes, pivots for Seoi Nage (Judo), and if uke posts, chains Ouchi Gari without a polite reset.

In Randori, the same player may hunt only foot sweeps (Deashi Harai, Kouchi Gari) for a minute, then change levels when weight shifts.

No-gi: the Osoto Gari (Judo) story remains—drive across the chest—but the sleeve hand becomes an elbow clamp.

Entries, counters, combinations

Build entries with static and moving Uchi-Komi, then nage-komi, then Randori.

Counters punish over-rotated hips—train kaeshi under a coach.

Classic links: inside↔outside reap; forward load↔back attack; hip sweep↔inner thigh (Harai Goshi/Uchi Mata (Judo)).

Compare with ne-waza

Nage-Waza scores by how uke lands. Ne-Waza scores by pin time or submission. Incomplete judo ignores either half.

IJF notes & safety

Under IJF 2026–28 rules, osaekomi scores yuko at 5–9s, waza-ari at 10–19s, and ippon at 20s. Yuko is live again and does not add up to waza-ari; two waza-ari make ippon.

Techniques must read as judo with continuity; bridging to avoid the back is scored against you.

Safety: Ukemi before hard throws; qualified coaching; tap early on the ground.

Modern IJF leg-grab bans mean freestyle pickups are not a free import—check Judo Rules.

Common mistakes

  • Muscling without Kuzushi.
  • Head-first entries with hips far away.
  • Single-shot attacks with no combination plan.
  • Skipping breakfalls, then fearing Randori.
  • Throwing and posing—no Ne-Waza follow-up.

Historically, Kodokan curricula organised throws so beginners met clear kuzushi stories before high-amplitude spectacle. That still matches how good clubs teach: foot sweeps and hip throws teach direction; shoulder throws teach loading; sacrifice throws teach commitment and ukemi trust.

Modern World Tour frequency data often elevates the seoi family and inside/outside reaps alongside classic uchi-mata highlight culture. Martial Index therefore ships Seoi Nage (Judo), Ippon Seoi Nage, Ouchi Gari, and Kouchi Gari in the same batch as Uchi Mata (Judo)—not because lore died, but because searchers and competitors both need the live meta.

When you watch IJF contests, notice how rarely a throw appears from a frozen bow stance. Athletes create motion, steal grips, and throw on the recovery step. Your uchi-komi should eventually look like that: moving, reactive, slightly messy, still technical.

Cross-training note: Brazilian jiu-jitsu pages on this site for osoto-gari, seoi-nage, and uchi-mata remain BJJ-context articles. Use them as cousins with clear labels; this pillar and its judo-prefixed children are the IJF/Kodokan line.

A practical weekly template many coaches approximate: ukemi warm-up, gripping rounds, uchi-komi themes, nage-komi on two tokui throws, then randori with a ne-waza finish requirement. That single template ties this pillar to ukemi, randori, and ne-waza.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is nage-waza only standing throws?

No—sutemi like Tomoe Nage count too. Tachi-Waza is the standing subset.

Why do my throws fail on bigger partners?

Usually missing Kuzushi or tsukuri. Fix Uchi-Komi before power.

Gi vs no-gi?

Same hips/legs; different grips.

Beginner throw?

Often O Goshi, Deashi Harai, Osoto Gari (Judo)—follow your coach.

Relation to BJJ takedowns?

Shared DNA; BJJ pages stay BJJ-labelled.

Pull guard in judo?

False attacks/stalls risk shido under Judo Rules.

Do I need ippon every time?

No—Waza-Ari, yuko, and pins win contests.

Where next?

Seoi Nage (Judo) or Kuzushi.

The Bottom Line

Map Nage-Waza through Kuzushi, Uchi-Komi, and Ukemi, then score under Judo Rules. Continue with Seoi Nage (Judo).
