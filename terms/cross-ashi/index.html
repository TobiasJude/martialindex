Cross Ashi

Cross ashi (cross ashi garami) is gym language for a crossed-leg entanglement that overlaps heavily with saddle / honey hole / inside sankaku naming. Expect inside heel-hook pressure once the secondary leg is controlled.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Cross ashi ≈ crossed inside entanglement—often paths into saddle/honey hole.
  • Parent: ashigarami. Sibling: inside ashi, outside ashi.
  • Verify IBJJF / ADCC + reaping before a gi A-game.
  • Entries: saddle entries. Defense: heel-hook defense.
  • Name drift is real—confirm which label your room uses for the triangle-legged inside position.

What Is Cross Ashi?

Cross ashi describes legs crossed relative to the opponent’s trapped leg to create a tighter inside entanglement. Many rooms use it interchangeably with early saddle / inside sankaku; others reserve it for a specific crossed-hook shape. Nest under ashigarami. Across: inside ashi, 50/50, saddle heel hook.

How It Works

Establish the inside line, cross or triangle your legs for secondary control, pin the far hip, and isolate the heel. The cross reduces their ability to stand out or hand-fight freely.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Control literacy plus legal ankle/toe options where allowed; heel-hook finishes are often restricted.

No-gi. Home for cross-ashi → saddle → inside heel-hook chains under permissive rulesets.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From butterfly or K-guard you reap/cross into the inside triangle, glue the secondary leg, and dig the heel—or reverse if they expose the far side.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Cross-ashi entry into full saddle / honey hole (inside sankaku).

Competition variation

Double-trouble / secondary-leg rides from the crossed configuration.

Related option

Bail to 50/50 or outside ashi when the cross collapses.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

See saddle entries. Defense starts with heel hiding (heel-hook defense).

How It Compares

Vs inside ashi: cross/saddle is typically tighter. Vs outside ashi: opposite line. Vs 50/50: shared risk vs one-sided dominance.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Cross-read reaping and heel hook.

Safety

Leg attacks load ligaments fast. Tap early, drill slowly, and never crank through pain. Partners should release immediately on tap or freeze.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming cross ashi always means honey hole without clarifying.
  • Cranking heels before secondary-leg control.
  • Ignoring reaping flags in IBJJF gi.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is cross ashi in BJJ?

A crossed inside-leg entanglement closely related to saddle/honey hole/inside sankaku naming, used mainly for inside heel hooks.

Is cross ashi the same as saddle?

Often overlapping in gym talk; some coaches distinguish an early crossed shape from a fully triangulated saddle.

Legal in IBJJF gi?

Heel-hook and reaping interactions vary—verify the current rulebook for your division.

How do you enter?

From open guard, butterfly, K-guard, backsteps, and other saddle-entry trees.

Main finishes?

Inside heel hook where legal; ankle locks/toe holds as legal mirrors.

How to defend?

Hide the heel, protect the knee line, hand-fight, and extract—see heel-hook defense.

Related notes?

Ashigarami, saddle, honey hole, inside ashi, saddle entries, 50/50, reaping.

The Bottom Line

Cross ashi is the naming bridge into heavy inside control—map it to your room’s saddle/honey hole language and keep the rulebooks open.
