Collar Sleeve to Back

Collar sleeve to back is the gi path that turns collar-sleeve control into back control when they post or turn—clustered with collar-sleeve sweep, take the back, and berimbolo-adjacent spins.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Collar + sleeve create the steering wheel.
  • When they post to stop a sweep, spin behind.
  • Hooks and seatbelt finish the take.
  • Gi-native; no-gi uses wrist/elbow analogs.
  • Do not chase the back without controlling the far arm.

What Is Collar Sleeve to Back?

This transition converts collar-and-sleeve open guard into rear mount. Parent: collar-sleeve guard. Destination: back control / take the back. Across: collar-sleeve sweep, berimbolo.

How It Works

Maintain collar and sleeve, off-balance them forward or sideways, and when they post or turn, hip-heist or invert around the far shoulder, insert hooks, and lock a seatbelt. Clear their near elbow so they cannot block the climb.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Native—collar is the steering grip.

No-gi. Wrist rides and head position approximate; true collar absent.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Collar-sleeve platform → they post on the sweep → you spin behind → hooks + seatbelt.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Sit-up / hip-heist back take from collar-sleeve.

Competition variation

Invert / berimbolo-adjacent spin when they stand heavy.

Related option

Failed back take returns to collar-sleeve sweep or DLR.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From collar-sleeve vs posture or failed toreando. Counters. Grip breaks, toreando, DLR pass if a hook appears.

How It Compares

Vs sweep: same platform, different finish. Vs pure berimbolo: berimbolo is a fuller invert system often from DLR.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

IBJJF gi back takes score; verify current back points on IBJJF rules.

Safety

Insert hooks carefully—avoid spinal twisting cranks while spinning.

Common mistakes

  • Spinning without far-arm control.
  • Losing the collar mid-take.
  • No hooks after you arrive.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is collar sleeve to back?

Using collar-and-sleeve control to spin behind into back control.

Gi-only?

True collar version yes; no-gi uses analogs.

When do I choose back over sweep?

When they post/turn and give the shoulder line.

What next?

Seatbelt, hooks, then rear-naked or collar chokes.

Common mistake?

Spinning without controlling the far elbow/arm.

Beginner-friendly?

Intermediate—after basic collar-sleeve sweeps.

Martial Index home?

Under collar-sleeve guard toward back-control.

The Bottom Line

Collar sleeve to back is the steering-wheel back take—keep the grips, wait for the post, then finish hooks like take the back teaches.
