Collar Choke

A collar choke is any Brazilian jiu-jitsu strangle that uses the gi lapel as the primary choking surface—most often from closed guard, mount, or back control. It is gi-only as a family (contrast rear naked choke).

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Lapel depth + angle + posture break beat raw squeeze.
  • Umbrella for cross-collar, loop, bow-and-arrow, baseball, paper cutter, clock, and related feeds.
  • Gi-only family—train no-gi analogs separately (RNC, arm-triangle, neckties).
  • Defend by hand-fighting collars early and tucking the chin.
  • Pillars: closed guard, mount, back control depending on entry.

What Is Collar Choke?

“Collar choke” is the catalog umbrella for strangles that feed a gi collar across the neck. Specific names—cross collar, loop, bow and arrow, baseball bat, paper cutter, clock—describe grip geometry and position.

Pedagogically it lives next to closed-guard attacks and mount pressure games. Without a gi, the family disappears and you migrate to naked-neck tools like the rear naked choke.

How Collar Choke Works

Get a deep collar grip (four fingers in is common), remove slack, place the blade of the wrist/forearm on the neck, and finish by curling wrists, extending legs, or rotating the body depending on the variant.

Bottom (guard). Break posture first—collar chokes without posture control are easy to posture out of.

Top. Stabilize the pin, then feed collars; do not abandon mount/side for a loose grip.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi — required. Collar chokes need lapels. Gi thickness, wash, and grip fighting change difficulty.

No-Gi — not applicable as collar tools. Use RNC, guillotine, arm-triangle, darce, and necktie families instead. Some “ezekiel” ideas translate with sleeves or no-gi biceps grips—see ezekiel entry.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From closed guard: deep cross collar, second collar or sleeve assist, curl and pull the head in. From mount: Roger-style deep first lapel, then second grip, wrists rotate. From back: bow-and-arrow feeds one collar and a pant.

Types / Variations

Cross-collar family

Opposing collar grips from guard or mount—see cross-collar-choke.

Loop / sliding lapel

One deep collar with head control or sliding second lapel—loop and clock cousins.

Back collar finishes

Bow-and-arrow and related back lapel strangles.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: closed guard posture breaks, mount pressure, side-control collar feeds, turtle clock setups, back seatbelt to collar.

Counters: posture, hand-fight collars, tuck chin, turn into the grip, or explode to escape the position before the second grip lands.

Chain to armbars and triangles when they defend collars with extended arms.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Collar chokes are legal in IBJJF gi divisions with standard sleeve/collar grip rules. They do not exist in no-gi divisions—use legal naked-neck submissions instead.

Safety

Apply gradually in training; collar bone pressure can be harsh. Tap early. Do not crank necks while drilling bow-and-arrow angles.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow grips with all squeeze, no depth.
  • Attacking collars before breaking posture from guard.
  • Abandoning a good pin for a desperate collar.
  • Ignoring no-gi training because collar chokes feel comfortable.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a collar choke in BJJ?

A strangle that uses the gi lapel across the neck—umbrella for many named gi chokes.

Do collar chokes work in no-gi?

No as lapel tools. Train RNC and other naked-neck submissions for no-gi.

Is cross collar the same as collar choke?

Cross collar is the most famous subtype; see cross-collar choke.

Best positions for collar chokes?

Closed guard, mount, back control, side control, and turtle (clock) are classics.

How do I defend collar chokes?

Hand-fight early, posture, tuck chin, and escape the position before the second grip sets.

Are they blood or air chokes?

Most high-percentage collar finishes are blood chokes (carotids) with some air/pressure hybrids depending on angle.

What should I learn first?

A basic cross collar from closed guard and mount, then bow-and-arrow from the back.

The Bottom Line

Collar choke is the gi strangle family: deep lapels, posture or pin control, and finish geometry. Name the subtype for details—and keep RNC sharp for no-gi.
