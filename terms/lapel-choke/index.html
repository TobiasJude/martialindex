Lapel Choke

A lapel choke uses the gi collar/lapel as the strangling structure—umbrella language for cross collar, bow and arrow, and related collar strangles. Usually blood choke / strangulation mechanics when the blade of the collar tracks the carotids.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Gi-only family under chokes / collar choke.
  • Named children: cross collar, bow and arrow, baseball-bat styles.
  • Mechanics: typically blood choke when applied cleanly.
  • Feed deep, remove slack, and use body alignment—not arm strength alone.
  • Medical caution: neck submissions can cause unconsciousness or airway injury. In training, tap early; partners release immediately on tap or loss of consciousness and seek qualified medical help if needed. This page is reference—not medical advice.

What Is Lapel Choke?

Umbrella term for submissions that use the opponent’s lapel/collar to strangle. Related pages: collar choke, cross-collar choke, bow-and-arrow choke.

How It Works

Insert one or both hands deep into the collar, set the blade of the fabric against the neck, remove slack, and apply pressure with legs/hips/shoulder alignment. Clean versions behave like blood chokes; poor angles crank the throat (air).

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Home environment—without a gi, the family disappears or becomes hand/arm analogs.

No-gi. No true lapel choke; study RNC and arm-triangle analogs instead.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From mount, feed a cross collar deep, second hand assists, elbows in, chest pressure—tap from vascular pressure.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Cross-collar from mount/guard.

Competition variation

Bow-and-arrow and rear lapel variants.

Related option

Baseball-bat and looping collar attacks from knee-on-belly/side.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

From mount, side, knee-on-belly, back, and closed guard. Defense: hand fighting, chin tuck, and bridging—see also RNC defense habits for rear versions.

How It Compares

Vs RNC: fabric vs arms. Vs generic collar choke: largely synonymous umbrella language.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Central to IBJJF gi competition scoring via submission.

Safety

Medical caution: neck submissions can cause unconsciousness or airway injury. In training, tap early; partners release immediately on tap or loss of consciousness and seek qualified medical help if needed. This page is reference—not medical advice.

Common mistakes

  • Shallow grips with pure arm curl.
  • Cranking the trachea and calling it “just a collar choke.”
  • Neglecting body alignment.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a lapel choke?

A gi submission that uses the collar/lapel to strangle the neck.

Is it a blood choke?

Clean applications usually are; poor angles can become air pressure.

Famous versions?

Cross collar, bow and arrow, baseball bat, and many rear lapel chokes.

No-gi equivalent?

No true lapel—use RNC and other arm-based strangles.

Key detail?

Deep feeds, slack removal, and body alignment.

Safety?

Tap early; release on tap; respect neck submissions.

Related notes?

Collar choke, cross collar, bow and arrow, blood choke, strangulation.

The Bottom Line

A lapel choke is the gi collar-strangle family—feed deep, track the carotids like a blood choke, and keep medical caution non-negotiable.
