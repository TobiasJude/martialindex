Crab Ride

Crab ride is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu back-take position/ride where your legs hook and control their hips from an angle—climbing toward rear mount and rear-naked choke finishes, popularized in modern no-gi instructionals (including Craig Jones material).

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Crab ride = hip-oriented ride into the back.
  • Goal is rear mount / RNC, not living forever in the ride.
  • Huge in modern no-gi; gi versions use pants grips.
  • Often entered from turtle, deep half, or failed passes.
  • Related back control, truck.

What Is Crab Ride?

Crab ride is a transitional back-take ride: your legs clamp and hook around their hips/waist from an off-angle while you climb toward seatbelt and rear mount. It is not always full back control yet—it is the ride that gets you there.

How Crab Ride Works

Hip control limits their ability to turn into you or stand cleanly. You maintain the ride, insert hooks, win the seatbelt, and settle into rear mount for chokes. Lose the hip connection and they turn belly-down or scramble free. Modern no-gi curricula emphasize crab ride as a high-percentage path off turtle and certain guard passes.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. Primary modern showcase—body locks and hooks without cloth.

Gi. Pants and belt grips can sticky the ride; same climb to the back.

What Does Crab Ride Look Like in Practice?

Jordan turtles. Alex attaches a crab ride on the hips, climbs hooks, wins seatbelt, and finishes a rear-naked choke from rear mount.

Types / Variations

Basic crab ride

Hip hooks + upper-body attachment, climbing toward the back.

Climb to rear mount

Insert hooks, seatbelt, settle—mission complete.

Common entries

Turtle, deep half exposures, failed guard passes, certain scrambles.

Escaping crab ride (bottom)

Turn into them, peel hooks, recover guard or stand before seatbelt locks.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Turtle attacks; deep-half/back exposures; scramble rides.

Top threats. Back take, RNC, occasional truck if they roll the wrong way.

Bottom counters. Don't stay static in turtle; peel hips free early.

Crab Ride vs Back Control

Factor Crab Ride Back Control
Status Transitional ride Settled rear mount / hooks
Primary job Get to the back Finish / control
Scoring Usually not yet Back points when hooks + control criteria met

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Points typically arrive once back control criteria are met—not merely from a loose ride. Know your federation's back-control definition.

Safety

Control neck cranks while climbing. Tap early to RNCs.

Common mistakes

  • Riding forever without inserting hooks / seatbelt.
  • Loose hip connection—they turn in.
  • Abandoning the ride to chase a submission too early.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is crab ride the same as back control?

No. Crab ride is the ride that climbs toward back control; rear mount with hooks is the settled position.

Who popularized crab ride instructionals?

Modern no-gi coaches (including Craig Jones material) made the ride a standard teaching topic; older wrestling/BJJ rides share DNA.

Crab ride vs truck?

Crab ride hunts the back; truck is a leg-entangled control often aimed at twister/banana-split style attacks.

Does it score points?

Usually once you achieve defined back control—not from the ride alone. Check your rules.

Best finish from crab ride?

Climb to rear mount and attack the rear-naked choke (or gi chokes).

How do I escape crab ride?

Peel hip hooks, turn into the attacker, recover guard or stand before the seatbelt settles.

The Bottom Line

Crab ride hooks the hips to climb into rear mount—modern no-gi back-take essential. Don't live in the ride; finish the back. See back control and truck.
