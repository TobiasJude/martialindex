Baseball Bat Choke

The baseball bat choke uses opposing palm orientations on the collars (like gripping a bat) and finishes with rotational pressure—classically from knee-on-belly or side control. It is gi-only.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Palm-up + palm-down collar grips; angle the forearm across the neck.
  • Spin/walk toward north-south to tighten—do not only squeeze in place.
  • Gi-only. Famous from knee-on-belly (Bernardo Faria-style pressure).
  • Sibling of paper cutter and cross collar in the pin-choke cluster.
  • Defend by hand-fighting collars and recovering frames before the spin.

What Is Baseball Bat Choke?

Named for the bat-like grip—one palm up, one palm down on the collars—the baseball bat choke is a rotational gi strangle. Pressure players love it from knee-on-belly because the spin costs little position when done correctly.

It lives under side-control / mount family attacks and cross-links to paper cutter and cross collar.

How Baseball Bat Choke Works

Set the two collar grips with space between hands appropriate to their neck, get the forearm angle across the throat, then rotate your body (often toward north-south) while keeping arms relatively straight so the collars cut.

Head position. Forehead near the far hip and a posted leg can pin them during the spin.

Bottom. Clear grips, shrimp early, and do not let knee-on-belly settle.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi — required.

No-Gi. Not available. Use north-south choke, arm-triangle, and darce-family attacks as pin alternatives.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Knee-on-belly: establish grips, then spin to north-south as the choke tightens—Bernardo-style. Side control: same grips with a shorter walk.

Types / Variations

Knee-on-belly baseball

Highest percentage teaching entry for many gyms.

Side-control baseball

Grips from side, rotate to finish.

Bottom baseball variants

Less common upside-down or bottom feeds—specialist.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: knee-on-belly after passes, side control collar feeds, failed cross collars that leave mixed grips.

Counters: peel grips, bridge and shrimp, turn into the choke carefully, recover half/closed before the spin.

If the spin is stuffed, keep side control and switch to paper cutter or mount.

Baseball Bat vs Paper Cutter

Factor Baseball Bat Choke Paper Cutter Choke
Grip story Bat grip on collars + spin Blade forearm across neck from pin
Classic start Knee-on-belly Side / north-south
Motion Rotational walk Shoulder drop / slice
Gi-only? Yes Yes (classic)

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi. Not a no-gi submission. Maintain legal knee-on-belly and grip rules.

Safety

The spin can finish suddenly—apply with control. Protect training partners’ necks.

Common mistakes

  • Squeezing bent arms without rotating.
  • Wrong forearm angle (no cut across the neck).
  • Abandoning base so they reverse during the spin.
  • Telegraphing grips without pressure.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the baseball bat choke?

A gi choke with palm-up/palm-down collar grips finished by rotating the body to cut the neck.

Is it gi-only?

Yes.

Best entry?

Knee-on-belly is the most taught; side control works too.

Why must I spin?

Rotation aligns the collars and forearm into a true strangle; stationary squeezes often fail.

Baseball vs paper cutter?

Different grip stories and finishes—see paper cutter.

No-gi alternative from the same pins?

North-south choke and arm-triangle families. See north-south choke.

Defense?

Clear collars early and escape knee-on-belly/side before the spin starts.

The Bottom Line

Baseball bat is grip geometry plus rotation: bat the collars, cut with the forearm, spin to finish. Pure gi—keep north-south choke ready for no-gi pins.
