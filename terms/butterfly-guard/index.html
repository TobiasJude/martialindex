Butterfly Guard

Butterfly guard is an open-guard system that hooks both feet inside the opponent's thighs to elevate and sweep—especially strong in no-gi with overhooks, and a natural gateway into X-guard and single-leg X.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Inside hooks + elevation = butterfly.
  • Choose an overhook or underhook side before sweeping.
  • Excellent in no-gi; still strong in gi with collar ties.
  • Gateway to X-guard and SLX when they post a leg.
  • Passes smash hooks and flatten your shoulders.

What Is Butterfly Guard?

Butterfly guard is an open guard where you sit (or recline) with both feet hooked inside the opponent's thighs like butterfly wings, using elevation to sweep or create angles. Marcelo Garcia made it world-famous; it remains a cornerstone of no-gi and a strong gi option when sleeves are contested.

How Butterfly Works

The hooks lift their knees while your upper body overhooks, underhooks, or collar-ties to choose a direction. Elevation plus a pull dumps them over your shoulder line for the classic butterfly sweep. From there you can come up to mount, take the back, or enter X-guard if they post a leg.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. Butterfly thrives—overhooks, underhooks, and wrist control replace fabric. It is a default seated home for many ADCC-style athletes.

Gi. Still excellent with collar ties and sleeve assists. Some athletes prefer spider/lasso for standing passers, but butterfly remains a kneeling-passer answer.

What Does Butterfly Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits in butterfly as Jordan kneels. Alex overhooks Jordan's right arm, elevates both hooks, and sweeps Jordan to the overhook side into mount—Marcelo-style timing.

Types / Variations

Seated butterfly

Upright torso, both hooks in—the reference.

Supine butterfly

More on the back for certain sweep angles and transitions.

Single butterfly / half butterfly

One hook inside half-guard structures.

Butterfly to X / SLX

When they post a leg, elevate into X-guard or single-leg X.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. Guard pulls to seats, recovering from half, opening closed guard into hooks.

Passes. Smash both hooks, force flat shoulders, knee cut after clearing hooks, body lock.

Counters. Reinsert hooks, switch to X/SLX, or stand to wrestle up.

Butterfly Guard vs X-Guard

Factor Butterfly X-Guard
Hooks Inside both thighs from seated X configuration under a standing/posted leg
Primary job Elevate and sweep Off-balance standing base; sweep or take back
Entry feel Often first seated home Often second stage after elevation

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Butterfly is legal everywhere. Sweeps score normally. When butterfly enters heel-hook ranges via SLX, follow your event's leg-lock rules.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Elevate with control so partners are not dumped onto their necks. Tap early in scramble submissions.

Common mistakes

  • Hooks without upper-body control—easy to smash.
  • Sweeping without choosing an overhook/underhook side.
  • In the gi: ignoring collar ties that would freeze posture.
  • Never transitioning to X when they post a long leg.
  • Sitting upright with hips stuck to the mat.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why do my butterfly sweeps fail?

You are elevating without upper-body control or without committing to one side. Secure an overhook or underhook, then lift and pull along that line.

Is butterfly better in no-gi?

It shines in no-gi because you are not competing with spider/lasso, but it is not no-gi-only. Collar ties make gi butterfly sticky too.

When should I go to X-guard?

When they post a long leg or stand to kill your elevation—slide into X or SLX instead of forcing a dead butterfly sweep.

Seated or on my back?

Seated is the default teaching position. Supine butterfly appears in transitions and against certain pressure.

How do people smash butterfly?

They flatten your shoulders, control your head, and clear both hooks—often with a body lock or heavy knee pressure. Keep frames and reinsert hooks early.

Butterfly vs half guard?

Butterfly elevates both legs from a seat; half traps one leg. Many athletes flow between half butterfly and full butterfly.

The Bottom Line

Butterfly hooks inside the thighs to elevate and sweep—Marcelo's classic, still modern in no-gi. When they post, move to X-guard or SLX.
