Guillotine from Guard

The guillotine from guard is a guillotine finished from closed guard (or butterfly): you catch the neck as they posture down or shoot, lock a grip, close your legs to kill rotation, and finish with a high elbow or arm-in structure—not a wild arch.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Closed guard is an excellent finishing platform because they cannot easily roll out.
  • Decide arm-in vs high-elbow / marcelotine before you squeeze.
  • Parent: guillotine. Standing cousin: front headlock.
  • Sit-up entries overlap hip-bump and kimura timing.
  • Gi collar can assist; the choke itself is no-gi native.

What Is Guillotine from Guard?

When someone dives their head into your closed guard—to pass, to hug, or after a failed shot—you can wrap the neck and finish a guillotine without ever standing up. Guard also lets you sit up, snap the head, and fall back with the choke already on.

It is one of the highest-percentage ways to finish any guillotine family because rotation escapes (their best defense) die when your legs are closed. That is why instructors tell people who catch a standing guillotine to sit to closed or half rather than wrestle a stalemate on the feet.

Martial Index keeps the parent guillotine for the whole family and this page for the guard-specific search (“guillotine from closed guard”).

How Guillotine from Guard Works

Break posture or meet a driving head. Wrap the choking arm so the wrist bone or radial edge sits under the chin / against the carotids—not as a forearm punch into the windpipe if you can help it.

Connect the support hand (high-elbow shelf, arm-in gable, or Marcelo-style fist/ball-and-socket). Close guard or at least pinch a high guard / butterfly hook so they cannot walk their knees around.

Finish on a diagonal: crunch, lift the elbow, and bring knees toward your chest rather than hyperextending your back. Fall slightly to the choking-arm hip, not straight backward.

If they hide the neck, convert to an arm-in finish, sweep, or front-headlock control rather than cranking a shallow chin strap.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar assists and sleeve frames help snap the head down; you can also mix a collar choke threat with the guillotine sit-up.

No-gi. Wrist and chin-strap fights dominate. Expect more arm-in catches when they drive a single-leg into your guard.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits up from closed guard as they hug the hips, snaps the neck, closes the guard, and finishes a high-elbow guillotine.

Jordan catches an arm-in as they shoot into guard, closes the legs, and taps them before they can rotate to the side.

Types / Variations

Closed-guard high elbow / marcelotine

Elbow shelves on the trap; knees to chest. See high-elbow and marcelotine pages.

Closed-guard arm-in

Their near arm is inside the loop—easier catch, more technical squeeze.

Butterfly / sit-up guillotine

Marcelo-style hook lift into the neck from seated guard.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: they posture down to pass, hug your hips, or shoot a sloppy single into your guard. Counters: posture, hide the chin, extract the head toward the free-arm side. Escapes: rotate, stand, or peel the grip before the guard closes—once the legs lock, tap sooner.

Guillotine from Guard vs Standing / Front Headlock

Factor From guard Standing / front headlock
Rotation defense Hard (legs closed) Easier (they can circle)
Entry Sit-up or they dive in Snap-down, sprawl, shot counter
Risk Getting stacked / passed if shallow Getting taken down if they lift
Best finish school High elbow or arm-in Same schools + darce/anaconda branches

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult guillotines are standard legal chokes; crank-only neck attacks can be treated differently—verify language in the current book. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

A bad guillotine is a neck crank. Finish as a choke (carotids / under-chin structure), tap early, and never rip someone over your shoulder in drilling.

Common mistakes

  • Arching straight back instead of crunching to the hip.
  • Open guard that lets them walk around the choke.
  • Squeezing a forearm into the windpipe with no structure.
  • Catching the neck then forgetting to close the legs.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Should I sit to guard if I catch a standing guillotine?

Often yes—closed or half kills their rotation. Verify you can sit safely without getting slammed (rule- and gym-dependent).

Arm-in or arm-out from guard?

Arm-out/high-elbow is a cleaner choke; arm-in is easier to catch on a shot. Learn both.

Is the marcelotine different?

It is Marcelo Garcia’s high-elbow finishing school—see the marcelotine page.

Gi collar instead?

You can mix collars, but this page is the naked front choke from guard.

What if they stand up in my guard?

Keep the grip, climb closed or half, or sweep. Do not let them stack you square.

Beginner legal?

Yes as a choke. Drill control, not cranks.

Chain if they hide?

Darce, anaconda, or sweep to mount with the neck still owned.

The Bottom Line

Guillotine from guard is catch, close the legs, crunch to the hip. Pick a school—high elbow, arm-in, or marcelotine—and link back to the parent guillotine.
