Outside Sankaku

Outside sankaku names an outside triangle configuration on the opponent’s leg/hips—related to outside ashigarami conversations—used either to threaten rotational leg attacks or to climb toward a back take when they turn.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Sankaku = triangle; “outside” marks the configuration relative to inside sankaku/saddle.
  • Can threaten heels or convert to back exposure depending on system.
  • Do not confuse with the body-triangle on the torso from back control.
  • Rule sets matter for heel-hook finishes—verify.
  • Sibling study: outside ashi, saddle, reaping.

What Is Outside Sankaku?

In Japanese-influenced coaching, outside sankaku describes triangulating from the outside path—distinct from the inside sankaku of the saddle. Some schools use it as a heel-hook seat; others emphasize the back-take when the opponent rotates away from the entanglement.

Martial Index places it in the leg-entanglement cluster so readers landing on the term get orientation and links, not a claim that every gym draws the diagram identically.

How Outside Sankaku Works

Establish outside-line control, lock a triangle configuration as your system defines, and choose: expose the heel for a legal finish, or use the triangle to off-balance and climb the back if they turn.

Maintain hip connection; a loose triangle becomes a temporary grapevine they can peel.

If inside opportunities appear, transfer toward saddle/honeyhole rather than forcing a bad outside finish.

Because schools draw “outside sankaku” slightly differently, treat Martial Index as orientation: compare your coach’s triangle lock and back-take branch on the mat before you memorize a single Internet diagram.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Prefer back-take and legal lower-body options when heel hooks are restricted.

No-gi. Heel-hook or back-take branching is common.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

A realize-style outside sankaku to back take shows the position as a bridge, not only a foot crush.

Another athlete uses outside sankaku angles to set outside heel hooks under freer rules.

Types / Variations

Finishing outside sankaku

Heel-hook oriented.

Back-take outside sankaku

Triangle used to expose the back.

Transfer outside sankaku

Stepping stone into other ashi.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from K-guard, RDLR, and ashi scrambles. Counters: peel triangle, hide heel, turn carefully. Escapes: clear locking foot, reclaim posture, pass or recover guard.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. See reaping.

Safety

Triangle pressure plus rotation can stack knees and spines—drill with control.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing with torso body triangle.
  • Forcing heel hooks in banned divisions.
  • Loose triangle with no hip control.
  • Ignoring the back-take branch.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is this the body triangle?

No—this is a leg entanglement triangle, not the torso lock from back control.

Same as outside ashi?

Closely related; naming differs by school—compare on the mat.

Finish or back take?

Both exist—system dependent.

IBJJF?

Verify heel-hook/reaping constraints.

Beginner priority?

Learn outside ashi fundamentals first.

Gi useful?

Yes for back-take branches and legal controls.

Next?

Outside ashigarami + back take.

The Bottom Line

Outside sankaku is the outside-triangle label in the leg game: threaten the heel when legal or climb the back—always cross-check your coach’s diagram and the current rulebook.
