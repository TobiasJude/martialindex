Single-Leg Takedown

The single-leg takedown captures one leg and finishes by driving, running the pipe, or dumping—adapted from wrestling into BJJ via hand fighting and chains to the double-leg.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Control the leg at the knee/ankle with head position tight.
  • Finish variety beats one dump.
  • Chain to double when they sprawl or pummel.
  • Gi pants grips change finishes.
  • Watch guillotines on head-outside mistakes.

What Is Single-Leg Takedown?

A single-leg isolates one leg—high (above knee) or low (ankle/heel)—then removes the base with direction changes. Wrestling pedigree is obvious; BJJ adds guillotine awareness and the option to convert a failed single into a front headlock or pull.

Setups include arm drags, post-and-pulls, and cleared wrists. See arm drag and the takedown hub.

Sport: IBJJF may score a completed single with top stabilization; ADCC-style events value the control journey differently—verify rules.

How Single-Leg Takedown Works

Clear hands, level-change, step to the leg, secure (hands connected), head on the correct side, then finish: run the pipe, dump, trip, or switch to double.

If they sprawl, do not cling forever—elevate, turn the corner, or abandon to front headlock.

Drill finishes from a static single for ten reps each direction before live entries.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips help control the captured leg; beware their collar attacks on your neck.

No-Gi. Pure wrestling body mechanics; sweat demands tighter head position.

Both: head outside can invite guillotines—know your variant.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex arm-drags, picks the near leg, runs the pipe, and lands in a pass-ready half.

Jordan sprawls; Alex switches to a double and cuts the corner.

Gi: Alex cups the pant cuff and finishes a low single to ankle control.

Types / Variations

High single

Above-knee control, dump/trip finishes.

Low single / ankle

Heel cup and directional pull.

Run the pipe

Rotate to collapse their standing leg.

Single to double

Chain when the second leg becomes available.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: drags, hand clears, snap-downs, and failed throws.

Counters: sprawl, whizzer, underhook, and peeling the grip while circling.

Escapes when caught: keep weight on the free leg, pummel head, and peel.

Single-Leg vs Double-Leg

Factor Single-Leg Double-Leg
Legs attacked One Both
Setup feel Often from angles/drags Often from level-change square
Common chain Single → double Double → lift/turn
Guillotine risk Head-outside variants Blast doubles if head low

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Treat scoring as rulebook-dependent: completed takedowns with control may score in IBJJF; confirm current criteria. Do not assume Olympic wrestling scoring applies.

Safety

Knee torsional finishes can injure. Partner taps early; control the captured knee line in training.

Common mistakes

  • Head on the wrong side.
  • No finish plan after the catch.
  • Holding a dead single under a hard sprawl.
  • Ignoring the guillotine.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a single-leg?

A takedown that isolates one leg then collapses their base.

Best finish for beginners?

Run-the-pipe and simple dumps with head position.

When do I switch to double?

When they open the second leg or sprawl in a way that gives both.

Gi tips?

Use pant control; protect your neck from collars.

Main danger?

Guillotines and knee injuries from bad twisting.

Setup ideas?

Arm drag and wrist clears.

Next?

Double-leg and ankle-pick pages.

The Bottom Line

The single-leg is BJJ’s most common wrestling loan. Catch clean, finish with direction, and chain to the double when the mat asks for it.
