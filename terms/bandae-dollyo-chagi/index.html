What Is Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning)?

Bandae dollyo chagi (반대 돌려차기) is the reverse turning kick. ITF curricula treat it as a core spinning tool.

The path is not Dwi Chagi (Back Kick). A back kick drives a linear heel; reverse turning travels on a horizontal hooking or slashing arc.

Compare with Spinning Hook Kick: many gyms use the English name for the same family. Martial Index keeps both so ITF and sport-English searchers land correctly.

WT athletes may still throw the mechanic as a highlight or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) counter, even if the Korean classroom name is less common.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning) behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning) Works

Look over the spinning shoulder, pivot on the ball of the Pivot Foot (Taekwondo), and bring the kicking leg through on a wide arc.

Strike with heel, sole, or blade depending on school. Retract or ride the spin back to Taekwondo Stance.

A small step-across often lines the power so the heel meets the target at the end of the arc, not after it.

Hands stay compact. A wild arm swing can speed the spin and also gift a punch lane.

Mechanically, successful bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

ITF sparring: opponent over-commits a turning kick; you spin the reverse turning kick to the head.

Pad: coach holds a side target; you step across, spin, and hook through without falling off the line.

Fight picture: bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Spinning on the heel instead of the ball of the {pivot-foot-tkd}.
  • Kicking while still facing the opponent so power is spent too early.
  • Confusing the path with {dwi-chagi} and thrusting linear.
  • Dropping the eyes and losing the target.
  • Throwing it as a first kick every exchange.
  • Treating bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether bandae dollyo chagi (reverse turning) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Bandae dollyo vs spinning hook?

Same broad family. This page keeps the Korean/ITF name; Spinning Hook Kick is the English sport/demo label.

Bandae vs dwi chagi?

Dwi Chagi (Back Kick) is a linear back kick. Bandae dollyo is a reverse turning arc.

WT legal?

A valid spinning kick to trunk or head can score under World Taekwondo Rules if surface and force satisfy Electronic Scoring (PSS).

Good counter?

Yes against over-committed turning kicks—see Counter Kick.

Learn hook first?

A standing Hook Kick (Gwidolligi) teaches the finish; the spin is a separate literacy.

The Bottom Line

Name the reverse turning kick clearly—then decide whether your gym means Spinning Hook Kick theater or an ITF scoring tool.
