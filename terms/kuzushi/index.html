Kuzushi

Kuzushi is judo’s off-balancing principle—moving uke’s weight onto an unstable edge so tsukuri and kake can finish a throw with efficiency instead of muscle.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Kuzushi precedes every real throw.
  • Directions beat arm-wrestling.
  • Reaction kuzushi catches steps.
  • Gi levers amplify; no-gi reframes handles.
  • Train it in Uchi-Komi.
  • Pairs with Ukemi for honest reps.

What is kuzushi?

Kuzushi (崩し) is off-balancing—the deliberate destruction of uke’s stable posture so a throw can fit.

Jigoro Kano placed it at the center of efficient judo: maximum effect with minimum wasted strength, which is why Martial Index pairs this concept page with the archive plate of Kano himself.

Searchers hit this term after coaches yell “pull first!” and after Uchi-Komi feels like a body check instead of a throw.

Link it early to Nage-Waza: every live throw page on this site assumes kuzushi is present.

How it works

Eight classic directions (happo no kuzushi) describe forward, back, sides, and diagonals—uke’s weight moves onto toes, heels, or an edge.

Hands, hips, and footwork share the job. A sleeve pull without hip displacement is only a suggestion.

Tsukuri follows: you fill the space kuzushi created. Kake finishes. Skip kuzushi and tsukuri becomes a collision.

In competition, kuzushi is often a reaction to uke’s step—see Deashi Harai and Kouchi Gari—not a slow theatrical lean.

Judogi vs no-gi

Gi grips amplify directional pulls. No-gi uses frames, head position, and elbow control to create the same weight shifts.

If you cross-train BJJ, translate “break posture” as kuzushi by another name—but keep judo’s standing directions sharp.

In practice

Tori wants Osoto Gari (Judo): first action is to float uke onto the near heel, not to swing the reaping leg at a flat foot.

For Seoi Nage (Judo), kuzushi is forward and up onto the toes so the back can load. Without that, seoi becomes a low back injury demo.

Types / variations

Action kuzushi (you pull/push), reaction kuzushi (you catch their step), and combination kuzushi (feint Ouchi Gari to load Uchi Mata (Judo)).

Training tools: Uchi-Komi with honest off-balancing; slow Randori rounds where only throws with clear kuzushi count.

Entries, counters, combinations

Entries: move uke, then enter—never the reverse.

Counters: when tori pulls themselves off-balance, uke may reverse direction into a throw of their own.

Combine with Ukemi: uke who trusts falling lets tori practice real kuzushi instead of babying every rep.

Compare with ukemi

Kuzushi is tori’s problem; Ukemi is uke’s safety skill. Both make hard Randori possible.

IJF notes & safety

Kuzushi itself is not scored—landings are—but without it you rarely earn Ippon or Waza-Ari.

Safety: do not crank necks to fake off-balance; use legs and hips.

Common mistakes

  • Arm-only pulls.
  • Entering before weight shifts.
  • Telegraphing a huge lean uke can step out of.
  • Confusing strength with balance-breaking.

Kano’s efficiency slogan is not poetry for the wall—it is a coaching diagnostic. If you are straining and uke is not floating, you are arm-wrestling a stable skeleton. Change their base first.

Try this feel test in uchi-komi: enter o-goshi only when uke’s belt line tips toward you. If the belt line stays flat, abort and rebuild the pull. That habit alone upgrades seoi and uchi-mata entries later.

Competition kuzushi is often ugly on film: a snap, a head position change, a stolen sleeve, a step that never looks like kata. Study both kata clarity and shiai mess. Your job is to recognise the weight shift, not to copy the aesthetic.

Defensively, posture and hip distance manage incoming kuzushi. Hands matter, but hips that stay under the shoulders survive longer. When you counter, you are often returning kuzushi along the line tori just opened.

Link reading: after this page, drill directions inside deashi-harai and osoto gari, then stress-test in randori.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is kuzushi a technique?

It’s a principle applied inside techniques.

Eight directions?

Classic happo model—use it as a map, not dogma.

Why do coaches say pull first?

Because entry without weight shift fails.

No-gi kuzushi?

Yes—head, elbows, hips.

Link to ippon?

Better kuzushi → better landings → more Ippon/Waza-Ari.

Solo drill?

Shadow entries help; partners are better.

Feints?

Fake Ouchi Gari to create kuzushi for Uchi Mata (Judo).

Related page?

Nage-Waza.

The Bottom Line

Without Kuzushi, Nage-Waza is weight training. Build it in Uchi-Komi, then test in Randori.
