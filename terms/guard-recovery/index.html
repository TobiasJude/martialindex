Guard Recovery

Guard Recovery (recover guard / recompose guard) is the skill of returning to a functional guard after a pass is partially or fully lost—using frames, hip escapes, inverted movements, and connection. It connects to Guard, Guard Retention in the Martial Index cluster map.

What Is Guard Recovery?

Guard Recovery is the skill of returning to a functional guard after a pass is partially or fully lost—using frames, hip escapes, inverted movements, and connection. In gym language you may also hear recover guard / recompose guard. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

Build frames, shrimp or invert to create space, insert hooks or knee shields, and recompose closed, half, or open guard before the passer settles a pin. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Sleeve and collar frames help; no-gi uses bone frames and underhooks.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, guard recovery usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard guard recovery as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (Guard, Guard Retention). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

Downstream of Guard Retention—retention prevents the pass; recovery fixes a lost one.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check Ibjjf Rules and Adcc Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck and spinal pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck and shoulders. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing guard recovery with a nearby sibling name.
