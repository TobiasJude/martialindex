Pass to Mount

Pass to mount is the climb from a cleared guard pass into mount—often via side control or directly from a knee cut. It obeys control before submit and the positional hierarchy.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Stabilize the pass before you climb.
  • Kill the near elbow-knee connection.
  • Use underhooks / crossface to flat them.
  • Don’t throw the leg into waiting half guard.
  • Mount maintenance starts on the transition.

What Is Pass to Mount?

A transition concept: the pass is not finished until you can advance or hold a pin safely.

Common paths: side control → mount, knee cut → mount, headquarters → mount.

Sits between pass to side and maintain mount on the top ladder.

How Pass to Mount Works

Clear the legs and win chest-to-chest or strong headquarters.

Address near-side frames; trap or float the elbow that blocks your knee.

Step or slide to mount with hips heavy; keep head control.

Settle low mount or high mount with a plan—see maintain mount.

If they turn, convert to technical mount or take the back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Crossface collars and underhook lapels freeze the turn.

No-gi. Shoulder pressure, underhooks, and wrist control replace cloth.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

After a toreando to side control, Pat isolates the far elbow and steps to mount.

Jamie’s knee cut lands; they underhook both sides and slide to mount instead of celebrating early.

Types / Variations

Via side control

Classic stabilize-then-climb.

Direct from knee cut

Slide through when frames die.

Via knee-on-belly

Force movement, then drop to mount.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Follows successful passes. Counters: elbow-knee insert, bridge-and-roll threats, half-guard catches. Drill with maintain mount immediately after arrival.

Pass to Mount vs Pass to Side

Factor Pass to mount Pass to side
Hierarchy Higher pin / more points (IBJJF-style) Stable hub, fewer angles at first
Risk Half-guard catch on the climb Reguard if you never settle
Offense Both arms + neck more available One arm hidden; control focus
When Frames dead, hips free Need to stabilize first

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF-style scoring typically awards pass then additional points for mount—verify current IBJJF / ADCC packets; control time matters.

Safety

Don’t knee into the neck on the climb; control the head as you step over.

Common mistakes

  • Climbing before the pass is stable.
  • Leaving space for elbow-knee.
  • Sitting high with no base.
  • Ignoring the back-take when they turn away.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is pass to mount?

Advancing from a cleared pass into the mount pin.

Must I go through side?

No—but side control is the safest hub for many athletes.

Biggest failure?

Getting caught in half guard mid-climb.

Points?

Often pass + mount under IBJJF-style rules—verify current book.

If they turn?

Technical mount or take the back.

Related principle?

Control before submit.

Next skill?

Maintain mount.

The Bottom Line

Pass to mount is the climb—clear, stabilize, deny elbow-knee, then own the mount without donating half guard.
