Americana from Side Control

The americana from side control is the americana attacked from side control: they frame near your neck, you pin that wrist to the mat, thread a figure-four, and paintbrush the shoulder—or switch to a kimura from side control if the arm rotates the other way.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Harder as a pure tap than the mount version—they can roll away from the rotation.
  • Use it as a chain with the kimura, not a single-finish religion.
  • Keep hips heavy; chasing the arm without a pin loses side control.
  • Parent: americana. Mount cousin: americana from mount.
  • Same figure-four family as the kimura—learn both directions.

What Is Americana from Side Control?

From side control the americana shows up when their near arm frames your neck or chest and you can glue that wrist to the mat beside their head. The lock is real, but the percentage is lower than mount because they can turn their back toward the floor and unload the shoulder.

That is why modern teaching treats the side-control americana as a switch inside the ude-garami family: if the hand travels behind the back, finish the kimura; if it stays by the head at 90°, finish the americana.

Martial Index nests this page under side control and the parent americana so “americana from side” searchers do not have to bounce through mount-only copy.

How Americana from Side Control Works

Establish chest-to-chest side control. When they frame, drop your head or top hand onto the wrist and pin it. Swim the bottom hand under the tricep and catch your wrist.

Keep their elbow off the ribs and their palm toward the ceiling/mat in that 90° L. Slide the hand toward the hip / along the mat and lift the elbow—same paintbrush as mount, with more body weight through your chest.

If they turn away, do not chase a dead americana. Walk to the kimura angle or reset the pin. If they turn in, your head-side elbow should already be blocking the ear line.

Never give up the cross-face entirely to hunt the arm; a shrimp now puts you in half guard with nothing.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar cross-faces freeze the frame so you can thread without losing the pin.

No-gi. Head position and underhook/cross-face races matter more. Expect to switch to kimura more often.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Sam pins a neck frame in side control, threads, and taps a newer partner with a clean paintbrush.

Riley feels them roll off the americana and immediately walks the same figure-four into a north-south kimura.

Types / Variations

Direct paintbrush from the frame

They hand you the wrist; you pin and finish.

Kimura ↔ americana switch

One figure-four family, two finish directions.

Scarf / modified scarf americana

Same lock from scarf hold when the near arm is trapped.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: neck frames, failed underhooks, or they reach across during an escape. Counters: elbow to ribs, turn away early, clasp hands. Escapes: shrimp before the pin, or roll toward the lock only while space remains.

Americana vs Kimura from Side Control

Factor Americana from side Kimura from side
Arm location Pinned near the head, 90° Away from the hip / posted
Rotation Paintbrush toward hip/head line Behind the back
Reliability as tap Lower than mount Higher if you walk to north-south
Best use Switch / punish a frame System grip + tap

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult americanas are standard legal shoulder locks. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Same shoulder-lock rules: slow pressure, instant release. Side-control americanas can also crank necks if you drive the head—keep the paintbrush on the arm.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving side control to chase a floating arm.
  • Expecting mount-level taps without extra pinning.
  • Forgetting the kimura switch when they rotate.
  • Lifting your hips and inviting the shrimp.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why do my side-control americanas fail?

They can roll off the rotation. Pin heavier or switch to a kimura.

Is this a white-belt move?

Yes as a pin-and-paintbrush; the switch game is intermediate.

Same grip as kimura?

Same figure-four idea, opposite finish direction.

Mount or side first?

Learn mount americana first—the flat back teaches the lock. Then add side control.

Gi help?

Sleeves help the pin; the lock itself is grip-optional.

Can I finish from scarf hold?

Often yes—the near arm is already trapped. See scarf hold.

Legal?

Standard adult shoulder lock—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

Americana from side control is a pin-and-switch lock: paintbrush when the arm is by the head, kimura when it travels behind the back. Keep side control while you hunt.
