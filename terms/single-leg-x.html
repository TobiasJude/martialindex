Single-Leg X (SLX)

Single-leg X (SLX) is an open-guard entanglement that clamps one of the opponent's legs—used to put their hands on the mat, sweep, take the back, or enter further leg positions, with very different legal endings in IBJJF versus ADCC-style rules.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • SLX clamps one leg as a modern open-guard hub.
  • Force hands to the mat, then sweep or transition.
  • Huge in no-gi; still useful for gi sweeps.
  • Connects to X-guard, 50/50, and ashi families.
  • Finish legally: know heel-hook rules for your event.

What Is Single-Leg X (SLX)?

Single-leg X (SLX), also called ashigarami-adjacent single-leg control in some lineages, is an open guard that entangles one of the opponent's legs with your legs in an X-like configuration while you control that leg's ankle/hip—used to sweep, stand them up into bad balance, or enter further entanglements. It is central to modern no-gi.

How SLX Works

You isolate one leg, clamp it with your legs, and use off-balancing (kicking their free foot, pulling the ankle, extending hips) to put hands on the mat. From there: sweeps, back takes, or transitions to X-guard, 50/50, or outside ashi depending on rules and preference. Lachlan Giles's SLX material is a common modern reference for linking attacks.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi. SLX is a star—entries from RDLR, seated guard, and butterfly are constant, and heel hooks often follow in ADCC-style rules.

Gi. Still useful for sweeps with pant grips; heel-hook endings are usually illegal in IBJJF, so emphasize sweeps and back takes.

What Does SLX Look Like in Practice?

From RDLR, Alex forces Jordan's hands to the mat, enters SLX on the lead leg, off-balances the free foot, and sweeps to top—or in no-gi, transitions toward a heel-hook position if the rules allow.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. RDLR, seated butterfly, collapsed X, standing single-leg counters.

Top counters. Extract the knee, hop to clear hips, smash into half, or reap-aware defenses depending on rules.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

SLX itself is legal. What you finish with is rule-set dependent: IBJJF restricts most twisting leg locks by belt; ADCC allows heel hooks in adult divisions per event rules. Know your bracket.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Heel hooks injure quickly—tap early, apply slowly, and coach-supervise leg lock rounds.

Common mistakes

  • Entering SLX without controlling the ankle.
  • Letting them extract the knee and pass.
  • Hunting heel hooks in IBJJF gi where illegal.
  • Ignoring sweep options that are always legal.
  • Soft hips that never put their hands on the mat.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is SLX the same as X-guard?

No. X-guard uses a full X under the hips; SLX focuses on one entangled leg. They connect constantly.

Can I use SLX in IBJJF?

Yes for control and sweeps. Most twisting foot locks remain restricted by belt—check the current rule book before hunting heel hooks.

How do I enter SLX?

Common paths: reverse De La Riva, seated butterfly, and collapsed X-guard. Force posture down, then clamp the leg.

Why can't I sweep from SLX?

Their free foot is alive and their hands are not on the mat. Kick the free foot and extend to break their base first.

SLX vs 50/50?

SLX is more unilateral; 50/50 mirrors both players' legs. Many chains move SLX into 50/50.

Is SLX only for leg locks?

No. Sweeps and back takes are always available and should be your IBJJF default.

The Bottom Line

SLX clamps one leg to break base, sweep, or enter further entanglements—modern no-gi's hub. Compare with X-guard and 50/50; always finish inside your rule set.
