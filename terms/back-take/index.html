Back Take

A back take is any sequence that exposes the opponent’s spine and lets you secure back control—typically via seatbelt plus hooks or a body triangle—from turtle, scrambles, berimbolo, or failed passes.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Back take = process; back control = result.
  • Upper body (seatbelt) and lower body (hooks/triangle) must both arrive.
  • Common doors: turtle rides, arm drags, berimbolo/kiss, failed guard passes.
  • Clear the near elbow / prevent the turn-in during the climb.
  • Finish culture: RNC first, then armbars when the neck is defended.

What Is Back Take?

In sport BJJ, athletes chase the back take because rear control offers high finishing rates and strong scoring. Conceptually it is the transition problem: how do you go from front/side/turtle relationships to chest-on-back with harness and hooks?

Martial Index treats back take as the concept hub linking berimbolo, kiss of the dragon, turtle, and the back-control detail pages (seatbelt, hooks, body triangle).

How Back Take Works

Create a turn-away or turtle: drag an arm, spin behind a sprawl, or invert from open guard. Immediately clasp a seatbelt before hunting hooks so they cannot dig underhooks and turn in.

Insert hooks (often far then near on turtle) or lock a body triangle. Climb toward the shoulders, hand fight, finish RNC—or isolate an arm for armbar from back.

If the take fails mid-way, do not release everything—keep chest connection and re-drag rather than resetting to neutral.

Measure success by residual control: after the spin or drag, can you still glue chest-to-back while installing hooks? If not, you attempted a highlight, not a back take.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel drags, bow-and-arrow setups, and cloth seatbelts enrich the take.

No-gi. Arm drags, body locks, and wrestling rides dominate.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Tony Casarez-style fundamentals show getting the back as a chain of controls, not a single jump.

A berimbolo ends with a clean back take only when seatbelt and hooks land together.

Types / Variations

Turtle back take

Ride, seatbelt, hooks.

Scramble / roll back take

Arm drag, matrix, granby-adjacent paths.

Guard-initiated back take

Berimbolo, kiss of the dragon, crab rides.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries listed above. Counters: keep elbows in, face the threat, dig underhooks early. Escapes: peel seatbelt, clear hooks, turn into the gap—see back-escape literacy.

Back Take vs Established Back Control

Factor Back take (transition) Back control (result)
Job Expose spine + attach Ride, score, finish
Key risk Incomplete hooks/seatbelt Hand-fight stalls
Metrics Did chest-to-back stick? Hooks/triangle + harness live
Training focus Entries & timing Retention & RNC

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Scoring for back control typically requires defined hook/position criteria—verify. The take itself is legal when free of illegal neck pressure. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Do not crank necks while spinning behind. Control the landing.

Common mistakes

  • Jumping on the back without a seatbelt.
  • Inserting hooks with chest off.
  • Abandoning a 90% take to chase a submission too early.
  • No plan after berimbolo lands.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is back take a position?

It is the transition into back control—the concept of exposing and securing the back.

Most important detail?

Seatbelt before or with hooks—upper body glue.

Best entries for beginners?

Turtle rides and simple arm drags before fancy inversions.

Gi vs no-gi?

Both; grips change, goal does not.

After the take?

RNC, then arm attacks—see back-control cluster.

Relation to berimbolo?

Berimbolo is one famous back-take engine.

Points?

Usually awarded for established back control—verify current definitions.

The Bottom Line

A back take is how you earn the spine: attach seatbelt, install hooks or a body triangle, then finish. Study entries from turtle and open guard, then live in the back-control detail pages.
