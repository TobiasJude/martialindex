Bow and Arrow Choke

The bow and arrow choke is a high-percentage gi finish from back control: one deep collar, a pant (or similar) grip, and legs that push while arms pull—gi counterpart energy to the rear naked choke.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Elbow of the choking arm behind their head; remove slack before you pull.
  • Push with the legs as you pull the collar—dual motion.
  • Gi-only. Primary back submission in IBJJF gi alongside collar variations.
  • If the choke stalls, the armbar is often right there.
  • Defend by hand-fighting the collar and escaping hooks early.

What Is Bow and Arrow Choke?

Named for the archer’s shape—collar pull on one end, pant control on the other, body as the bow—the bow and arrow is the standard gi back choke taught worldwide. It converts seatbelt back control into a lapel strangle without needing the RNC grip.

Pillar home is back control; it cross-links to collar-choke siblings and contrasts directly with rear naked choke for no-gi.

How Bow and Arrow Choke Works

From back control, feed a deep collar on the choking side, get the choking elbow behind their head, grip the far pant (or trap an arm), fall to the side, and extend: pull collar, push with legs, drive their neck into the wrist.

Detail. Slack kills the choke—elbow position removes it. Prevent them from rotating belly-down into you without control.

Bottom. Two-on-one the collar arm, clear hooks, and escape before the pant grip sets.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi — required for the classic lapel + pant version.

No-Gi. Use rear naked choke, seatbelt armbars, and body-triangle pressure instead.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Hooks in, seatbelt on: feed collar, cup pant, fall to the hip, extend like drawing a bow. Tap often comes mid-extension.

From incomplete back or turtle transitions, some athletes hit bow-and-arrow as hooks enter.

Types / Variations

Standard bow and arrow

Collar + pant + leg push from back.

Underside vs overside

Fall direction variants that change armbar availability.

Arm-trap bow

Trap an arm instead of/in addition to pant for crucifix-like control.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: settled back control, turtle to back, failed RNC attempts that leave collars free (gi), chair sit from technical mount.

Counters: peel collar, hide chin, escape hooks, turn into the choking arm carefully, or stand if rules/space allow.

Chain to armbar when they defend the collar by extending the near arm.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal and extremely common in IBJJF gi. In no-gi events use RNC instead. Pant grips must follow uniform rules.

Safety

Control the fall in drilling; neck lateral stress can be intense. Tap early.

Common mistakes

  • Choking elbow in front of the head (slack remains).
  • Pulling without leg push.
  • Falling without controlling rotation.
  • Abandoning to nothing when the armbar is available.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the bow and arrow choke?

A gi back choke using a deep collar and pant grip with leg drive to strangle.

Is it gi-only?

The classic form yes. No-gi uses RNC.

Why is elbow position important?

The choking elbow behind the head removes lapel slack so the wrist can cut the neck.

What if they defend the collar?

Often the armbar appears—switch without losing hip connection.

How do I defend it?

Hand-fight the collar early and escape hooks—see back escape.

Bow and arrow vs cross collar?

Cross collar is usually mount/guard dual collars; bow and arrow is back control collar + pant.

Can beginners learn it?

Yes—after basic back control and collar feeds are safe.

The Bottom Line

Bow and arrow is gi back control finished: deep collar, elbow behind the head, pant grip, push-pull extension. When the kimono is off, hunt the rear naked choke instead.
