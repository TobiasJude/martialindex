Darce Choke

The darce (D’Arce) choke is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu front-headlock submission that threads your arm under the opponent’s near armpit and across the neck, locking to your other arm to strangle—closely related to the anaconda, with huge no-gi presence and full gi viability.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Darce threads under the near armpit and across the neck—opposite “first move” feel from the anaconda.
  • Front-headlock family staple in no-gi; works in gi too.
  • Often appears when guillotines fail or when they turn away from side pressure.
  • Compare carefully with anaconda.
  • Legal adult IBJJF blood choke; tap early.

What Is the Darce?

The darce (popularized spelling of D’Arce) is an arm-through choke from the front headlock / head-and-arm family. Your arm travels under their near armpit, across the neck, and locks to your other biceps/arm to compress the carotids.

How Darce Works

Clear space under the armpit, thread deep, connect the lock, cut an angle (often falling to the near side), and squeeze. Depth beats arm strength.

Gi vs No-Gi

No-gi is the cultural home, but gi darces work—fabric can even help you pin posture. Expect more collar distractions in gi; expect faster scrambles in no-gi.

>What Does the Darce Look Like in Practice?

From a sprawled front headlock, Alex threads under Jordan’s armpit, locks the darce, and falls to the shoulder to finish as Jordan tries to tuck the chin.

Types / Variations

Arm-through mechanics

Depth, lock type (gable/S-grip/biceps), and shoulder pressure.

From side control / turtle

When they turn in or turtle, the near armpit opens.

Chains

Guillotine ↔ darce ↔ anaconda is a modern chase-the-neck loop.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter on sprawls, side-control turns, and turtle. Escape by clearing the armpit early, posting, and improving posture—tap when locked.

Darce vs Anaconda

FactorDarceAnaconda
Thread pathUnder near armpit firstAround the head/neck differently—often far-arm feed
Common rollFall to shoulderRolling finish iconic
SharedFront headlock familyFront headlock family

Exact instructional cues differ by coach; the practical point is to recognize both sides of the head-and-arm chase. See anaconda.

Shoulder pressure. After the arm is through, many finishes improve when you drive the near shoulder into their neck/jaw line while you cut the angle—pure arm squeeze without body weight stalls against strong necks.

Turtle entries. When a passer’s head sits beside a turtled opponent, the near armpit often opens for a darce thread. Clear the near elbow and commit depth before you fall to the finish.

Spelling and attribution. Sport usage settled on “darce”; historical D’Arce naming appears in older articles. Martial Index standardizes on darce for URLs and titles while acknowledging both.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal adult IBJJF/ADCC.

Safety

Tap to chokes. Release on tap immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Threading shallow.
  • No angle after the lock.
  • Forcing darce when anaconda/guillotine is the open door.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How do you spell it—Darce or D’Arce?

Both appear; Martial Index uses Darce as the common sport spelling, noting D’Arce historically.

Darce vs anaconda in one sentence?

Different arm-through paths on the same front-headlock tree—learn both.

Works in gi?

Yes.

Best entry for beginners?

From a clear sprawl front headlock with a coach watching depth.

They defend by tucking—what next?

Improve depth/angle or switch to guillotine/anaconda.

Is it a blood choke?

Yes when applied correctly.

Related to arm triangle?

Same broad head-and-arm idea; different body orientation. See arm triangle.

The Bottom Line

The darce threads under the near armpit and across the neck for a front-headlock strangle. Pair it with anaconda and guillotine so every defensive turn still meets a choke.
