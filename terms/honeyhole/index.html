Honey Hole

The honey hole is a dominant inside leg entanglement—widely treated as synonymous with saddle / inside sankaku and 411—used to isolate a leg and finish inside heel hooks (and related locks) when the ruleset allows.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Honey hole ≈ saddle ≈ 411 in most gyms—names differ more than mechanics.
  • This page emphasizes finishing habitat and 10th Planet–era naming culture.
  • Control first (hip + knee), then heel exposure.
  • Cross-read 411 for synonymy/IBJJF angles without duplicating every paragraph.
  • Verify heel-hook and reaping rules before competition.

What Is Honey Hole?

Popularized in English-speaking no-gi via 10th Planet and internet culture, honey hole labels the sticky inside entanglement where one leg is triangulated and the hip is pinned—the same family Danaher-line coaches often call the saddle. Martial Index keeps the slug because athletes search the name they heard in the locker room.

Rather than copy-paste the saddle article, treat this entry as the “finish culture” twin: how players talk about entries from SLX, the satisfaction of locking the hole, and why the position ends matches under ADCC-style rules.

How Honey Hole Works

Arrive via ashi transfers or direct entries, lock the inside triangle on their trapped leg, cross your inside hook past the far hip, and glue their knee. Optional: gather the second leg (double trouble) before the finish.

Expose the heel, connect bones, rotate through the knee. If they hide the heel, attack secondary options (kneebar angles, toe holds where legal) or improve control rather than ripping blindly.

For naming clarity: if your coach says saddle or 411, you are usually in the same neighborhood—confirm details on the mat.

Finish culture also means knowing when not to rip: if the heel is buried and the secondary leg is free, improve double-trouble control before you rotate.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Often not an A-game under IBJJF heel-hook bans—use as conceptual crossover.

No-gi. Celebrated finishing seat for inside heel hooks.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Galvão-style SLX entries into honey hole show the position as a destination, not a random scramble.

A 10P class may shout “honey hole!” for the same lock another room calls saddle.

Types / Variations

Standard honey hole

Inside triangle + cross hip hook.

Double-trouble honey hole

Both legs controlled—max finish rate.

Entry-focused honey hole

Emphasis on SLX/K-guard pathways into the lock.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

See also saddle entries. Counters/escapes mirror inside sankaku defense: hide heel, clear cross hook, counter-entangle to 50/50, or stand carefully with posture.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Pair with 411, saddle, reaping.

Safety

Same knee risks as saddle heel hooks—tap early, drill slow.

Common mistakes

  • Treating honeyhole as unrelated to saddle.
  • Finishing without hip control.
  • Posting illegal reaping angles in IBJJF.
  • Copying online finishes without partner context.

The Bottom Line

Honey hole is the locker-room name for the sticky inside entanglement. Use it to finish inside heel hooks when legal—and cross-link 411 and saddle so naming never splits your study.
