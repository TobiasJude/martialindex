Back Step Pass

The back step pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu half-guard pass where the top player steps the free leg backward over the bottom player—clearing to side control—while managing underhooks so they do not take the back.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Back-step the free leg over—while winning the underhook/head fight.
  • High-percentage vs half; dangerous if they own the underhook.
  • Gi pants help freeze the trapped leg; no-gi needs tighter upper-body control.
  • Blind back-steps gift quarter guard back takes.
  • Related: half guard, knee cut.

What Is Back Step Pass?

The back step pass is a top half-guard pass: instead of cutting the knee forward, you step your free leg backward over their torso toward the far side, clearing the trapped leg and landing in side control or north-south angles. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

How Back Step Pass Works

Control their head and near underhook (or deny theirs), free or pin the trapped knee as needed, then back-step the free leg over while keeping weight on their torso. Stabilize side control before they recover hooks or take your back.

Bottom player's job. Win the underhook, attack the back as they step, or catch quarter guard on the trailing ankle.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips freeze the trapped leg; collar crossface helps.

No-gi. Underhook and head position are everything—expect more back-take races.

MMA context. Back-stepping can expose you to upkicks if posture rises—stay connected.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex flattens Jordan in half, wins the crossface, back-steps the free leg over, and settles into side control.

Gi. Pants grip stops Jordan from recovering the trapped knee.

No-gi. Alex prioritizes the underhook battle before the step.

Types / Variations

Classic half-guard back step

From topside half to side control via the rear step.

Submission threats during the step

Kimuras, armbars, and back exposures if they turn the wrong way.

When to back-step vs knee-cut

Back-step when the knee cut is stuffed but you still own upper-body control.

Safe back-step checklist

Underhook won, head controlled, trapped leg managed, then step.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Top half after a stuff; when knee cut dies; from smash half.

Counters. Underhook and back take, quarter-guard ankle trap, bridging into the step.

Failure mode. Stepping while they own the underhook.

Back Step vs Knee Cut

Factor Back Step Knee Cut
Direction Free leg steps back/over Lead knee cuts forward
Common start Top half guard Headquarters / half
Back-take risk Higher if underhook lost Different—trailing ankle/quarter
Feel Rotate over Slice through

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Pass scores when stabilized past the legs.

Safety

Do not torque their trapped knee while stepping. Control the fall onto side control.

Common mistakes

  • Back-stepping into their underhook.
  • Ignoring the trailing ankle (quarter guard).
  • Rising posture mid-step.
  • No crossface—getting bridged.
  • In no-gi: soft head control.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the back step pass?

A half-guard pass where you step the free leg backward over the bottom player to clear to side control.

When should I back-step instead of knee-cut?

When the knee cut is stuffed but you still control the upper body and can safely rotate over.

Why do I get my back taken on back steps?

They won the underhook. Do not step until you clear or neutralize that underhook.

Does back step work in no-gi?

Yes, but underhook and head fights are sharper without pants grips.

How does quarter guard relate?

If your trailing ankle is trapped during/after the step, they may have quarter guard and a back-take path.

Common submissions during back step?

Kimuras and armbars if they post poorly; otherwise prioritize the pass.

Is back step a pressure pass?

It often pairs with pressure half-guard top control, though the step itself is a rotational clear.

The Bottom Line

The back step pass clears half guard by stepping the free leg over—only after winning the underhook fight. See half guard, knee cut, and quarter guard.
