Inside Trip (Clinch)

The inside trip (clinch) is the MMA/over-under version of an ouchi-gari style reap: step inside, hook the near leg, and drive so they fall over their own base.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Deep underhooks make inside trips high-percentage.
  • Step deep between the legs.
  • Drive chest-to-chest as you reap.
  • Chain outside when they float the hook.
  • Watch knees and guillotines in the pocket.

How Inside Trip (Clinch) Works

Pairs with Pummel battles—without inside position the reap is easy to float.

Win underhook(s) → close hips → deep inside step past midline → reap near heel → drive with chest so they cannot hop.

Mechanically, successful inside trip (clinch) work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Same as ouchi gari?

Mechanically related; MMA accents underhooks, gloves, and cage finishes.

Inside vs outside?

Inside with deep underhooks and hips close; outside with an angle on the far leg.

Common landing?

Side control or half guard—settle before wild punches.

Safe for beginners?

Yes with cooperative reps; add strikes only under a coach.

The Bottom Line

Inside trips reward deep steps and honest underhooks—if the reap floats, keep the grip and chain outside.
