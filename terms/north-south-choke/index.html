North-South Choke

The north-south choke finishes from north-south by clamping the neck with the armpit/lat while controlling the head—famous in Marcelo Garcia’s game and viable in gi and no-gi (unlike most collar tools).

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Head facing away, arms cleared, lat/armpit down, hips heavy, drive with toes.
  • Works without lapels—flagship no-gi pin choke next to paper cutter (gi).
  • Pillars: north-south / side control. Enter from side-control transitions.
  • If an arm stays in, the finish dies—clear both arms.
  • Contrast with rear naked choke (back) and paper cutter (gi blade).

What Is North-South Choke?

The north-south choke is a smothering blood choke from the north-south pin: wrap the neck with your near armpit/lat, control the far shoulder, clear their arms, and drive weight through the clamp. Marcelo Garcia made it a high-percentage modern staple.

It is listed under the side-control cluster in Martial Index (north-south-choke member) and bridges to paper cutter when lapels are available.

How North-South Choke Works

From side control, elevate and switch to north-south while feeding the choking armpit onto the neck. Turn their face away from your body, clear both of their arms, grip near the far shoulder/back, drop hips, get off the knees, and drive forward with the toes while the head stays heavy.

Key. Chin toward you kills the choke—reorient the head. An arm inside the clamp also kills it.

Bottom. Frame, avoid giving the neck, and escape side control before NS settles—or hand-fight the shoulder grip and turn in carefully.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Still excellent; some athletes add collar assists, but the lat clamp does not require them. Paper cutter remains a gi alternative from the same pin.

No-Gi. Primary pin choke from NS—no lapel needed. This is a major reason to learn NS control in no-gi.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Marcelo-style: crossface to elevate the head, switch hands, drop the lat on the neck, clear arms, clamp and drive. Finish often without a dramatic scramble.

Types / Variations

Classic lat NS choke

Armpit/lat on neck, shoulder grip, toe drive.

Arm-in variations

Specialist entries when an arm is trapped—harder finishes.

Side-to-NS entry

Most common path from settled side control.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: side control switches, failed mount attempts into NS, scarf-to-NS transitions, after stuffing underhook escapes.

Counters: frames before the switch, turn into them carefully, peel the shoulder grip, shrimp and reclaim a knee, or turtle with neck protection as last resort.

If the choke fails, keep NS control and switch to paper cutter (gi) or arm attacks.

North-South Choke vs Paper Cutter

Factor North-South Choke Paper Cutter Choke
Main tool Lat / armpit clamp Forearm blade + collar
No-gi? Yes Classic no
Head rule Face away from you Face controlled for blade
Same pin? North-south Side / north-south

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi and no-gi when applied as a choke (not a pure neck crank). ADCC-style rules likewise allow it as a standard neck submission.

Safety

Distinguish choke pressure from crank. Ease off if the neck twists without carotid pressure. Tap early.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving their arm inside the clamp.
  • Allowing the face to turn into your body.
  • Staying on the knees so hips never get heavy.
  • Abandoning NS entirely when a small adjustment would finish.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the north-south choke?

A choke from north-south that clamps the neck with the armpit/lat while controlling the head and clearing arms.

Does it work in no-gi?

Yes—that is one of its main advantages over collar pin chokes.

Who is it associated with?

Marcelo Garcia’s instruction and competition use made it famous in modern BJJ.

NS choke vs paper cutter?

NS choke is lat-based and no-gi friendly; paper cutter is a gi forearm blade. See paper cutter.

Why does my NS choke fail?

Usually an arm inside, face turned wrong, or hips too light on the knees.

How do I enter?

From side control, elevate and switch to NS while feeding the armpit to the neck.

Defense?

Escape side control early—see side-control escape.

The Bottom Line

North-south choke is lat, head orientation, clear arms, heavy hips. It is the pin choke that travels to no-gi—keep paper cutter for gi blades from the same family of pins.
