What Is Momtong Makki (Middle Block)?

Momtong makki (몸통막기) covers middle-section attacks with the forearm.

Variants include inward and outward middle blocks depending on curriculum naming.

Central to Poomsae and belt tests.

Compare with Olgul Makki (High Block): high block rises; middle stays on the trunk line.

Martial Index files Momtong Makki (Middle Block) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Momtong Makki (Middle Block) Works

Chamber, then travel the forearm to a finishing position outside or across the midsection with elbow angle intact.

Reaction hand pulls; stance settles.

Do not let the elbow wing into an unstable T-shape unless the variation demands it.

Power comes from body rotation and drop into stance, not arm muscle alone.

Mechanically, successful momtong makki (middle block) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, momtong makki (middle block) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill momtong makki (middle block) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Line drill: middle blocks alternating sides in Ap Kubi (Forward Stance).

Form segment: repeated middle blocks that judges watch for height consistency.

Fight picture: momtong makki (middle block) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply momtong makki (middle block) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Finishing too wide or too tight.
  • Elbow collapse.
  • Rising onto toes during the block.
  • Uneven heights left/right.
  • Rushing past the settle.
  • Treating momtong makki (middle block) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick momtong makki (middle block) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so momtong makki (middle block) connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether momtong makki (middle block) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, axe kicks, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe momtong makki (middle block) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put momtong makki (middle block) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Momtong vs body block English?

Same idea—midsection forearm block.

Inward or outward first?

School order varies; learn both eventually.

Link to kicks?

Blocks teach chambers useful for overall coordination with kicks like Ap Chagi (Front Kick).

Sparring transfer?

Indirect—parries and frames appear more than formal makki shapes.
