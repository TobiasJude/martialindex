Key Takeaways

  • Chokes = Submissions that threaten unconsciousness by restricting blood flow (or rarely airflow) at the neck—gi collar strangles and no-gi arm/leg-assisted strangulations.
  • Mechanics: Isolate the neck from a dominant position or guard, clear the chin line, and apply even bilateral pressure until the opponent taps.

What Are Chokes?

Chokes (strangles) in Brazilian jiu-jitsu are submissions that threaten unconsciousness by restricting blood flow through the carotids or, less commonly, airflow through the trachea. On Martial Index this page is the submission-family hub for neck attacks—gi collar strangles and no-gi arm/leg-assisted strangulations—sitting under Submission alongside joint locks.

How Chokes Work

High-percentage chokes start from control: isolate the neck, remove frames under the chin, and apply even pressure. Classic map: rear naked choke from back control; triangle from closed guard; guillotine and front headlock fronts; arm triangle from side control or mount; gi tools like cross-collar, bow and arrow, Ezekiel, brabo, and paper cutter; plus north-south finishes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi adds lapel feeds and collar depth—fuel for bow-and-arrow, cross-collar, and Ezekiel variants. No-gi replaces fabric with biceps, forearm blades, and figure-four structures (RNC, darce/anaconda family, guillotines). Blood chokes remain the training priority in both uniforms.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

You take the back, fight the bottom hand, seat the RNC blade under the chin, and squeeze with body expansion—or from closed guard you lock a triangle, control posture, and finish as they post. From side control a brabo or arm-triangle threatens when they turn in.

Types / Variations

Back & mount strangulations

RNC, mounted triangles, Ezekiel from mount, and back-collar attacks like bow and arrow.

Guard & front-headlock chokes

Triangle, guillotine, front headlock entries, and crucifix choke when an arm is trapped.

Side-control & gi specialty

Arm triangle, brabo, paper cutter, north-south, cross collar.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries are positional. Counters: chin tuck, hand-fight early, turn to a safer pin, or explode before the second hand seals. Late escapes after a fully locked blood choke are low-percentage—tap when vision tunnels.

How It Compares

Chokes end the match via the neck; joint locks threaten structural damage to limbs. Neck cranks (can-opener-adjacent spinal torque) are a different, higher-risk category—this hub emphasizes strangulation mechanics, not crank catalogs.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Most blood chokes are legal across IBJJF and ADCC; some neck cranks and spinal locks are restricted by organization and belt. Verify IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules for your division.

Safety

Apply gradually; never rip chokes. Release immediately on tap. Encyclopedia reference only.

Common mistakes

Squeezing without under-chin depth; cranking the face instead of the carotids; abandoning position to chase a choke.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What are chokes in BJJ?

Submissions that threaten unconsciousness by restricting blood (or rarely air) at the neck.

Are chokes the same as neck cranks?

No—blood chokes target carotids; cranks torque the spine/cervical structure and are higher-risk and often rule-restricted.

Gi or no-gi chokes first?

Learn RNC and triangle in both; add collar attacks when you train gi regularly.

What should you chain next?

If a choke fails, keep position and switch to a sibling rather than abandoning control.

Common mistake?

Squeezing the face/jaw without under-chin depth.

Is it beginner-friendly?

Yes for basic blood chokes with slow application; advanced lapel systems come later.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Under Submission as the strangulation-family hub beside Joint Locks.

The Bottom Line

Use Chokes as the map for neck finishes: pick a positional home (back, guard, side), then drill two live cluster pages.
