Inside Ashi Garami

The inside ashi garami is a foundational ashi configuration: your inside leg crosses the trapped thigh with a far-hip frame while the outside leg hooks the knee line. Primary habitat for inside heel hooks and legal ankle locks when rules allow.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Inside ashi = inside leg across thigh + far-hip frame + outside knee-line hook—parent ashigarami.
  • Main finishes: inside heel hook, straight ankle lock, climb to saddle/honey hole.
  • Verify IBJJF vs ADCC heel-hook and reaping language.
  • Defense: heel-hook defense—hide the heel, protect the knee line.
  • Control first; do not chase the finish without hip and secondary-leg management.

What Is Inside Ashi Garami?

Inside ashi garami is the ashi shape where your inside leg threads across the opponent’s trapped thigh and your foot frames the far hip, while the outside leg hooks behind their knee/hamstring. Under ashigarami beside outside ashi, saddle/honey hole, and 50/50. Up: ashi entries, single-leg X. Across: cross ashi, saddle entries.

How It Works

Pinch the knees around the thigh, angle hips toward the trapped leg, and control the foot/ankle so they cannot spin free. The far-hip frame stops them closing distance; the outside hook blocks extraction.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Often paired with legal ankle locks where allowed; deep heel-hook chains frequently collide with IBJJF restrictions—verify the book.

No-gi. Primary no-gi habitat under ADCC-style rules for inside heel hooks and transitions into saddle.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From seated open guard you catch a step, thread the inside leg to the far hip, hook the outside knee line, and dig for the heel—or stand to sweep if finishes are illegal that day.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard inside ashi with far-hip frame and outside hamstring hook.

Competition variation

Inside ashi → roll or backstep into saddle / inside sankaku when the secondary leg is available.

Related option

Legal-mirror day: same control, finish straight ankle without illegal knee lines.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: open guard / SLX, failed pass recovery, ashi entry trees. Counters: hide the heel and use heel-hook defense habits.

How It Compares

Vs outside ashi: inside vs outside line. Vs saddle: saddle triangles both legs inside. Vs 50/50: one-sided control vs mutual entanglement.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Cross-read reaping and heel hook.

Safety

Leg attacks load ligaments fast. Tap early, drill slowly, and never crank through pain. Partners should release immediately on tap or freeze.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing the heel before the far-hip frame and knee pinch are set.
  • Ignoring secondary-leg management.
  • Running heel-hook A-games in restricted IBJJF gi without a legal plan.
  • Rolling the wrong way and exposing the knee line.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is inside ashi garami in BJJ?

An ashi entanglement with your inside leg across the opponent’s thigh and a far-hip frame, mainly used to attack inside heel hooks and related leg locks.

Is inside ashi the same as the saddle?

No. Saddle/honey hole/inside sankaku usually triangles both of your legs on the inside; inside ashi is the looser precursor.

Are heel hooks from inside ashi legal in IBJJF?

Often restricted in standard adult gi—verify the current IBJJF and event rulebooks.

What finishes work from inside ashi?

Inside heel hook where legal, straight ankle lock, toe hold where legal, and transitions to saddle or 50/50.

How do you defend inside ashi?

Hide the heel, keep a safer knee line, hand-fight grips, and extract—see heel-hook defense.

Gi or no-gi home?

No-gi/ADCC-style rulesets are the primary competitive home; gi use depends on legal finishes and reaping language.

Related catalog notes?

Ashigarami, outside ashi, saddle, honey hole, 50/50, heel hook, reaping, IBJJF rules, ADCC rules.

The Bottom Line

Treat inside ashi garami as the inside-line control node in the ashi family—set the frame, then finish or climb to saddle only under legal rules.
