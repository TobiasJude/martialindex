Scissor Kick

A scissor kick in TKD is a switching two-kick picture. Compare with Double Kick and Narae Chagi; do not confuse it with a grappling scissor sweep.

What Is Scissor Kick?

Scissor kick (often kawi-chagi family or gym English) describes two kicks that pass each other like blades—different legs or different heights in one phrase.

It sits next to Double Kick and Narae Chagi. Some gyms use the words interchangeably; others reserve “scissor” for an opposite-leg switch in the air.

This is not a BJJ scissor sweep and not a playground floor scissor. Martial Index files it under Taekwondo kicking.

Sport use is situational. PSS may register two trunk hits if both contacts are legal; many scissors are demo tools.

Martial Index files Scissor Kick under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Scissor Kick behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Scissor Kick Works

Chamber one leg, fire, and let the other leg chamber as the first retracts so the hips scissor.

Keep the torso stacked. A wild lean turns the scissor into two off-balance singles.

Land in Taekwondo Stance. Air scissors that end in a split stance are photos, not recoveries.

Decide the heights before you jump: body-body, body-head, or a switch-leg pair.

Mechanically, successful scissor kick work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, scissor kick is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill scissor kick daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Pad: low target then high target on a hop—coach watches whether both kicks have a real Chamber (Bal Deung).

Demo: jumping scissor turning kicks for a crowd; class: a hop scissor at mid height for control.

Fight picture: scissor kick appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply scissor kick after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Calling any {double-kick} a scissor.
  • No chamber on the second blade.
  • Confusing this with a ground scissor sweep.
  • Throwing it as a first-round {kyorugi} plan.
  • Landing with crossed feet.
  • Treating scissor kick as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick scissor kick inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so scissor kick connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether scissor kick appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe scissor kick as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put scissor kick behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep scissor kick scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on scissor kick, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for scissor kick under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Scissor vs double?

Double Kick is the two-beat family. Scissor emphasizes the switch of legs or heights.

Scissor vs narae?

Narae Chagi is a named double roundhouse. Overlap is real; names vary by gym.

Does it score twice?

Each legal impact can score under Electronic Scoring (PSS).

BJJ page?

No. Different art, different mechanic.

Learn first?

Honest Double Kick and Chamber (Bal Deung) before you add air time.

The Bottom Line

Use scissor as a clear switch of leg or height—or just say Double Kick and train the two honest beats.
