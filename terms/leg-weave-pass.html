Leg Weave Pass

The leg weave pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu guard pass that threads (weaves) your leg through the bottom player’s legs to pin a hook or knee shield, then clears to side control—especially common against half and knee-shield structures.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Weave to pin the near leg/shield, then walk or switch hips past—do not leave the weave floaty.
  • High-percentage vs knee shield and certain open-half frames.
  • Gi pants grips help lock the weave; no-gi uses shin pressure and underhooks.
  • Bad weaves invite lockdown, deep half, or back takes—control the head/hip line.
  • Cousins: smash, hip switch, and force-half strategies.

What Is Leg Weave Pass?

A leg weave pass threads your near or far leg through the bottom player’s leg configuration so their hook or knee shield is trapped against the mat or your shin. With the weave locked, you can smash, hip-switch, or walk to side control without the shield resetting.

Lachlan Giles–influenced curricula made gi and no-gi weave details mainstream; older smash passers did similar pinning without the modern name. It is a connection pass as much as a pressure pass—home under guard passing / half guard, clustered with hip switch and force half.

How Leg Weave Pass Works

Win upper-body connection (crossface, underhook, or head control), insert the weave so the dangerous frame is neutralized, then clear the free hip while keeping their shoulders flat.

Weave quality. A shallow weave that does not pin the knee line is just a free hook for them. Deep enough to kill elevation; not so tangled that you cannot walk.

Bottom player’s job. Prevent the weave insertion, elevate into butterfly, enter deep half or lockdown, or underhook and sit up the moment the passer’s head floats.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips secure the woven leg and far ankle. Collar and sleeve help flatten. Knee-shield players answer with frames and lapel grips that stall insertion.

No-gi. Shin angle, hip heaviness, and underhooks replace fabric. Weaves must be tighter because sweat and mobility let shields pop free.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Against knee-shield half, the passer crossfaces, weaves the near leg under the shield thigh, pins the shield flat, and walks the hips past into side control—or switches to a smash if the weave only partially kills the frame.

From open half, a weave can trap the inside hook long enough to free the passer’s trapped knee and finish like a smash or knee slice.

Types / Variations

Knee-shield weave

Thread under/behind the shield thigh to glue it down, then clear.

Inside-hook weave

Trap the half-guard hook so you can free your knee and pass.

Weave into smash / mount

Use the weave only to kill frames, then chest-smash or mount when space appears.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: failed knee cut into half, body-lock attempts that leave a shield, and headquarters where the inside hook is sticky.

Counters: lockdown, deep half, invert, or strip the weave and recover butterfly. Top contingencies: if entangled, simplify to force-half or back-step rather than forcing a bad weave.

Leg Weave vs Knee Cut

Factor Leg Weave Knee Cut / Slice
Primary tool Threaded leg pin Cutting knee across thigh
Best vs Knee shield / sticky half HQ and open/half splits
Risk Lockdown / deep half Underhook / half recovery
Feel Trap then walk Wedge then drive

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal across IBJJF gi and no-gi. Watch entanglement rules in some no-gi events if the weave turns into a prolonged leg entanglement without progress.

Safety

Do not twist the trapped knee while weaving. Avoid dumping weight onto a hyperextended knee during smash finishes from a weave.

Common mistakes

  • Weaving without a crossface—getting underhooked immediately.
  • Shallow weave that gives them a free hook and nothing for you.
  • Forcing the weave into lockdown instead of abandoning early.
  • Walking past before the shield is truly flat.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a leg weave pass?

A pass that threads your leg through theirs to pin a hook or knee shield, then clears to side control.

Is leg weave only for gi?

No. Gi grips help, but no-gi weaves are standard against knee shields when shin pressure is honest.

Leg weave vs smash pass?

The weave is often the pinning detail that makes a smash possible against a strong shield.

Why do I end in lockdown?

Your weave was too deep or you lost head position. Free your knee earlier or switch to back-step / force-half.

Can beginners use leg weave?

Yes, with a simple shield-pinning weave and slow walk. Prioritize crossface and chest heaviness over fancy threading.

Does it work on butterfly?

Sometimes as a smash precursor, but classic weaves shine more against half and knee-shield than pure butterfly.

What submissions follow a weave pass?

Side-control attacks—americana, kimura, arm triangle—once the near hip and shoulder are stable.

The Bottom Line

Leg weave is a pin with your legs: neutralize the shield or hook, keep the head/hip connection, and walk past. If the weave turns into their entanglement, change the pass early.
