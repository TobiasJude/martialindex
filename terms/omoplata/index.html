Omoplata

The omoplata is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu shoulder lock that wraps your legs around the opponent’s arm to torque the shoulder—used as a submission, sweep, and transition tool from closed and open guard in both gi and no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Omoplata = legs wrapping the arm to attack the shoulder (and create sweeps).
  • Often as useful as a sweep/back-take engine as a tap.
  • Gi pants/sleeve help; no-gi uses overhooks and tighter hip connection.
  • Related shoulder pressure family to the kimura, different tools.
  • Legal adult IBJJF; tap early on shoulder torque.

What Is the Omoplata?

The omoplata (Portuguese for scapula/shoulder blade) traps the opponent’s arm with your legs—typically sitting through so their arm is between your legs—and leans forward to torque the shoulder. It is a signature BJJ guard attack with deep sweep and transition value. It is a key branch of closed guard.

How Omoplata Works

Isolate an arm (often after a failed triangle/triangle attempt or triangle setup), triangle-ish wrap with the legs around that arm, sit up/through, control their hip or posture, and finish by bowing chest toward the mat—or sweep if they roll.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and pants grips help you break posture and stop them pulling the arm free.

No-gi. Harder to keep the arm without fabric—use overhooks, wrist control, and faster hip motion. Still fully viable.

Sweeps. Both rule sets prize the omoplata sweep when they roll to relieve shoulder pressure.

>What Does the Omoplata Look Like in Practice?

From closed guard, Alex climbs toward a triangle, switches to an omoplata on Jordan’s right arm, sits through, and either finishes the shoulder lock or sweeps when Jordan rolls.

Types / Variations

From closed / open guard

Primary home—including spider/lasso entries in gi.

Sweep and roll contingencies

If they roll, follow to mount/side or re-roll; crucifix options exist in some systems.

Finishing details

Control the far hip, keep the arm trapped, bow forward—don’t just yank the wrist.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter from triangle failures, shoulder pressure, and open-guard sleeve dominance. Escape by pulling the arm free early or rolling correctly—tap when the shoulder is loaded.

Triangle–omoplata–armbar triad. Many guard systems treat these as one decision tree: climb for triangle, switch to omoplata if the arm is stranded, swing to armbar if they posture. Learning them as a set raises finishing rates more than isolating one move.

Far-hip control. The most common reason omoplatas fail is the opponent walking their hips around your legs. Pin the far hip with a hand or hook before you bow for the finish.

Gi spider/lasso entries. Sleeve-heavy open guards create natural omoplata paths when you clear a foot and sit through. No-gi athletes replace those with overhooks and wrist rides.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal adult IBJJF/ADCC shoulder attack.

Safety

Tap early. Shoulder cartilage is not a trophy.

Common mistakes

  • No hip control—they walk around.
  • Hunting only the tap and missing the sweep.
  • Loose leg wrap that frees the elbow.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why do they always roll out?

Often you lack far-hip control. Pin the hip, or treat the roll as a sweep opportunity.

Omoplata good in no-gi?

Yes, with tighter arm control and faster transitions.

Same as kimura?

Same shoulder target, different tools. See kimura.

From triangle attempts?

Classic conversion path.

Hard for beginners?

Coordination-heavy but teachable early with drilling.

Legal everywhere?

Standard adult sport yes; follow kids’ rules if applicable.

Finish without sitting up?

Some variants exist; most classroom finishes sit through and bow.

The Bottom Line

The omoplata attacks the shoulder with your legs from guard—and often sweeps when they roll. Build it with the triangle and kimura as a connected upper-body attack system.
