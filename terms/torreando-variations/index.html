Toreando Variations

Toreando variations are the branches of the bullfighter family: grip changes, direction switches, leapfrog steps, and chains into other guard passes. Start from toreando / bullfighter, then compare slash and standing pass.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Variations share the outside-redirect idea.
  • Side switches beat stubborn recomposes.
  • Grip height (knee vs ankle) changes the problem.
  • Chain to knee-cut, stack, or body lock.
  • Study the parent toreando page first.

What Is Toreando Variations?

Rather than one technique, toreando variations are a menu: same cape idea, different grips and footwork.

They live under guard passing next to speed passing and slash.

Use this page as a cluster map; use toreando-pass and bullfighter-pass for the core mechanic.

How Toreando Variations Works

Baseline: two grips, push legs aside, circle. Variation 1: switch sides when they hip-escape. Variation 2: leapfrog or knee-on-belly finish.

Low ankle grips beat long legs; high knee grips beat seated butterflies that want underhooks.

When they lock a De La Riva or lasso, abandon pure toreando for knee cut or leg drag.

Always finish with hip control—clearing feet without pinning the hip is only half a pass.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grip height and sleeve control define most textbook variations.

No-gi. Shin rides and wrist control replace fabric; expect more sprawl finishes.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex’s right-side toreando meets a strong recompose; they reverse to the left and hit knee-on-belly.

Jordan uses ankle cups no-gi, fakes a bullfighter, then folds into a body lock when the bottom player sits up.

Types / Variations

Side-switch toreando

Dial left/right as they hip escape.

Leapfrog / knee-on-belly finish

Clear legs and land elevated pin.

Chain-off variations

Exit to knee cut, stack, or leg drag.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from standing open guard and after closed-guard breaks. Counters: DLR, lasso, worm. Escapes: invert or long-hook before the circle completes.

Toreando Family vs Knee-Cut Family

Factor Toreando / bullfighter family Knee-cut family
Primary path Around the legs Through / across the thigh
Best vs Extended feet Connected half / HQ
Sibling pages Bullfighter, slash Knee-cut vs half
Shared pillar Guard passing Guard passing

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Same pass-stabilization standards as other passes—verify current IBJJF language.

Safety

Watch necks when opponents invert under a committed toreando.

Common mistakes

  • Only having one side.
  • Grips too high with no ankle plan.
  • No chain when DLR appears.
  • Celebrating before the hip is pinned.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What counts as a toreando variation?

Any outside bullfighter-family pass that redirects both legs and circles—grip and footwork differ.

Should I learn variations before the basic pass?

No—own the basic toreando/bullfighter first.

Gi grips or no-gi first?

Gi pant grips teach the geometry; then translate.

How do I beat De La Riva?

Often you don’t with pure toreando—chain to other passes.

Is slash a variation?

Related outside idea; kept on its own page for search clarity.

Common mistake?

Backing up instead of circling.
