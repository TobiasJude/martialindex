Points Scoring (IBJJF)

Points scoring in IBJJF-style Brazilian jiu-jitsu rewards athletes who achieve and stabilize higher positions in the hierarchy—from takedowns and sweeps through passes into mount and back control.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Points pay for stabilized positional progress—not momentary touches.
  • Verify current point values and hold-times in the official IBJJF rulebook; this page stays conceptual.
  • Advantages are not points; see the advantages entry.
  • Strategy: climb the hierarchy while denying the opponent’s climb.
  • Cross-link penalties, ADCC, and core pillars.

What Is Points Scoring (IBJJF)?

Under IBJJF competition logic, matches can end by submission, but many are decided by accumulated positional points. The federation publishes which actions score and how long control must be shown—Martial Index will not invent those numbers here because rulebooks update.

Conceptually, scoring tracks a hierarchy familiar to every gym: taking the opponent down or sweeping to top, passing the guard, and securing pins such as knee-on-belly variants, side control progressions, mount, and back control.

Points are the primary scoreboard layer. Advantages and penalties resolve ties and punish stalling—study them as a system with IBJJF Rules.

How Points Scoring (IBJJF) Works

Train “score then improve”: when you land a takedown or pass, stabilize before hunting a low-percentage finish that gives the position back.

Bottom players score by sweeping to a stable top; chain sweeps from closed guard, half guard, and open guards rather than exploding once.

Top players score by clearing legs and establishing the next pin—see guard passing systems.

Before every tournament, re-read the current IBJJF scoring articles. Do not rely on a teammate’s memory of “how many points mount is.”

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi IBJJF scoring uses the same positional ideas with collar-and-sleeve control slowing exchanges.

No-gi IBJJF still scores positions, but legal technique lists and attire differ—verify the no-gi book separately.

ADCC-style events are not IBJJF points culture—see ADCC Rules Overview.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex passes to side control, holds the pin cleanly, then knees-on-belly—each step is a conscious scoring climb rather than frantic submission spam.

Jordan sweeps from half guard but immediately loses top; the referee may not credit a full score without stabilization—advantages might appear instead.

Sam leads on points and chooses safe pressure passing instead of diving on a foot lock that could reverse the match.

Types / Variations

Takedowns & sweeps

Bottom-to-top or standing-to-top actions that must meet control standards in the live book.

Guard passes

Clearing the legs into a dominant torso pin—linked to guard passing.

Dominant pins

Mount, back with hooks/body triangle concepts, knee-on-belly families—exact definitions are official.

Near scores

Actions that almost stabilize often become advantages instead of points.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries to points: clean wrestling, guard retention into sweeps, and structured passing sequences.

Counters: scramble back to guard, reverse immediately, or threaten submissions that force the opponent to abandon the pin.

“Escapes” from a points deficit: create chaos ethically—stand-up, re-pull carefully under rules, or hunt a submission rather than farming weak advantages.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Verify all numeric values, hold durations, and referee signals in the current IBJJF rulebook PDF. This page explains why scoring exists—not the live table. Related: IBJJF Rules, advantages, penalties.

Safety

Chasing points is not an excuse to crank submissions or slam. Stabilize ethically; tap culture still rules training.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting outdated point charts from memory.
  • Touching mount for half a second and celebrating.
  • Leading on advantages and stalling into penalties.
  • Ignoring takedown training because “I’ll just pull guard.”

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What actions score points in IBJJF?

Positional climbs such as takedowns/sweeps, passes, and dominant pins—exact values and hold-times are only authoritative in the current official rulebook.

Why won’t Martial Index list the numbers?

Rulebooks revise. Conceptual literacy lasts; invented tables mislead competitors.

Do submissions give points?

A finished submission ends the match. Near submissions may yield advantages—see the advantages entry.

Is knee-on-belly a pin family?

Yes conceptually; definitions and scoring sit in the live IBJJF text.

How does this differ from ADCC?

ADCC delays/shapes scoring differently and prioritizes finishes—see ADCC rules.

Can penalties affect points?

Yes—accumulated stalling/fouls can feed the scoreboard. Study penalties.

Best training drill?

Positional rounds that start after a pass or sweep and require you to keep the pin for a referee-style count.

The Bottom Line

IBJJF points are a language of stabilized progress. Learn the hierarchy, then verify every number in the current official rulebook before you compete.
