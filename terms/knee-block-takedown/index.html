Knee-Block Takedown

The knee-block takedown blocks or traps the lead knee while you drive the torso—closely related to the Knee Tap, often set from strikes or collar ties.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Off-balance first; the knee block finishes.
  • Block the lead knee from outside or with a post.
  • Pairs with level-change after punches.
  • Protect the neck—guillotine risk.
  • Chain to trips if they hop.

How Knee-Block Takedown Works

Create collar-tie or underhook (or punch into a level change) → palm/forearm against the lead knee's path → drive torso past that line.

Hughes-style underhook-to-knee-tap DNA still applies; modern versions add Dirty Boxing setups.

Mechanically, successful knee-block takedown work is a chain: establish grip or angle → remove a base or frame → apply the scoring action → recover posture with hands alive.

If distance or fence angle is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close without a pin and you collide into frames, whizzers, or submission threats.

Common Mistakes

  • Blocking without driving.
  • Reaching so far you lose head position.
  • Telegraphing a deep shot.
  • Soft underhook on the blocked side.
  • Treating knee-block takedown as a highlight instead of a position-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down or hips light.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.
