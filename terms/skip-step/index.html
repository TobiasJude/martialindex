Skip Step

A skip step is a bounce-close. Compare with Sliding Step: skip is springier and easier to read; slide is quieter.

What Is Skip Step?

A skip step (hop step) brings the rear foot toward the lead and then fires the lead kick as you take the space.

It is the default feeder for lead Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), Cut Kick (Taekwondo), and many Fast Kick (Bal Bal) rallies.

Compare with Sliding Step: same job (range), different texture (hop vs glide).

A skip that goes only up is wasted. The hop must travel.

Martial Index files Skip Step under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Taekwondo style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Skip Step behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Skip Step Works

Rear foot skips to (or past) the lead heel; the lead knee is already thinking Chamber (Bal Deung).

Keep the head from bobbing like a pogo stick. Quiet head, busy feet.

Land the skip into the kick, not as a separate pose, then recover to Taekwondo Stance.

Mix skips with slides so opponents cannot time a single rhythm.

Mechanically, successful skip step work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, skip step is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill skip step daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Skip-lead body, skip-lead body, skip-head.

Coach claps; you skip-kick on the clap, not before.

Fight picture: skip step appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply skip step after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Skip into cut

Skip into Cut Kick (Taekwondo).

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Skip into score

Common Mistakes

  • Skipping only upward.
  • Skipping into a square stance.
  • Telegraphing every skip with a huge dip.
  • Never sliding, so every close is a hop.
  • Skipping while already in punching range.
  • Treating skip step as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick skip step inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so skip step connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether skip step appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe skip step as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put skip step behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Taekwondo Stance, Dollyo Chagi (Turning Kick), and Kyorugi (Sparring) into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for spinning kicks and head-height targets where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “roundhouse,” “cut kick,” “hook,” and “feint.” Martial Index’s Taekwondo entries keep skip step scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated Muay Thai or Boxing articles that share casual English labels.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on skip step, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for skip step under your next card (WT vs ITF, PSS vs paper scoring, headgear) so training intensity matches the bout.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Skip vs slide?

Sliding Step glides; skip step hops. Choose based on how much you can afford to be read.

Skip vs switch?

Skip usually keeps the lead; switch changes it.

Is it a kick?

No. It is footwork that feeds a kick.

PSS?

Skips help you arrive with enough force for Electronic Scoring (PSS).

ITF?

Hopping entries exist; some ITF schools prefer stepping or sine-wave advances.

The Bottom Line

Skip to steal a step—then make the lead kick worth the tell.
