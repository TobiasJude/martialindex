De La Riva Guard

De La Riva guard is an open-guard system built around an outside hook on the opponent's lead leg—used to off-balance standing passers, sweep, berimbolo, or transition to reverse DLR and SLX, with richer collar-sleeve tools in the gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Outside hook on the lead leg defines classic DLR.
  • Primary jobs: off-balance standing passers, sweep, or take the back.
  • Gi adds collar-sleeve and berimbolo chains; no-gi uses ankles/wrists and SLX links.
  • RDLR is the twin for knee-cut resistance.
  • Compare with spider when choosing hook vs bicep-foot games.

What Is De La Riva Guard?

De La Riva guard (DLR) is an open guard where you hook your outside foot around the opponent's lead leg from the outside (the classic DLR hook), usually while controlling an ankle, sleeve, or collar. Named after Ricardo De La Riva, it is the default answer many gi and no-gi athletes use against a standing passer.

How De La Riva Works

The outside hook off-balances their lead leg while your other foot frames the hip or bicep. Pulling the ankle and extending the hook dumps them toward back exposures, berimbolo entries, or simple trip sweeps. Reverse De La Riva (RDLR) flips the hook inside for different pass resistance and leg-entanglement entries.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar-and-sleeve DLR, pants grips, and berimbolo chains dominate sport gi. You can hang on fabric while you invert.

No-gi. Ankle control, wrist control, and tighter hooks replace sleeves. Lachlan Giles and others connect DLR/waiter structures to heel hooks and sweeps without pants. Berimbolo volume drops; SLX and K-guard connections rise.

Shared. The off-balancing hook remains; only the handles change.

What Does De La Riva Look Like in Practice?

Jordan stands in Alex's guard. Alex places a DLR hook on Jordan's right lead leg, grips the ankle, frames the far hip, and sweeps Jordan forward into a scramble toward mount—or, in the gi, grips a sleeve and berimbolos to the back.

Types / Variations

Classic DLR hook

Outside hook + ankle/sleeve control; basic trip and back-take paths.

Collar-sleeve DLR (gi)

Adds upper-body steering for berimbolos and light sweeps.

Reverse De La Riva

Inside hook; great against knee cuts; feeds 50/50 and SLX.

Waiter / DLR hybrids

Under-the-leg control structures popularized in modern no-gi open guard.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. From open guard when they stand; from seated guard; from failed closed guard.

Passes against DLR. Long step, smash the hook, knee cut after clearing the hook, leg drag, pressure into half.

Counters. Re-hook, invert, switch to RDLR, or elevate into X/SLX when they clear the outside hook.

De La Riva vs Spider Guard

Factor De La Riva Spider Guard
Primary tool Outside hook on the lead leg Feet on biceps with sleeve grips
Best against Standing passers Standing or kneeling with sleeve access
Gi dependency Useful in both; richer in gi Heavily gi-dependent
Common next Berimbolo, RDLR, SLX Lasso, triangle, sweep to mount

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

DLR is legal. Berimbolo and inversion chains are legal in IBJJF with standard reaping awareness when you enter certain leg positions. ADCC athletes use stripped-down DLR without sleeve reliance.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Inversions need clear mats. Tap early if DLR entries become heel hooks in no-gi.

Common mistakes

  • Soft hook that they step out of immediately.
  • No frame on the far hip—getting smashed flat.
  • In no-gi: waiting for sleeve grips that never come.
  • Inverting without ankle control.
  • Ignoring RDLR when the knee cut starts.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is De La Riva only a gi guard?

No. The hook works in no-gi; you just control ankles and wrists instead of sleeves and play fewer berimbolos.

DLR or RDLR against the knee cut?

Reverse De La Riva is usually the better first answer to a knee cut; classic DLR shines when they stand with a clear lead leg.

Do I need to berimbolo to use DLR?

No. Basic trip sweeps and back exposures work without inversion. Berimbolo is optional depth for gi athletes.

How do I stop them clearing my DLR hook?

Pin the ankle, keep the hook active (not dead), and frame the far hip so they cannot step over freely.

What comes after DLR?

Sweeps to mount/side control, back takes, RDLR, or elevations into X/SLX depending on their reaction.

Spider or DLR first for gi beginners?

Either works; DLR teaches standing-passer timing, spider teaches sleeve coordination. Many schools teach basic DLR sweeps early.

The Bottom Line

De La Riva hooks the standing passer's lead leg to off-balance, sweep, or take the back. Build gi collar-sleeve chains or no-gi ankle control, then explore spider and SLX as neighbors.
