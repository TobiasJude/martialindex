Butterfly Guard Pass

The butterfly guard pass beats butterfly hooks via float, fold/smash, or body-lock pressure—clustered with float pass, butterfly sweep, and butterfly to X-guard.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Kill elevation before you drive.
  • Float when light; fold/body-lock when heavy.
  • Watch the underhook that seeds butterfly sweeps.
  • X-guard appears if a hook slips deep—have an answer.
  • Gi and no-gi both central.

What Is Butterfly Guard Pass?

Butterfly guard pass is the top menu vs inside hooks. Parent: butterfly. Live children: float pass, folding, body lock, smash.

How It Works

Control ankles/hips or lock a body connection, float over collapsing hooks or smash them flat, clear to half or side, and block rehooks. Do not give the underhook that powers their sweep.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Belt and pants help pin hooks; collar can break posture.

No-gi. Float and body-lock families dominate modern no-gi.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Seated butterfly → you float over a kicking hook → settle side; or body-lock and walk the hips free.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Float pass over light hooks.

Competition variation

Folding / smash when they are flat.

Related option

Body-lock connection pass when you win torso control.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Vs seated butterfly. Counters. Butterfly sweep, to X-guard, elevators.

How It Compares

Vs X-guard pass: butterfly is earlier elevation; X is deeper. Vs half hub: many butterfly passes intentionally force half.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Legal everywhere; ADCC body-lock meta is huge.

Safety

Float carefully—do not knee partners in the face while hovering.

Common mistakes

  • Driving chest into live hooks.
  • Giving the underhook.
  • No plan when one hook becomes X.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How do you pass butterfly?

Float, fold/smash, or body-lock—kill elevation and underhooks first.

Is float pass the only answer?

No—smash and body-lock are co-equal branches.

Gi or no-gi?

Both; no-gi loves float/body-lock.

What next?

Side, mount, or forced half then continue.

Common mistake?

Smashing into live hooks or donating underhooks.

Beginner-friendly?

Folding pressure yes; float timing is intermediate.

Martial Index home?

Under butterfly next to float-pass and X transitions.

The Bottom Line

Butterfly guard pass is a menu—master float and body-lock, and know the X entry they want.
