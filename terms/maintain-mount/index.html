Maintain Mount

Maintain mount means keeping mount against bridge and roll and knee-elbow escapes—wide base, follow the hips, deny frames—then finish under control before submit.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Base first—post before you attack.
  • Follow hips; don’t get left behind.
  • Deny elbow-knee inserts.
  • Post the free limb vs upa traps.
  • High / technical mount when they turn.

What Is Maintain Mount?

Retention skill for the mount pillar after pass to mount.

Two classic threats: diagonal bridge-and-roll and frame-driven elbow escapes.

Pairs with maintain side control and maintain back as the “keep the pin” trio.

How Maintain Mount Works

Land low and wide; toes active, knees not glued helplessly.

When they bridge, post free hand/foot toward the bridge and ride it out.

When they frame, slide to follow hips; grapevine or float as taught.

Elevate to high or technical mount if they turn to a side.

Only isolate arms/neck when the pin is stable—see control-before-submit.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar and sleeve help pin posts; watch their upa traps on your sleeves.

No-gi. Underhooks and head control; expect slipperier bridges.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Sam posts the free hand at 45° as the upa comes, keeps mount, then goes high mount.

Alex feels the elbow insert, recenters, and grapevines before the shrimp frees a knee.

Types / Variations

Low wide mount

Base vs bridges.

High / technical mount

Answer the turn.

Grapevine retention

Kill hip escape power.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

After any mount arrival. Bottom counters: bridge-and-roll, knee-elbow, hip bump. Your answers are posts, follows, and elevation—not panic submissions.

Maintain Mount vs Maintain Side Control

Factor Maintain mount Maintain side
Threats Upa + elbow escape Frame-and-shrimp + underhook turn
Base Wider, more mobile hips Perpendicular pin, head/hip
Advance Already high on ladder Climb to mount/back
Submit window Both arms freer Often one arm hidden

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Maintaining mount protects mount points already scored; verify control definitions in current IBJJF / ADCC rules.

Safety

Don’t drop all weight on the diaphragm without awareness in training; give partners exit ramps.

Common mistakes

  • Attacking with no base.
  • Getting both arms trapped for the upa.
  • Sitting static while they shrimp.
  • Ignoring technical mount when they turn.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is maintain mount?

Keeping mount against the main escapes.

Main threats?

Bridge-and-roll and knee-elbow / frame escapes.

First habit?

Base and post—then attack.

If they turn?

Technical / high mount or take the back.

Gi tip?

Don’t let both arms get trapped.

Related principle?

Control before submit.

Sibling pages?

Maintain side / maintain back.

The Bottom Line

Maintain mount is keep the throne—base, follow, deny frames—then submit without donating the pin.
