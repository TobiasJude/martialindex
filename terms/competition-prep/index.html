Competition Prep

Competition prep is the planned ramp into a tournament: know competition formats, study IBJJF rules or ADCC rules, sharpen an A-game, taper intelligently, and manage weight safely (see cutting weight for BJJ).

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Rules and bracket shape the camp.
  • A-game over novelty.
  • Peak, don’t redline every week.
  • Same-day weigh-ins punish crash cuts.
  • Simulate match day once.

What Is Competition Prep?

Periodized training toward a date—not random hard rounds forever.

Includes technical, physical, and mental layers.

Hobbyists can “prep light”; professionals lengthen camps.

How Competition Prep Works

Confirm federation, gi/no-gi, match times, and illegal techniques for your belt.

Build a written game plan from 2–3 positions.

Raise specific sparring early; taper volume late while keeping sharpness.

Plan travel, sleep, and nutrition with your coach.

Review film of your own rounds more than highlight reels.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Grip endurance and collar threats matter; check uniform regs.

No-gi. Wrestling entries and leg-entanglement rulesets (IBJJF vs ADCC) change prep.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Keenan-style phase thinking: foundations, intensification, then competition-specific readiness.

A first-timer reads IBJJF scoring a week out, knows advantages matter, and stops hunting only submissions.

Types / Variations

First tournament

Rules literacy, nerves management, one reliable path to points or submissions.

Ruleset-specific

IBJJF points/advantages vs ADCC submission emphasis change drilling priorities.

Camp length

Often 4–12 weeks; masters and hobbyists may run shorter, lower-volume camps.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Enter prep with a registered division and honest walking weight.

Counter panic by returning to the written plan.

Escape overtraining: deload if sleep and mood crash.

Competition Prep vs Cutting Weight

Factor Competition prep Cutting weight
Scope Whole camp Mass management subset
Success metric Perform on match day Make weight without wrecking performance
Risk Overtraining Dehydration / bad cuts
Owner Athlete + coach Athlete + coach + clinician when needed

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Read the current IBJJF book for your belt and age; ADCC athletes should study ADCC rules separately—prep is ruleset-native.

Safety

Do not invent medical protocols from the internet. Extreme dehydration is discouraged—see cutting-weight-bjj.

Common mistakes

  • Learning new guards the week of the event.
  • Cutting double-digit pounds for same-day weigh-ins.
  • Ignoring legal/illegal technique lists.
  • Skipping breakfast after weigh-in when rules allow fueling.
  • No plan for between-match recovery.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How long should prep be?

Often 6–8 weeks for serious amateurs; shorter for local opens if base training is solid.

Do I need a strength program?

Helpful, but don’t start a brand-new max-strength cycle two weeks out.

What about nerves?

Rehearse warm-ups and breathing; nerves are normal—see breathing/pacing concepts.

The Bottom Line

Competition prep is a rules-aware, tapered plan around a small A-game—not a month of random suffering.
