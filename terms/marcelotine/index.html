Marcelotine

The Marcelotine is Marcelo Garcia’s high-elbow guillotine: wrist bone under the chin, a compact fist or ball-and-socket grip, the elbow shelved on the trap, and a knees-to-chest crunch rather than a wild back-fall. It is a named detail set inside the high-elbow school.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Not a totally different choke—Marcelo’s standards for a high-elbow finish.
  • Wrist bone / skinny forearm under the chin, not a meaty biceps curl.
  • Elbow locks on the trapezius; hands lift toward your own chin.
  • Butterfly and closed-guard sit-ups are signature entries; mount finishes exist too.
  • Read high-elbow guillotine for the broader school.

What Is Marcelotine?

Marcelotine is gym shorthand for the way Marcelo Garcia finished guillotines against much larger opponents: a tight, technical, high-elbow strangle that does not rely on arching power. Instructors borrowed the nickname the way “Eddie” sometimes labels rubber-guard trivia—useful, slightly informal, and worth defining.

Mechanically it lives inside the high-elbow / arm-out family. The differences people point to are grip (fist or ball-and-socket, not a sloppy gable), contact surface (wrist bone under the jaw), and body (knees pull them in; you rarely flop straight backward).

Martial Index gives the nickname its own page because people search it, then sends you back to guillotine, high-elbow, and closed guard / butterfly guard for the platforms.

How Marcelotine Works

From butterfly or closed guard, break posture with elbow control or a sit-up (hip-bump timing). Wrap so the bony wrist sits under the chin and rotate the choking arm until the elbow can shelf on their trap.

Connect a compact grip—fist in the support hand, or a short ball-and-socket—not a wide hug. Lift the hands toward your chin as you bring knees to chest.

Fall to a hip if you fall at all. A straight back-fall opens their posture and leaks the choke. If they give you the mount instead, keep the elbow shelved and finish from top with weight through the wrists.

Double-elbow control from butterfly (Marcelo’s hook-lift) is a signature entry: lift just enough to make the neck arrive, then lock. Do not telegraph a naked neck hunt.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Marcelo’s fame is no-gi and gi; cloth adds elbow/sleeve controls for the lift. The finish remains a naked high elbow.

No-gi. The version most people mean. Butterfly vs a kneeling passer is the classroom picture.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex double-elbows from butterfly, hook-lifts, shelves the elbow, and taps without ever opening a wide gable.

Jordan misses the butterfly lift, sits to closed guard, and finishes the same Marcelotine structure from the hip.

Types / Variations

Butterfly / hook-lift Marcelotine

Signature: lift the passer, neck arrives, elbow shelves.

Closed-guard Marcelotine

Sit-up or hip-bump timing into the same grip.

Mount / top Marcelotine

If they dump you or you beat them to mount, keep the shelf and finish with wrist weight.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: butterfly elbow control, closed-guard sit-up, stuffed shot into guard. Counters: hide the chin, sprawl their hook-lift, extract the head. Escapes: posture and peel before the elbow shelves—late escapes are taps.

Marcelotine vs Generic Low-Elbow Guillotine

Factor Marcelotine / high elbow Low-elbow “arch” guillotine
Contact Wrist bone under chin Meaty forearm / biceps
Elbow High on the trap Low, pulling toward the hips
Body Knees in, crunch Back arch, legs long
Feel Choke Often a crank

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult guillotines are standard legal chokes. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Treat it as a choke. If you only feel neck bend, you have drifted to a crank—reset the elbow shelf.

Common mistakes

  • Falling straight backward with long legs.
  • Wide, weak gable instead of a compact grip.
  • Initiating a naked neck hunt without elbow control.
  • Calling every guillotine a Marcelotine—keep the name for the detail set.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is Marcelotine a real official name?

It is widely used gym/search language for Marcelo’s high-elbow details, not a separate Kodokan kata.

Same as high-elbow?

Same school. This page is the named Garcia detail set.

Do I need butterfly guard?

It is a signature entry, not the only one. Closed guard and front headlock also feed it.

Fist or gable?

Marcelo-style teaching often uses a compact fist / ball-and-socket. Use what keeps the elbow high.

Works on bigger people?

That was the point of Marcelo’s version—structure over arching strength.

Gi or no-gi?

Both. No-gi is the picture most people search.

Arm-in Marcelotine?

People usually mean arm-out. If the arm is in, see the arm-in page.

The Bottom Line

The Marcelotine is Marcelo’s high-elbow standard: wrist bone, elbow on the trap, knees in. Use it as a named lens on the high-elbow guillotine, not as a mysterious third choke.
