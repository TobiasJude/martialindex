Blast Double

A blast double is a run-through Double Leg (Wrestling)—penetration, level change, and horsepower through the hips.

  • Parent is wrestling-double-leg.
  • Penetrate then drive.
  • Head up.
  • Chain if sprawled.

What Is Blast Double?

The blast double is a finish style inside the Double Leg (Wrestling) family: you penetrate, lock both legs, and drive the opponent backward/off their base with force.

Parent: Double Leg (Wrestling). Requires Penetration Step and Level Change.

It contrasts with high-crotch finishes and outside trips—this is linear power.

In Wrestling, blast doubles appear in folkstyle and freestyle when the pocket opens.

Martial Index files Blast Double under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Blast Double behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Blast Double Works

Change levels, take a deep penetration step, gather both legs, keep your head up on the correct side, and drive off the back foot through their hips.

Do not stall on the knees in front of a ready Sprawl (Wrestling).

If they sprawl, chain to a high crotch, lift, or re-shot—don’t die on the first blast.

Finish in control for the takedown; then decide ride vs turn by rule set.

Mechanically, successful blast double work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, blast double is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how blast double is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of blast double either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Out-of-bounds and mat returns differ by rule set.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Hand fight → level change → blast double runs them to the boundary.

Fake single; blast double when they overprotect one leg.

Match picture: blast double appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply blast double after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Linear blast finish

Straight-line drive through the hips.

Common Mistakes

  • Head down—get crossfaced or choked in MMA cross-training.
  • Short penetration.
  • No drive off the trail leg.
  • Ignoring sprawl defense literacy.
  • Treating blast double as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle blast double inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so blast double connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether blast double appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Drive sled or partner-drag doubles.

Live: doubles only, sprawl defense live on the other side.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe blast double as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of blast double stays stable even when accents change.
