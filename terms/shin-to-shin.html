Shin-to-Shin

Shin-to-shin is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard connection where your shin meets theirs—creating a sticky entry platform for sweeps, wrestling-ups, single-leg X, and K-guard style entanglement paths.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Shin against their shin = connection before hooks.
  • Feeds SLX, K-guard, and simple trip sweeps.
  • Works gi and no-gi; grips change the timing.
  • Passers must clear the shin connection early.
  • Parent open guard.

What Is Shin-to-Shin?

Shin-to-shin (STS) is an open-guard posture where you sit or recline with your shin pressed to their lead shin—connecting before you commit to DLR hooks or full entanglements. It is a transitional language as much as a destination. It is a key branch of open guard.

How Shin-to-Shin Works

Matching shins lets you feel their weight shift. You can trip/sweep the lead leg, come up to wrestle, or elevate into SLX / K-guard. Upper-body grips (sleeve, ankle, wrist) time the move. Lose the shin connection and they step past into a free pass.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and pants help freeze the lead leg while you play STS.

No-gi. Wrist/ankle control and faster SLX/K entries—STS is a brief doorway.

What Does Shin-to-Shin Look Like in Practice?

Alex sits, places shin on Jordan's lead shin, grips the ankle, and either sweeps the lead leg or elevates into SLX when Jordan tries to toreando.

Types / Variations

Basic shin-to-shin

Seated connection, active hips, ready upper-body grip.

To single-leg X

Elevate and lace into SLX when they post or step.

To K-guard

Use the shin connection to create the inside angle for K-guard entries.

Passing shin-to-shin

Clear or step over the shin early; don't linger in their connection.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From seated guard when they offer a lead leg; from failed DLR; as a reset between open-guard phases.

Bottom threats. Trips, SLX, K-guard, wrestling-ups.

Top counters. Kick the connection off, long-step, or smash before they elevate.

Shin-to-Shin vs Seated Guard

Factor Shin-to-Shin Seated Guard
Focus Shin connection to a lead leg Upright seated open-guard posture
Next step SLX / K / trip Any open-guard hook system
Specificity Narrow connection Broad posture category

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Entanglement finishes follow heel-hook/reaping rules of the event.

Safety

Control dumps on trip sweeps. Tap early to heel hooks off STS→ashi chains.

Common mistakes

  • Dead shin with no grip or elevation plan.
  • Letting them clear STS without transitioning.
  • Forcing heel hooks under IBJJF rules.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is shin-to-shin a full guard system?

It is more a connection/entry posture that feeds larger systems (SLX, K-guard, sweeps).

STS before or after DLR?

Often before—connect shin-to-shin, then decide on DLR/RDLR/SLX based on their reaction.

Does it work in no-gi?

Yes—especially as a doorway to SLX and K-guard.

How do I pass shin-to-shin?

Break the shin connection immediately, keep hips long, toreando or step past before they elevate.

Common sweep from STS?

Lead-leg trip/off-balance sweeps and elevations into SLX that finish as sweeps under points rules.

STS vs butterfly?

Butterfly uses hooks under the thighs; STS matches shin to shin as a connection. See butterfly.

The Bottom Line

Shin-to-shin connects shin to shin to sweep, wrestle up, or enter SLX/K-guard. Treat it as a doorway inside open guard and seated guard.
