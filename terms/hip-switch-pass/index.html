Hip Switch Pass

The hip switch pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu half-guard pass that changes hip angle to clear a knee shield or free a trapped knee, then finishes toward side control—often chained with weave, smash, and knee cut threats.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Switch hips to kill the shield/hook angle—then walk or cut; do not stall mid-switch.
  • Pillar home: half guard top game under guard passing.
  • Gi: pants + collar help pin after the switch; no-gi: underhook and head pressure.
  • Cluster siblings: leg weave, force half, back step.
  • If they underhook during the switch, flatten or abandon into smash/back-step.

What Is Hip Switch Pass?

A hip switch pass uses a deliberate change of hip orientation—often from chest-facing to a more side-oriented drive—to remove the framing power of a knee shield or to free a trapped knee inside half guard. It is less a single “move” than a pressure mechanic inside modern half-guard passing.

It lives under the guard passing pillar and clusters with leg weave, smash, force half, and knee slice finishes once the shield is cleared.

How Hip Switch Pass Works

Win a crossface or underhook, load weight so the bottom player cannot shrimp freely, switch your hips to collapse their knee-line frame, then free your trapped knee and pass.

Why the switch works. Knee shields live on angles. Changing your hip line takes their shin off your hip and puts your chest or shoulder into a driving path toward side control.

Bottom player’s job. Re-angle the shield early, underhook the switching hip, enter deep half or butterfly recovery, or lock lockdown before the free knee escapes.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips on the far leg and collar drives after the switch keep shoulders flat. Sleeve frames from bottom try to freeze you mid-switch.

No-gi. Head position and underhooks replace fabric. Switches must be heavier because shields return faster—pair with pressure passing habits.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From knee-shield half, the passer crossfaces, switches hips to flatten the shield, frees the trapped knee, and either smashes through or slices to side control—same cluster as knee cut once headquarters appears.

Against sticky inside hooks, a hip switch can precede a leg weave pin or a back step if the near underhook is lost.

Types / Variations

Shield-clear hip switch

Primary white/blue pattern: switch to kill knee shield, then walk or cut.

Free-the-knee switch

Emphasis on extracting the trapped knee before any cut or smash.

Switch into knee slice / cut

Use the switch only to win angle, then finish with the knee-slice family.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: failed knee cut into half, body-lock stalls, and headquarters where the shield resets. Counters: underhook, deep half, lockdown, invert. Contingencies: if the switch floats, go to smash, weave, or back-step rather than forcing a bad cut—see also force half.

Hip Switch vs Back Step

Factor Hip Switch Back Step
Primary goal Clear shield / free knee forward Step back to free knee / change side
Pillar Half-guard top under guard passing Half-guard top under guard passing
Cluster mates Weave, smash, knee slice Force half, smash, quarter guard
Risk if floated Underhook / sweep Giving up the back / scramble

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal in IBJJF gi and no-gi. No special restrictions. In sport no-gi, prolonged floating mid-switch without progress can look like stalling.

Safety

Do not dump sideways torque into the bottom player’s trapped knee while switching. Keep neck posture honest when crossfacing.

Common mistakes

  • Switching without crossface—eating the underhook.
  • Stopping mid-switch with a live knee shield still on your hip.
  • Ignoring cluster exits (weave/smash/back-step) when the cut is not there.
  • Chasing mount before the near hip is cleared.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a hip switch pass?

A half-guard pass that changes hip angle to clear a knee shield or free a trapped knee, then finishes to side control.

Is hip switch the same as knee cut?

No. Hip switch clears angle; knee cut/knee slice is often the finish once the line is open.

Where does it sit in the catalog?

Under guard passing, clustered with half-guard top passes: weave, force half, smash, back step, knee slice.

Gi or no-gi?

Both. Gi grips stabilize after the switch; no-gi needs heavier head/underhook connection.

Why do I get swept on the switch?

You floated. Keep chest weight and deny the underhook, or abandon into back-step/smash.

Hip switch vs leg weave?

Switch changes your hip line; weave pins their leg with yours. Often used in sequence against the same shield.

Good for white belts?

Yes—teach a slow shield-clear switch before complex headquarters systems.

The Bottom Line

Hip switch is an angle tool inside the half-guard passing cluster: clear the shield, free the knee, then finish with smash, weave, or knee slice—linked up to guard passing.
