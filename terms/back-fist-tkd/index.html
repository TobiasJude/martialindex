What Is Back Fist (Deung Jumeok)?

Deung jumeok (등주먹) is the back fist. The striking surface is the back of the first two knuckles.

It appears throughout Poomsae (notably several taegeuk) and in ITF Taekwondo hand-technique menus.

WT Kyorugi (Sparring) punches to the head are restricted; do not assume a back fist is a high-percentage Olympic tool.

Compare with Jirugi (Punch): different surface, different path, same need for hip and stance.

Martial Index files Back Fist (Deung Jumeok) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Back Fist (Deung Jumeok) Works

Chamber the fist near the opposite shoulder or hip per school, then whip the elbow so the knuckles snap to the target—often the philtrum or temple line in forms.

The elbow stays slightly soft. Hyperextending a back fist is how you collect joint stories.

Stance is usually Ap Kubi (Forward Stance) or a walking stance in forms; fighting stance in self-defense drills.

Retract. A posed back fist with the arm hanging is not a completed technique.

Mechanically, successful back fist (deung jumeok) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, back fist (deung jumeok) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill back fist (deung jumeok) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Taegeuk: front kick, back fist, middle block—one phrase.

Pad: light target at face height; snap and return without winding a huge loop.

Fight picture: back fist (deung jumeok) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply back fist (deung jumeok) after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Hitting with the wrist instead of the knuckles.
  • A huge wind-up that telegraphs.
  • Locked elbow.
  • Using it as a WT head-punch plan.
  • No retraction.
  • Treating back fist (deung jumeok) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick back fist (deung jumeok) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so back fist (deung jumeok) connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether back fist (deung jumeok) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Deung jumeok vs jirugi?

Jirugi (Punch) is a straight punch with the front knuckles. Back fist whips the back knuckles.

Olympic legal?

WT punch rules are narrow. Treat this as forms/self-defense/ITF unless your card says otherwise.

Target?

Often the philtrum line in Poomsae.

ITF?

Yes—back fist is a regular hand technique.

Related block?

Often paired with Momtong Makki (Middle Block) or Olgul Makki (High Block) in patterns.

The Bottom Line

Keep the back fist as a snapping forms and close-range tool—not as a secret Olympic punch.
