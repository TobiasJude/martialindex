What Is IBJJF Rules Overview?

IBJJF competition is a points-and-submission sport emphasizing positional hierarchy. Public explainers and published books commonly describe two-point actions (such as takedowns/sweeps/knee-on-belly), three-point guard passes, and four-point mount/back—each tied to control/stabilization concepts. Treat any number you memorize as provisional until you confirm the current book.

Strategic literacy: guard pulls are common because bottom guard can still score sweeps; takedowns pay when criteria are met. Illegal techniques vary by belt/age.

ADCC contrast (conceptual): submission-oriented no-gi with different point/advantage cultures and broader legal submissions (e.g., heel hooks in many ADCC contexts). Link via your existing heel-hook and takedown study—not via invented ADCC page claims. See heel hook legality notes on that page.

How IBJJF Rules Overview Works

Before a tournament: download the current IBJJF rules PDF from official channels, read scoring, fouls, and your belt’s illegal list.

Watch a qualified rules explainer, then re-check the PDF for anything that sounded absolute.

In training, practice stabilizing positions for the published count rather than bouncing through.

Coach Q&A beats internet arguments.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

CRITICAL: This page is a high-level overview for orientation. It is not legal advice and not a substitute for the official IBJJF Rules Book. Advantage definitions, penalty ladders, illegal move lists, guard-pull language, and point values must be confirmed in the current publication for your event date. When in doubt, ask the event organizer.

Safety

Rules literacy prevents fouls that injure: know what is illegal in your division before cranking it in the gym “because ADCC allows it.”

Common mistakes

  • Memorizing a blog as gospel.
  • Mixing ADCC legality into IBJJF matches.
  • Not stabilizing positions when your book requires it.
  • Ignoring belt-specific illegal lists.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is this the official rulebook?

No—verify the current IBJJF PDF.

What usually scores?

Positional actions like takedowns, sweeps, passes, mount, back—confirm values and stabilization.

Are guard pulls legal?

Generally with conditions—read the current book.

How is ADCC different?

Different federation, scoring culture, and often legal submissions—study ADCC’s own rules.

Do advantages still exist?

Check the current IBJJF publication for your date.

Belt matters for illegals?

Yes—matrices differ; verify yours.

The Bottom Line

IBJJF rules shape how sport BJJ is played—but only the live rulebook is authoritative. Use this overview for orientation, verify everything, and contrast ADCC incentives without mixing the books.
