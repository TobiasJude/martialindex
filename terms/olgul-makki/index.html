Olgul Makki (High Block)

Olgul makki is the high block: rise the forearm above the head line—completing the triad with Arae Makki (Low Block) and Momtong Makki (Middle Block).

What Is Olgul Makki (High Block)?

Olgul makki (얼굴막기) is the high/rising block covering against downward or high-line attacks in fundamentals.

It is ubiquitous in early patterns and basics tests.

Compare with Arae Makki (Low Block): opposite vertical job.

Like other classical blocks, sport sparring uses it less literally than footwork and checks.

Martial Index files Olgul Makki (High Block) under Taekwondo so searchers get definition, mechanics, WT/ITF context, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

How Olgul Makki (High Block) Works

Chamber low or across per school, then rise the forearm to a roof-like finish above and slightly in front of the forehead, elbow bent appropriately.

Do not smash your own skull with a vertical forearm crash—angle matters.

Stance settles; reaction hand pulls.

Eyes look under/through the block line, not at the floor.

Mechanically, successful olgul makki (high block) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, olgul makki (high block) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill olgul makki (high block) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Basics: high block in forward stance down the floor.

Pattern: opening high blocks that set the tone for precision.

Fight picture: olgul makki (high block) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply olgul makki (high block) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Forearm too flat or too vertical.
  • Shoulder jammed into ear.
  • Blocking behind the head.
  • Weak reaction hand.
  • Skipping chamber.
  • Treating olgul makki (high block) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick olgul makki (high block) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so olgul makki (high block) connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether olgul makki (high block) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, axe kicks, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe olgul makki (high block) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put olgul makki (high block) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Olgul meaning?

Face/head-line section in common teaching language.

Same as rising block?

Yes—English often says high or rising block.

After this in the triad?

Cycle back to Arae Makki (Low Block) for complete literacy.

Sparring?

Conceptual roof defense; practical sport defense differs.

Common test fail?

Finish position too low (becomes a middle block).

The Bottom Line

Make olgul makki a true roof—not a lazy middle block wearing a high-block name.
