Granby Roll

The granby roll is an inversion escape that rolls over the shoulder from turtle (or under rear bodylock pressure) to reclaim guard or scramble—cousin to other turtle escapes.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Tuck chin, roll diagonally over the near shoulder, catch legs as you come through.
  • Pillars: turtle and back control. Cluster with turtle escape and back escape.
  • Works best when their hips are light; heavy hips need a push-off or different escape.
  • Gi waist grips and hooks change timing; no-gi bodylocks demand sharper inversion.
  • Neck safety first—never force a granby under a locked choke.

What Is Granby Roll?

The granby roll is a wrestling-derived inversion: from turtle or a seated/bodylock back situation, you roll over one shoulder, invert underneath, and recover guard or reverse orientation before hooks fully settle.

It is a high-mobility escape in the turtle/back cluster—useful, athletic, and easy to mistime into side control if their weight is heavy on the hips.

How Granby Roll Works

Look through the legs, drop the near shoulder, tuck the chin, and roll diagonally—not straight back. As you invert, open the legs to catch them in guard or create a scramble.

Timing. Prefer moments when knees are on the mat or hips are light. If they are riding heavy, push with the toes to unload first or choose another escape.

Top. Sprawl heavy, follow the roll, keep waist control, and hunt front headlocks if the head stays available.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Belt and pants grips can glue you in turtle and slow the roll; collar threats (clock choke) punish slow granbys. Clear grips or invert before the clock sets.

No-Gi. Bodylocks and seatbelts replace belt grips; head granby variations appear against rear bodylocks in ADCC-style rules.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Turtle with light hip pressure: look away, roll near shoulder, land in closed or open guard. Against a rear bodylock, a head granby can dump them and reverse orientation.

If the roll stalls mid-invert, protect the neck and fight to a tight turtle or stand—do not leave the throat exposed.

Types / Variations

Turtle granby to guard

Classic shoulder roll from turtle into guard recovery.

Head granby vs bodylock

Head-led inversion to escape rear bodylock without shoulders crashing.

Granby scramble

Incomplete invert that becomes a scramble to stand or reverse.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: turtle after a failed pass, sprawled turtles, rear bodylock without hooks, light hip rides.

Counters: heavy hips, waist lock, follow the roll into mount/side, clock choke (gi), or seatbelt + hooks.

Chain with sit-outs, rolls to guard, and standing escapes when granby is stuffed.

Granby Roll vs Basic Turtle Escape

Factor Granby Roll Basic Turtle Guard Recovery
Athleticism Higher inversion demand Simpler roll/sit options
Best vs Light hips / bodylock Varied turtle pressure
Failure Stuck mid-roll → side control Slower but often safer
Cluster Turtle + back Turtle escape hub

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal movement. In gi, be aware of collar chokes (clock choke) while turtling. In ADCC-style no-gi, granbys appear often against bodylocks—train neck safety.

Safety

Tuck the chin; do not roll on a straight neck. Stop drilling if cervical discomfort appears. Never granby under a finishing choke.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling straight back instead of diagonally.
  • Inverting under heavy hip weight without unloading.
  • Leaving the chin up.
  • Forcing granby when a sit-out or stand is available.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a granby roll in BJJ?

An inversion over the shoulder from turtle or back pressure used to recover guard or scramble.

When should I not granby?

When hips are heavy, a choke is locked, or a simpler sit-out/stand is available.

Is granby only from turtle?

Turtle is common, but head granbys also escape rear bodylocks and some back situations.

Gi vs no-gi?

Gi adds belt/pants grips and clock-choke danger; no-gi emphasizes bodylocks and freer inversions.

How do I practice safely?

Solo shoulder rolls, then cooperative turtle granbys with light pressure before live resistance.

What if they follow the roll?

Expect the scramble—protect neck, fight grips, and be ready to turtle tight or stand.

Related escapes?

See turtle escape and back escape for non-inversion options.

The Bottom Line

The granby roll is inversion as escape: diagonal shoulder roll when hips are light, chin tucked, legs ready to catch. Mistime it under heavy weight and you gift side control.
