X-Guard

X-guard is an open-guard entanglement that forms an X under the opponent's standing or posted base—used to off-balance, sweep, or expose the back, often entered from butterfly and connected to single-leg X.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Legs in an X under their base: classic X-guard.
  • Primary jobs: sweep standing passers or take the back.
  • Works in gi and no-gi; pants help in gi, ankles in no-gi.
  • Often entered from butterfly when they post a leg.
  • Collapses naturally into SLX.

What Is X-Guard?

X-guard is an open guard entanglement where your legs form an X under the opponent's standing (or posted) base—typically one shin behind their knee/thigh and the other across—so you can off-balance, sweep, or take the back. Marcelo Garcia popularized it from butterfly entries.

How X-Guard Works

From under their hips you control a leg and the far ankle/hip, then extend and twist the X to dump them forward, backward, or into back exposure. It is a dominant sweeping platform against standing passers who leave a long leg.

Gi vs No-Gi

Both. X-guard is healthy in gi and no-gi. Gi adds pant grips on the far leg; no-gi uses ankle and hip control. Heel-hook connections are more emphasized in no-gi rule sets that allow them.

What Does X-Guard Look Like in Practice?

From butterfly, Jordan posts a long right leg. Alex slides into X-guard, controls the far ankle, extends the X, and sweeps Jordan backward into mount—or comes up on a single leg.

Types / Variations

Classic X-guard

Full X under the hips with far-leg control.

X to back

Off-balance that exposes the back instead of a flat sweep.

X to SLX

Collapsing into single-leg X when they free a hook.

Ashi / entangle links

Modern no-gi paths into outside/inside ashi depending on rules.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. Butterfly elevation, failed standing passes, DLR when they step in, SLX expansions.

Passes / counters from top. Back out of the X, knee cut if hooks loosen, smash the bottom shoulder, extract the far leg.

X-Guard vs Single-Leg X

Factor X-Guard Single-Leg X
Legs involved Full X under the hips Entangles primarily one leg
Feel More sweeping platform More entanglement / entry hub
Common path From butterfly vs standing From RDLR, seated, or collapsed X

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

X-guard is legal. Know reaping and heel-hook rules for your event when you connect to modern ashi configurations.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Control the fall on sweeps. Tap early to heel hooks if your room allows them from X/SLX.

Common mistakes

  • Entering X without far-leg control.
  • Letting them extract the far foot.
  • Forcing X against a kneeling passer with no elevation.
  • Ignoring SLX when the X collapses.
  • Sweeping without coming up—landing back in their guard.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

How do I enter X-guard?

Most commonly from butterfly when they post a long leg—elevate, slide under the hips, and lock the X with far-leg control.

X-guard or SLX?

X when you have both hooks under the hips for sweeping; SLX when you are entangled on one leg for entries and modern ashi play.

Does X-guard work in the gi?

Yes—add pant grips on the far leg. The skeleton is the same as no-gi.

Why do I get stacked in X?

You lost far-leg control or let them put both hands on the mat over your head. Off-balance early and keep their posture broken.

Can I heel hook from X?

In rule sets that allow it, X/SLX families connect to heel hooks—but only train that under coaches who teach safe leg locks.

What if they kneel?

X is weaker against a tight kneeling passer. Return to butterfly or half rather than forcing X.

The Bottom Line

X-guard puts an X under a standing base to sweep or take the back—Marcelo's elevation sequel. Connect to SLX and enter from butterfly.
