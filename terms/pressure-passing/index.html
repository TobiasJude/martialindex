Pressure Passing

Pressure passing is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu guard-passing style that prioritizes heavy connection, chest-to-chest or chest-to-shin smash, and slow clearing of the legs—over-under, smash pass, and stack families live here.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Glue to the hips/shins—do not hover and dance.
  • Classic tools: smash pass, over-under, stack, body pressure knee cuts.
  • Works for all sizes when connection is honest; lighter athletes still succeed with angles.
  • Soft chests get inverted—keep posture inside the smash.
  • Compare: speed passing; parent: guard passing.

What Is Pressure Passing?

Pressure passing is the close-range branch of guard passing that uses body weight, tight connection, and smash mechanics to immobilize the bottom player's hips and clear the legs. It contrasts with circling speed passes that stay more upright and mobile.

How Pressure Passing Works

You remove space: chest on shins or torso, underhooks or overhooks as needed, and incremental hip advancement. The bottom player's frames collapse under sustained weight, opening smash passes, over-unders, and stacks. Stabilization still matters—pressure without a pin is just a long scramble.

Bottom player's job. Create frames early, invert or hip-escape before the glue sets, and fight underhooks.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel and pants grips amplify smash control; stacks with collar grips are common.

No-gi. Underhooks, head position, and raw chest pressure replace cloth; body-lock hybrids blur into connection passing.

MMA context. Pressure passing limits striking space from bottom while advancing position.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex forces Jordan's knees together, drops chest pressure, and walks to side control via a smash pass.

Gi. Alex grips pants to pin both ankles.

No-gi. Alex uses an underhook and head drive without pants grips.

Types / Variations

Smash pressure

Fold the legs and chest-smash through—see smash pass.

Over-under pressure

Overhook one leg, underhook the other—classic pressure path (over-under pass).

Stack pressure

Drive their knees toward their face and circle—see stack pass.

Pressure knee cuts

Knee cut with heavy crossface and hip connection rather than a light slice.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From combat base; after stuffing sweeps; when speed passes fail and you need to glue.

Counters. Early frames, underhook races, inversion, and forcing the passer upright again.

Common failure. Chest too high—giving inversion space.

Pressure vs Speed Passing

Factor Pressure Speed
Distance Close / glued More upright / circling
Tempo Slow grind Fast redirects
Athlete bias Often heavier or strong connectors Often agile / grip-heavy
Classic passes Smash, over-under, stack Toreando, leg drag, long step

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Pressure stalls can draw referee warnings—advance or attack. Passes score when stabilized.

Safety

Stacks can crank necks—apply gradually. Let partners breathe in training smash positions.

Common mistakes

  • Hovering instead of connecting.
  • Pressure without a clearing path.
  • Ignoring underhook fights.
  • Gas tank mismanagement—holding smash with no advance.
  • In no-gi: hunting lapels that are not there.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is pressure passing?

A close-range guard-passing style that uses heavy connection and smash mechanics to clear the legs.

Is pressure passing only for heavyweights?

No. Connection and angles matter more than mass—light athletes pressure-pass successfully worldwide.

Pressure passing vs speed passing?

Pressure glues and grinds; speed circles and redirects. Most high-level games mix both.

Best pressure passes to learn first?

A basic smash pass, an over-under, and a pressure knee cut cover a lot of gyms.

How do I stop pressure passers?

Early frames, underhooks, and creating space before the chest glues—see guard retention.

Does pressure passing work in no-gi?

Yes—body locks and underhooks often replace pants grips.

Why am I exhausted when I pressure pass?

You may be muscling without skeletal alignment. Relax into bone structure and advance in small increments.

The Bottom Line

Pressure passing clears guard by gluing and smashing—not by racing around. Pair it with speed and connection tools.
