Worm Guard

Worm guard is a gi-only open-guard system that feeds a lapel around the opponent's lead leg—using the jacket as a tangle to stall knee cuts and create sweeps and back takes, as popularized by Keenan Cornelius.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Lapel wrapped around their lead leg = worm guard.
  • Gi-only; no true no-gi version.
  • Stalls knee cuts; sets sweeps and back takes.
  • Related to other lapel guards (squid, ringworm, etc.).
  • Compare with lasso: leg wrap vs arm wrap.

What Is Worm Guard?

Worm guard is a gi-only open guard popularized by Keenan Cornelius in which you feed a lapel around the opponent's lead leg (and related structures) so the jacket itself becomes a tangle—blocking knee cuts and creating sweep and back-take paths. It sits among modern lapel guards alongside squid, ringworm, and other Lapel Encyclopedia cousins.

How Worm Guard Works

You grip and feed a lapel under/around their leg, then use hooks (often deep De La Riva flavors) and the far sleeve or pants to off-balance. The lapel prevents simple knee-slice paths because their own jacket pins the leg line. Sweeps, berimbolo-adjacent back takes, and transitions into other lapel guards follow when they try to clear the wrap.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi only. Worm guard requires a jacket lapel. There is no true no-gi worm guard—without fabric the mechanic vanishes.

No-gi substitute. Use DLR/RDLR, SLX, and butterfly without pretending a rashguard is a lapel.

Gi meta. Still a high-level IBJJF tool; passers study over-under and specific lapel clears to answer it.

What Does Worm Guard Look Like in Practice?

Alex pulls guard, feeds Jordan's left lapel around Jordan's right lead leg, establishes a deep hook, and off-balances Jordan into a sweep when Jordan tries to knee cut—the lapel stalls the cut long enough for the sweep to land.

Types / Variations

Standard worm

Lapel around the lead leg with supporting hooks/grips.

Deep DLR worm

Combines deep De La Riva hook with the lapel wrap for back takes.

Lapel cousins

Squid, ringworm, gubber, and related Keenan-system guards—siblings, not synonyms.

Worm to sleeve games

Transitions into lasso / spider when lapels fail or sleeves appear.

Entries, Counters, and Passes

Entries. From open guard and De La Riva when a lapel is free; from failed passes where you retain sleeves/lapels; deliberate lapel feeds from seated guard.

Passes. Clear or free the lapel early, pressure with over-under styles, force grip breaks, avoid knee cutting blindly into the wrap.

Counters. Re-feed the lapel, switch sides, or abandon to DLR/lasso if the jacket is lost.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Worm guard is legal in IBJJF gi. It does not exist in no-gi. Maintain action—endless lapel nesting without a sweep can draw stalling attention.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Lapel wraps can torque knees if cranked recklessly—apply sweeps with control and communicate with partners.

Common mistakes

  • Feeding a lapel with no hook—easy to pass.
  • Trying worm concepts in no-gi.
  • Knee cutting into a live worm as the passer.
  • Forgetting sleeve/pants secondary grips.
  • Camping in worm without a sweep or back-take plan.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Does worm guard work in no-gi?

No. It needs a lapel. Use DLR, SLX, and butterfly instead.

How is worm different from lasso?

Worm wraps a lapel around their leg; lasso wraps your leg around their sleeved arm. Both are gi tangles aimed at different limbs.

Who invented worm guard?

Keenan Cornelius popularized and systematized worm guard in modern competition and instructionals.

How do people pass worm guard?

Free or clear the lapel early, avoid blind knee cuts into the wrap, and use pressure passing that addresses the jacket tangle.

Do I need deep De La Riva with worm?

Deep DLR hooks pair well for back takes, but basic worm with solid secondary grips already stalls many passers.

Is worm guard still relevant?

Yes in high-level gi. Passers have answers, so you need sweeps and transitions—not a single lapel trick.

The Bottom Line

Worm guard feeds a lapel around the lead leg to stall passes and sweep—Keenan's gi puzzle with no no-gi twin. See lasso for arm wraps and De La Riva for the hook chassis underneath.
