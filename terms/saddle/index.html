Saddle (Inside Sankaku)

The saddle (also inside sankaku or cross ashi) is a dominant inside leg entanglement that triangles one of the opponent’s legs while your inside leg crosses their hip line—primary home of the inside heel hook. Closely related naming: honey hole and 411.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Saddle ≈ inside sankaku ≈ cross ashi; honeyhole/411 are closely related / often synonymous labels.
  • Pinch the trapped knee, control the far hip, then expose the heel.
  • Double-trouble (both legs controlled) upgrades finishing percentage.
  • IBJJF vs ADCC changes whether this is a primary A-game—verify rules.
  • Cross-link sibling pages; do not treat names as unrelated positions.

What Is Saddle (Inside Sankaku)?

In Danaher/no-gi jargon, the saddle is the inside triangle entanglement around one leg with a cross-body inside hook—immobilizing the hip so inside heel hooks become high percentage. Japanese-influenced coaches may say inside sankaku; older 10th Planet circles may say honey hole or 411 for very similar geometry.

Martial Index keeps separate entries to capture search intent and light naming history, but mechanics overlap heavily—read honey hole and 411 as sibling aliases, not alien positions.

How Saddle (Inside Sankaku) Works

From outside or inside ashi, thread your outside leg through to form the figure-four triangle on their trapped leg while your inside foot hooks past their far hip. Squeeze knees, flatten their trapped knee toward your chest, and secure the secondary leg when possible (double trouble).

Hand fight to expose the heel, connect your wrist bones, and rotate through the knee line as a unit—never yank only with arms.

If they boot-scoot or invert, ride the entanglement; do not abandon structure to chase a foot.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Often constrained by heel-hook/reaping rules—use for control literacy and legal finishes only as the book allows.

No-gi. Signature ADCC-era position for inside heel hooks.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Quinn enters via false-reap style mechanics, locks the saddle, controls the far hip, and finishes an inside heel hook under no-gi rules.

In an IBJJF gi week, Quinn drills the same entry but stops at legal control/ankle-lock angles—rulebook first.

Types / Variations

Top saddle

You sit more above—strong hip pressure.

Bottom saddle

Inverted or underneath variations—retention & counters matter.

Double-trouble saddle

Both legs controlled—peak finishing environment.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: outside ashi transitions, SLX backsteps, K-guard, turtle rolls. Counters: extract heel, stand carefully, counter to 50/50. Escapes: hide the heel, clear the cross hook, scramble out before the triangle seals.

Saddle vs Outside Ashigarami

Factor Saddle (inside) Outside ashigarami
Primary heel hook Inside Outside (often)
Triangle lock Inside sankaku figure-four Usually no inside triangle
Control feel Very sticky More dynamic
Naming cousins Honeyhole / 411 Outside sankaku debates

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. See reaping, heel hook, 411.

Safety

Inside heel hooks torque knees rapidly. Tap at the first rotational threat; never “tough out” saddle finishes.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every ashi “the saddle.”
  • Finishing with arms only—no hip unity.
  • Ignoring secondary-leg control.
  • Running saddle A-games in restricted IBJJF gi without a legal plan.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is saddle the same as honeyhole?

Often yes in gym speech—closely related/synonymous inside entanglement. We keep separate SEO entries with cross-links.

Why “inside sankaku”?

Sankaku = triangle; inside refers to the triangle configuration on the leg.

Best finish?

Inside heel hook when legal; otherwise legal ankle locks / positional wins per rules.

How to enter?

Commonly from outside ashi or SLX-family transitions—coach detail matters.

Safe for hobbyists?

Only with clear consent and slow rotational drilling.

Gi competition?

Verify heel-hook and reaping legality for your division.

Read next?

Honey hole for naming/finish culture; 411 for synonymy + IBJJF notes.

The Bottom Line

Saddle is the modern inside entanglement: triangle the leg, cross the hip, finish inside when rules allow. Treat honey hole and 411 as naming siblings, not separate religions.
