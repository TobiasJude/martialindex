Toe Hold

A toe hold is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu foot lock that uses a kimura-style grip on the foot to twist and fold the toes/foot—common from ashi, 50/50, and truck transitions, with belt/rule limits to verify.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Kimura on the foot: push and pull together.
  • Grip near the pinky-toe line—not too low on the ankle.
  • Knees above their knee line to stop spirals.
  • Often legal later than ankle locks / restricted vs heel hooks—verify.
  • Chains with ankle lock and outside heel threats.

What Is Toe Hold?

The toe hold applies rotational and folding pressure through the foot using a figure-four (kimura) grip: one hand on the foot near the toes, the other under the Achilles/heel line, then coordinated torque. It can attack foot structures and, poorly applied, dump force into the knee—control matters.

See also straight ankle lock and heel hook.

How Toe Hold Works

From ashi or secondary entanglement, secure the foot, set the kimura grip with middle finger near pinky toe, suck the elbow, and finish with simultaneous push on the toes and pull under the Achilles while driving hips into the leg.

Keep your knees covering their knee line so they cannot spiral free.

If the foot slips, reconnect or switch to ankle lock/heel hook per rules.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips help isolate the foot before the kimura.

No-Gi. Fast foot fighting; sticky ashi control is everything.

Legality is belt/organization dependent—check before you hunt in IBJJF brackets.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In ashi, Alex seats a kimura on Jordan’s foot, drives hips in, and finishes the toe hold as Jordan tries to turn out.

Jordan shows the heel for a hook; under ADCC-style rules Alex might switch—under many IBJJF gi rules Alex stays on toe hold/ankle.

Types / Variations

Ashi toe hold

Standard teaching finish.

50/50 and secondary

When mutual entanglements expose the foot.

Truck / back-step toes

Secondary from truck family transitions.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: ashi, SLX, 50/50, truck to foot exposures.

Counters: hide the foot, keep toes flexed protectively, clear the kimura early.

Escapes: spiral carefully without dumping your own knee—tap early.

Toe Hold vs Straight Ankle Lock

Factor Toe Hold Straight Ankle Lock
Grip Kimura on foot Forearm bite on Achilles
Primary feel Twist/fold foot Compress Achilles
IBJJF pattern Often later belts Often earlier pathway
Chain partner Heel hook (rules allowing) Heel hook / belly-down

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Toe holds are frequently limited by belt in IBJJF-style competition—verify the current IBJJF rulebook. They are distinct from heel hooks in how referees categorize them; do not assume interchangeability.

Safety

Easy to injure feet and knees if you rip. Slow finishes; stop on tap immediately.

Common mistakes

  • Gripping too low so the foot slips.
  • Only pushing toes without the pull.
  • Leaving knee line open.
  • Using toe holds illegal for your belt.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a toe hold?

A kimura-style foot lock twisting/folding the foot.

Legal for blue belts?

Often not in IBJJF—check current rules for your belt.

Vs estima lock?

Related foot attacks; Estima is a distinct sock-lock geometry.

Gi or no-gi?

Both.

Main detail?

Push and pull together with correct finger line.

Defense?

Hide toes; break the kimura early.

Chain to heel hook?

When rules allow and the heel seats.

The Bottom Line

Toe hold is the kimura of the foot: pinky-line grip, push-pull, cover the knee line. Verify legality and keep ankle lock / heel hook in the same conversation.
