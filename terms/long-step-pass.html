Long Step Pass

The long step pass is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu open-guard pass that uses a long outside step past the bottom player's hooks—often with sleeve control—to clear the legs and stabilize side control, famously developed in early-2000s and AOJ lineages.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Long outside step + grip control—not a tiny shuffle.
  • Keeps you past hooks before they reattach.
  • Gi sleeves are classic; no-gi needs wrist/shin substitutes.
  • Stepping without posture invites sweeps—stay connected.
  • Family: speed passing; cousins: leg drag, toreando.

What Is Long Step Pass?

The long step pass clears open-guard hooks by taking a deliberately long step outside and past the legs while controlling grips, then dropping into a pin. It is a cornerstone of modern speed-passing systems associated with Art of Jiu Jitsu and earlier longstep innovators. It sits inside the broader guard passing family.

How Long Step Pass Works

Establish sleeve or wrist control, create an angle, step the far leg long past their near hook, and clear the second hook as you turn toward side control. Keep posture so butterfly and DLR sweeps cannot elevate you.

Bottom player's job. Shorten the step with frames, re-hook the stepping leg, and attack the posture with sweeps.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Dual sleeve control is the classic long-step engine.

No-gi. Harder—use wrist rides, shin staples, and hybrid redirects.

MMA context. Respect upkicks while stepping outside.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex controls both sleeves, angles off Jordan's hooks, takes a long outside step past the near leg, and settles to side control.

Gi. Sleeves freeze Jordan's hands throughout.

No-gi. Alex substitutes wrist control and a shin pin.

Types / Variations

Classic long step

Outside long step from standing vs open guard.

From reverse DLR / lasso

Clear reverse hooks with the long step geometry.

Chaining to knee cut / leg drag

If the long step stalls, redirect into related passes.

Stabilizing the pass

Hip connection after the step—pass is not done mid-air.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Standing vs seated/supine open guard with sleeve dominance.

Counters. Frames on the hip, re-hooking the stepping leg, forcing you back to the centerline.

Failure mode. Tiny steps that never clear hooks.

Long Step vs Leg Drag

Factor Long Step Leg Drag
Primary action Long outside step Drag leg across + pin
Grip bias Sleeves classic Pants/sleeve or shin staple
Feel Clear past hooks Pin then clear
Back path Less automatic More when they turn

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Stabilize for pass points.

Safety

Do not plant awkwardly on a partner's knee line while stepping. Control the descent.

Common mistakes

  • Short steps that never clear.
  • No grip control—empty athleticism.
  • Losing posture to butterfly.
  • In no-gi: copying sleeve patterns with nothing in the hands.
  • Not connecting to the hips after the step.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the long step pass?

An open-guard pass using a long outside step past the hooks while controlling grips, then stabilizing a pin.

Who popularized the long step?

Early-2000s innovators and later Art of Jiu Jitsu systems made longstep mechanics famous at the highest levels.

Long step vs toreando?

Long step emphasizes a committed outside step past hooks; toreando emphasizes continuous circling redirects.

Does long step work in no-gi?

Yes with adapted controls, but sleeve-driven versions are harder—expect hybrids.

How do you defend the long step?

Frame the hip, re-hook the stepping leg, and break their posture before the step lands—see guard retention.

Can beginners learn long step?

Yes—start with sleeve control and exaggerated step length in positional drills.

What if the long step fails?

Chain into toreando, leg drag, or knee cut rather than freezing mid-step.

The Bottom Line

The long step pass clears open-guard hooks with a committed outside step and grip control—then stabilize. See speed passing and leg drag.
