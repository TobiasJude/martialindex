Disqualification

Disqualification ends a match because of a serious rules violation. Exact foul ladders live in the current IBJJF or ADCC books—this page stays high-level.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • DQ is a referee decision that awards the match to the opponent after a serious foul.
  • Foul severity and accumulation rules vary by organization—verify the current book.
  • Related tools include warnings and penalties before a full DQ in some systems.
  • Know legal techniques for your belt—see IBJJF illegal moves.
  • High-level only: not a complete foul list.

What Is Disqualification?

Disqualification (DQ) is a match-ending referee action when an athlete commits a foul the rulebook treats as severe—or accumulates fouls to a terminal threshold under that organization’s system.

How It Works

Referees may warn, penalize, or DQ depending on the published ladder. Athletes should understand serious misconduct, illegal techniques for their division, and unsportsmanlike conduct categories in the current book.

Gi vs No-Gi

Uniform fouls differ (gi grips vs skin-level attire), but the DQ concept—ending the match for a foul—remains. Always read the format you entered.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

An athlete applies a technique illegal for their belt; the referee stops the match under current guidance and signals DQ—study ahead so this is not your highlight.

Types / Variations

Common patterns

Technical DQ for illegal attacks; conduct DQ; medical/coach-related stoppages that events categorize differently.

Gym / sport variants

Local shows may use house rules. Majors stick closer to federation books.

Beginner notes

Ask your coach for a pre-comp rules brief. Nervous athletes often foul by accident—slow hands on illegal entries.

How to Apply / Practical Tips

Build a legal A-game. Tell your corner to shout legal reminders. If warned, adapt immediately.

How It Compares

Different from losing on points or submission. Related to—but not identical with—penalty accumulation; see penalties.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Verify current rulebook. Martial Index does not invent a complete DQ foul table. Use official IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules.

Safety

Many DQ-worthy actions are restricted because they endanger opponents—respect that intent in training too.

Common mistakes

Arguing instead of adapting; copying social-media “legal” claims; ignoring belt-gated legality.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What causes disqualification in BJJ?

Serious rule violations as defined by the event’s current rulebook—details vary.

Is every illegal move an instant DQ?

Not always—some systems use warnings/penalties first; verify the book.

Where do I verify?

Current IBJJF or ADCC (or promotion) official rules for your event.

DQ vs penalties?

Penalties may accumulate; DQ ends the match—see penalties page.

Can my corner get me DQ’d?

Coach conduct rules exist in many books—corners should know them.

Does this page list every foul?

No—high-level only by design.

Prevention tip?

Legal A-game + rules homework the week of the event.

The Bottom Line

Avoid disqualification the boring way: verify the current rulebook, keep a legal A-game, and adapt the moment a referee warns you.
