Standing Pass

A standing pass uses the feet to break and pass closed guard—winning posture, opening the ankles, then choosing toreando, knee-cut, or stack. See breaking / opening closed guard, combat base, and guard passing.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Stand with base—don’t get your ankles picked.
  • Posture and grip control before the break.
  • After open: toreando, slash, knee-cut, or stack.
  • Combat base is the seated cousin.
  • Pillar: guard passing.

What Is Standing Pass?

Standing is both a break method and a pass platform. Many white-belt curricula teach stand-up open + outside pass as the first reliable closed-guard answer.

It belongs under guard passing and tightly cross-links the closed-guard break concepts in this batch.

Compare kneeling pressure paths and combat base when standing is risky.

How Standing Pass Works

Inside closed guard, control sleeves/wrists, posture, plant a foot, and stand with wide base.

Open the ankles with a knee drive or step-back stretch, then immediately pass—hesitation invites open-guard attacks.

Favorite finishes: bullfighter, slash, knee cut.

If they threaten a sit-up sweep while you stand, return to combat base or re-posture rather than hopping on one foot.

Timing cue: the moment the ankles unlock is the most dangerous second for both athletes—bottom can triangle a bent-over passer; top can tearando a flat bottom player. Train the stand-break-pass as one continuous phrase.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar control make standing safer; pant grips help the break.

No-gi. Wrist control and hand fighting matter; expect more ankle picks and technical stand-ups from bottom.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex stands in base, opens closed guard, and bullfighters to side control.

Jordan stands, feels a sweep coming, drops to combat base, then knee-pulls open.

Types / Variations

Stand to toreando / slash

Outside finish after the break.

Stand to knee-cut

Headquarters after opening.

Stand to stack

When they keep feet high.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from any closed-guard top. Counters: ankle picks, triangle when posture breaks, butterfly as you stand. Escapes for bottom: open first and attack before the pass starts.

Standing Pass Platform vs Combat Base

Factor Standing platform Combat base platform
Height On the feet One knee up
Break style Stretch / step-back Knee-pull friendly
Risk Ankle attacks Less mobile sometimes
Related opening-closed-guard combat-base

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Standing to pass is legal; jumping guard rules are a separate topic—verify current book if relevant to your division.

Safety

Protect your own knees when they triangle or ankle-pick during the stand-up.

Common mistakes

  • Standing with feet together.
  • Looking down and losing posture.
  • Opening then freezing.
  • No pass plan after the break.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a standing pass?

A closed-guard break and pass approach that uses a standing base.

Is it only toreando?

No—standing is the platform; finishes vary.

Safer than stacking?

Different risks—ankle attacks vs neck pressure.

Gi tip?

Control sleeves before you stand.

No-gi tip?

Wide base and wrist control.

When to use combat base instead?

When standing feels unstable or ankle picks are live.

Pillar?

Guard passing.

The Bottom Line

Standing pass is a platform: posture, open, then choose an outside or pressure finish under guard passing.
