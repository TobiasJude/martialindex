BJJ · Concept

Frames

Frames are skeletal structures—forearms, shins, knees—that create space and stop weight from smashing you flat, bridging half guard, frame escapes, and guard retention.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Bones beat muscles for space.
  • Layer frames: legs first, then arms, then head.
  • Frames buy time for hip escapes—not forever.
  • Cross-cluster: half guard, side control, mount escapes.
  • Pair with shrimp and posture resets.

What Is Frames?

A frame is a rigid line that transfers an opponent’s weight into the mat (or away from your chest) so your hips can move. Classic examples: forearm across the hip in side control, knee-shield in half guard, shin across the biceps in open-guard retention.

Frames are not pushes. Pushing with soft elbows collapses; framing with stacked shoulder-elbow-wrist (or hip-knee-shin) holds. See weight distribution from the top side—you are denying their ideal load path.

As a bridge concept, frames appear in frame escape, knee shield, Z-guard, and every retention layer before a pass becomes side control.

How Frames Works

Build frames early—before the crossface lands. Orient on your side when possible so the top player’s weight hits the frame instead of your diaphragm.

Stack joints: wrist under elbow under shoulder. On legs, keep the knee inside the hip line for shields.

Convert: frame → shrimp/hip escape → insert knee → recover guard or stand. Frames without hip movement only delay the smash.

Drill: partner applies slow side-control pressure; you cycle elbow-knee frames into shrimp recoveries for timed rounds.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel frames and sleeve posts add friction; beware grips that turn your frame into their handle.

No-Gi. Pure bone frames and head position; sweat makes soft posts fail faster.

Same skeleton logic both ways.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Bottom half guard, Alex’s knee shield and forearm frame stop the crossface long enough to underhook and come up.

Side control, Alex’s inside forearm at the hip plus a near-side elbow frame creates the first inch for a shrimp.

Open guard retention, Alex’s shin across Jordan’s bicep kills the toreando grip path.

Types / Variations

Arm frames

Forearm/shoulder lines against hips, neck, and shoulders.

Leg frames / shields

Knee shield, shin frames, and long-leg posts.

Wedges

Inserting structure into seams (elbow-knee) to pry space.

Retention layers

Frames as mid-layers after hooks fail but before full pass.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: any time top pressure arrives—establish before the flatten.

Counters: opponents walk around frames, peel elbows, or smash through soft posts—re-angle and re-layer.

Escapes: frames into shrimp, technical stand-up, or knee insertion; do not hold a dying frame forever.

Frames vs Connection

Factor Frames Connection
Job Create space / deny weight Remove space / stick chest-to-chest
Typical actor Bottom / retaining Top passer / pressure player
Feel Hard lines, wedges Heavy glue, no daylight
Links Frame escape Connection passing

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Frames themselves are not scored; the escapes and recoveries they enable can lead to sweeps (2) or simply deny pass points (3). Verify current IBJJF/ADCC books for illegal neck pressure if a “frame” becomes a crank.

Safety

Do not drive bony frames into the throat as a shortcut. Train frames as space tools; tap to unexpected neck load.

Common mistakes

  • Soft, bent elbows sold as frames.
  • Framing forever without hip escape.
  • Letting the crossface land before the first frame.
  • Using the head as a lone frame under smash.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a frame in BJJ?

A skeletal structure that creates space and redirects weight so you can move your hips.

Frames vs posts?

Posts often mean hands on the mat for base; frames usually contact the opponent’s body to make space.

Best beginner frames?

Knee shield and forearm-across-hip from side control pressure.

Do frames work in no-gi?

Yes—bone alignment matters more when fabric friction disappears.

Why do my frames collapse?

Elbows bent, shoulders soft, or hips not escaping while you hold.

How do passers beat frames?

Angles, peeling, and smothering soft lines—then you must re-layer.

What should I read next?

Frame escape, pressure, and half-guard pillars.

The Bottom Line

Frames buy the inch that hip escapes need. Build them early, stack the bones, and convert space into guard—not into a static hold.
