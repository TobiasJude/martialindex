Guillotine Defense

Guillotine defense keeps you alive against guillotine attacks from the clinch, guard, and mount. Get the choking hand under control, improve forehead/chest posture, and escape before the high-elbow finishes. Related: air choke / blood choke notes.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Parent: guillotine. Early: both hands fight the choking wrist/elbow.
  • Posture: forehead or chest connection to stop the curl.
  • Escapes differ standing vs guard vs mounted—learn each stage.
  • Mechanics may blend air and blood pressure—tap early.
  • Related: RNC defense, triangle defense.

What Is Guillotine Defense?

Defenses versus guillotine / front-headlock chokes. Up: chokes. Across: anaconda, darce family awareness.

How It Works

Address the choking arm immediately (two-on-one or peel). Keep the head from being curled down—forehead to mat or chest to chest. Then circle, sprawl, or stack depending on whether you are standing, in guard, or mounted.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control helps peel; watch them switch to arm-in variations.

No-gi. Wrist rides and shoulder pressure replace fabric; expect arm-in and high-elbow finishes.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Standing guillotine → control wrist → forehead down → circle to the choking-elbow side → extract head.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standing / clinch escapes.

Competition variation

Closed-guard guillotine escapes (posture + stack/turn).

Related option

Mounted guillotine emergencies (hip escape + hand fight).

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by front headlock entries after shots or posture breaks. Offense answers: high elbow, angle, arm-in switches.

How It Compares

Vs RNC defense: front vs rear. Vs darce/anaconda: different arm paths, similar hand-fight priorities.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal.

Safety

Defense pages prioritize survival: protect neck/joints first, create frames, then escape. Tap early in live training; do not ego through chokes or joint locks. Throat pain means respect air mechanics—tap.

Common mistakes

  • Pulling the head straight up into a tighter curl.
  • Ignoring the choking hand to grab legs.
  • Staying square instead of circling to the elbow side.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is guillotine defense?

Hand fighting, posture, and escapes used against guillotine chokes.

First priority?

Control the choking arm and stop the head from curling down.

Standing vs guard?

Circle/sprawl standing; posture and turn/stack in guard.

Air or blood?

Guillotines can be either or both—see air-choke and blood-choke notes.

Mounted guillotine?

Emergency hand fight plus hip escape; tap early if locked.

Related attacks?

Anaconda and darce often follow failed guillotine scrambles.

Related notes?

Guillotine, chokes, air choke, blood choke, RNC defense.

The Bottom Line

Guillotine defense is front-headlock survival—win the hand fight, fix posture, then exit before the finish lands.
