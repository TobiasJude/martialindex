Collar Sleeve Sweep

The collar sleeve sweep is a classic gi reverse from collar-sleeve guard using cross-collar and sleeve grips with a hip foot and bicep foot—clustered with spider, De La Riva, and collar-sleeve to back.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Collar + sleeve + foot on hip + foot on bicep = the standard platform.
  • Pull collar to break posture; push legs to dump toward the controlled sleeve.
  • Chains to balloon, tripod, and back takes.
  • Top answers: toreando, grip breaks, DLR pass after they plant a hook.
  • Gi-native; no-gi uses wrist/elbow analogs.

What Is Collar Sleeve Sweep?

Collar sleeve sweep is the fundamental open-guard reverse built on cross-collar and sleeve control with one foot on the hip and one on the biceps. It is signature offense from collar-sleeve guard. Up: collar-sleeve, open, spider. Across: scissor, balloon, DLR sweep.

How It Works

Win collar and sleeve, place the sleeve-side foot on the bicep and the collar-side foot on the hip, pull them forward while extending both legs to dump toward the sleeve side, then follow to top.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Native home—fabric grips make the platform sticky.

No-gi. Use wrist rides, elbow control, and shin frames as analogs; true collar is unavailable.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Standing or kneeling passer over collar-sleeve → posture break → push-pull dump → you follow into mount or side.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard hip-foot + bicep-foot collar-sleeve sweep.

Competition variation

Balloon / overhead variation when they are light on the toes.

Related option

Transition into collar-sleeve to back when they post and turn.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From closed guard opening; from failed spider. Counters. Strip grips, toreando, or force them into a passable DLR.

How It Compares

Vs spider: spider emphasizes shin-on-bicep extension; collar-sleeve emphasizes upper-body grips. Vs scissor: scissor often starts closer without the bicep foot.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

IBJJF gi staple; check IBJJF rules for juvenile grip limits.

Safety

Do not yank the neck—pull to break posture, not to crank cervical flexion.

Common mistakes

  • Feet too low (on belly instead of hip/bicep).
  • Sweeping without collar posture break.
  • Failing to follow up to top.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the collar sleeve sweep?

An open-guard reverse using collar and sleeve grips with feet on hip and bicep to dump the passer.

Is it gi-only?

True version is gi; no-gi uses wrist/elbow analogs.

How do you set it up?

Collar + sleeve, hip foot, bicep foot, pull and extend toward the sleeve side.

What should you chain next?

Mount/side, balloon, or collar-sleeve to back.

Common mistake?

Feet placed too low or sweeping without posture break.

Is it beginner-friendly?

Yes—core white-belt open-guard curriculum.

Where does it fit on Martial Index?

Under collar-sleeve guard in the open-guard mesh.

The Bottom Line

Collar sleeve sweep is the textbook open-guard reverse—master the platform, then branch into back takes and spider distance games.
