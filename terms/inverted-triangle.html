Inverted Triangle

The inverted triangle (reverse triangle / gyaku sankaku) is a triangle choke with reversed orientation: your hips and their head do not line up like a guard triangle. It often appears from bottom side control, failed passes, or when their arm is already between your legs.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Opportunistic, not a closed-guard staple—look for the arm stuck between your legs.
  • Turn to your side; a flat back kills the bite (Estima-style details).
  • Parent: triangle choke. Do not confuse with rear triangle (from the back).
  • Can attack from bottom side—rare offense in a bad position.
  • Same figure-four rules: exclude the extra shoulder, cut an angle.

What Is Inverted Triangle?

An inverted / reverse triangle flips the usual picture. In a front triangle from guard, your head is roughly toward theirs. In an inverted triangle you are more parallel or head-toward-hips, with the figure-four still trapping neck plus one arm. Judo’s gyaku-sankaku and BJJ “reverse triangle” point at this geometry.

The highest-percentage opportunistic trigger is bottom side control (or a smashed open guard) when their arm is already between your legs. Estima-family instructionals made the tight reverse triangle a real competition weapon, not a circus trick.

Martial Index keeps it next to side triangle and rear triangle so the sankaku map has all four compass points: front, mount, side, rear, inverted.

How Inverted Triangle Works

If an arm is trapped between your legs, frame their neck and hip, turn onto your side, and throw the far leg over the head. Lock the figure-four tight—Estima’s “shoot past, then lock” beats a lazy clip.

Pinch, pull the head, and walk your hips so the cutting edge is on the neck. The trapped arm should not be allowed to pummel out.

From top, reverse triangles also appear when you pass and they leftover an arm; you can finish or use it as control into an armbar.

If the lock is loose, abandon into a regular front triangle, side triangle, or just escape side control. A sloppy inverted triangle is a gift of your back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control helps keep the trapped arm. Pants grips can help you spin under a passer.

No-gi. Wrist control and timing. Expect it as a scramble finish more than a held position.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex is stuck under side control, traps an arm between the legs, turns to the side, and locks a reverse triangle.

Jordan shoots a reverse triangle during a pass, misses the tight lock, and uses the threat to recover guard.

Types / Variations

Bottom side-control inverted

The Estima-style opportunistic attack.

Passing / open-guard inverted

They leave an arm while passing; you invert the sankaku.

Top inverted / control

Less common finish, useful control into arm attacks.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: arm between the legs under side control, leftover arm during a pass, single-leg scrambles. Counters: extract the arm, keep the head heavy, do not post the second hand into the trap. Escapes: posture the head out before the figure-four bites.

Inverted Triangle vs Rear Triangle

Factor Inverted / reverse Rear triangle
Where you sit Often bottom or beside, head toward hips Behind them on the back
Trigger Arm between the legs / side smash Defended RNC / back control
Search language Reverse, gyaku, inverted Ura, ushiro, rear
Read with Side control escapes + triangle parent Back control + RNC

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult triangle chokes are standard legal submissions. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Inverted triangles can twist necks if you crank instead of cutting an angle. Turn to your side, tap early, and never wrench a stacked partner’s head.

Common mistakes

  • Staying flat on your back while you try to lock.
  • Confusing this with a rear triangle from the back.
  • Locking loose and giving up your own back.
  • Forcing it when the arm is not actually trapped.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Inverted vs reverse triangle?

Same family in most gyms. Some coaches split hairs; we treat them as one search cluster.

Same as rear triangle?

No. Rear is from the back. Inverted is a flipped front/side geometry.

Can white belts use this?

As an opportunistic drill, yes. Do not make it your only side-control escape.

Estima triangle?

Related high-level reverse-triangle teaching. Watch mechanics, do not copy-paste names blindly.

Gi needed?

No. Cloth just helps hold the arm.

What if I miss?

Recover frames and escape side control. The triangle was a bonus, not the only plan.

Legal?

Standard adult triangle—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

Inverted triangle is opportunistic sankaku: trap the arm, turn to your side, lock tight. Distinguish it from the rear triangle and keep the parent triangle choke as the theory hub.
