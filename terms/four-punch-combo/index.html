Four-Punch Combo

Four punches stretch the Three-Punch Combo—classic 1-2-3-2 Combo finishes the hook string on another Cross.

  • Fourth shot often finishes.

What Is Four-Punch Combo?

Category page for length-four strings under Combination. The teaching star is usually 1-2-3-2 Combo.

Also includes double jab + cross + hook, or hook-cross-hook-cross patterns.

Parent: Combination. Compare with Three-Punch Combo and 1-2-3-2 Combo.

How Four-Punch Combo Works

Build from a clean three-punch base, then add the fourth only when breath and guard survive.

Often the fourth shot is the Finishing Punch (hard cross). Earlier shots set.

Exit immediately—four punches invite counters if you stay on the center line.

Mechanically, successful work with Four-Punch Combo is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, four-punch combo lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill four-punch combo daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Beginner tape: one-two-hook-two as in short “how to box” progressions.

Advanced: four punches with a Slip inserted—offense plus defense woven.

Fight picture: four-punch combo appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply four-punch combo after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Gasping after punch three, winging punch four.
  • No foot adjustment mid-string.
  • Ignoring exit angles.
  • Treating four-punch combo as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox four-punch combo inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so four-punch combo connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether four-punch combo appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe four-punch combo as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put four-punch combo behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep four-punch combo scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on four-punch combo, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is 1-2-3-2 the only four-punch?

No, but it is the standard teaching four.

When to use fours in sparring?

When you are already landing threes cleanly and recovering.

Breath tip?

Exhale each punch; never hold through all four.

The Bottom Line

A four-punch combo is earned by a healthy three—not by collecting punches for the bag noise.
