What Is Gyaku-Zuki?

Gyaku-zuki (逆突き) is the reverse punch. In Karate it is the workhorse power hand from Zenkutsu-Dachi, contrasting Oi-Zuki.

Searchers want hip rotation, whether the rear heel stays down, and how it compares to a boxing cross—similar idea, karate chamber and Kime culture differ.

Pairs with Kizami-Zuki setups in Kumite and follows many blocks in kihon.

Martial Index files Gyaku-Zuki under Karate so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Karate style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Gyaku-Zuki behaves in kihon, kata, and kumite.

How Gyaku-Zuki Works

Lead leg forward; rear fist chambers with Hikite on the lead hand. Rotate hips toward the target; extend the rear punch; finish with Kime; recover Kamae.

Rear leg drives; avoid spinning the front foot out of control. Shoulder stays stacked—do not lean the head off-line casually.

From blocks (Soto-Uke, Uchi-Uke, Age-Uke), gyaku-zuki is the standard counter in countless drills.

Mechanically, successful gyaku-zuki work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Karate Stance with Kamae alive.

If distance (maai) is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: punches need Hikite and Kime; kicks need chamber and retract; blocks need a counter path into Gyaku-Zuki or Oi-Zuki.

Dojo vs Competition / Rules Notes

In traditional dojo training, gyaku-zuki lives inside the kihon–kata–kumite triangle—form quality matters as much as fight utility.

Under WKF sport karate, scoring and contact limits reshape how gyaku-zuki appears in matches versus full-contact styles such as Kyokushin.

Gym culture may drill gyaku-zuki daily; grading and competition may shrink the toolkit based on syllabus and ruleset.

Confirm the dojo syllabus and bout agreement before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

WKF scoring values clean mid/high punches; full-contact gyms may emphasize body gyaku differently.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Static gyaku-zuki in zenkutsu—coach watches hip and rear heel.

Kumite: Kizami-Zuki probe, then gyaku-zuki straight down the middle.

Dojo picture: gyaku-zuki appears when Karate Stance and timing say so, then yields to the next technique in a combination or to a reset of Kamae.

Gym test: can you apply gyaku-zuki after a feint or Kizami-Zuki setup and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Chudan gyaku-zuki

Mid-level reverse punch—default power shot in kihon.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives kumite pressure, not only line kihon.

Common Mistakes

  • Arm-only punch with frozen hips.
  • Lifting the rear heel and spinning out.
  • Dropping the lead hand with no hikite purpose.
  • Leaning the head over the punch.
  • No recovery to kamae.
  • Treating gyaku-zuki as a highlight instead of a distance-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down and no hikite.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Line-drill gyaku-zuki inside full kihon sets; return to Karate Stance and Kamae after every attempt.

Pads or makiwara: technical → moving → forced follow-up so gyaku-zuki connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat solo fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film kata and kumite weekly and ask whether gyaku-zuki appears with honest Kime.

Progress intensity with a sensei—especially for kicks, spins, and full-power Oi-Zuki.

Pad gyaku after every block in a 3-minute round—no punch without a prior uke.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe gyaku-zuki as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor maai, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare ryuha, you will see stylistic accents: some put gyaku-zuki behind heavy Kihon repetition; others behind application-minded Kata or sport Kumite. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What does gyaku-zuki mean?

Reverse punch—rear hand vs lead leg.

Gyaku vs oi-zuki?

Reverse vs lunge/same-side stepping punch.

Heel down or up?

Styles differ; many insist rear heel drives without flapping—follow your coach.

Is it like a cross?

Cousins in idea; karate emphasizes chamber, hikite, and syllabus kime.

Best setup?

Often Kizami-Zuki or a block-counter pattern.

The Bottom Line

Make Gyaku-Zuki a hip technique—Hikite the lead, rotate, finish with Kime, then reclaim Kamae.
