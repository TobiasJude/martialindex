Spinning Hook Kick

A spinning hook kick is Hook Kick (Gwidolligi) plus a turn. Compare with Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning), the ITF reverse-turning name for the same broad family.

What Is Spinning Hook Kick?

A spinning hook kick rotates the body, then hooks the heel or sole through the target—usually the head.

It is the English label athletes search. Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning) is the Korean/ITF reverse-turning cousin. Hook Kick (Gwidolligi) is the non-spin hook.

It is not Dwi Chagi (Back Kick). Back kick is linear. This kick hooks.

In WT it appears as a highlight Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) or a punish when someone over-commits. It is not a volume tool like Fast Kick (Bal Bal).

How Spinning Hook Kick Works

Look, pivot on the Pivot Foot (Taekwondo), lift the kicking knee, extend past, and hook.

Spot the target over the shoulder. Blind spins create highlight-reel misses and real collisions.

Finish facing them, hands up. A spinning hook that ends with your back turned is a Counter Kick clinic.

Progress from standing Hook Kick (Gwidolligi) to a 180 spin, then to jump versions that feed 540 Kick.

Mechanically, successful spinning hook kick work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, spinning hook kick is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill spinning hook kick daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Opponent’s turning kick misses; you spin the hook to the open head.

Pad on a stick: slow spin, hook, walk it around, then add speed.

Fight picture: spinning hook kick appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply spinning hook kick after a Feint Kick and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to Kick Combination work instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Kicking too early in the spin.
  • Spinning on the heel.
  • Confusing the path with {dwi-chagi}.
  • Throwing it every time you are tired.
  • No standing hook literacy first.
  • Treating spinning hook kick as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick spinning hook kick inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so spinning hook kick connects to a Kick Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so Counter Kick timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether spinning hook kick appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, aerials, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) or Head Kick (Olgul Chagi) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe spinning hook kick as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put spinning hook kick behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Spinning hook vs bandae dollyo?

Same broad family. This page is the English name; Bandae Dollyo Chagi (Reverse Turning) is the ITF/Korean reverse-turning name.

Spinning hook vs hook?

Hook Kick (Gwidolligi) has no spin.

WT legal?

A legal spinning head or trunk kick can score; confirm World Taekwondo Rules technique bonuses.

Safe progression?

Standing hook, then slow 180, then pads, then partners.

540?

540 Kick is extra rotation often finished with this hook.

The Bottom Line

Earn the standing hook first—the spin is only a delivery system.
