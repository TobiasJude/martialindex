Deep Half Guard

Deep half guard is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu half-guard branch where you pull the opponent's trapped leg across your centerline—creating waiter's sweeps, back exposures, and pressure-passer answers popularized by athletes like Bernardo Faria.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Their trapped leg across your body—not just beside it.
  • Classic payoff: waiter's sweep, back take, and Faria-style topside.
  • Gi grips (pants, sleeve, belt) stabilize; no-gi uses underhooks and head control.
  • Escape the crossface by getting deeper, not by muscling up.
  • Parent: half guard; feeds back control.

What Is Deep Half Guard?

Deep half guard is a bottom half-guard configuration where you insert yourself under the opponent's hips and pull their trapped leg across your torso—often cradling the thigh or ankle while your head and shoulders occupy the space under their center of gravity. Instead of fighting chest-to-chest, you attack from underneath their base.

It is a major branch of half guard, famous in the gi through Bernardo Faria's pressure-to-deep-half system and widely used as a recovery when knee shields get smashed.

How Deep Half Guard Works

By owning their leg across your body, you unbalance them laterally. The classic waiter's sweep elevates and rolls them over the trapped leg. Other paths include coming up behind for the back, transitioning to truck, or climbing into topside half / mount after the sweep. Head position (under their far hip or along the thigh) and a tight connection to the trapped leg are the non-negotiables.

Top player's job. Flatten you before you get deep, win the crossface, backstep out of the cradle, or sprawl and free the knee. Many passers panic and post into the waiter's path—good deep-half players bait that post.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pants grips on the trapped leg, sleeve control, and belt/lapel anchors make deep half sticky and sweep-friendly. Faria-style entries from smashed half are gi classics.

No-gi. Still viable with underhooks, wrist control, and head position—but expect faster scrambles and more transitions into SLX, lockdown escapes, or back takes rather than long parked deep half.

Against pressure passers. Deep half is a designed answer to smash and over-under pressure when shields fail.

What Does Deep Half Guard Look Like in Practice?

Jordan smashes Alex's knee shield. Instead of dying flat, Alex dives under, pulls Jordan's trapped thigh across the belt line, and settles into deep half. Alex elevates into a waiter's sweep, finishes on top, and settles into side control.

Gi. Alex grips Jordan's pants near the knee and a sleeve to freeze the post.

No-gi. Alex cups the thigh, wins a far underhook, and may take the back instead of the waiter if Jordan posts hard.

Types / Variations

Classic / waiter's deep half

Leg across, head under, elevate and roll—the foundational sweep path.

Faria-style deep half

Gi-heavy entries from pressure half, often with heavy grips and a methodical climb to topside after the sweep.

No-gi deep half

Shorter connection windows; emphasize underhooks, head position, and exits to back or SLX.

Deep half to truck / back

When they defend the waiter by posting, scoop behind into truck or rear mount entries.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From smashed knee shield; from underhook half when they crossface hard; pulling deep half on purpose against standing passers; after failed lockdown.

Bottom threats. Waiter's sweep, back take, truck, topside half transitions, occasional leg entries if rules allow.

Top counters. Prevent depth early, sprawl and free the knee, backstep, or force them flat before the cradle locks.

Deep Half vs Standard Half

Factor Deep Half Standard Half
Leg position Across your centerline Beside you / between legs
Primary offense Waiter, back, truck Underhook, shield, dogfight
Vs smash Designed answer Often where smash starts
Gi stickiness Very high with pants grips High with shield + lapels

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal everywhere. Sweeps score; the position itself does not. Transitions into truck/twister or heel hooks depend on the rule set—know your event before hunting neck cranks or reaping paths.

Safety

Communicate when drilling waiter's sweeps (neck and shoulder posts). Tap early if the scramble enters kneebars or twisters.

Common mistakes

  • Staying "half deep"—enough to get crossfaced, not deep enough to sweep.
  • Losing the trapped leg connection while hunting the waiter.
  • In no-gi: parking without underhooks and getting extracted.
  • Forcing waiter when the back is free—take the higher-percentage path.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is the waiter's sweep?

The classic deep-half roll that elevates their trapped leg and turns them over—often finishing in topside half or side control.

When should I go to deep half instead of fighting the shield?

When the shield is folded and you still own one leg—get under their hips instead of muscling into a lost crossface battle.

Does deep half work in no-gi?

Yes. Expect shorter windows and more back-take / SLX exits than long parked gi deep half.

How do I pass deep half?

Stop them getting deep, free your knee, sprawl or backstep, and avoid posting into the waiter's path. Flatten early.

Is deep half only for smaller people?

No. Faria-style deep half is famous for pressure-oriented athletes. Size helps differently; mechanics matter more.

Deep half vs lockdown?

Lockdown figure-fours their leg for control and electric-chair paths; deep half cradles across the body for waiters and backs. See lockdown.

Can deep half lead to the truck?

Yes—especially when they post to stop the waiter and expose the far hip. See truck.

The Bottom Line

Deep half pulls their trapped leg across your centerline for waiter's sweeps and back takes—the classic answer when shields get smashed. Gi grips sticky; no-gi races to backs and SLX. See half guard and knee shield.
