Waza-Ari

Waza-ari is judo’s half score: a near-ippon throw or a 10–19 second osaekomi, with two waza-ari combining into ippon while sitting above yuko.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Near-ippon throw or 10–19s pin.
  • Two make Ippon.
  • Beats any yuko count.
  • Shapes lead/chase tactics.
  • Know landing criteria.
  • Part of Judo Rules.

What is waza-ari?

Waza-ari (技あり) is judo’s half score—a near-ippon throw or a 10–19 second pin.

Two waza-ari equal Ippon. One waza-ari beats any number of yuko under Judo Rules.

How it works

Throw waza-ari: landing more than 90° on the side / near-back without fully meeting ippon’s four criteria.

Pin waza-ari: Osaekomi for 10–19 seconds.

Strategically, a single waza-ari forces the opponent to chase; defending a lead shapes Randori-like contest pacing.

Judogi vs no-gi

As with Ippon, the label is IJF grammar. Other sports use different takedown values.

In practice

Tori’s Osoto Gari (Judo) dumps uke hard onto the side—referee signals waza-ari. Tori then hunts a second score or a pin instead of arguing for ippon.

Forms

Throw waza-ari; pin waza-ari; the awasete combination into Ippon.

Tactics

Do not gift a second waza-ari by diving. Play positive but selective.

From a waza-ari lead, ne-waza that risks little can still add yuko/pin time.

Compare with ippon & yuko

Ippon ends it. Waza-ari stacks. Yuko (5–9s pins / lesser landings) only breaks ties beneath equal waza-ari counts—it never becomes waza-ari.

IJF notes & safety

Under IJF 2026–28 rules, osaekomi scores yuko at 5–9s, waza-ari at 10–19s, and ippon at 20s. Yuko is live again and does not add up to waza-ari; two waza-ari make ippon.

Know your referee signals; argue less, train landings more.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking old ‘two yukos’ lore still upgrades scores.
  • Stopping attack after one waza-ari when a pin is available.
  • Confusing yuko landings with waza-ari.

Waza-ari is where many close contests live. Learning to recognise a side landing versus a yuko landing keeps athletes from arguing with referees and lets them adjust technique mid-match.

Throw construction for waza-ari still starts with kuzushi. A soft trip that barely turns uke may not score; a committed reap that rotates through the shoulder axis often will. Watch Kodokan reference throws, then watch World Tour applications.

Leading by waza-ari changes risk calculus: you can hunt a second score, or you can force the opponent to open up and punish the desperation attack. Both are valid; neither is “stalling” if you continue positive judo within the rules.

On the ground, do not ignore the 10-second threshold. Athletes sometimes escape at nine and celebrate—under 2026 rules that may already be a yuko for the holder, and at ten it is waza-ari.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Does one waza-ari end the match?

No.

Pin window?

10–19 seconds.

Yuko upgrade?

Never into waza-ari.

Awasete?

Second waza-ari → Ippon.

Side landing?

Often waza-ari if beyond yuko criteria.

Lead management?

Attack without gifting the equalizer.

Ne-waza after waza-ari?

Yes—add pin time carefully.

Old rules nostalgia?

Train the 2026 three-tier system.

The Bottom Line

Treat Waza-Ari as a live weapon under Judo Rules, then finish with Ippon or a second score.
