Side Triangle

The side triangle (yoko sankaku) is a triangle choke locked perpendicular to your partner, usually from top side control or north-south. It is both a choke and a control position, and it pairs famously with the kimura from side control as a dilemma.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Not a guard triangle turned sideways by accident—it is a top-side sankaku.
  • Kimura + side triangle is the dilemma: they defend one, they open the other.
  • Parent: triangle choke. Platform: side control.
  • Also a single-leg counter when they pick a leg and leave a neck.
  • Control first; the choke is one of several finishes (wristlock, kimura, armbar).

What Is Side Triangle?

Yoko sankaku sits you roughly perpendicular, legs triangling the neck and an arm (or the far arm, depending on the entry) while you are still the top player. Catch wrestlers and judo players used the position as control; modern BJJ popularized the kimura dilemma from it.

It is distinct from a rear triangle (you are not on the back) and from an inverted triangle (you are not usually the bottom player). The search phrase “side triangle” is what gyms say when they do not want to say yoko sankaku.

Martial Index nests it under side control and the triangle family so kimura hunters and sankaku hunters both arrive.

How Side Triangle Works

From side control or north-south, a common entry is a kimura attempt that they defend by clasping hands. You step a leg under the head, fall to the far hip, and lock the triangle around the neck / far arm.

Tighten the figure-four, pull the far arm, and squeeze. If they unlock the hands to save the neck, both of your hands can finish the kimura on the near arm. If they re-clasp, return to the choke. That loop is the dilemma.

Another picture: they shoot a single; you sprawl a leg over the neck and lock a side triangle as a counter. Same perpendicular sankaku, different start.

If the triangle is loose, keep it as control—wristlocks and armbars live here—or reset to side control rather than falling into their guard.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve isolation makes the far-arm pull easier; you can also mix wristlocks on the cloth.

No-gi. The dilemma still works. Wrist control replaces sleeves.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex attacks a north-south kimura, they clasp, and the side triangle becomes the choke. They unclasp—kimura. They reclasp—triangle.

Jordan stuffs a single with a side triangle and finishes without ever settling in classic side control.

Types / Variations

Kimura-dilemma side triangle

The Gordon / Melanson picture from north-south or side.

Single-leg counter side triangle

Sprawl the neck line as they pick a leg.

Control-first yoko sankaku

Hold the position, then pick choke, kimura, or wristlock.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: defended kimura from side/north-south, step-over from side control, stuffed single. Counters: do not clasp into the dilemma without a plan, extract the head, keep the near elbow tight. Escapes: turn into the triangle before it locks or peel the figure-four foot.

Side Triangle vs Rear Triangle

Factor Side triangle (yoko) Rear triangle (ura)
Your place Perpendicular, usually top Behind, on the back
Famous chain Kimura dilemma RNC / armbar from back
Start spots Side control, north-south, singles Back control, turtle
Parent Triangle + side control Triangle + back control

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult triangle chokes and kimuras are standard legal submissions. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Perpendicular triangles can load necks if you crank. Finish as a choke, tap early, and apply wristlocks slowly.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing the choke and ignoring the kimura when they unclasp.
  • Loose triangle that they rotate out of into your guard.
  • Confusing this with a rear triangle from the back.
  • Ripping a wristlock to “make something happen.”

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Yoko sankaku or side triangle?

Same idea. Yoko sankaku is the Japanese name; side triangle is the gym English.

Why does the kimura pair with it?

Clasped hands save the kimura and feed the far arm to the triangle; unlocking saves the choke and feeds the kimura.

Is this a beginner move?

The dilemma is intermediate. White belts can still learn the picture.

From guard?

This page is the top-side perpendicular triangle, not a failed front triangle that happens to be sideways.

Gi needed?

No. Sleeves just help isolation.

Single-leg version legal?

The choke is a standard triangle—verify takedown/slam language in your book if you counter a shot.

Next reads?

Kimura from side control and the parent triangle.

The Bottom Line

Side triangle is perpendicular sankaku from top: lock it from side or north-south, then run the kimura dilemma. Keep side control as home if the figure-four is not real yet.
