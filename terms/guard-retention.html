Guard Retention

Guard retention is the Brazilian jiu-jitsu skill of keeping your guard alive—and recovering it when a pass starts—using frames, hip movement, connection layers, and timing against pressure and speed passes.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Retention = prevent the pass + recover when connections break.
  • Frames, underhooks, and hip heists beat panic butt-scooting alone.
  • Gi grips buy time; no-gi needs earlier frames and faster hips.
  • Square hips under a toreando die—angle and layer your defenses.
  • Pairs with guard passing as the opposite skill.

What Is Guard Retention?

Guard retention is the defensive-offensive skill of maintaining bottom guard connections when the top player tries to pass. It includes early prevention (frames, grips, angles) and late recovery (hip escapes, inversions, re-guards) after a connection breaks. It is a key branch of open guard.

Modern systems categorize retention by pass type: around the legs, under the legs, through the legs, and close-range smash.

How Guard Retention Works

You manage distance and block the passer's preferred path. Against toreando/leg drag, use long frames and hip movement. Against knee cuts and body locks, fight inside space and underhooks. Against stacks, protect posture and reclaim legs early. Retention succeeds when you return to an active guard—not when you merely delay the pin.

Passer's job. Clear frames, connect to the hips, and stabilize past the legs into side control or mount.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve, collar, and pants grips create sticky frames and slow passers down.

No-gi. Fewer anchors—frames must be earlier; expect more body-lock and wrestling-style pressure.

MMA context. Retention under strikes favors tight elbows and getting up when guard is compromised.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Jordan starts a toreando; Alex posts long frames on the biceps, hip-escapes to an angle, and reestablishes De La Riva hooks before Jordan connects to the hips.

Gi. Alex adds sleeve grips to freeze Jordan's hands.

No-gi. Alex frames on shoulders/upper arms and recovers seated butterfly.

Types / Variations

Frames and connection layers

Primary/secondary frames, inside/outside position, and layered grips so one broken connection is not a pass.

Against around / under passes

Answers to toreando, leg drag, and stack-style paths.

Against through-the-legs passes

Answers to knee cut, smash, and headquarters pressure.

Recovery to offense

Retention that immediately returns to sweeps and submissions—not endless defense.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Training methods. Positional rounds starting mid-pass; specific pass-vs-retention games; drilling hip heists and inversions safely.

When retention fails. Elbow escape from side control, turtle intelligently, or stand up—do not die under a pin hunting a miracle hook.

Passer counters. Clear the first frame, pin the secondary, and connect hips before legs return.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

No points for retention itself—points come from sweeps and from denying the pass. Passers score when they clear and stabilize.

Safety

Inversions and neck bridges carry risk—progress gradually under coaches. Tap early if a stack becomes a cervical crank.

Common mistakes

  • Flat, square hips under speed passes.
  • One lonely frame with no secondary layer.
  • Retaining forever without returning to offense.
  • In no-gi: late frames.
  • Panic inversion into the passer's pressure.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is guard retention?

The skill of preventing guard passes and recovering guard connections when a pass starts.

How is retention different from just holding closed guard?

Retention covers all guards and the dynamic recovery after connections break—not only squeezing closed guard.

Best drills for guard retention?

Positional sparring from mid-pass, frame-replacement drills, and controlled inversion/hip-heist practice.

Gi vs no-gi retention?

Gi grips buy time; no-gi requires earlier frames and faster hip movement.

How do I retain against pressure passers?

Fight inside space, underhooks, and early frames; do not let them glue your knees together—see pressure passing.

How do I retain against toreando?

Long frames, angled hips, and re-hooking before they connect to your belt line—see speed passing.

When should I abandon retention and turtle or stand?

When frames are gone and a pin is inevitable—turtle or stand on purpose rather than getting mounted for free.

The Bottom Line

Guard retention keeps your guard alive with frames, layers, and hips—then returns you to offense. Study it alongside guard passing.
