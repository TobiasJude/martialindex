Knee on Belly

Knee on belly is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu pin where one knee drives into the opponent's midsection while the other foot posts for base—worth two IBJJF points, brutal on breathing, and a launchpad for armbars, gi baseball-bat chokes, and climbs to mount.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • One knee on the belly, one foot posted: mobile pressure worth 2 IBJJF points.
  • Forces frames that open armbars and chokes; also a bridge to mount/back.
  • Gi: baseball bat and collar tools; no-gi: armbars, head position, mount climbs.
  • From bottom, forearm-frame the knee—do not push with flat hands.
  • More mobile and scorier than side control; less stable if your post is lazy.

What Is Knee on Belly?

Knee on belly (knee on stomach) is a top position where one knee drives into the opponent's midsection while the other leg posts out for base, usually with hand posts on the hips or far collar/armpit. It is a mobile pin: less glued than side control, more oppressive for breathing, and worth two points in IBJJF when stabilized.

Athletes use it to score, to force desperate frames that open armbars and chokes, and to transition to mount or back takes when the bottom player turns away from the pressure.

How Knee on Belly Works

The posted foot and the driving knee create a diagonal of weight that makes inhaling hard and shrimping awkward. Hands control the far hip and near sleeve or neck so they cannot sit up into your knee. If they push the knee with both hands, they often gift an armbar; if they turn in, you may take the back; if they turn away, mount appears.

Base first. A narrow post invites the sweep. Keep the posted foot alive and your hips over the knee.

Do not camp forever. Knee on belly is a threat and a scoring tool. When they solve the pressure, drop back to side control or climb to mount rather than getting reversed.

Gi vs No-Gi

Grips. Gi knee on belly loves belt, pants, and collar grips—especially the baseball bat choke setup Bernardo Faria popularized in instructionals. No-gi uses wrist control, head posts, and chest pressure without fabric handles.

Submissions. Gi: baseball bat, paper cutter, armbar when they push the knee. No-gi: armbars, D'Arce entries, step-over chokes, transitions to mount attacks. Without lapels, the famous baseball bat disappears.

Escapes. Same idea—bridge and insert the knee to half guard—but gi bottom players can pant-grip your posted leg; no-gi players frame the knee with a forearm (not a flat hand) and hug the far leg into deep half, as Bernardo teaches.

What Does Knee on Belly Look Like in Practice?

From side control, Sam grips Jordan's belt and far collar, hops the near knee onto the belly, and posts the far foot. Jordan gasps and pushes the knee with both hands. Sam swings for an armbar.

Gi. Sam already has a thumb-in collar; the second hand feeds palm-up for a baseball bat as Sam spins toward north-south and finishes.

No-gi. Sam posts on the far hip and near shoulder, rides the scramble when Jordan turns, and settles in mount instead of hunting a lapel choke that does not exist.

Types / Variations

Standard knee on belly

Knee in the solar plexus / belly, far foot posted, hands controlling hips and head.

Near-side knee ride

Knee closer to the near hip to block re-guard while you look at armbars.

Far-side / jump-over

Switching the knee across when they turn, often into mount or reverse KOB angles.

Baseball-bat setup (gi)

Collar grips established before or during the hop to KOB for an immediate choke threat.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. From side control, from a completed pass, from mount when you need mobility, from north-south as you reverse direction.

Bottom escapes. Keep elbows tight (do not gift armbars), forearm-frame the knee, bridge, and pull their leg into half or deep half; turn to your knees only if you can win the race to grips.

Top answers. When they push the knee, attack the arm; when they shrimp hard, drop to side control; when they turn away, take mount or the back.

Mobility High Medium
Breathing pressure Very high High but usually flatter
Stability vs sweeps Easier to reverse if lazy Generally more stable

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

IBJJF awards 2 points for knee on belly with the knee on the belly/chest and the other foot on the mat, stabilized three seconds. You can score KOB after a pass (stacking with the 3-point pass). Moving from mount down to KOB does not add points. ADCC also recognizes knee on belly scoring in its point rules.

Safety

Train under qualified instruction. Driving the knee into the floating ribs with malice can injure partners—apply controlled pressure. Tap early to armbars spun from KOB.

Common mistakes

  • Posting the foot too close and getting swept.
  • Leaving arms extended so they hug your posted leg.
  • In no-gi: setting baseball-bat grips that need a lapel.
  • Camping in KOB until they solve it and reverse you.
  • Pushing their knee with flat hands from bottom and walking into an armbar.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Do I get pass points and knee-on-belly points together?

Yes—if you pass and stabilize knee on belly, refs commonly award the pass (3) and then KOB (2) when each criteria is met. Confirm with the referee's signals in your bracket.

Why do I get swept from knee on belly?

Your posted foot is dead or too close, or your hands are not controlling the far hip. Widen the post, keep weight on the knee, and be ready to drop to side control.

Is the baseball bat choke only from knee on belly?

No, but KOB is a high-percentage setup in the gi because the grips and angle appear naturally when you hop up from side control.

Does knee on belly work in no-gi?

Yes as pressure and a transition tool. The lapel baseball bat does not; replace it with armbars, mount climbs, and head-and-arm attacks.

How should I escape knee on belly?

Elbows in, forearm on their knee, bridge, and pull their leg into half or deep half. Avoid posting both hands on the knee.

Should I stay in knee on belly or go to mount?

Score and threaten from KOB, then take mount when they turn or when you need a stabler pin. Do not treat KOB as a forever hold.

The Bottom Line

Knee on belly is the mobile midsection pin: score two points, crush breathing, force mistakes, then finish or climb. Gi athletes add baseball-bat chokes; no-gi athletes ride reactions into mount and armbars. See side control for the stabler sibling pin.
