411 / Honey Hole

411 (sometimes written 4-11) is a widely used nickname for the same inside leg entanglement many call the honey hole or saddle (inside sankaku). This entry clarifies synonymy, light naming history, and rule context—mechanics live primarily on the sibling pages.

Key Takeaways

  • 411 ≈ honey hole ≈ saddle for practical purposes—confirm with your coach’s details.
  • Numeric nickname spread through 10th Planet / online no-gi culture.
  • Use this page for naming + IBJJF literacy; use honeyhole/saddle for deeper mechanics.
  • Do not memorize illegal lists from forums—verify current rulebooks.

What Is 411 / Honey Hole?

Ask three gyms what “411” means and you will usually see the same inside entanglement: a triangulated leg with cross-hip control feeding inside heel hooks. The number stuck as slang; honey hole and saddle describe the same neighborhood with different branding.

Martial Index intentionally avoids rewriting the entire saddle manual here. Instead we answer the search “what is 411 in BJJ?” and point you into the mechanical twins so content stays differentiated but complete.

How 411 / Honey Hole Works

Mechanically: lock inside sankaku on the trapped leg, cross the far hip, pinch knees—see saddle and honey hole for step detail and finishes.

For competition week: map your 411 drills onto the rule set. If heel hooks or reaping angles are restricted, keep the position as a control puzzle or switch to legal lower-body attacks.

Language tip: if a partner says 411 and you say saddle, align on shared checkpoints (triangle lock, hip cross, heel exposure) before arguing labels.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. 411 conversations often become “what’s legal this year?”—open the IBJJF PDF.

No-gi. Nickname appears constantly in heel-hook classrooms.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists. Especially relevant when mapping legal attacks inside the 411/honeyhole/saddle geometry—see also reaping.

Safety

Nickname changes nothing about knee risk—same tap culture as saddle heel hooks.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming 411 is a totally different position.
  • Duplicating study without reading saddle/honeyhole.
  • Trusting outdated legality charts.
  • Cranking through taps because “it’s just drilling 411.”

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is 411 different from honey hole?

Usually no—closely synonymous. Local coaches may emphasize tiny details; ask them.

Is 411 different from saddle?

Same family; saddle is the Danaher-line preferred English label for inside sankaku.

Why the number?

Gym/internet slang that stuck—treat it as a nickname.

Where are mechanics?

Start with saddle and honey hole.

IBJJF legal?

Depends on attacks and reaping definitions—verify current book.

ADCC?

Generally more permissive for heel-hook games—still verify event rules.

Should beginners learn the name?

Learn the position; pick up nicknames as you go.

The Bottom Line

411 is mostly a nickname with SEO gravity: treat it as honey hole/saddle, study mechanics on those pages, and verify current rules before you hang a heel hook in competition.
