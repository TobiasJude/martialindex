Single Leg (Wrestling)

A wrestling single leg isolates one leg—high or low—then finishes by driving, dumping, or lifting while you manage the whizzer and sprawl.

  • Prefixed slug avoids colliding with BJJ's page.
  • Chain with high, low, and high-C variants.
  • Manage whizzer and sprawl early.
  • Head position decides whether you score or get turned.

What Is Single Leg (Wrestling)?

The wrestling single-leg is a foundational takedown: attack one leg, control it, and put the opponent to the mat under freestyle or folkstyle rules.

Martial Index keeps this page at Single Leg (Wrestling) because BJJ single-leg already covers Brazilian jiu-jitsu framing—compare when useful, do not conflate.

Variants in this batch include High Single, Low Single, High Crotch, and Ankle Pick (Wrestling).

In Wrestling, the single is often safer than a double against heavy sprawls if you keep head position legal and the corner turn sharp.

Martial Index files Single Leg (Wrestling) under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Single Leg (Wrestling) behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Single Leg (Wrestling) Works

From Wrestling Stance, set up (collar clear, outside step, snap), Level Change, and Penetration Step to the target leg.

Secure the leg with a high clasp above the knee or low clasp at the ankle, head tight to the hip or thigh as the finish requires.

Finish: run the pipe (circle toward the captured leg), dump inside or outside, elevate, or transition to a double if the far leg comes close.

Expect a Whizzer and sprawl—peek the head, keep the hips under, and chain rather than freeze.

Mechanically, successful single leg (wrestling) work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → Level Change → entry or control → finish or recovery.

If stance collapses or the Penetration Step stalls short, even textbook grips fail—too high and you meet a Sprawl (Wrestling); too loose and you invite a Whizzer or scramble.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: shots need sprawl literacy; ties need hand-fighting; pins need Near Fall awareness in folkstyle.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, single leg (wrestling) is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how single leg (wrestling) is valued once the wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-attack cousins of single leg (wrestling) either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle par terre.

Illegal headlocks and potentially dangerous holds can appear in scramble singles—release when warned.

In folkstyle, ensure clear control on the mat for the takedown.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Folkstyle dual: single to a high finish, cover for the takedown, then ride.

Freestyle bout: single to exposure turn in one continuous chain.

Match picture: single leg (wrestling) appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply single leg (wrestling) after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Head on the wrong side of the hip—getting cradled or crossed.
  • Holding the leg without driving the corner.
  • Pulling yourself onto your own butt.
  • Ignoring the whizzer until you are flattened.
  • Treating single leg (wrestling) as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent sprawls or bridges.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe single leg (wrestling) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or a Level Change that never gets under the opponent’s hips.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

High single vs low single?

High controls nearer the knee or hip; low attacks the ankle. Both are singles with different finishes.

How is this different from BJJ single-leg?

Compare Single-Leg Takedown (BJJ): BJJ finishes often prioritize mat returns into guard or pass contexts; wrestling prioritizes control criteria and freestyle exposure.

What if they whizzer?

Keep the head free, circle, threaten the dump, or clear the Whizzer before elevating.

Can I single in Greco?

No—leg attacks are illegal in Greco-Roman Wrestling.

Best beginner finish?

Run-the-pipe corner drive after a clean high single.

Does a single score the same as a double?

Under the same rule set, a completed takedown scores as a takedown; amplitude and exposure can add more in freestyle.

The Bottom Line

The wrestling single is a system: entry, control height, corner, and finish—train it as a family, not a single highlight.
