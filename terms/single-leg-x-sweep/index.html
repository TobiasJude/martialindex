What Is Single-Leg X Sweep?

Single-Leg X Sweep is offensive reversals from single-leg X (SLX / ashigarami entry) that dump the opponent while threatening heel hooks and technical stand-ups. In gym language you may also hear SLX sweep. Train it as a named pattern with clear entries and exits—not a vague vibe.

How It Works

Bite the near leg in SLX, elevate the hips, off-balance toward their free leg or rear, and either come up on top or enter outside ashi / saddle for leg locks. Control beats speed: establish the grips or frames that make the next decision obvious.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi vs no-gi notes. Pant grip helps pin the ankle; no-gi clamps shin-to-armpit and hand-fights the heel.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

In live training, single-leg x sweep usually appears as a short window: you recognize the grips/angles, commit the mechanics above, and either finish the position change or chain to a sibling technique when they defend.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Standard single-leg x sweep as described in the hero video and fundamental classes.

Competition variation

Competition variation: tighter grips, earlier chains, less telegraphing.

Related option

Related option: transition into a sibling from the Related notes when the primary finish stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries come from the parent position(s) (Single-Leg X). Counters include framing early, removing the key grip, and forcing a different cluster (for example recovering guard or changing the pin). Escapes prioritize creating space before the structure fully sets.

How It Compares

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Generally legal in BJJ; check IBJJF Rules and ADCC Rules for crank/reaping/slam nuances when relevant to this pattern (especially neck, spinal, and knee pressure).

Safety

Communicate, tap early, and avoid ballistic force on the neck, shoulders, and knees. This page is reference—not live coaching.

Common mistakes

Rushing without grips; stalling without a next move; confusing single-leg x sweep with a nearby sibling name.
