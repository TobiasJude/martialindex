Twister

The twister is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu (and MMA) spinal-twist submission popularized by Eddie Bravo—typically built from twister side control, truck, and crab ride—with strict legality and safety constraints.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Control the hips/legs first (truck/TSC), then the upper body twist.
  • Illegal or restricted in many IBJJF contexts—verify.
  • Neck/spine risk is serious—supervised training only.
  • Banana split and calf slicer are sibling truck attacks.
  • Not a casual “fun crank.”

What Is Twister?

The twister rotates the upper body against a controlled lower body, loading the spine. In the Bravo system it often flows from twister side control into truck/back-control hybrids, then a grapevine of arm and head control to finish the twist.

Pillars/links: twister side control, truck, crab ride, back control.

How Twister Works

Establish lower-body control so the hips cannot follow (truck or equivalent). Capture an arm/head configuration that lets you rotate the torso opposite the hips, then apply gradual twist.

If the twist is illegal in your rules, use the same controls to take the back or mount instead.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Possible but culturally less emphasized than no-gi 10th Planet rooms.

No-Gi / MMA. Famous finishes exist; still respect gym and event rules.

Never assume “viral MMA finish” means “legal Saturday IBJJF.”

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From twister side control, Alex rolls to truck, captures Jordan’s arm and head, and begins the twister—stopping immediately on tap in training. Under many IBJJF rules Alex would abandon the spinal finish for back takes.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: TSC, truck, crab-ride transitions, failed banana splits that leave upper-body access.

Counters: keep hips free to follow the turn; hand-fight early; escape truck.

Escapes: relieve twist and reconnect hips to shoulders—tap early if spine loads.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Twister / spinal-twist finishes and some truck-family attacks are heavily restricted or illegal under many IBJJF-style rule sets. Verify current IBJJF vs ADCC (or promoter) rules before competing. Train spine torque only under qualified coaches.

Safety

Spine and neck injuries can be catastrophic. Many gyms ban hard twister drilling. If trained at all: micro-pressure, verbal taps, coach present, instant release.

Common mistakes

  • Hunting twisters in banned brackets.
  • Snapping the twist.
  • Skipping truck/TSC fundamentals.
  • Treating it as a joke submission.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a twister?

A spinal-twist submission from truck/TSC-family controls.

Legal in IBJJF?

Often no—verify current rules.

Who popularized it in BJJ?

Eddie Bravo / 10th Planet.

Safe to drill?

Only with strict culture and supervision—many gyms avoid hard drilling.

Related positions?

Twister side control, truck, crab ride.

Backup if illegal?

Back take, mount, calf slicer per rules.

Same as banana split?

No—split stretches legs; twister twists spine.

The Bottom Line

Twister is the spinal endgame of truck-family control: lock the hips, rotate the torso, stop instantly on tap. Prefer legal back takes when rules ban it—and always read TSC / truck.
