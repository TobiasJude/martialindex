Roll Under

Roll under dips and travels beneath a hook's arc—weight transfers, eyes up—so you exit the danger line and load a counter.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Roll under hooks, not every straight.
  • Transfer weight; do not just bend the neck.
  • Step when you need range for the counter.

What Is Roll Under?

Roll under is the under-hook travel skill closely related to Bob and Weave: you drop and move your head along a U so a Hook passes over, then rise on the far side.

Compared with a pure Duck, roll under includes lateral travel; compared with Weave Out, it emphasizes the under-punch path that often sets an immediate Counterpunch.

It pairs with Slip sequences—slip the straight, roll under the follow-up hook—inside coherent Head Movement.

Martial Index files Roll Under under Boxing so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Roll Under behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Roll Under Works

As the hook comes, bend knees, shift weight from one foot toward the other, keep elbows in and chin tucked, and move the head under the arc without diving face-first.

Eyes track the opponent; hands stay ready for Glove Block insurance or to fire on the rise.

Static rolls stay in place; stepping rolls add a short step to keep Boxing Range for the return Cross or Lead Hook.

Mechanically, successful roll under work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair this tool with the rest of the defensive layer: Slip/Parry for straights, Bob and Weave literacy for hooks, and honest Guard Hand Position always.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, roll under lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows as weapons—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill roll under daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Partner throws jab–hook; you Slip Right the jab and roll under the hook, then return a rear straight.

Coach Anthony-style note: on some sides you must step with the roll or the counter falls short.

Fight picture: roll under appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply roll under after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Types / Variations

Static roll

U-path without a big step; good when you already own mid-range and the counter is a short hook.

Drill this variation with movement—step in, step out, and reset—so it survives sparring pressure, not only pad stations.

Stepping roll

Add a step under the punch to keep power-hand range—critical when a static roll leaves you short.

Common Mistakes

  • Rolling with the head only.
  • Eyes down.
  • Rising straight into the next hook.
  • Rolling under straights that needed a slip.
  • Treating roll under as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Slip-line: roll under the rope side to side.

Pads: jab–hook feeds with mandatory roll then counter.

Sparring theme rounds: only roll under hooks, slip straights.

Shadowbox roll under inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so roll under connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether roll under appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, head movement, and counters.

Study Notes

Study Bob and Weave as the parent pattern; roll under is the named under-hook application.

Film whether your roll creates angle or merely ducks in place.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Roll under vs bob-and-weave?

Bob and Weave is the broader pattern; roll under names the under-hook travel.

Roll under vs duck?

Duck sits under straights; roll under travels under hooks.

Do I always counter after?

Plan one—rolling without a next action wastes the miss.

Open guard while rolling?

No—keep Guard Hand Position or a tight Defensive Shell.

Beginner first?

Learn shallow rolls on the slip line before live hooks.

The Bottom Line

Get under the hook with your legs—then rise with a punch, not a pose.
