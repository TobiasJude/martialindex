Frame and Shrimp

Frame and shrimp pairs skeletal frames with the shrimp (hip escape) to create space from side control or mount, then insert a knee and recover guard. It is the workhorse under the escape hierarchy.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Frames first—then move hips.
  • Bridge or turn to unweight before the shrimp.
  • Insert knee to elbow; don’t lead with a naked knee.
  • Repeat until you reclaim half or full guard.
  • Pair with bridge-and-roll when they overcommit.

What Is Frame and Shrimp?

A composite escape: frames deny chest-to-chest glue; the shrimp relocates hips so you can rebuild a guard barrier.

Core answer to settled side control; also the second half of many mount escapes after an elbow gets inside.

Closely related to knee-elbow escape and elbow escape—Martial Index groups them as frame-driven hip escapes.

How Frame and Shrimp Works

Get at least one forearm inside (hip or neck line); turn to your side so you can breathe and push.

Bridge diagonally or bump to disconnect their elbow/knee clamp, then shrimp hips away.

Insert the near knee into the pocket and connect knee-to-elbow; extend to create distance.

Reclaim half guard, knee shield, or full guard—then neutralize before you attack.

If they chase, re-frame and shrimp again; don’t stall flat under a finished pin.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Lapel and sleeve frames add sticky posts; beware crossface collars while you shrimp.

No-gi. Forearm and shin frames; expect slipperier clamps—emphasize hip angle and repeated shrimp.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Sam frames the hip, bridges to free the near elbow, shrimps, and inserts the knee to recover closed guard.

Riley gets smashed flat; they turn to a side, micro-breathe, re-frame the neck, and chain two shrimps into half guard.

Types / Variations

Side-control frame-and-shrimp

Hip + neck frames, bridge, shrimp, knee insert.

Mount elbow-to-shrimp

Elbow inside, then shrimp to half/guard.

Bridge-then-shrimp

Unweight first when their elbow clamps the hip.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

From bottom side control, north-south edges, and mount after an elbow enters. Counters: crossface, hip switch, knee-on-belly. Cross-link maintain side control to understand what you are fighting.

Frame and Shrimp vs Bridge and Roll

Factor Frame and shrimp Bridge and roll
Primary home Side control / mount frames Mount with reachable arm
Goal Recover guard Reverse to top
Engine Frames + shrimp Trap + diagonal bridge
Chain Into recover/recompose guard Fake into elbow escape

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal under sport rules; escape urgency rises with pin quality—verify event scoring for pass/mount already awarded via IBJJF / ADCC packets.

Safety

Don’t crank necks with desperate frames; turn to your side to breathe before big bridges under heavy partners.

Common mistakes

  • Shrimping with no frames.
  • Leading with the knee into a jammed hip.
  • Staying flat and panicking.
  • Stopping after one shrimp when they reclamp.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is frame and shrimp?

Build frames, create space with a shrimp, insert the knee, recover guard.

From which positions?

Mostly side control; also mount after an elbow gets inside.

Why frame first?

Without frames, shrimps get reclamped instantly.

Related to knee-elbow?

Yes—same family of frame-driven hip escapes.

What if they clamp the hip?

Bridge first to disconnect the elbow, then shrimp.

End goal?

Recover or recompose a functional guard, then neutralize.

Breath tip?

Turn to your side so the diaphragm can work under pressure.

The Bottom Line

Frame and shrimp is the space-making pair—structure, then hips—that turns settled pins back into a playable guard.
