Body Lock Passing System

The body lock passing system connects your hands around the torso to pin hips, clear the knee line, and pass half or butterfly. It extends body-lock pass into a full plan under guard passing, often when knee cut vs half stalls.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Hands connected = hip pin + narrowed base—balance matters.
  • Clear the knee line before celebrating.
  • Primary vs butterfly and half; pairs with pressure passing.
  • Abandon path from failed knee cuts.
  • Study parent body-lock-pass for the core grip.

What Is Body Lock Passing System?

A system page: entries, knee-line clears, half-guard troubleshooting, and finishes—not only the gable grip photo.

It is a pressure / connection approach: deny space, then methodically extract legs.

Live sibling: body-lock-pass; compare over-under and knee-cut.

How Body Lock Passing System Works

Enter from sprawl, headquarters, or by stuffing butterfly hooks—connect hands low around the waist/hips.

Keep head position and shoulder pressure while you walk the knee line past their knees.

Vs half: face them away, arch their back, and free the trapped foot without giving underhooks.

Finish to side control or mount; watch guillotine and kimura counters while hands are locked.

System checkpoint list many coaches use: (1) hands connected, (2) head side chosen, (3) knee line past their knees, (4) trapped foot extracted, (5) crossface or underhook as you settle. Miss a checkpoint and the body lock becomes a wrestling hug.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Belt assists exist, but the no-gi body lock still translates.

No-gi. The modern default—hand fights define the meta.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex stuffs butterfly, locks a low body lock, clears the knee line, and walks to side control.

Jordan’s knee cut dies on a shield; they connect hands and restart the body-lock system.

Types / Variations

Low body lock

Hands at hip line—common no-gi path.

Headquarters / sprawl entries

Enter without giving easy underhooks.

Half-guard troubleshooting

Face-away, arch, and extract the trapped foot.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries vs seated butterfly and after failed knee cuts. Counters: guillotine, kimura, inversion. Escapes for bottom: create hip space or pummel underhooks before the knee line clears.

Body Lock System vs Over-Under

Factor Body lock system Over-under pass
Connection Hands around torso Overhook + underhook on legs
Best vs Butterfly / half Open guard with feet on hips
Balance demand High (narrow base) High (tripod pressure)
Sibling Body-lock-pass Over-under-pass

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Pass scoring still requires clearing legs and stabilizing—verify current IBJJF book. Body lock itself is generally legal control.

Safety

Don’t crank the spine; clear the knee line patiently and watch your own balance.

Common mistakes

  • Locking hands without a knee-line plan.
  • Standing tall with a high center of gravity.
  • Ignoring guillotine threats.
  • Forcing vs deep underhooks instead of adjusting.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a body lock passing system?

A plan that uses a torso clasp to pin hips, clear the knee line, and pass half or butterfly.

Different from body-lock-pass page?

This page emphasizes system stages and troubleshooting.

When over knee cut?

When shields and frames make the slice low-percentage.

Gi or no-gi?

Famous in no-gi; still useful in gi.

Main danger?

Guillotines and loss of balance from the narrow base.

Key checkpoint?

Knee line cleared before you walk to the side.

Related pillar?

Guard passing.

The Bottom Line

The body lock system is connection first: clasp, clear the knee line, then finish—especially when knee-cut paths stall.
