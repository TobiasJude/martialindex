Arm Drag

An arm drag pulls the opponent’s arm across to take their back angle—setting singles, ankle picks, go-behinds, and even seated entries from the takedown cluster.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Drag to the hip, not just the wrist.
  • Chest to their shoulder line as you pass.
  • Immediate next action required.
  • Works gi and no-gi with different handles.
  • Also a guard tool—not only standing.

What Is Arm Drag?

The arm drag creates a 45-degree world: control near wrist/triceps, pull across, step to their outside, and arrive behind the elbow line. From there, options explode—leg attacks standing, seatbelt, or mat returns.

Origins sit in wrestling; BJJ uses drags from standing and from seated/butterfly contexts. See hand fighting and back control.

Sport value: often the setup rather than the scored event—the takedown or back take scores.

How Arm Drag Works

Control the wrist, slide to triceps/elbow, pull across your center, post or step past, connect to the hip/back.

Standing finishes: single, double, pick, or mat return to back.

Seated: drag to back clinch or sweep paths.

Drill: drag to hold the angle for three seconds before finishing.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve drag is the fabric version; same angle goals.

No-Gi. Wrist-to-triceps drag is standard.

Both: a drag without a step is only a tug.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex clears a collar grip with a snap, drags, and picks the far ankle.

Alex drags to a single and knee-cuts after the fall.

Seated, Alex drags Jordan’s arm and takes the back.

Types / Variations

Standing arm drag

Go-behind and shot setups.

Gi sleeve drag

Fabric-powered angle.

Seated / butterfly drag

Back takes and sweeps.

Drag chains

Pick, single, body lock follow-ups.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: hand fights, grip breaks, and their extended posts.

Counters: elbow heavy, re-pummel, and circle to face them before they connect.

If the drag fails, do not linger square—reset hands.

Arm Drag vs Underhook

Factor Arm Drag Underhook
Primary gift Angle / back access Shoulder elevation
Feel Pull across Climb under
Standing next Shot / go-behind Body lock / trip
Guard next Back take / sweep Dogfight / sweep

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

The drag itself is rarely a scoring position; the ensuing takedown or back control may be. Confirm IBJJF back-take and takedown criteria in the current book.

Safety

Shoulder and elbow yanks can injure. Drag with body, not arm-only yanking. Control the mat return.

Common mistakes

  • Wrist-only tug with no step.
  • Dragging into their strength without angle.
  • Getting the angle then freezing.
  • Exposing your own back while chasing.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is an arm drag?

Pulling their arm across to take a back angle.

Standing or seated?

Both—standing for shots, seated for backs/sweeps.

Gi version?

Sleeve drag with the same angle goals.

Best follow-ups?

Single, ankle pick, go-behind, seatbelt.

Common error?

No footwork after the pull.

Defense?

Heavy elbow and face them early.

Next?

Ankle pick and single-leg.

The Bottom Line

Arm drags buy the angle that honest shots and back takes need. Pull to the hip, step past, and spend the angle immediately.
