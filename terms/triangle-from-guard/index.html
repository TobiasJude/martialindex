Triangle from Guard

The triangle from guard is the classic triangle choke (sankaku) from closed guard: isolate one arm with the head inside your legs, lock a figure-four, cut angle perpendicular, and squeeze—often chaining with the armbar from guard.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • One arm in, one arm out—head controlled by the strangling thigh.
  • Angle matters as much as squeeze—bite the figure-four then pivot.
  • Pull their trapped arm across to tighten the carotid squeeze.
  • Parent: triangle choke; sibling chain: armbar/omoplata.
  • Posture breaking starts the story—do not chase triangles against stacked giants without angle.

What Is Triangle from Guard?

From closed guard the triangle is the signature blood choke: your legs form a triangle around their neck and one shoulder. It remains a competition staple because it punishes posture mistakes and pairs with armbars when they pull the arm out.

Martial Index isolates the guard variant for learners searching this exact phrase while linking the umbrella triangle page for broader sankaku theory.

How Triangle from Guard Works

Break posture, control a sleeve/wrist, and bring their arm across. Open guard, throw your leg over the shoulder/neck line, lock figure-four (shin behind knee), and pinch.

Cut angle by scooting your hips toward the trapped-arm side until you are more perpendicular. Pull the head down, elevate hips, and drag the trapped arm across your centerline.

If they posture stack, angle out or switch to armbar/omoplata. If they pull the arm free, you may already be halfway to an armbar.

Angle drills beat squeeze drills: set a timer for ten slow triangles that emphasize hip travel toward the trapped arm before any hard finishing pressure.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar grips make isolation easier; some add lapel assists.

No-gi. Overhooks and head control replace cloth—expect more posture fights.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

A class triangle from closed guard shows figure-four lock then angle—not pure strength squeeze.

A competitor chains failed triangle into armbar without resetting closed guard.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: failed cross-collars, overcommitted punches (MMA), sleeve drags. Counters: posture, stack, hide the arm, clear the locking foot. Escapes: posture + stack, or dive to side carefully; early prevention beats late escape.

Triangle vs Armbar from Guard

Factor Triangle from guard Armbar from guard
Target Neck (blood choke) Elbow (joint)
Arm count One in / one out Attacked arm extended
Shared setup Posture break Posture break
Chain ↔ Armbar ↔ Triangle

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal submission across standard divisions. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

Do not crank the neck; adjust angle for blood choke. Tap when vision tunnels.

Common mistakes

  • Squeezing without angle.
  • Both arms inside the triangle.
  • Crossing feet instead of figure-four.
  • Ignoring the armbar when they pull out.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Blood or air?

Proper triangle is primarily a blood choke via carotids.

Most common error?

No angle—pure squeeze against posture.

Chain to armbar?

Yes—when they extract the arm or posture into it.

Gi required?

No.

Figure-four which way?

Shin behind opposite knee—coach the lock side for your setup.

Parent page?

Triangle choke.

Mount triangle?

Different variant—this page is guard-specific.

The Bottom Line

Triangle from guard is posture break, one-arm isolation, figure-four, angle, squeeze. Chain it with armbar from guard so every defense opens another door.
