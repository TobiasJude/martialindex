BJJ · Submission

Armbar

The armbar (juji gatame) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu submission that isolates an opponent’s arm between your legs and hyperextends the elbow against your hips—available from closed guard, mount, back, and many transitional positions, with different handles in gi versus no-gi.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Armbar = control the arm, pin the shoulders/head relationship, extend the hips through the elbow.
  • Classic from closed guard and mount; also from back and scrambles.
  • Gi sleeve grips help isolation; no-gi uses wrist control, overhooks, and tighter head control.
  • Pairs with the triangle—same climbs, different finishes.
  • Legal in IBJJF adult divisions as a standard joint lock; tap early to protect the elbow.

What Is the Armbar?

The armbar is a straight armlock: you trap one arm, position your hips against the opponent’s elbow, and extend so the joint hyperextends. In judo it is juji gatame; in BJJ it is a cornerstone submission from white belt through black belt. It is a key branch of closed guard.

It is not “legs over the head and yank.” High-percentage armbars control the near shoulder and head relationship so the elbow cannot hide, then finish with hip extension—not arm strength.

How Armbar Works

Isolate the arm. Get the elbow across your centerline with the thumb pointed up (or controlled so it cannot turn into a free escape).

Pin the posture. Knees squeeze, one leg over the head or across the face line, hips close to the shoulder.

Finish. Pinch knees, pull the wrist, elevate hips. If they stack, adjust angle or transition to triangle/omoplata rather than muscling a bad line.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve control makes isolation cleaner from closed guard; collar ties help break posture before the swing.

No-gi. Wrist control, overhooks, and head position replace sleeves. High-elbow / Williams structures often precede the armbar when fabric is gone.

Defenses. Gi defenders grab their own lapel or your pants; no-gi defenders clasp hands and stack. Your contingencies change with the handles available.

Mount finishes. Same skeletal finish; gi may add collar control while you climb to S-mount.

>What Does the Armbar Look Like in Practice?

From closed guard, Alex breaks Jordan’s posture, isolates the left arm, opens the guard, pivots, and throws the right leg over Jordan’s head. Hips extend; Jordan taps.

In no-gi, Alex uses an overhook and wrist control instead of a sleeve, then the same pivot—slower isolation, same finish geometry.

Types / Variations

From closed guard

The classroom armbar: posture break, isolation, pivot, finish. See closed guard.

From mount / S-mount

Climb, isolate, swing or step over into juji. S-mount shortens the path.

From the back and transitions

When they defend the RNC by extending an arm, the armbar appears. Also from failed triangles.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Closed guard, mount, back, scrambles, and as a conversion from triangle or omoplata.

Escapes. Stack and hide the elbow; hitchhiker; turn the thumb and pull free early. Late escapes are risky—tap when the elbow is loaded.

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Standard adult IBJJF legal joint lock in gi and no-gi. Youth divisions may restrict some joint locks—check the current rulebook for kids’ classes. Legal in ADCC.

Safety

Tap early. Elbows do not always hurt dramatically before damage. Do not crank on a stacked partner’s neck to finish.

Common mistakes

  • Swinging without controlling the head/shoulder.
  • Finishing with arms instead of hips.
  • Ignoring the stack defense.
  • In no-gi: waiting for a sleeve that is not there.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Why do people say juji gatame?

Judo name for the cross-body armlock. Same family as the BJJ armbar.

My armbar always gets stacked—what now?

Improve angle before you extend, pinch knees, or transition to triangle/omoplata. Do not arm-wrestle the stack.

Gi or no-gi—where is armbar easier?

Gi isolation is often easier with sleeves; no-gi finishes are still high percentage with wrist/overhook control.

Is the armbar safe to spar hard?

With early taps and controlled drilling, yes. Ego finishing is how elbows get injured.

Armbar from mount or guard first?

Most beginners learn guard first; mount finishes are equally essential. Learn both.

How does it connect to the triangle?

Shared climbs. If the choke fails, attack the arm; if the arm hides, re-lock the triangle. See triangle choke.

Can white belts use armbars in competition?

In typical adult IBJJF divisions, yes. Confirm juvenile/kids rules separately.

The Bottom Line

The armbar hyperextends the elbow with your hips after isolating an arm—classic from closed guard and mount in both gi and no-gi. Control the shoulder relationship, finish with hips, and keep the triangle ready when they defend.
