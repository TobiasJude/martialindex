Double-Under Escape

The double-under escape answers double-under pass pressure—do not let hips rise into a full stack, frame, hip escape/shrimp, and pummel the legs inside. Sibling: stack escape.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 16 Sep 2026 · Updated 16 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Parent threat: double-under pass.
  • Priority: keep hips down / create a side before the fold locks.
  • Tools: frames, shrimp, leg pummel. Related: stack escape.
  • Elbow-push and sit-up counters appear when timing is early.
  • Late stacks need limp-leg / granby style exits—train them carefully.

What Is Double-Under Escape?

Bottom retention versus double underhooks under both legs used to stack and pass. Up: escape, closed guard retention. Across: stack escape, single-under pass awareness.

How It Works

As soon as both underhooks appear, frame against shoulders/face, shift hips to a side, make legs heavy, and pummel feet back inside. If they lift, fight the grip, limp-leg to free knees, then reinsert hooks. Do not stay flat on your upper back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar frames and belt grips help sit-up counters (Marcelo-style elbow pushes).

No-gi. Wrist pins to hips and foot-on-hip frames replace cloth.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Double unders lock → pin wrists to hips → hip out → pummel feet inside → recover open or closed guard.

Types / Variations

Primary pattern

Early grip break + leg pummel.

Competition variation

Elbow-push / sit-up sweep counters.

Related option

Late limp-leg or granby when already stacked.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Triggered by double-under passing. Top answers: tighter clasp, higher stack, switch to stack pass finishes.

How It Compares

Vs stack escape: double-under escape specializes the bilateral-underhook entry into the stack problem.

Special Considerations

Rules & sport context

Universal.

Safety

Defense pages prioritize survival: protect neck/joints first, create frames, then escape. Tap early in live training; do not ego through chokes or joint locks. Avoid cervical folding—create a side early.

Common mistakes

  • Staying flat while hips rise.
  • Waiting until fully stacked to frame.
  • Pummeling without addressing their clasped hands.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a double-under escape?

Retention and counters against a passer with underhooks under both legs.

First priority?

Stop a full stack—angle off and frame early.

Pummel meaning?

Reinsert your legs between you and the passer after breaking their underhook control.

Gi counters?

Elbow-push sit-ups and collar frames are classic.

Related page?

Stack escape for when you are already folded.

Movement primitives?

Shrimp / hip escape.

Related notes?

Double-under pass, stack pass, shrimp, hip escape.

The Bottom Line

Double-under escape is anti-stack retention—frame early, shrimp to a side, and pummel the feet home before the fold owns your neck.
