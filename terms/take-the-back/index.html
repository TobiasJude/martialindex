Take the Back

Take the back is the family of transitions into back control—from turtle, mount turns, and guard entries including berimbolo. Secure seatbelt and hooks, then maintain back before you chase the RNC.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Chest-to-back before fancy hooks.
  • Seatbelt early; hooks second.
  • Block the near hip so they can’t sit to guard.
  • Don’t cross ankles.
  • Control before you finish the choke.

What Is Take the Back?

A transition concept into the highest-percentage control for many athletes.

Live siblings: back take (technique family page), berimbolo, turtle rides, gift-wrap paths.

Feeds maintain back and control before submit.

How Take the Back Works

Win chest-to-back connection and a seatbelt (or strong harness).

Control the near hip to stop the sit-back to guard.

Insert bottom hook, then top hook—or body triangle when available.

Fall to the choking-arm side; hand-fight for the neck.

If hooks fail, keep seatbelt and ride—see maintain back.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Collar seatbelts and bow-and-arrow threats; still prefer RNC mechanics often.

No-gi. Seatbelt + hooks / body triangle; hand-fighting is the whole game.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From turtle, Rayron-style collar transfer opens the hook insert for Avery.

When mount turns away, Jordan gift-wraps and chairs to the back.

Types / Variations

Turtle to back

Seatbelt, hip block, hooks.

Mount/technical mount to back

Follow the turn with seatbelt.

Guard / berimbolo paths

Invert and spin to back exposure.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries from turtle, failed passes (they turn), mount escapes that expose the back, and guard systems like berimbolo. Counters: face the attacker, peel hooks, clear seatbelt.

Take the Back vs Maintain Back

Factor Take the back Maintain back
Job Arrive with connection Keep connection under escape
Focus Seatbelt + first hooks Hand-fight, hip lock, micro-adjust
Failure Loose chest, free hip Chasing choke without control
Next Maintain, then submit RNC / collar when chin opens

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Back control with hooks typically scores under IBJJF-style rules—verify current IBJJF / ADCC criteria and time.

Safety

Never cross ankles behind them (footlock danger); ease body-triangle pressure in training.

Common mistakes

  • Hunting hooks with no seatbelt.
  • Crossing the feet.
  • Abandoning chest-to-back.
  • Forcing RNC before control is stable.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is take the back?

Transitions that put you behind with chest-to-back control.

First priority?

Seatbelt / chest connection—then hooks.

Berimbolo?

One popular guard path to back exposure—live sibling page.

Ankle cross?

Don’t—ankle lock danger.

Points?

Usually for established back control with hooks—verify rulebook.

Then what?

Maintain back, then finish.

From mount?

When they turn away—follow with seatbelt.

The Bottom Line

Take the back is the arrival—seatbelt, hip block, hooks—then maintain before you strangle.
