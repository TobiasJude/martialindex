Head and Arm Control

Head and arm control is a foundational Brazilian jiu-jitsu side-control configuration: crossface (or shoulder pressure) on the head plus control of the near arm—stopping frames, enabling mount transitions, and opening kimuras, Americanas, and arm-triangles.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Crossface + near-arm control = the default side-control pin for most gyms.
  • Stops shrimp frames and sets mount, kesa, and submission paths.
  • Gi collars/sleeves amplify the crossface; no-gi uses shoulder pressure and underhooks.
  • Soft crossface invites the underhook escape—keep the shoulder heavy.
  • Parent: side control; cousins: scarf hold, knee-on-belly.

What Is Head and Arm Control?

Head and arm control is the standard side control pin where the top player applies shoulder/crossface pressure against the opponent's head or neck while controlling the near arm—often trapping it or pinning it to the mat. It is the first side-control shape most beginners learn.

The configuration denies the near-side frame that fuels shrimps and underhook escapes, creating time to attack or advance to mount.

How Head and Arm Control Works

Crossface pressure turns their head away and flattens the near shoulder. Near-arm control removes their best frame. Hips stay heavy on the torso while knees block hip escapes. From here: climb to mount, switch to kesa, step to knee-on-belly, or attack Americana/kimura/arm-triangle.

Bottom player's job. Frame early, pummel the underhook, turn to knees, or shrimp before the crossface locks. Never let both the head and near arm die at once.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Far collar and sleeve grips amplify the crossface; paper-cutter chokes become available.

No-gi. Shoulder pressure, head position, and underhooks replace cloth; expect faster wrestle-ups if crossface softens.

MMA context. Head-and-arm control creates short punching lanes while limiting their near-side defense.

What Does Head and Arm Control Look Like in Practice?

Alex clears Jordan's knees, drops a heavy crossface, traps the near arm, and pins. When Jordan frames, Alex switches to knee-on-belly and mounts.

Gi. Alex grips the far collar to drive the crossface.

No-gi. Alex uses shoulder pressure and a gable behind the arm/head without collar grips.

Types / Variations

Classic head-and-arm pin

Crossface + near-arm trap, chest heavy—default side control.

To mount and knee-on-belly

Use the pin to clear the near elbow and step over to mount or pop to knee-on-belly.

Submission paths

Americana, kimura, arm-triangle, paper-cutter (gi), and step-over armbars.

To kesa / north-south

Sit to scarf hold or circle to north-south when the pin stalls.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries. Any successful guard pass; sprawl residuals; failed mount escapes that land you on the side.

Top threats. Mount, KOB, Americana/kimura, arm-triangle, kesa.

Bottom counters. Underhook and turn to knees, frame-and-shrimp to guard, invert, or force north-south you can more easily escape.

Head and Arm vs Scarf Hold

Factor Head and Arm Scarf Hold
Torso angle More parallel chest-to-chest Hips toward their head
Primary pressure Crossface + near arm Head wrap + near arm
Mount path Very direct Often via sit-through adjustments
Roll risk Lower if hips heavy Higher if hips high

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Legal. Stabilized side control scores the pass in IBJJF when criteria are met.

Safety

Crossface pressure can irritate necks and jaws—apply gradually in training. Tap early to shoulder locks.

Common mistakes

  • Soft crossface—underhook escapes return.
  • Chest too high—creating shrimp space.
  • Ignoring the near elbow before trying to mount.
  • In the gi: not using collar feeds that freeze the head.
  • In no-gi: abandoning underhooks on the far side.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is head and arm control in BJJ?

A side-control pin using crossface pressure on the head plus control of the near arm—the default side-control shape for many gyms.

How is it different from scarf hold?

Head-and-arm is more chest-to-chest; scarf hold orients hips toward the head with a head wrap.

How do you escape head and arm side control?

Frame early, pummel an underhook, and shrimp or turn to knees before the crossface fully flattens you.

Best submissions from head and arm control?

Americana, kimura, arm-triangle, paper-cutters in the gi, and mount transitions that finish later.

Does it work in no-gi?

Yes—replace collar crossfaces with shoulder pressure and tight near-arm control.

How do you mount from head and arm?

Clear the near elbow, control the head, and step the far knee over while keeping hips heavy.

Why does my side control feel light?

Usually a soft crossface and space at the hips. Lower your chest, drive the shoulder, and block the near hip with your knee.

The Bottom Line

Head and arm control is the default side-control pin: crossface plus near-arm control to stop escapes and feed mount and submissions. See side control, scarf hold, and mount.
