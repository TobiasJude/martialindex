Straight Ankle Lock

The straight ankle lock (Achilles lock) is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu leg lock that bites the Achilles with the forearm while hips extend—foundational from single-leg X, ashi garami, and 50/50, and a gateway into modern heel-hook games.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Bite high on the Achilles; toes toward armpit.
  • Hip extension + shoulder fall beats arm curl alone.
  • Legal pathway in many IBJJF divisions where heel hooks are not—verify.
  • Control the knee line before finishing.
  • Sibling: toe hold; parent hub: heel hook family literacy.

What Is Straight Ankle Lock?

The straight ankle lock traps the foot so the blade of your forearm compresses the Achilles while you arch away. It is often the first legal leglock students learn in gi rooms and remains high-percentage in no-gi when heel hooks are unavailable or as a threat that forces movement into other entanglements.

Link it to SLX, 50/50, and the broader heel hook encyclopedia hub.

How Straight Ankle Lock Works

Enter ashi/SLX (or outside ashi), get on the correct hip, walk the grip until toes seat in the armpit, bite the Achilles with the bony forearm, hike hands high, tuck the elbow under, then fall to the shoulder and extend hips.

Belly-down finishes appear when they stand or turn; keep the bite tight before you flip.

If the foot slips, reconnect entanglement rather than arm-curling air.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Pant grips help enter and retain ashi; finish is the same.

No-Gi. Primary modern home alongside heel hooks—ankle lock remains the “legal” threat in many rule sets.

Do not confuse “legal somewhere” with “legal in your bracket”—check the book.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

From SLX, Alex sweeps Jordan to the hip, seats the toes, bites the Achilles, and finishes with a shoulder fall and hip extension.

Jordan boots the foot; Alex switches to a toe-hold threat or outside heel configuration depending on rules.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: SLX sweeps, shin-to-shin, outside ashi, 50/50.

Counters: turn the heel out early, boot the foot, clear the bite, stand with inside position carefully.

Escapes: relieve Achilles pressure and reclaim knee line—tap if the tendon screams.

Straight Ankle Lock vs Heel Hook

Factor Straight Ankle Lock Heel Hook
Target feel Achilles compression Knee rotation via heel
IBJJF pattern Often wider legality Often restricted in gi
Control ask Bite + hip extension Knee line + heel seat
Role Gateway / primary in many gi rooms King of many no-gi rules

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Straight ankle locks are among the more broadly legal lower-body attacks in many IBJJF eras/divisions, but belt and age limits still apply—verify the current IBJJF rulebook. Heel hooks remain a separate legality question.

Safety

Tendon pain can spike. Apply slowly; never yank a stuck foot. Partners with Achilles history should modify.

Common mistakes

  • Finishing on the calf instead of Achilles.
  • Leaving the knee line free.
  • Arm curling without hip extension.
  • Ignoring rule differences vs heel hooks.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

What is a straight ankle lock?

An Achilles compression lock finished with hip extension from ashi-style control.

Same as heel hook?

No—different mechanism and often different legality.

Best positions?

SLX, ashi garami, 50/50.

Gi legal?

Often yes with belt limits—check current IBJJF.

Defense tip?

Clear the bite early; protect the Achilles angle.

When to switch to heel hook?

When rules allow and the heel seats with knee-line control.

Belly-down useful?

Yes against stand-ups—keep the bite first.

The Bottom Line

Straight ankle lock is the Achilles gateway of the leg game: seat the toes, bite high, extend hips. Wire it to SLX, heel hook, and toe hold—and verify your rulebook.
