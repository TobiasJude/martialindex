Uchi-Komi

Uchi-komi is judo’s repetitive entry training—fitting into throws again and again so kuzushi and tsukuri hold up when randori gets noisy.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Rehearsal for throws.
  • Static + moving both count.
  • Quality over zombie reps.
  • Leads to nage-komi.
  • Combination uchi-komi builds renzoku.
  • Partners’ Ukemi enables intensity.

What is uchi-komi?

Uchi-komi (打ち込み) is repetitive entry practice—fitting into throws again and again without necessarily completing every dump.

It grooves Kuzushi and tsukuri so Randori is not your first teacher.

How it works

Tori enters to the edge of kake; uke receives safely. Sets of 10–20 per side beat one exhausted max-effort.

Moving uchi-komi adds steps; static builds shape. Both matter for Seoi Nage (Judo), Uchi Mata (Judo), and ashi family throws.

Nage-komi (full throws) follows when Ukemi and control are ready.

Judogi vs no-gi

Gi uchi-komi uses standard grips. No-gi uchi-komi must invent honest hand fights—still useful for athletes who also train BJJ.

In practice

Three sets of Ouchi Gari uchi-komi, then link the last rep into Uchi Mata (Judo) without resetting grips—combination uchi-komi.

Types / variations

Static; moving; combination; resistance bands/partners; solo shadow entries.

Programming

Quality > zombie reps. Film your hip line.

Pair with Randori the same week so grooves meet chaos.

Compare with randori

Uchi-Komi is the rehearsal; Randori is opening night.

Safety

Uke’s job is sacred—protect their Ukemi.

Stop if knees/backs complain; adjust angles.

Common mistakes

  • Speed without kuzushi.
  • Always throwing the same side.
  • Never progressing to nage-komi.
  • Ignoring partner fatigue.

Uchi-komi is where timing hides. Count cadence sometimes, but also do reaction uchi-komi where uke steps randomly and you only enter on the weight change—especially for deashi and kouchi.

Combination sets prevent single-throw robots: ouchi entry into uchi-mata finish without re-gripping from scratch; kouchi into seoi; osoto feint into harai. Write two combination cards on the whiteboard and rotate them weekly.

Resistance should rise gradually. Cooperative uke builds shape; stubborn uke builds honesty. Switching without communication builds injuries.

Solo shadow uchi-komi travels well on road trips, but check it against a partner weekly or your angles drift. Film belt-line height and lead-leg placement.

End blocks with two to four nage-komi reps so the nervous system remembers completion—then bow and change roles. That rhythm feeds randori better than endless incomplete taps on the sleeve.

Training notes

Program this topic beside the judo style hub and the live siblings in Related notes. Prefer qualified coaching over video-only learning; use Martial Index pages as maps, not as unsupervised curricula.

If you are building a personal syllabus, pair concept pages with one throw and one ne-waza skill each week, then test in constrained randori. Track whether your ukemi, grips, and pin clock awareness improve—not only whether you “hit” a highlight throw.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Throw every rep?

Not required—many reps stop at fit-in.

How many?

Quality sets beat endless junk.

Solo?

Shadow helps; partners better.

Kids?

Yes with supervision.

No-gi uchi-komi?

Yes with adapted grips.

Vs nage-komi?

Nage-komi completes the throw.

Bored?

Add movement and combinations.

Before randori?

Ideal warm skill block.

The Bottom Line

Groove Kuzushi with Uchi-Komi, protect partners’ Ukemi, then open Randori.
