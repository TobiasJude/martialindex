Power Jab

A power jab is a committed Jab—more Power Line, more step—trading some snap speed for heavier impact.

Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Committed lead straight under Jab.
  • Step and punch together.
  • Mix with Snap Punch so it stays surprising.
  • Rear hand stays on the phone.

What Is Power Jab?

The power jab sits under the Jab pillar: same lead hand, straighter path, but intentional mass behind it.

Fighters search it when the snappy jab is ignored and they need the lead hand to hurt, stall pressure, or set a harder Cross.

Contrast with Snap Punch / speed jab and with Pawing Jab (which is not meant to hurt).

Martial Index files Power Jab under Boxing so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Boxing style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Power Jab behaves in stance, range, and exchanges.

How Power Jab Works

Step and punch together: lead foot and fist land as one so you do not step-then-arm-push.

Rotate the lead hip and extend the lead shoulder; exhale; snap or drive through without collapsing the chin forward.

Rear hand stays home. Recover to Boxing Stance. If you need pure speed, switch back to a Snap Punch jab.

Mechanically, successful power jab work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Boxing Stance with hands alive.

If Boxing Range is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you claw air; too close and you collide into clinches or wild exchanges you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: straights need Slip/Parry awareness; hooks need Bob and Weave literacy; power shots need a clean Power Line.

Amateur vs Pro / Rules Notes

Under pro boxing rules, power jab lives inside a hands-only striking sport—no kicks or elbows—so recovery and clinch etiquette differ from Muay Thai cousins.

Amateur boxing (Olympic/AIBA-style heritage and modern open-headgear variants) historically nudged higher guards and different scoring optics; still, chin-up habits lose.

Gym culture may drill power jab daily; fight night may shrink the toolkit based on opponent style and judging.

Confirm the bout agreement and glove size before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Pad round: alternate three snap jabs and one power jab so the opponent (or mitt holder) cannot time a single rhythm.

Fight: power jab to the chest to freeze feet, then Cross or Lead Hook.

Fight picture: power jab appears when Boxing Range and timing say so, then yields to the next punch in a Combination or to an exit.

Gym test: can you apply power jab after a Feint and still recover your guard? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Pushing the jab (arm-only shove).
  • Lunging the head ahead of the fist.
  • Dropping the rear hand.
  • Throwing every jab as a power jab until the lead shoulder dies.
  • Treating power jab as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowbox power jab inside full rounds; return to Boxing Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so power jab connects to a Combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether power jab appears in the right Boxing Range band.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for shell defenses, check hooks, and pull counters.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe power jab as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor Boxing Range, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put power jab behind heavy boxing pressure; others behind movement and counters. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

Use internal links in this entry as a study path—move from pillars like Boxing Stance, Jab, and Slip into siblings, then return here after sparring to re-read mistakes with fresh context.

Safety note: encyclopedia pages are reference material, not live coaching. Train under qualified instructors, especially for head movement and power punching where mistiming injures partners.

Keep vocabulary precise. Fighters overload words like “guard,” “pressure,” and “timing.” Martial Index’s Boxing entries keep power jab scoped so you are not dumped into unrelated BJJ articles that share casual language.

A practical weekly plan: two technical rounds focused on power jab, one situational sparring theme that forces it, and one film review asking where timing failed.

If you compete, write a one-line rule note for power jab under your next card (glove size, round length, amateur vs pro judging) so training intensity matches the bout.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Power jab vs regular jab?

Same hand and line; power jab adds step/hip mass. Regular snap jab prioritizes speed and recovery.

Will it telegraph?

More than a flicker, less than a haymaker—if you only ever power-jab, yes. Mix with snap and feint.

Body power jab?

Yes—see body-jab with more commitment, still recover high.

Best follow-ups?

Cross, lead hook, or exit step—build a combination, do not admire it.

Strength or technique?

Technique first: kinetic chain beats arm muscle.

The Bottom Line

Use the power jab when you need the lead hand to matter—then get back to variety so it does not become a metronome opponents time.
