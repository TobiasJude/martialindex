Rear Triangle

The rear triangle (ura sankaku / ushiro sankaku) applies a triangle choke from behind: on the back, you thread a leg over the shoulder and around the neck, lock a figure-four, and finish with hip angle instead of a rear naked choke.

Published 11 Sep 2026 · Updated 11 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • It is a triangle from the back—not a body triangle (different job).
  • Use it when RNC hand fighting stalls; keep the back while you switch weapons.
  • Angle the hips toward the trapped arm so the calf cuts a carotid, not the spine.
  • Parent: triangle choke. Platform: back control.
  • Chains with armbars and RNCs from the same control.

What Is Rear Triangle?

A rear triangle locks your legs in a figure-four around their neck and one arm from behind. You are still the back player; the choking tool just switches from arms to legs. Judo language (ura / ushiro sankaku) shows up in instructionals for a reason—it is an old sankaku idea, newly high-percentage in modern no-gi.

It is easy to confuse with a body triangle. A body triangle wraps the waist for control. A rear triangle wraps the neck line for a choke (and often still controls the back). You can have one, then the other; they are not synonyms.

Martial Index files it under back control and the triangle family, with siblings inverted triangle, side triangle, and mounted triangle.

How Rear Triangle Works

From hooks or a failing RNC, isolate one arm (cross-grip or kimura seatbelt helps) and step or throw a leg over that shoulder so the shin can cross the neck.

Lock the figure-four on the trapped-arm side. Scoot your hips so the calf sits on the side of the neck, not across the back of the skull. Pinch the knees.

Pull the head, walk toward the trapped arm, and squeeze. Hands can still strip defenses or attack the trapped arm with an armbar.

If the angle is wrong you will only crank the spine. Reset the hip walk rather than squeezing harder.

Gi vs No-Gi

Gi. Sleeve and collar helps isolate the arm before the leg threads. You can also mix bow-and-arrow threats.

No-gi. The modern boom. Wrist control and kimura seatbelts replace cloth.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Alex cannot win the chin, threads a rear triangle, and finishes the choke without leaving the back.

Jordan uses the rear triangle as control, then switches back to a figure-four RNC when the hands finally open.

Types / Variations

Over-shoulder rear triangle

Leg crosses the neck from the back—the usual choke picture.

Under-armpit / control rear triangle

More control, different finishing options (arm attacks, cranks to avoid).

Turtle / side rear triangle

Entries that never look like a textbook rear-mount first.

Entries, Counters, and Escapes

Entries: defended RNC, kimura seatbelt, turtle back attacks. Counters: keep the arm in, face the threat, peel the locking foot. Escapes: turn into the triangle early or extract the head—tap if the calf is already on the carotid.

Rear Triangle vs Rear Naked Choke

Factor Rear triangle Rear naked choke
Tool Legs (sankaku) Arms (hadaka-jime)
When Hands sealed / chin down Chin window available
Control leftover Often still on the back Must keep seatbelt + hooks
Confusable with Body triangle (waist) Short choke / palm-to-palm

Special Considerations

IBJJF, ADCC, and sport context

Adult triangle chokes are standard legal submissions. Always verify the current IBJJF and ADCC (or event) rulebooks for division, belt, age, and gi vs no-gi. Martial Index gives orientation—not invented illegal-move lists.

Safety

A mis-angled rear triangle can crank the neck and spine. Walk the hips to a carotid cut; tap early; no trophy squeezing.

Common mistakes

  • Locking across the back of the neck instead of the side.
  • Calling a body triangle a rear triangle.
  • Abandoning all upper-body control while you fuss with the feet.
  • Squeezing a square, un-walked figure-four.

Related notes

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Is a rear triangle the same as a body triangle?

No. Body triangle = waist control. Rear triangle = neck-and-arm sankaku from behind.

When do I choose it over RNC?

When the chin is sealed or you already have a leg over the shoulder.

Judo name?

Ura or ushiro sankaku—same family.

Gi or no-gi?

Both; no-gi made it famous again.

Can I armbar from here?

Often yes—the trapped arm is already isolated.

Beginner move?

Intermediate. Learn front triangles and back control first.

Legal?

Standard adult triangle—verify your current book.

The Bottom Line

Rear triangle is sankaku from the back: thread the leg, lock the figure-four, walk the hips to a carotid cut. Use it when the RNC stalls, and do not confuse it with a body triangle.
