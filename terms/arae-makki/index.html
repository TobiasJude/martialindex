Arae Makki (Low Block)

Arae makki is the low block: chamber and drop the forearm across the low line—foundation defense beside Momtong Makki (Middle Block) and Olgul Makki (High Block).

What Is Arae Makki (Low Block)?

Arae makki (아래막기) is the low section block using the outer forearm to cover the lower trunk/groin line in fundamentals.

It appears early in nearly every curriculum and throughout Poomsae.

Compare with Momtong Makki (Middle Block): middle block covers the mid line; low block covers down.

Sport Kyorugi (Sparring) defense leans more on footwork than classical blocks—but block literacy still builds structure.

How Arae Makki (Low Block) Works

Chamber the blocking arm (often near the opposite shoulder/ear line per school), then drive downward across the low target line, finishing with a firm forearm angle and fist orientation per standard.

Hips and stance (Ap Kubi (Forward Stance), Ap Seogi (Walking Stance), etc.) settle with the block.

Other hand chambers or pulls to the hip as coached (reaction hand).

Eyes stay up—do not watch your own wrist.

Mechanically, successful arae makki (low block) work is usually a chain: stance integrity → timing → impact or control → recovery to Taekwondo Stance with hands alive.

If distance is wrong, even perfect form fails—too far and you kick air; too close and you collide into clinches or jammed chambers you did not plan.

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: kicks need Arae Makki (Low Block)/Momtong Makki (Middle Block) literacy when drills reverse roles; sparring tools need Hogu and Electronic Scoring (PSS) awareness.

WT / ITF / Rules Notes

Under World Taekwondo (Olympic) competition, arae makki (low block) is interpreted through World Taekwondo Rules, Kyorugi (Sparring) scoring zones, and often Electronic Scoring (PSS)—not bare gym aesthetics.

ITF Taekwondo schools may share Korean vocabulary while differing on sine-wave motion, hand techniques, and pattern (tul) emphasis; do not assume WT footage equals ITF syllabus.

Gym culture may drill arae makki (low block) daily; contest night may shrink the toolkit based on weight class, PSS sensor thresholds, and coaching strategy.

Confirm the event agreement (WT, ITF, open, recreational) before importing sparring volume into contest intensity.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Basics: step into forward stance with arae makki on each count.

Pattern: opening moves of many Taegeuk forms feature low blocks.

Fight picture: arae makki (low block) appears when range and timing say so, then yields to the next kick or exit back to Taekwondo Stance.

Gym test: can you apply arae makki (low block) after a feint and still recover guard and balance? If not, connect it to combinations instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Finishing with a floppy wrist.
  • Blocking too far across the body (over-rotation).
  • Rising the shoulder into the ear.
  • Looking down.
  • Skipping chamber entirely.
  • Treating arae makki (low block) as a highlight instead of a range-and-timing skill.
  • Skipping recovery—landing then posing with hands down.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadowkick arae makki (low block) inside full rounds; return to Taekwondo Stance after every attempt.

Pads: technical → moving → forced follow-up so arae makki (low block) connects to a combination.

Partner drills beat bag-only fluency; feed realistic rhythm so counter timing is part of the skill.

Film sparring weekly and ask whether arae makki (low block) appears in the right range band for Kyorugi (Sparring) or forms context.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for spinning kicks, axe kicks, and PSS-targeted Palding (Trunk Scoring) work.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe arae makki (low block) as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under stress, poor distance, or a stance that collapses when pressure arrives.

When you compare gyms, you will see stylistic accents: some put arae makki (low block) behind heavy Olympic pressure kicking; others behind traditional Poomsae precision. The encyclopedia definition stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Inner or outer forearm?

Typically outer forearm for standard arae makki—confirm school.

Used in sparring?

Classical shape less than footwork; still trains structure.

Next block?

Momtong Makki (Middle Block).

Stance pairing?

Often Ap Kubi (Forward Stance) or walking stance in forms.

Same as karate down block?

Related idea across arts; use Taekwondo naming and chamber standards.

The Bottom Line

Make arae makki crisp and settled—messy low blocks telegraph every later technique in a form.
