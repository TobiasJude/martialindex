Inside Trip

An inside trip reaps the inside leg while you control the body—often off a Underhook (Wrestling)—to dump the opponent.

By Tobias Mesevage · Published 12 Sep 2026 · Updated 12 Sep 2026 · Plain markdown

Key Takeaways

  • Inside trip attacks the inside leg.
  • Needs upper-body control.
  • Sibling of outside trip.
  • Finish on top.
  • Know Greco leg limits.

What Is Inside Trip?

An inside trip is a takedown that uses your leg against the opponent’s inside/near leg while upper-body control (underhook, body lock) keeps them from posting.

It thrives in Greco-friendly clinches and freestyle/folkstyle pocket fighting.

Sibling: Outside Trip. Related: Knee Pick, Lateral Drop.

In Wrestling, inside trips punish square stances and lazy feet in the clinch.

Martial Index files Inside Trip under Wrestling so searchers get definition, mechanics, and live batch links—not a thin glossary stub.

Read this beside the Wrestling style hub when you need the art’s big picture; keep the focus on how Inside Trip behaves across freestyle, folkstyle, and Greco-Roman contexts.

How Inside Trip Works

Secure an Underhook (Wrestling) or tight Body Lock, step your tripping leg behind their inside leg, and drive/rotate them over the blocked foot.

Keep head position safe and finish on top—don’t trip yourself into a scramble underneath.

If they step out, chain to an outside trip or snap.

Level and posture matter: too upright and you get countered; too low and you lose the upper-body lock.

Mechanically, successful inside trip work is usually a chain: Wrestling Stance integrity → entry or control → finish, score, or recovery.

If posture collapses or hips float, even textbook grips fail—top riders meet Sit-Out and Switch Escape pressure; shooters meet a Sprawl (Wrestling).

Pair offense with the defensive layer this tool implies: rides need Escape literacy; turns need Near Fall awareness; trips need base recovery.

Rules / Style Notes

Under United World Wrestling freestyle rules, inside trip is judged through takedowns, exposure, and activity—not riding time.

Under NCAA/NFHS folkstyle, control, escapes, reversals, and near falls reshape how inside trip is valued once wrestlers hit the mat.

Greco-Roman bans holds below the waist, so leg-coupled cousins of inside trip either disappear or migrate into upper-body variants.

Confirm the rule set before importing gym habits into contest intensity—folkstyle riding does not equal freestyle Par Terre.

Greco-Roman prohibits using legs to trip—do not import freestyle trips into Greco bouts.

What Does It Look Like in Practice?

Underhook digs; inside trip dumps them to the hip for two.

Body lock inside trip when they square up in the Greco clinch.

Match picture: inside trip appears when stance, ties, and timing say so, then yields to a finish, a Go-Behind, or a reset to neutral.

Room test: can you apply inside trip after a Collar Tie or Russian Tie exchange and still recover posture? If not, chain it instead of isolating forever.

Common Mistakes

  • Tripping without upper-body control.
  • Hooking the wrong leg.
  • Falling under instead of across.
  • Telegraphing the step against elite wrestlers.
  • Treating inside trip as a highlight instead of a stance-and-timing skill.
  • Shooting or turning without a recovery plan when the opponent bridges or scrambles.
  • Ignoring sibling tools that set or cover the technique.

Training Notes

Shadow-wrestle inside trip inside full rounds; return to Wrestling Stance after every attempt.

Partner drills: technical → moving → forced follow-up so inside trip connects to a chain (shot → finish, or ride → turn).

Live situational wrestling beats drill-only fluency; feed realistic hand fights so timing is part of the skill.

Film practice weekly and ask whether inside trip appears with clean hip position and a real Level Change when shots are involved.

Progress intensity with a coach—especially for throws, neck ties, and pinning combinations.

Underhook pummel → inside trip live.

Chain drills: inside fake to outside.

Study Notes

Coaches often describe inside trip as “simple to show, hard to own.” The gap is usually not a missing secret—it is missing reps under hand-fighting stress, a collapsed Wrestling Stance, or hips that never get under the opponent.

When you compare rooms, you will see stylistic accents: freestyle rooms push Exposure chains; folkstyle rooms push ride-and-turn; Greco rooms push upper-body locks. The encyclopedia definition of inside trip stays stable even when accents change.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in forums, and under instructional videos—answered in Martial Index voice.

Inside vs outside trip?

Which of their legs you attack relative to your position.

Legal in Greco?

Trips with legs are restricted in Greco—upper-body throws dominate; confirm rules.

Folkstyle?

Fully used in freestyle/folkstyle contexts where legs are legal.

Best setup?

Deep Underhook (Wrestling).

Defense?

Keep feet active, underhook them back, step over the trip.

Related throws?

Lateral Drop, headlock throws.

The Bottom Line

Trip with a lock, not with hope—upper body first, foot second.
