What Is Wrestling?

Wrestling covers several closely related grappling sports. Freestyle and Greco-Roman are Olympic styles; folkstyle (collegiate) dominates scholastic wrestling in the United States. All emphasize taking the opponent down and controlling them—usually toward a pin—without punches or kicks.

Greco-Roman restricts attacks to the upper body. Freestyle and folkstyle allow leg attacks such as singles and doubles. Scoring systems differ, but the shared athletic core is level changing, penetration steps, chain wrestling, and hip control.

How Wrestling Works

A wrestling exchange often starts with stance and motion, then a shot or throw-by, then scramble management. On top, rides and turns expose the back; on bottom, escapes and reversals reset the position. There are no gi grips and no sport submissions in standard scholastic/Olympic wrestling—control and exposure score instead.

In MMA and submission grappling, wrestling is frequently the entry system: takedowns create the top position from which BJJ submissions or ground striking happen. Defensively, sprawling and front-headlock scrambles are foundational.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in gyms, and when fighters compare styles.

Folkstyle vs freestyle vs Greco—what’s the difference?

Folkstyle emphasizes riding and control time common in U.S. schools. Freestyle scores exposures and allows leg attacks under Olympic rules. Greco-Roman forbids attacking below the waist.

Does wrestling include chokes?

Standard scholastic and Olympic wrestling do not finish by submission choke. Front headlocks and cradles are control tools; blood chokes belong to submission grappling / BJJ / judo newaza contexts.

Why do MMA coaches recruit wrestlers?

Because takedown offense and takedown defense decide where the fight happens. Top control also sets up strikes and submissions.
