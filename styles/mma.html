What Is Mixed Martial Arts?

Mixed martial arts (MMA) is a combat sport in which athletes may strike and grapple under a unified ruleset (commonly a variant of the Unified Rules). Fights can end by knockout, technical knockout, submission, referee stoppage, or judges’ decision.

Unlike single-discipline sports, MMA rewards cross-training: a wrestler’s takedown must survive a striker’s clinch; a BJJ submission must be set up against punches; a boxer’s jab must account for kicks and level changes.

How Mixed Martial Arts Works

MMA phases roughly into range striking, clinch, takedown exchanges, and ground fighting (including ground-and-pound and submissions). Cage or ring walls change angles compared with open mats. Weight classes, glove rules, and illegal techniques (for example certain small-joint manipulations or strikes to the back of the head) shape what “legal” looks like in a given promotion.

Vocabulary on Martial Index bridges disciplines: BJJ terms such as guard and rear naked choke appear inside MMA ground exchanges; Muay Thai clinch and boxing footwork appear when the fight stays up.

FAQ

Questions we hear on the mats, in gyms, and when fighters compare styles.

Is MMA its own martial art?

MMA is a sport and ruleset more than a single traditional art. Athletes assemble a game from multiple disciplines. Gyms may teach “MMA classes,” but the techniques still come from boxing, wrestling, BJJ, Muay Thai, and related systems.

Why do so many MMA finishes look like BJJ?

Because once the fight hits the ground, positional control and blood chokes—especially the rear naked choke—transfer cleanly. Striking on the ground (ground-and-pound) is the other major finishing path.

How is MMA different from sport BJJ?

MMA allows strikes, usually uses smaller gloves, and often limits stalling on the fence. Sport BJJ focuses on grappling scoring and submissions without punches.
