How to start Brazilian jiu-jitsu

A practical first-class guide to BJJ: choosing a class, gi vs no-gi, the positions to learn first, and how to review what you learned.

Choose a beginner class

Look for a class that teaches positions, partner cooperation, and how to stop an exchange. Tell the coach it is your first session and ask how they pair new students. A useful trial class leaves you able to name one position and explain one goal; it does not need to leave you exhausted. Ask about fees, loan equipment, and whether sparring is optional before you book.

Gi or no-gi?

A gi is the jacket and trousers used in many BJJ classes; athletes can use permitted fabric grips. No-gi usually uses a rashguard and shorts and relies on grips on the body. Start with the format your chosen beginner class actually teaches. You do not need to settle on a permanent preference before trying either. Follow the school’s clothing policy, remove jewelry, keep nails short, and bring clean training clothes.

Understand the map before collecting submissions

Start with the difference between guard and a pin. From guard, the bottom person uses their legs to manage the other person. From mount or side control, the top person has moved past those legs. Learn to identify where you are, whether you are on top or bottom, and the immediate goal: retain position, escape, or improve control. A submission name is more useful once you know the position it comes from.

Make the first session cooperative

Agree on the task and pace with your partner. Ask your coach to demonstrate tapping and immediate release before practising submissions. During a new drill, rehearse the position and reset rather than racing to finish. You can decline a round or stop a drill. The coach should be able to explain what a beginner is expected to do and what they should leave for later.

A simple learning loop

Before class, read one entry that matches the lesson. After class, write three short notes: where the position starts, its main goal, and one thing you could not make work. Ask that question at the next class. Review the same position from both roles before adding another branch. This makes the catalog a memory aid for coached practice rather than a list to memorize.

Check your understanding

Can you explain why closed guard and mount are different even though one person is between the other person’s legs in each? In closed guard the bottom player’s legs wrap around the top player. In mount the top player straddles the bottom player’s torso. If those pictures are still unclear, revisit the two entries before studying attacks.
